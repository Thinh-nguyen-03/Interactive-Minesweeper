# C106: David Sacks: Holdings, Ethics Waivers & AI & Crypto Policy

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Type:** Check · Tech & Media Companies

## The Claim

> What did David Sacks's ethics waivers allow him to keep, and which policies he shaped affect those holdings?

## Bottom Line

The two White House waivers, both read in full, document the core facts.

## What Holds Up

- Sacks served as an unpaid special government employee (SGE) under a 130-day limit while remaining a Craft Ventures partner.
- The 5 March 2025 crypto waiver let Sacks work on digital-asset policy while he and Craft kept stakes in digital-asset companies.
- A second waiver covered AI, issued in mid-2025 (mostly accurate)
- Sacks and Craft kept hundreds of AI and crypto-linked holdings (NYT: 708 tech investments, 449 AI-related, 20 crypto) (mostly accurate)
- Sacks shaped the GENIUS Act and crypto policy while Craft held crypto-infrastructure stakes such as BitGo (mostly accurate)
- While czar, Sacks co-hosted a $1.5M-per-person crypto and AI fundraiser for the pro-Trump super PAC MAGA Inc (mostly accurate)
- Lawmakers and watchdogs asked for answers or investigations, and oversight bodies responded.
- Sacks, Craft and the White House dispute the conflict-of-interest reporting; Sacks threatened or sued the NYT (mostly accurate)

## What Needs Correcting

- OGE approved Sacks's waivers and found he had no conflicts (partly accurate)
- C34 says Craft's remaining crypto stakes were 'about 3.7% of its assets'. The waiver's Schedule A gives 3.714% of Sacks's own total investment assets, not of Craft's assets.
- C34 and C54 date the AI waiver to 'June or July 2025' or 'mid-2025'. The memo is undated, but its text places it in March 2025 and the NYT says he received both waivers in March. It was posted in a June 2025 folder on whitehouse.gov.
- C34 says Sacks 'became PCAST co-chair' in March 2026. Both March 2025 waivers already say he agreed to co-chair PCAST, and Warren's 6 March 2025 letter addresses him as PCAST chair. In March 2026 he left the czar post, kept the PCAST role, and PCAST's members were named.
- C34 says he left 'at the 130-day limit' as if settled. Sacks says his non-consecutive 130 days were used up, but no day count has been published, and the question of whether he passed the limit in 2025 has not been answered.
- C34 reports, attributed to Sacks, that 'OGE approved his waivers'. The waivers say the White House Counsel granted them after consulting OGE; OGE's position is not public.
- C34's 7.8% BitGo stake is the NYT's figure. BitGo's S-1 (excerpt) shows 7,421,851 Class A shares in three Craft I funds, with Sacks making their investment and voting decisions. The crypto waiver puts BitGo at 2.468% of his total assets. The percentage of BitGo was not checked against the filing.
- C34's relationship dates the crypto divestment to 2025-01. The waiver says liquid crypto was sold before 20 Jan 2025, BITW on 22 Jan 2025, and some private holdings (Animoca, Republic, CoinList, Sequoia funds) were still being sold in March 2025.
- C65 and C68 contain no substantive Sacks findings (C65 lists him only as an entity pointing to C34), so they need no correction. C68's Nvidia section could cross-reference the NYT reporting on Sacks's chip-export role.

## What Is Unproven

- Sacks exceeded the SGE limit of 130 days in a 365-day period.
- Sacks or people close to him profited from the strategic bitcoin reserve announcements.
- Sacks drove chip-export decisions (UAE deal, China sales) that benefited Nvidia and AI companies he invests in.
- The AI Action Plan and related orders favoured companies in Sacks's portfolio, such as defence start-ups.
- Sacks's All-In podcast and related businesses gained from his government role, including through the July 2025 AI summit.

## In More Detail

- David Sacks served as an unpaid special government employee while staying a Craft Ventures partner.
- White House Counsel David Warrington granted him limited waivers under 18 U.S.C.
- 208(b)(1): for digital assets on 5 March 2025, and for AI in an undated memo whose text places it in March 2025 (it was posted in a June 2025 folder).
- The waivers record more than $200M of crypto sales (at least $85M his own) and pending sales of Meta, Amazon, TSMC and xAI.
- They also list what he kept: Craft fund stakes in about 20 digital-asset companies (BitGo largest, under 2.5% of his total assets), small AI start-up stakes, and hundreds of software and hardware holdings, including Palantir directly.
- New York Times reporting (30 Nov 2025) links his policy work on the GENIUS Act, chip exports and the AI Action Plan to companies he or Craft hold, and to his All-In podcast.
- No authority has found a violation.
- Whether he exceeded the 130-day limit was never answered with a day log: he left the czar post on 26 March 2026, saying his non-consecutive 130 days were used up, and stayed on as PCAST co-chair.
- Sacks, his spokeswoman and the White House deny any conflict; Sacks calls the Times story a 'hoax' and a 'nothing burger', and the Times stands by it.

## Sub-Claims

### 1. Sacks served as an unpaid special government employee (SGE) under a 130-day limit while remaining a Craft Ventures partner.

**Confirmed** · Evidence: Documented

- Both waivers (read in full) say he 'agreed to be a special government employee serving as Special Advisor for AI and Crypto' and is 'expected to work ... for 130 days or less in a one-year period' (18 U.S.C. 202(a)).
- The March waiver names him chair of the President's Working Group on Digital Asset Markets.
- Both waivers also say he 'agreed to co-chair' PCAST, so he held the PCAST title from early 2025, not only from March 2026.
- The waivers call him co-founder and general partner of Craft Ventures.
- The NYT reports he is not paid for government work.
- Warren's letters note that SGE status lets him keep earning from Craft (18 U.S.C. 209).
- Trump announced the appointment on 5 Dec 2024, and he began work on or around 20 Jan 2025 (Warren-Stansbury letter, read in full).
- Dates: early 2025; March 2026; 5 Dec 2024; 20 Jan 2025
- Sources: [1](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Memo-David-Sacks-3.5.2025-1.pdf) [2](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/David-Sacks.pdf) [5](https://www.warren.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/david_sacks_sge_letter_91625.pdf) [9](https://eforum2025.blogspot.com/2025/12/silicon-valleys-man-in-white-house-is.html)

### 2. Sacks exceeded the SGE limit of 130 days in a 365-day period.

**Unverified**

- Grade: allegation without a finding. (1) Record: the 17 Sept 2025 letter from Warren, Stansbury and six others (read in full) calculated that working every calendar day from 20 Jan 2025 would put his 130th day at 29 May 2025, and every business day at 25 July 2025.
- The letter cited OGE guidance that any day with more than de minimis work counts.
- It asked for his SF-50 and a day-by-day list by 1 Oct 2025.
- The letter quotes the SF Standard: the White House said it 'counted' only days he worked as czar, and Craft said he split weeks between D.C. and Silicon Valley. (2) No day log, SF-50 or reply was found. (3) On 26 March 2026 Sacks told Bloomberg he had 'used up' his time; TechCrunch (read in full) calls it a 'non-consecutive 130-day stint'.
- He moved to PCAST co-chair, which DL News and TechCrunch describe as advisory. (4) No OGE, inspector-general or other finding on the day count was found.
- How the White House counted days, and whether a new 365-day period began in 2026, is not public.
- Dates: 17 Sept 2025; 20 Jan 2025; 29 May 2025; 25 July 2025; 1 Oct 2025

### 3. The 5 March 2025 crypto waiver let Sacks work on digital-asset policy while he and Craft kept stakes in digital-asset companies.

**Confirmed**

- Grade: documented (waiver read in full: 8-page memo plus schedules A-C, signed by White House Counsel David Warrington on 5 March 2025).
- Sold, per the memo: he and Craft sold all liquid crypto (including BTC, ETH and SOL) before 20 Jan 2025; he sold his Bitwise 10 Crypto Index Fund (BITW) on 22 Jan 2025 and his Coinbase and Robinhood stock; he sold LP stakes in Multicoin and Blockchain Capital; Craft sold its Multicoin and Bitwise Asset Management interests.
- Over $200M divested in total, 'at least $85 million' directly his.
- He also started selling LP stakes in Sequoia funds and about 90 other VC funds; the Sequoia funds (1.2% of his assets) were set to close on 31 March 2025.
- Still held (Schedule A): Craft-fund stakes in 20 digital-asset companies totalling 3.714% of his total investment assets.
- The largest are BitGo (2.468%) and Lightning Labs (1.131%); the others include dYdX Trading, Chia Network, Dapper Labs, Fold, River Financial and Talos.
- Schedule B: Beldore Capital Fund (an S&P 500 exchange fund, 5.07%) and AL Venture/AngelList (0.03%).
- Schedule C (sale pending): Animoca Brands, Republic, CoinList, Sequoia funds.
- Reasoning: the interests are 'not so substantial' (5 C.F.R. 2640.301(b)(4)), and the role needs industry expertise.
- Limits: no new digital-asset holdings; Craft to be instructed likewise; consult an ethics official if holdings pass 5% of his assets.
- The waiver covers only 'particular matters of general applicability', not matters involving specific parties.
- It says SGEs cannot get certificates of divestiture, so his sales were taxable.
- OGE was 'consulted' beforehand.
- Amounts: Over $200M; at least $85 million; 1.2%; 3.714%; 2.468%
- Dates: 5 March 2025; 20 Jan 2025; 22 Jan 2025; 31 March 2025
- Sources: [1](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Memo-David-Sacks-3.5.2025-1.pdf) [18](https://levernews.com/trump-issues-ethics-waiver-for-his-ai-crypto-czar) [4](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/oge_sacks_letter.pdf)

### 4. A second waiver covered AI, issued in mid-2025.

**Mostly accurate** · Evidence: Documented (39-page PDF, memo read in full, schedules skimmed)

- It is a 'Limited Waiver Pursuant to 18 U.S.C.
- 208(b)(1) Regarding A.I.
- Assets', signed by Warrington but undated on its face.
- Its text says TSMC and Amazon 'will be fully sold by March 31, 2025', Meta by the end of Q2 2025, and the Sequoia stakes 'by the end of this month'.
- That places the signing in March 2025, and the NYT says he received both waivers 'in March'.
- The file sits in a June 2025 upload folder on whitehouse.gov, which is likely why outlets call it a June or July waiver.
- Contents: direct AI interests pending sale (Schedule A: Meta 1.762%, Amazon 0.034%, TSMC 0.035% of his total assets); Craft-fund AI interests of 0.045% (Schedule B: xAI pending sale, plus Groq, Etched, Runway, Stability AI and others at about 0%).
- It states he started or finished divesting 'over 99%' of potentially conflicting holdings and that Craft funds did so for 'over 97%'.
- The waiver also covers software-as-a-service interests (Schedules C and D, 'just over 13%' of his assets) and hardware interests (Schedules E and F, under 2%), 'out of an abundance of caution'.
- It says many SaaS companies 'do not currently use' AI materially but 'many ... are likely to'.
- Palantir is listed as a direct SaaS interest at 1.819%.
- Limits: Craft must consult the White House Counsel's office before closing new investments.
- The waiver does not cover specific-party matters or matters affecting Craft Ventures itself beyond the listed fund interests.
- Amounts: 1.762%; 0.034%; 0.035%; 0.045%; about 0%
- Dates: March 31, 2025; March 2025; June 2025
- Sources: [2](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/David-Sacks.pdf) [9](https://eforum2025.blogspot.com/2025/12/silicon-valleys-man-in-white-house-is.html) [10](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/30/new-report-examines-how-david-sacks-might-profit-from-trump-administration-role/)

### Summary

The two White House waivers (both read in full) document the core of this brief:

  - Kept: Craft-fund stakes in 20 digital-asset companies, worth 3.714% of *his* total assets. The largest are BitGo (2.468%) and Lightning Labs (1.131%).

- **Responses.** Sacks, his spokeswoman and the White House deny any conflict. Sacks calls the Times story a "nothing burger" from a "hoax factory". The Times stands by it.

**Evidence rule applied.** Holding an asset, or working on a policy that helps a company in one's portfolio, is not by itself a violation. Section 208 bars participation in particular matters affecting one's financial interest unless a 208(b)(1) waiver finds the interest "not so substantial". Both waivers make that finding for matters of general applicability only, not for matters involving specific parties.

### Corrections to older checks
- **C34, Craft's crypto stakes.** C34 says "about 3.7% of its assets". The waiver figure, 3.714%, is a share of Sacks's own total investment assets, not of Craft's assets.
- **C34 and C54, AI waiver date.** They date it to "June or July 2025" or "mid-2025". The memo is undated; its text points to March 2025, and it was posted in a June 2025 folder.
- **C34, PCAST.** C34 says he "became PCAST co-chair" in March 2026. He was designated co-chair in 2025 (both waivers, and Warren's March 2025 letter). In March 2026 PCAST became his sole role and its members were named.
- **C34, the 130-day limit.** C34 treats his departure "at the 130-day limit" as settled. It rests on Sacks's own statement; no day count has been published.
- **C34, "OGE approved".** This is Sacks's characterization. The documents say OGE was only consulted.
- **C34, BitGo 7.8%.** This is the NYT's figure. The S-1 shows 7,421,851 shares in Craft I funds, voted by Sacks, and the waiver puts BitGo at 2.468% of his assets.
- **C34, divestment date.** The relationship dated "2025-01" should be refined: liquid crypto was sold before 20 January, BITW on 22 January, and some private holdings were still being sold in March 2025.
- **C65 and C68.** No substantive Sacks content, so no correction is needed. C68's Nvidia section could cross-reference this check.
