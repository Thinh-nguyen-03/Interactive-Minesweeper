# C49: The pardon-lobbying market and post-pardon SEC dismissals

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate (confidence: medium)

## Lead investigated

> Leads from C36: a paid pardon-lobbying industry around Trump's second term — lobbyists and allies (Roger Stone, Ches McDowell/Checkmate, Teresa Goody Guillén, others) reportedly charging large fees to seek clemency; reported asking prices of $1M+; pardons of donors or their relatives (Paul Walczak, Trevor Milton, Changpeng Zhao, Carlos Watson commutation, others); the SEC dropping civil cases after criminal pardons; the firing of pardon attorney Liz Oyer and Ed Martin's role as pardon attorney; total restitution wiped out (House Judiciary Democrats' estimate ~$1.3B+). Verify figures, named cases, and official justifications.

## Follows up on

- [[C36 Clemency for other crypto and donor-linked figures]]
- [[C09 Binance SEC case dropped and CZ pardon]]

## Summary

A paid clemency-lobbying market is well documented. The WSJ (Dec 2025) reported a $1M going rate and success-fee offers of up to $5-6M. NOTUS counted more than $2.1M in 2025 lobbying disclosures that name pardons. NBC reported Fred Daibes paid Keith Schiller's firm $1M, and 60 Minutes filmed Burkman and Wohl pitching a $300K pardon. The SEC dropped its civil cases against Milton, Watson, Ozy and Archer after clemency, and Liz Oyer was fired and replaced by Ed Martin. Several details in the brief need correcting: Watson is not a documented donor, the House Democrats' estimate is now about $1.7B, Stone's $600K from Ver was disclosed as exit-tax lobbying, and Martin left the post in Aug 2026. No grant has been shown to be bought, and the White House denies any pay-to-play.

## Corrections

- The brief lists Carlos Watson among donor-linked grants; no Watson donation was found. His case ran through Alice Marie Johnson and reform advocates.
- The House Judiciary Democrats' $1.3B figure (Jun 2025) was raised to about $1.7B in their Aug 21, 2026 'Pardons, Inc.' report, which also counts forfeitures.
- Roger Stone's $600K from Roger Ver was disclosed as exit-tax lobbying, not a pardon, and Ver received a DOJ deferred prosecution deal, not clemency (C36).
- Ed Martin is no longer pardon attorney: Trump announced his departure on Aug 21, 2026.
- Changpeng Zhao is not a documented personal donor; his link is the Binance lobbying fees and the WLF/USD1 business ties (C09).
- Todd Blanche, who fired Oyer as Deputy AG, is now Attorney General; Pam Bondi is described as the former AG.

## Denials and context

- White House: clemency is vetted by the White House Counsel and the DOJ and decided by the president. It calls profiting off pardons 'detestable' and says lobbying money is 'foolishly' wasted.
- Trump rejected nearly 6,000 clemency applications (Reason, Jul 26, 2026), which argues against a simple pay-for-pardon market.
- Paid pardon advocacy is legal and has long existed; the new elements are the scale, the fees and the bypassing of the DOJ Pardon Office.
- Many large payments bought no clemency (Daibes $1M, Boosie $600K), and Boosie says he was scammed.
- Burkman and Wohl deny wrongdoing. Stone denies lobbying the executive branch on Ver's case. Blanche denies the Gibson link to Oyer's firing.
- The restitution totals are partisan estimates with acknowledged caveats (some sums already paid, unclear collectability), and Milton's restitution was never set.
- The SEC gave no public rationale for the post-pardon dismissals; critics such as Richard Painter call them political.

## Full report

**Verdict: mostly-accurate** (confidence: medium)

This is a follow-up lead from C36 and C09; the video does not cover it. The record confirms the core of the brief:
- A large, paid clemency-lobbying market exists.
- Reported fees of $1M or more.
- Pardons of donors or their relatives.
- The SEC dropping civil cases after clemency.
- The firing of Liz Oyer and Ed Martin's appointment.

Several details in the brief are outdated or wrong (see Corrections). No evidence shows a pardon was bought, and the White House denies pay-to-play.

**Method note:** Many primary pages could not be read here. The WSJ, Bloomberg Law and NYT are paywalled. The House Judiciary Democrats' site (democrats-judiciary.house.gov) does not resolve from this session. Figures from those outlets come from search-result excerpts and secondary coverage across several outlets. They are flagged where relevant.

### What the brief says
The brief describes a paid pardon-lobbying industry (Roger Stone, Ches McDowell/Checkmate, Teresa Goody Guillén), asking prices above $1M, and donor-linked grants (Walczak, Milton, CZ, Watson). It also covers SEC civil cases dropped after pardons, Oyer's firing and Ed Martin's role, and about $1.3B in restitution wiped out.

### Sub-claims

1. **A paid pardon-lobbying industry: confirmed.**
   - NOTUS counted more than $2.1M in 2025 lobbying disclosures that name a "pardon" or "clemency". This is likely an undercount, because many pardon lawyers do not register.
   - NBC (Jun 2025) and NPR/NOTUS (Jul 19, 2026) describe a business that runs on access to White House aides. The DOJ Pardon Office is largely bypassed.
   - Paying for advocacy is legal.

2. **Asking prices of $1M or more: confirmed (as reported).**
   - Bloomberg Law (May 2025) found lawyers quoting $1M fees.
   - The WSJ (Dec 2025; read via Political Wire, Boing Boing and Raw Story) reported $1M as the standard rate and success fees of up to $6M. It also reported that Binance offered up to $5M.
   - NBC: Fred Daibes, who bribed Sen. Menendez, paid $1M to Keith Schiller's Javelin Advisors in Q1 2025. He got no pardon and dropped the firm in Jun 2025.
   - The House Judiciary Democrats' Aug 2026 report says intermediaries charged up to $2M per engagement.
   - Many of these figures come from anonymous sources.

3. **Stone, McDowell and Goody Guillén charged large fees: partly-accurate.**
   - The Binance payments are confirmed (C36): $450K to Checkmate and about $290K to Goody Guillén.
   - Stone's $600K from Roger Ver was disclosed as exit-tax lobbying, so NOTUS kept it out of its pardon total. Ver fired Stone in Dec 2025.
   - Other documented players: Jack Burkman and Jacob Wohl, who were paid just under $1M by Joseph Schwartz (Washington Post, via the Daily Beast). Schwartz was pardoned Nov 14, 2025. Boosie BadAzz paid them $600K, got no pardon and is seeking $300K back in arbitration.
   - 60 Minutes (Sep 2026) filmed Burkman and Wohl pitching a $300K pardon.

4. **Donor or relative pardons (Walczak, Milton, CZ): mostly-accurate.**
   - Milton and his wife gave more than $1.8M in Oct 2024. He was pardoned Mar 28, 2025.
   - Walczak was pardoned Apr 23, 2025, weeks after his mother Elizabeth Fago attended a $1M-per-head Mar-a-Lago dinner (NYT, May 27, 2025). His application cited her fundraising, and about $4.4M in restitution was voided. Whether she paid the $1M is unclear.
   - Zhao is not a documented personal donor. His link runs through Binance's lobbying and WLF business (C09).

5. **Watson's commutation as donor-linked: misleading.**
   - Trump commuted Watson's sentence on Mar 28, 2025, voiding about $36M in restitution and about $60M in forfeiture.
   - Watson credited pardon czar Alice Marie Johnson. No donation was found, and the White House gave no justification.

6. **SEC drops civil cases after pardons: confirmed.**
   - The SEC dismissed its cases against Milton (Sep 2025), Watson and Ozy Media (about Sep 18-19, 2025), and Devon Archer. A pardon does not legally erase civil liability.
   - The SEC later waived penalties for ex-Ozy executives Samir Rao and Suzee Han.
   - Bloomberg Law (Apr 9, 2026) counted $176M in restitution and fines originally owed by pardoned securities-fraud defendants. The SEC declined comment.

7. **Oyer fired; Martin as pardon attorney: confirmed, with an update.**
   - Then-Deputy AG Todd Blanche fired Oyer on Mar 7, 2025, after she declined to recommend restoring Mel Gibson's gun rights. Bondi restored them in Apr 2025.
   - Oyer sued for reinstatement on Sep 3, 2026. Blanche, now AG, denies knowing about the Gibson matter.
   - Martin was named pardon attorney on May 8, 2025, and posted "No MAGA left behind" after the Scott Jenkins pardon.
   - He lost the Weaponization Working Group in Feb 2026. Trump announced his departure on Aug 21, 2026. No successor was found.

8. **About $1.3B+ in restitution wiped out: mostly-accurate.**
   - The Jun 17, 2025 staff memo estimated about $1.3B, with roughly $680M tied to Milton, whose restitution was never set. The memo itself warns that some sums were already paid and that collectability is unknown.
   - The Aug 21, 2026 "Pardons, Inc." report raised the estimate to about $1.7B, now including forfeitures.
   - Both are partisan estimates. The White House dismissed the 2025 memo.

9. **Official justifications: confirmed.**
   - The White House says clemency is the president's constitutional power, vetted by the White House Counsel and the DOJ.
   - Leavitt says Trump "finds it detestable that anyone would even attempt to profit off pardons".
   - A spokesperson said lobbying money is "foolishly" wasted; attribution varies between Bis and Leavitt.
   - Schwartz: the White House cited $5M already paid in restitution, his health and a harsh sentence.
   - Milton: Trump said he was targeted for supporting Trump.

10. **Implied: pardons were bought: unverified.**
    - The money flows are documented, but a quid pro quo is not.
    - Big spenders such as Daibes and Boosie got nothing, and about 6,000 applications were rejected (Reason, Jul 2026).
    - Rep. Dave Min and Sen. Peter Welch opened a probe on May 7, 2026, with letters to 17 recipients including David Gentile, and escalated on Jun 15. They have no subpoena power.

### Corrections
- Watson is not a documented donor.
- The $1.3B estimate has been updated to about $1.7B (Aug 2026).
- Stone's $600K from Ver was disclosed as exit-tax lobbying, and Ver got a DOJ deferred prosecution deal, not clemency.
- Ed Martin left the pardon attorney post (announced Aug 21, 2026).
- CZ is not a personal donor.
- Blanche is now Attorney General.

### Denials and context
- The White House denies pay-to-play.
- Burkman and Wohl deny wrongdoing. Stone denies lobbying the executive branch on Ver's case. Blanche denies the Gibson link to Oyer's firing.
- Paid advocacy is legal. What is new is the scale and the bypassing of the DOJ office.
- Critics such as Richard Painter call the SEC dismissals political.
