# C107: Scott Bessent & Paul Atkins: Holdings & Divestiture Deadlines

**Verdict:** Partly accurate · high confidence  
**Type:** Check · Regulators & Enforcement

## The Claim

> Did Scott Bessent and Paul Atkins divest on time, and which of their decisions touch former clients or holdings?

## Bottom Line

Bessent missed his divestiture deadline, which the Office of Government Ethics documented in an 11 Aug 2025 letter to the Senate Finance Committee: the deadline was 28 April 2025 and his first certification showed only partial compliance.

## What Holds Up

- Scott Bessent disclosed Key Square Group, North Dakota farmland and other assets and agreed to divest them within 90 days of confirmation.
- The Office of Government Ethics told the Senate that Bessent missed his divestiture deadline.
- Bessent's divestitures were completed, partly through amendments that extended deadlines or let him keep assets.
- Bessent publicly announced the farmland divestment, after earlier calling himself a soybean farmer during China trade talks.
- Paul Atkins disclosed Patomak Global Partners, crypto-related interests and his wife's family wealth, and agreed to divest.
- Atkins met his divestiture deadlines.

## What Needs Correcting

- **Claimed:** Bessent 'said in Oct 2025 that he had divested' the farmland (C65)  
  **The record shows:** In October 2025 he said 'I'm actually a soybean farmer'; he announced the divestment on CBS on 7 Dec 2025, and OGE filings show the land was exchanged for a non-recourse promissory note from Glacier Ridge, LLP ($5M-$25M) under an ethics-agreement amendment with a 15 Dec 2025 deadline.
- **Claimed:** 'about $25M of North Dakota farmland' (C65)  
  **The record shows:** The nominee report values the farmland at $5,000,001-$25,000,000 (a range; $25M is the ceiling), with $100,001-$1,000,000 of crop-linked rent.
- **Claimed:** Atkins held 'about $1M of Anchorage Digital equity' and 'Securitize call options worth $2.5-5M' (C54)  
  **The record shows:** Reported ranges are $250K-$500K for Anchorage (Anchor Labs, Inc.) and $250K-$500K for the Securitize options, plus $1M-$5M in Off the Chain Capital; C65 already uses the lower figures.
- **Claimed:** Disclosure showed 'up to ~$327M in family assets' (C54)  
  **The record shows:** Bloomberg Law's headline says Atkins and his wife are worth 'at least $327 million'; the bound depends on how the ranges are summed, so 'at least' is the reported wording.
- Claimed (C54, C65): no buyer for Patomak is public. Still true as of Nov 2026; add that Atkins certified full compliance on 18 Jul 2025 with no waivers or authorizations, and that OGE issued him two Certificates of Divestiture (OGE-2025-099, OGE-2025-160).
- **Claimed:** Bessent had failed to divest Verizon and Archer Daniels Midland (CLC complaint, Jul 2025)  
  **The record shows:** Both were sold on 4 Mar 2025, though this became public only after the complaint (278-T of 14 Jul 2025 and the 2026 annual report).
- Warren's letter of 5 Aug 2025 puts Atkins's 90-day mark at 9 Jul 2025; counting from confirmation on 9 Apr 2025 it falls on 8 Jul 2025. OGE puts Bessent's 90-day deadline at 28 Apr 2025; CLC used 27 Apr.

## What Is Unproven

- Bessent's farmland or other holdings created a conflict with China trade, farm aid or tariff decisions.
- The $20 billion Argentina support package benefited investors linked to Bessent's former colleagues.
- SEC decisions under Atkins touched former Patomak clients or his former holdings, and he recused where required.

## In More Detail

- He then amended his ethics agreement three times, kept three private holdings with Treasury's approval, and in December 2025 exchanged his North Dakota farmland for a $5M-$25M non-recourse note from the buyer.
- He certified full compliance on 5 Dec 2025.
- Atkins certified on 18 Jul 2025 that all his divestitures, including Patomak, were done on time, with no waivers; the Patomak buyer has not been made public.
- Claims that either man's decisions (China soybean deals, farm aid, the Argentina swap, SEC case dismissals) served his own holdings, former clients or friends are allegations: no authority has found a conflict, and the one review on record (Treasury OIG, on a mis-reported JPMorgan stake, as recorded in his 2026 annual report) found no knowing violation.

### 1. Scott Bessent disclosed Key Square Group, North Dakota farmland and other assets and agreed to divest them within 90 days of confirmation.

**Confirmed** · Evidence: Documented (nominee OGE 278e and ethics agreement, both read in full)

- Bessent's nominee report (signed 28 Dec 2024) lists Key Square Group LP, the investment firm he founded, valued at over $50 million, with $2,787,000 in management fees and incentive allocations plus a $1,041,134 guaranteed payment; Key Square funds holding S&P 500 and Nasdaq ETFs, currency positions and put options (each line 'over $50,000,000' or $1M-$25M); iShares Bitcoin Trust ($250K-$500K); and High Plains Acres LLP, holding farmland in Burleigh, Kidder, Eddy, Benson and Wells counties, North Dakota, valued at $5,000,001-$25,000,000 with $100,001-$1,000,000 of rent tied to corn and soybean prices.

- Three hedge-fund interests (Totem Macro, Totem Management Seed, Old Farm Partners) were to be redeemed quarterly, finishing by September 2025.
- He kept several family LLCs and trusts with a recusal commitment.
- He was confirmed on 27 Jan 2025.
- Amounts: over $50 million; $2,787,000; $1,041,134; over $50,000,000; $1M-$25M
- Dates: 28 Dec 2024; 9 Jan 2025; September 2025; 27 Jan 2025

### 2. The Office of Government Ethics told the Senate that Bessent missed his divestiture deadline.

**Confirmed** · Evidence: Documented (OGE letters read in full)

- On 11 Aug 2025 OGE's Deputy Director for Compliance, Dale Christopher, notified Senate Finance Chairman Mike Crapo under Program Advisory PA-19-09 that Bessent 'has failed to timely comply with certain terms' of his ethics agreement: he had to comply with certain provisions by 28 April 2025 and certify by 5 May 2025.
- He submitted a certification on 5 May 'indicating partial compliance'; that form, signed 2 May, ticks 'No' on completing divestitures and says divestitures 'have been initiated but not yet completed for all assets' and no 278-T had been filed.
- OGE wrote that a further amendment was needed and 'there is currently no timeline for resolution', and that it had advised Treasury ethics officials to emphasise that avoiding conflicts with his holdings was his personal responsibility.
- On 13 Aug 2025 OGE added that Treasury said Bessent was committed to completing the remaining divestitures by 15 Dec 2025, that the assets were 'illiquid and are not readily marketable', and that he had been and would remain recused from matters affecting them, with a screening memorandum given to his front office.
- A missed deadline documented by OGE is a fact; OGE's letters do not find that he took part in any conflicting matter.
- Dates: 11 Aug 2025; 28 April 2025; 5 May 2025; 5 May; 2 May

### 3. Bessent's divestitures were completed, partly through amendments that extended deadlines or let him keep assets.

**Confirmed**

- Grade: documented (278-Ts, certifications, amendments and 2026 annual report, read in full). (e) 278-T signed 5 Dec 2025: Totem Management Seed sold 23 Sep 2025 ($250K-$500K), Greengage 27 Nov 2025. - Who controls Glacier Ridge, LLP is not public in what we found.

## Denials & Context

- Treasury told OGE that Bessent had worked to divest since January 2025, that the remaining assets were illiquid and restricted, and that he was and would remain recused from matters affecting them.
  - Bessent said in August 2025 that he had sold about 96% of what he had to divest (as reported by The New York Times, cited in the DDF/CLC letter).
- Treasury's ethics official certified each Bessent filing as compliant with applicable laws, and the final certification of 5 Dec 2025 states all required divestitures were completed.
  - According to the ethics official's comment on the 2026 annual report, the Treasury OIG found no knowing violation over the mis-reported JPMorgan stock (no separate OIG document was found).
- Sen. Ron Wyden said the Treasury secretary was picking and choosing which ethics requirements to follow (as reported by Banking Dive).
  - No public statement from Sen. Crapo was found.
- Bessent says the Argentina package was not a bailout and that Treasury profited; Treasury says the $2.5B draw was fully repaid in December 2025.
  - Druckenmiller says he did not communicate with Bessent about Argentina; a Citrone spokesman declined to comment.
- The SEC says Atkins takes his obligations seriously and is upholding the commitments in his ethics agreement; an Atkins spokesperson said he recused from the Archer dismissal.
  - Trump Media called the Patomak subpoena effort meritless.
- Holding an asset, or a decision that helps a former employer, client or colleague, is not by itself a violation. 18 U.S.C.
  - 208 applies to particular matters with a direct and predictable effect on the official's own or imputed interests; the impartiality rule (5 C.F.R. 2635.502) covers former employers and clients for one year.
  - Neither covers friends or former colleagues.
- Research note: Atkins's nominee report, 278-Ts and annual report are released only on written request to OGE and were not read; his figures come from press reports.
  - A full-text New York Times article on the Archer recusal and on Druckenmiller was not read.

## Full Report

**Verdict:** partly-accurate (confidence: high)

**Question:** Did Scott Bessent and Paul Atkins divest on time, and which of their decisions touch former clients or holdings?

### Summary

- **Bessent missed his deadline. That is documented.** The Office of Government Ethics (OGE) told Senate Finance Chairman Mike Crapo on 11 Aug 2025 that Bessent "has failed to timely comply with certain terms" of his ethics agreement. The deadline was 28 April 2025. His first certification (signed 2 May, submitted 5 May 2025) ticked "No" on divestitures and said they had "been initiated but not yet completed for all assets".
- **How it was resolved:** there were three amendments. In May 2025 he added two omitted resignations. In June 2025 Treasury's ethics official found that three private holdings (Presight, Half Acre WSW, Cambrian Biopharma) posed no conflict risk, so he kept them under recusal. In December 2025 he admitted he "was unable to divest ... within 90 days ... due to their illiquid nature" and set a new date of 15 Dec 2025. Under that amendment his partnership exchanged its North Dakota farmland for a **non-recourse promissory note from Glacier Ridge, LLP valued at $5M-$25M**. He signed his final certification on 5 Dec 2025. His 2026 annual report (OGE-certified 22 Sep 2026) shows the note.
- **Atkins met his deadlines. That is also documented.** He was confirmed on 9 April 2025 and signed his certification on 18 July 2025, before the 14 Aug due date. It confirms all resignations and divestitures were done on time, including Patomak Global Partners, Securitize, Anchorage (Anchor Labs) and Off the Chain. It records no waivers and no authorizations, and two OGE Certificates of Divestiture exist. Who bought Patomak, and for how much, is still not public.
- **Decisions touching holdings, former clients or friends:**
  - Bessent's China soybean deal and farm aid
  - the Argentina swap and investors who are his former Soros colleagues
  - SEC dismissals under Atkins, including the Devon Archer case, from which Atkins reportedly recused

  The decisions themselves are documented. **That any of them served the official's own interests is an allegation.** No authority has made such a finding. The one review on record went the other way: according to a Treasury ethics official's comment on Bessent's 2026 annual report, the Treasury Inspector General found "no knowing violation" over his spouse's mis-reported JPMorgan stock.

This extends C52, C54 and C65, and corrects them in a few places (see Corrections).

**Evidence rule applied:** OGE's documentation makes the missed deadline a fact. A conflict counts only as an allegation unless an authority has found one. Holding an asset, or making a decision that helps a former employer, client or friend, is not by itself a violation.

### The rules that apply

- **18 U.S.C. 208:** no personal and substantial participation in a *particular matter* that directly and predictably affects the official's own or imputed financial interests, unless a waiver or exemption applies. Broad policy, such as trade strategy, is usually not a particular matter.
- **5 C.F.R. 2635.502:** a one-year bar on specific-party matters involving a former employer or a client served in the past year, unless the official is authorized to take part. It does not cover friends or former colleagues.
- **Ethics agreements:** divestiture within 90 days of confirmation (5 C.F.R. 2634.802). OGE may grant an extension only for "unusual hardship". OGE notifies the Senate committee when an official misses a deadline (Program Advisory PA-19-09).
