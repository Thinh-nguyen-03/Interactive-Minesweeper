# C95: The Presidential Salary Pledge

**Verdict:** Partly accurate · medium confidence  
**Type:** Check · Family Ventures & Wealth

## The Claim

> Has Donald Trump given up his presidential salary in his second term, and where has each payment gone?

## Bottom Line

Trump has said repeatedly in his second term that he does not keep his $400,000 presidential salary.

## What Holds Up

- Trump has said he gives up his presidential salary in his second term.
- First-term comparison: Trump donated his salary quarterly to federal agencies (mostly accurate)
- By law the president is paid $400,000 a year plus allowances.

## What Needs Correcting

- Donating the salary is a pure cost to Trump, with no tax offset (partly accurate)
- Trump is the only president, possibly besides George Washington, to donate his salary (false)
- Claimed: Trump donates his whole salary each quarter in his second term. The record shows one documented recipient (the White House Historical Association) and one documented total ($66,000 from January to October 27, 2025, according to the association). Nothing is documented for later quarters.
- Claimed: the second-term salary goes to federal agencies, as in the first term. The record shows that the only documented second-term recipient is a private nonprofit, not a government agency.
- Claimed: Trump is the only president besides Washington to give up his pay. The record shows that Hoover and Kennedy also did, and that Washington accepted his salary.
- Claimed: the second-term donations total $400,000 (one aggregator's figure for the first paycheck). The record shows that pay is monthly at about $33,333 gross, and the recipient itself reports $66,000 through late October 2025.
- Claimed (Wall Street Journal, October 25, 2025, since corrected): Trump had contributed $50 million to the White House Historical Association since January. The record shows: the association's October 27, 2025 statement calls that untrue and credits him with $66,000 of salary.

## What Is Unproven

- Each quarter's salary in the second term has been donated, with documented recipients and dates.

## In More Detail

- On August 6, 2025 he said his first paycheck went to the White House Historical Association, a private nonprofit.
- That association's October 27, 2025 statement, read in full here, credits him with $66,000 of salary since January 2025; the same statement says a Wall Street Journal report that he had given the association $50 million was untrue.
- About $307,000 of salary had accrued by then, and roughly $729,000 had accrued by mid-November 2026.
- No donation in any later quarter is documented in public through mid-November 2026: the White House has not published quarterly announcements as it did in 2017-2020, and no agency has announced receiving one.
- The claim that he gives his salary away is therefore documented only in part.
- His first-term record is much fuller: at least about $1.4M of $1.6M is documented, with the last two quarters of 2020 unaccounted for.
- His recurring statement that he is the only president besides George Washington to donate his salary is false, because Herbert Hoover and John F. Kennedy also did.

## Sub-Claims

### 1. Trump has said he gives up his presidential salary in his second term.

**Confirmed**

- Before the term began, in the December 2024 Meet the Press interview, he said he would not accept the salary.
- On May 4, 2025, also on Meet the Press, he said he gives his entire salary back to the government.
- Asked whether he would also donate his crypto profits, he answered 'I never thought of that.'
- On August 6, 2025 he wrote on Truth Social that his first 'Paycheck' went to the White House Historical Association (the American Presidency Project's digest of White House announcements records it on that date).
- In a May 2026 interview reported by the Washington Examiner, he said he gives 100% of the salary to White House preservation.
- At the launch of a government services website at the Andrew Mellon Auditorium on September 29, 2026 he said: 'I don't take my salary ...
- I decided not to, including in my first term. Do I get thanks for it? None.'
- At a Hispanic Heritage Month event in the East Room the next day, September 30, 2026, he said (wording from a search excerpt of MS NOW's report): 'In my first term, I donated it and I don't think I ever got a story about it ...
- But in my second term, I donated it, too.'
- He repeated the point in a post-election 60 Minutes interview, as reported by FactCheck.org on November 10, 2026 ('I don't take a salary').
- These are his own statements, not records of payment.
- Amounts: 100%
- Dates: December 2024; May 4, 2025; August 6, 2025; May 2026; September 29, 2026

### 2. Each quarter's salary in the second term has been donated, with documented recipients and dates.

**Unverified**

- Only one recipient is documented: the White House Historical Association (WHHA).
- It is a private 501(c)(3) nonprofit, not a federal agency.
- Trump's August 6, 2025 Truth Social post named it but gave no amount.
- The WHHA's October 27, 2025 statement, which mainly disputed a Wall Street Journal report about its role in the ballroom project, thanked the president for giving part of his salary to its work: $66,000 since January.
- The statement, read in full, also says the Wall Street Journal had reported that the president contributed $50 million to the association since January, that this was 'not true', and that the newspaper corrected the error.
- About $306,800 of gross salary had accrued between January 20 and October 27, 2025 (pro rata at $400,000 a year).
- The $66,000 is close to two months of gross pay ($66,667), but no source says which pay periods it covers, so that match is our own inference.
- No donation is documented for October 2025 onward, which covers Q4 2025 and Q1-Q3 2026.
- CNN's Daniel Dale reported in late September 2026 that Trump had not made public any evidence of donating his full second-term salary.
- Unlike 2017 to mid-2020, the White House has issued no quarterly donation announcements, and no agency has announced a gift.
- A second review in November 2026 again found none: searches for White House, agency and association announcements, and the association's press-release index (latest item 10 November 2026), show no further salary gift.
- Amounts: $66,000; $50 million; About $306,800; $400,000; $66,667
- Dates: August 6, 2025; October 27, 2025; January 20; October 2025; late September 2026
- Sources: [1](https://www.whitehousehistory.org/press-room/press-releases/statement-by-the-white-house-historical-association-on-october-27-2025) [2](https://www.breitbart.com/politics/2025/08/06/trump-donates-presidential-paycheck-to-white-house-historical-association/) [8](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/trump-boasts-donating-presidential-salary-191408226.html) [9](https://www.rawstory.com/trump-2677968710/) [34](https://www.whitehousehistory.org/press-room/press-releases)

### 3. First-term comparison: Trump donated his salary quarterly to federal agencies.

**Mostly accurate**

- From April 2017 to August 2020 the White House publicly announced quarterly gifts to federal agencies, often with a photo of the check.
- The documented recipients are as follows: - Q1 2017: National Park Service, $78,333.32 (pro rata). - Q2 2017: Education Department, $100,000. - Later quarters: HHS (opioids), Transportation, Veterans Affairs, Small Business Administration, NIH's alcohol institute (NIAAA), Homeland Security, Agriculture and the Surgeon General's office. - March and May 2020: HHS, for coronavirus work. - Q2 2020: National Park Service, $100,000 (check dated July 13, 2020).
- Fact-checkers place some of these in different quarters, so the per-quarter mapping varies between sources.
- Forbes found records for at least $1.4M of the $1.6M he earned.
- No record was found for July 2020 to January 20, 2021, and the White House, Treasury and OPM did not account for those months.
- In 2017 the White House initially declined to release documentation.
- Amounts: $78,333.32; $100,000; at least $1.4M; $1.6M
- Dates: April 2017; August 2020; May 2020; July 13, 2020; July 2020

### 4. By law the president is paid $400,000 a year plus allowances.

**Confirmed**

- 3 U.S.C. 102, read in full on govinfo, sets the salary at $400,000 a year, 'to be paid monthly' (about $33,333 gross a month), plus 'an expense allowance of $50,000' for official duties.
- The statute itself says any unused part of that allowance reverts to the Treasury and that none of it is included in the president's gross income (wording added in 2004).
- The $400,000 rate replaced $200,000 under a 1999 law that took effect at noon on January 20, 2001. 3 U.S.C.
- 103 allows up to $100,000 a year for travel, spent at the president's discretion and accounted for by his own certificate (search excerpt). 3 U.S.C.
- 105 covers official entertainment and White House Office expenses, with amounts set in annual appropriations.
- A commonly cited $19,000 entertainment figure could not be confirmed.
- On leaving office, a president receives a pension equal to Executive Schedule Level I pay under the Former Presidents Act (CRS RS20115, search excerpt).
- Whether Trump draws the $50,000 expense allowance this term is not publicly documented.
- Amounts: $400,000; about $33,333; $50,000; $200,000; up to $100,000
- Dates: January 20, 2001
- Sources: [15](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title3/html/USCODE-2024-title3-chap2-sec102.htm) [16](https://law.justia.com/codes/us/title-3/chapter-2/sec-103/) [17](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/RS20115.html) [18](https://checkyourfact.com/2019/12/04/fact-check-donald-trump-salary-hoover-kennedy-washington)

### 5. Donating the salary is a pure cost to Trump, with no tax offset.

**Partly accurate**

- The salary is taxable wages.
- A gift to a federal agency (IRC 170(c)(1)) or to a 501(c)(3) such as the WHHA can be claimed as a charitable deduction if he itemizes, subject to adjusted-gross-income caps, and an unused deduction carries forward.
- So a donation can offset the income tax on the salary in whole or in part.
- CNBC (2020) reported that tax experts saw this possibility.
- His first-term returns, released by the House Ways and Means Committee in December 2022, show about $2M of charitable deductions in 2017, a little over $500,000 in each of 2018 and 2019, and none in 2020.
- Tax experts quoted by CNBC and USA Today said the returns cannot show whether the deducted gifts were the salary donations.
- No second-term tax return is public, so whether he has claimed a deduction for the 2025 WHHA gift is unknown.
- Amounts: about $2M; over $500,000
- Dates: December 2022
- Sources: [29](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/03/05/trump-may-snag-a-tax-break-for-donating-his-salary-to-stop-coronavirus.html) [30](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/01/11/did-trump-donate-his-salary-in-2020-tax-returns-dont-tell-full-story.html) [31](https://news.yahoo.com/fact-check-trump-donated-portions-195735245.html)

### 6. Trump is the only president, possibly besides George Washington, to donate his salary.

**False**

- Herbert Hoover and John F. Kennedy both gave away their presidential salaries.
- George Washington initially declined pay but accepted the $25,000 salary.
- Trump repeated the claim on August 6, 2025, in May 2026 and again in a November 2026 interview, in which he added 'I don't know if that's right.'
- CNN's Daniel Dale also disputed his September 30, 2026 statement that his first-term donations never got press coverage: 'They were regularly covered when he announced them.'
- Amounts: $25,000
- Dates: August 6, 2025; May 2026; November 2026; September 30, 2026
- Sources: [18](https://checkyourfact.com/2019/12/04/fact-check-donald-trump-salary-hoover-kennedy-washington) [4](https://www.nbcpalmsprings.com/2025/08/06/president-trump-donates-presidential-salary-to-white-house-renovation-projects) [11](https://www.factcheck.org/2026/11/factchecking-trumps-post-election-60-minutes-interview/) [10](https://atlantablackstar.com/2026/10/04/trump-thinks-hes-a-selfless-president-for-giving-up-salary/) [9](https://www.rawstory.com/trump-2677968710/)

## Denials & Context

- Trump's position: he has said repeatedly (May 2025, August 2025, May 2026, September 2026, November 2026) that he donates his salary, and that he gets no credit for it.
- White House Historical Association: in its October 27, 2025 statement it thanked the president for contributing part of his salary ($66,000 since January) and said a reported $50 million contribution from him was not true.
  - It did not respond to press requests for comment in August 2025.
  - In its statements on the ballroom it disputed parts of a Wall Street Journal report about its role.
- Critics: CNN's Daniel Dale said Trump has released no evidence of donating his full second-term salary.
  - The Maddow Blog (MS NOW) and others argue that his disclosed 2025 income, at least about $2.2B by most counts (see C29), dwarfs the $400,000 salary.
  - That is a comparison of scale, not evidence about the donations.
- Context: the WHHA is a private nonprofit that funds White House acquisitions and preservation.
  - Its ties to Trump's White House renovations, including a separate $2.5M gift linked to East Wing documentation, have been the subject of Washington Post reporting.
  - Trump himself linked his salary gift to the renovations.
- Context: in the first term, after declining at first in 2017, the White House published regular announcements and check photos.
  - Even so, the final six months of first-term pay were never documented.
  - A gap in announcements is not proof that no donation was made, and a statement of intent is not proof that one was.

## Full Report

**Verdict: partly accurate** (confidence: medium)

Trump has said many times in his second term that he does not keep his $400,000 salary. Only part of that is documented:

- **What is on record.** On August 6, 2025, Trump said his first paycheck went to the White House Historical Association (WHHA), a private nonprofit. The WHHA's statement of October 27, 2025 credits him with **$66,000** of salary given since January, and calls untrue a newspaper report that he had given it $50 million.
- **What has accrued.** About $307,000 of salary had accrued by that date. Roughly **$729,000** had accrued by mid-November 2026.
- **What is missing.** No later donation, to any recipient, is publicly documented (rechecked to mid-November 2026). The White House has issued no quarterly announcements this term, as it did from 2017 to mid-2020.
- **The first term.** The record there is much fuller. At least about $1.4M of $1.6M is documented, and the last two quarters of 2020 are unaccounted for.
- **"Only president" claim.** His recurring claim to be the only president besides Washington to donate his salary is false. Hoover and Kennedy also did.

**Source limits.** The original research could open no page and relied on search-result excerpts. In the November 2026 second review, the WHHA statement [1], the statute [15], the Washington Examiner [3][6], Breitbart [2], Atlanta Black Star [10], FactCheck.org [11], the American Presidency Project [33] and the WHHA press-release index [34] were read in full. The other sources remain search excerpts.

### What was checked
Whether Donald Trump has given up his presidential salary in his second term. This covers what he has said, which quarterly payments are documented as donated (when, how much, to whom), how that compares with the first term, what the salary and allowances are by law, and whether a donation is an unrecovered cost to him.

### Sub-claims

#### 1. Trump says he gives up the salary: confirmed (as statements)
- **December 2024, Meet the Press (president-elect).** He said he would not accept the salary, as in his first term. [14]
- **May 4, 2025, Meet the Press.** He said he gives his entire salary back to the government. Asked whether he would also donate his crypto profits, he said "I never thought of that." [12][13]
- **August 6, 2025, Truth Social** (date confirmed by the American Presidency Project [33]). "My first 'Paycheck' went to the White House Historical Association, as we make much needed renovations to the beautiful 'People's House.'" He called himself the only president "(with the possible exception of the Late, Great George Washington)" to donate his salary. [2][3][4]
- **May 2026 interview** (Washington Examiner). He said he gives 100% of the salary to White House preservation. [6]
- **September 29, 2026, launch of a government services website, Andrew Mellon Auditorium.** "I don't take my salary ... I decided not to, including in my first term. Do I get thanks for it? None." [10]
- **September 30, 2026, Hispanic Heritage Month event in the East Room.** "In my first term, I donated it and I don't think I ever got a story about it ... But in my second term, I donated it, too." (wording from a search excerpt) [7][8]
- **November 2026, post-election 60 Minutes interview** (FactCheck.org, November 10, 2026). "I don't take a salary." He again said every other president took the salary, then added "I don't know if that's right." [11]

These are his own statements. None of them is a record of payment.

- **The recipient.** The WHHA is a private 501(c)(3) nonprofit, not a federal agency. That is a change from the first term, when every announced gift went to an agency.
- **The WHHA statement.** Its October 27, 2025 statement was mainly a rebuttal of parts of a Wall Street Journal report on its role in the ballroom project. - **No later evidence.** CNN's Daniel Dale reported in late September 2026 that Trump had not made public any evidence of donating his full second-term salary. A second review in November 2026 repeated the search and checked the WHHA's press-release index (latest item November 10, 2026): nothing further. [34]
- **What the gap means.** It shows only that no donation is documented. It does not show that none was made.

#### 3. First-term comparison: mostly accurate
- **Announcements.** From April 2017 to August 2020, the White House announced gifts by quarter, often with a photo of the check. [19][20][22]
- **Q1 2017.** National Park Service, $78,333.32 (pro rata). [19]
- **Q2 2017.** Department of Education, $100,000, for a STEM camp. [20]
- **Later quarters.** HHS (opioids), Transportation, Veterans Affairs, Small Business Administration, NIH's alcohol institute (NIAAA), Homeland Security, Agriculture and the Surgeon General's office (August 2019). [22][23][28]
- **2020.** HHS for coronavirus work (March 3 and May 22, 2020), then the National Park Service, $100,000 (check dated July 13, 2020, announced August 2020). [24][25][26]
- **Quarter mapping.** Fact-checkers (VERIFY, Snopes, PolitiFact) assign several of these gifts to different quarters, so the exact mapping varies by source.
- **The gap.** Forbes (2021) found records for at least $1.4M of the $1.6M he earned. It found no record for July 2020 to January 20, 2021. [27]
- **Early 2017.** The White House at first declined to release proof. [21]

#### 4. What the law provides: confirmed
- **Salary (3 U.S.C. 102, read in full).** $400,000 a year, "to be paid monthly" (about $33,333 gross). A 1999 law doubled it from $200,000, effective at noon on January 20, 2001. [15][17]
- **Expense allowance (same section).** "An expense allowance of $50,000" for official duties. The statute itself (since a 2004 amendment) says any unused part reverts to the Treasury and that none of it counts as the president's gross income. [15][17][18]
- **Travel (3 U.S.C. 103).** Up to $100,000 a year, spent at the president's discretion and accounted for by his own certificate. [16]
- **Official entertainment and White House Office expenses (3 U.S.C. 105).** Amounts are set in annual appropriations. The often-quoted $19,000 entertainment figure could not be confirmed.
- **Pension.** After leaving office, a president receives a pension equal to Executive Schedule Level I pay (Former Presidents Act). [17]
- **Not public.** Whether Trump draws the expense allowance is not public.

#### 5. Is a donation an unrecovered cost? Partly accurate
- **The salary is taxable wages.** A gift to a federal agency, or to a 501(c)(3) such as the WHHA, can be deducted as a charitable contribution if he itemizes. The deduction is capped by adjusted gross income, and any unused part carries forward, so the gift can offset the tax on the salary. [29]
- **First-term tax returns** (released by House Ways and Means, December 2022) show charitable deductions of:
  - about $2M in 2017
  - a little over $500,000 in each of 2018 and 2019
  - none in 2020 [30]
- **What the returns cannot show.** Tax experts said they cannot tell whether those deductions were the salary gifts. [30][31]
- **Second term.** No second-term return is public.

#### 6. "Only president besides Washington to donate his salary": false
- Herbert Hoover and John F. Kennedy also gave away their salaries.
- George Washington first declined pay but accepted the $25,000 salary. [18][4]
- Daniel Dale also rejected Trump's September 2026 statement that his first-term gifts got no coverage: "They were regularly covered when he announced them." [8][9]

### Corrections
- **Claimed:** the whole salary is donated every quarter of the second term. **The record shows:** one recipient and $66,000 documented, through late October 2025. Nothing is documented after that.
- **Claimed:** the money goes to federal agencies, as in the first term. **The record shows:** the only documented recipient is a private nonprofit.
- **Claimed:** he is the only president besides Washington to donate his salary. **The record shows:** Hoover and Kennedy did too, and Washington accepted his pay.
- **Claimed:** his first second-term "paycheck" was $400,000 (one aggregator). **The record shows:** pay is monthly, and the recipient reports $66,000 in total through October 27, 2025.
- **Claimed** (Wall Street Journal, October 25, 2025, since corrected): he had given the WHHA $50 million since January. **The record shows:** the WHHA calls that untrue and reports $66,000 of salary.

### Other side
- **Trump.** He has said consistently that he donates his salary and gets no credit for doing so.
- **White House.** No on-record second-term statement from a spokesperson on where each payment went was found. Neither Karoline Leavitt nor any other spokesperson was found addressing it.
- **WHHA.** It thanked the president for his salary contribution in its October 2025 statement and rejected the reported $50 million figure. It did not answer press questions in August 2025. The Washington Post reported in October 2026 that it had avoided public conflict with the president. It says a separate $2.5M donor gift paid for East Wing documentation, not for the demolition or the ballroom. [1][32]
- **Critics.** CNN's Daniel Dale and the Maddow Blog say the second-term donations have not been shown in full. They also say his disclosed 2025 income (at least about $2.2B by most counts; see C29) is far larger than the salary. [7][8] That is a point of scale, not evidence about the donations.
- **Ledger.** `data/ledger/C95.json` records the statutory salary accrued (about $725,000-$730,000) and the one documented forgone amount ($66,000). The second row is part of the first, so the two must not be added.
