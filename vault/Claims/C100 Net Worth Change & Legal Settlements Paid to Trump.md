# C100: Net Worth Change & Legal Settlements Paid to Trump

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Type:** Check · Family Ventures & Wealth

## The Claim

> How has Trump's estimated net worth changed since January 2025, and what have companies paid to settle his lawsuits?

## Bottom Line

Forbes and Bloomberg both estimate Donald Trump's fortune at several billion dollars, but neither shows a steady rise.

## What Holds Up

- Forbes estimates of Donald Trump's net worth from late 2024 to Nov 2026.
- Bloomberg Billionaires Index estimates of Trump's and his family's wealth.
- Most of the gain came from crypto and licensing, not stock, and much of the fortune is paper value (mostly accurate)
- Liabilities: the New York civil fraud judgment and the E. Jean Carroll judgments.
- ABC/Disney paid $15M plus $1M in fees to settle Trump's defamation suit (Dec 2024), directed to his presidential library.
- Meta paid $25M (Jan 2025) to settle Trump's account-suspension suit, mostly for the library.
- X paid about $10M (Feb 2025) to settle Trump's account-suspension suit (mostly accurate)
- Paramount paid $16M (July 2025) to settle the '60 Minutes' suit, directed to the library.
- YouTube/Alphabet paid $24.5M (Sept 2025), with $22M directed to the White House ballroom.
- Trump sought about $230M from the Justice Department through administrative claims.
- Other settlements or pending suits; law-firm pledges (kept separate).

## What Needs Correcting

- Settlement money directed to the library foundation is accounted for (partly accurate)
- Claimed (C33): Meta paid about $22M of $25M to the library. The record shows: Trump's 2025 OGE disclosure lists $24.5M of net proceeds paid to the library foundation from the Meta settlement; Meta told lawmakers $22M was designated. The difference is unexplained. (Applied to C33 on 16 Nov 2026.)
- Claimed (C33): ABC paid $15M to the library. The record shows: the disclosure lists the ABC settlement at $16M with net proceeds paid to the foundation, while ABC's own March 2026 letter says the $15M was still in escrow pending release. The $1M difference and the timing are unexplained. (Applied to C33.)
- Claimed (C33): no payment went to Trump personally. The record shows: the disclosure reports $8M from X as Trump's own settlement income with no note that it went to the foundation; whether he kept it is not documented. The statement holds for ABC, Meta, Paramount and YouTube. (Applied to C33.)
- Claimed (C33): X paid about $10M. The record shows: the press figure is unchanged, but the disclosure lists $8M; how the other roughly $2M was split (fees, other parties) is unknown.
- Claimed (C55): Bloomberg put the family at about $6.7B after the crypto slide. The record shows: Bloomberg's 20 Jan 2026 feature gives $6.8B and a secondary report citing Bloomberg gives about $6.6B; C55 also predates Bloomberg's 6 June 2026 figure of $7.8B and the Forbes 400 figure of about $7.0B (15 Sept 2026).
- Claimed in secondary coverage: Forbes's March 2026 list shows $8.4B of assets and $1.1B of liabilities. The record shows: those numbers net to $7.3B, the Sept 2025 total, not $6.5B, so the attribution appears wrong; treated as unverified.
- Claimed in some coverage: Forbes put Trump at about $6.3B on election day 2024. The record shows: Forbes's 2024 Forbes 400 figure was $4.3B (as of 1 Sept 2024), and Forbes's Sept 2026 article says he is about $2.7B richer than when he won the election at about $7.0B, implying about $4.3B. The $6.3B figure matches Bloomberg-range numbers; treated as unverified for Forbes.
- Claimed in this check's first draft: some coverage gives a 2024 Forbes 400 figure of $3.9B, and the Forbes component split for Sept 2026 was unverified. The record shows: Forbes's own Oct 2024 article gives $4.3B (No. 319), and Forbes's own Sept 2026 breakdown confirms meme coin $183M, World Liberty tokens $78M, stablecoin business $233M and licensing and management $655M.
- Claimed in this check's first draft: YouTube's $24.5M is the amount directed to the Trust for the National Mall (ledger range $22M-$24.5M). The record shows: only $22M went to the Trust; $2.5M went to co-plaintiffs.

## In More Detail

- Forbes had him at $4.3B in the 2024 Forbes 400, a record $7.3B in Sept 2025, $6.5B in March 2026 and about $7.0B in Sept 2026, which Forbes says is about $2.7B more than when he won the 2024 election.
- Bloomberg had the family at $7.7B (Sept 2025), $6.8B (Jan 2026) and $7.8B (June 2026, after adding a stablecoin stake).
- Forbes attributes most of the gain to crypto and overseas licensing, not stock.
- Most of the fortune is illiquid or paper value: real estate and golf, a Trump Media stake and crypto.
- Five companies settled his suits for about $90.5M in headline terms (ABC $15M plus $1M fees, Meta $25M, X about $10M, Paramount $16M, YouTube $24.5M).
- Trump's 2025 OGE disclosure, read in full here, lists $86.5M of settlements: $56.5M of 'net proceeds' paid to his library foundation (ABC $16M, Paramount $16M, Meta $24.5M), $22M from YouTube paid to the Trust for the National Mall for the White House ballroom, and $8M from X with no note that it went anywhere else.
- The X money is therefore reported as Trump's own settlement income; lawmakers have asked whether it later reached the foundation, and no answer was found.
- ABC's own letter shows its money was still in escrow in March 2026.
- Separately, the roughly $230M Trump claimed from the Justice Department was withdrawn in a May 2026 IRS settlement that paid him nothing; a judge later found that suit collusive, and the appeal is pending.

### 3. Most of the gain came from crypto and licensing, not stock, and much of the fortune is paper value.

**Mostly accurate**

- Forbes editor Dan Alexander said almost none of the $3B gain from fall 2024 to fall 2025 came from stock; crypto ventures and overseas licensing were the main drivers.
- PolitiFact rated Trump's '60 Minutes' remark crediting stocks Mostly False (9 Nov 2026), citing Alexander's estimate that stock was about 10% of his roughly $415M of liquid assets in fall 2024.
- Asked about the gain, Trump said 'you should've had stock' and that he devotes almost no time to his net worth.

- The rest is real estate, golf, licensing businesses and a Trump Media stake that is listed but held in trust and large relative to trading volume.
- Crypto token values are themselves volatile and partly locked; Bloomberg excludes locked tokens altogether.
- The ratio is our own arithmetic.
- A secondary figure of '$8.4B assets, $1.1B liabilities' attributed to Forbes March 2026 nets to $7.3B, the Sept 2025 total, so it appears misattributed and is unverified.

- Per the NYT (Oct 2025), Trump filed two Federal Tort Claims Act administrative claims in 2023-24, over the Russia investigation and the 2022 Mar-a-Lago search, together seeking about $230M. Trump said on 21 Oct 2025 he 'could be' seeking it: 'I'm not looking for money.
- I'd give it to charity or something' (CNN, read in full); DOJ declined comment.
- On 18 May 2026, Trump, his sons and the Trump Organization settled their $10B suit against the IRS over the leak of his tax returns.
- DOJ said they would receive a formal apology but 'no monetary payment or damages of any kind', and that Trump's team agreed to drop the administrative claims (CBS News, read in full).
- DOJ instead created a $1.776B 'anti-weaponization fund' from the Judgment Fund for others, for which Trump was not eligible.
- Reporting also says the deal gave audit protections; that is single-source.
- In July 2026, Judge Kathleen Williams found the suit collusive and brought in bad faith, voided reliance on the settlement and sanctioned Trump's counsel, including Alejandro Brito.
- Acting AG Blanche told Congress the fund was 'not moving forward, period'.
- On about 29 Sept 2026 an 11th Circuit panel declined to stay the sanctions, saying Trump and his lawyers offered no evidence that the suit and settlement were not collusive; the underlying appeal is pending.
- Net result: no payment to Trump is documented.
- Amounts: about $230M; $10B; $1.776B
- Dates: Oct 2025; 2023-24; 21 Oct 2025; 18 May 2026; July 2026

- July 2026: Judge Kathleen Williams (S.D. Fla.) found the suit collusive and brought in bad faith. She barred reliance on the settlement and sanctioned Trump's counsel, including Alejandro Brito.
- Acting AG Todd Blanche told Congress the fund was "not moving forward, period."
- On about 29 Sept 2026 an 11th Circuit panel declined to stay the sanctions, saying Trump and his lawyers offered no evidence that the suit and settlement were not collusive. The underlying appeal is pending. [53]
- **No payment to Trump is documented.**
