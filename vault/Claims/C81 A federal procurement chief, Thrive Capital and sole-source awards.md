# C81: A federal procurement chief, Thrive Capital and sole-source awards

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Type:** Connection check · Tech and media companies

## The claim

> Did the head of the GSA's Federal Acquisition Service invest in a Thrive Capital fund while his office dealt with Thrive-backed companies such as Ramp and Databricks?

## Bottom line

Documented: Josh Gruenbaum, as GSA's FAS commissioner, arranged early meetings with Thrive- and Founders Fund-backed Ramp over a SmartPay pilot of up to $25M, prompting Rep. Connolly's May 2025 letter.

## What holds up

- GSA's Federal Acquisition Service commissioner Josh Gruenbaum (ex-KKR, ex-Moelis) helped arrange early meetings between GSA and Ramp, a startup backed by Thrive and Founders Fund, about a SmartPay charge-card pilot worth up to $25M.
- Rep. Gerald Connolly opened a congressional probe of the Ramp courtship.
- GSA later awarded Thrive-backed Databricks a sole-source contract.
- Thrive and Ramp deny any improper contact.
- Beyond Josh Kushner: the same official worked directly for Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff on Gaza diplomacy and the Board of Peace, while holding a Josh Kushner fund investment.

## What needs correcting

- GSA's administrator blocked a Ramp award in April 2026 and asked Gruenbaum to step aside; he then left FAS (partly accurate)
- Gruenbaum, who led GSA's OneGov programme, struck government-wide deals with AI companies in which Thrive holds stakes (OpenAI, Anthropic and others) (partly accurate)
- Aggregators give the Databricks award as $18M; contract records show a ceiling of $1,824,000 (order 47QPCA26F0011, Feb 2026, five months).
- 2026 write-ups describe Rep. Connolly as probing the Thrive investment; his only letter was dated 2025-05-09, was about Ramp's courtship, and he died on 2025-05-21, a year before the WSJ disclosure.
- The Databricks order was issued by GSA's Technology Transformation Service (within FAS), not FAS acquisition centres directly; the claim does not say Gruenbaum signed it and no source does.
- The claim's 'OneGov deals with OpenAI, Anthropic and others in which Thrive holds stakes': Thrive's OpenAI stake is documented, but no primary source shows a Thrive stake in Anthropic.
- Ramp's lead investor is Founders Fund (Thiel); Thrive is a participant, though it reportedly co-led a 2026 round.

## What is unproven

- Gruenbaum personally invested $100,001-$250,000 in a Thrive Capital vehicle in March 2025.
- After leaving government, Gruenbaum joined Valor Equity Partners, founded by DOGE veteran and Musk ally Antonio Gracias.

## In more detail

- FAS's TTS gave Thrive-led Databricks a sole-source order with a $1.824M ceiling in Feb 2026, not $18M. Gruenbaum's own $100,001-$250,000 stake, reportedly in a Databricks-linked Thrive SPV, and the claim that GSA's administrator blocked a Ramp award and pushed him out rest on a single WSJ report seen only via aggregators.
- He says he disclosed, got ethics approval and recused himself, and Thrive denies discussing contracts.
- The family layer is that he simultaneously worked for Jared Kushner on Gaza and the Board of Peace; a Thrive stake in OneGov vendor Anthropic is unverified.

### 1. GSA's Federal Acquisition Service commissioner Josh Gruenbaum (ex-KKR, ex-Moelis) helped arrange early meetings between GSA and Ramp, a startup backed by Thrive and Founders Fund, about a SmartPay charge-card pilot worth up to $25M.

**Confirmed** · Evidence: Documented

- ProPublica (2025-04-17) reported that senior GSA officials met Ramp executives at least four times before a public SmartPay opportunity opened (a Commercial Solutions Opening, RFI issued 2025-03-20), and that Gruenbaum organized some of the meetings.
- The pilot was worth up to $25M within SmartPay, which the coverage calls a roughly $700B program.
- Ramp's first and largest investor is Peter Thiel's Founders Fund (seven rounds).
- Thrive participated in Ramp's June 2025 Series E and later rounds, and per Bloomberg sources co-led a reported ~$1.85B round at about $60B in Oct 2026 (Ramp has not confirmed).
- GSA rejected any suggestion of preferential contracting.
- Gruenbaum had been at KKR for almost five years (director, credit team) and earlier at Moelis.
- What this does not show: no source says Ramp was ever awarded the pilot.
- Amounts: up to $25M; roughly $700B; ~$1.85B; about $60B
- Dates: 2025-04-17; 2025-03-20; June 2025; Oct 2026

### 4. GSA later awarded Thrive-backed Databricks a sole-source contract.

**Confirmed** · Evidence: Documented for the award (contract records); the link to the investment is single-source

- GSA's Technology Transformation Service (part of FAS, which Gruenbaum ran) posted a sole-source justification on 2026-02-10 for 'Databricks Engineering Services' (order 47QPCA26F0011 under MAS contract 47QTCA25D00D3), citing FAR 8.401(b) and GSAR 538.7104-3(b)(3).
- The order is fixed-price, five months, with a ceiling of $1,824,000 (HigherGov).
- That settles the aggregators' '$18M' figure as an error; the WSJ figure is $1.8M. Thrive led Databricks's $10B Series J in Dec 2024 at a $62B valuation (Gunderson Dettmer, Alternatives Watch).
- Per the WSJ as relayed, the award came about ten months after the investment, and Gruenbaum says he recused himself.
- What this does not show: no source says Gruenbaum signed or steered this order, and a $1.8M software-services order is immaterial to a ~$100B+ company.
- The issue is the appearance of a conflict and whether the recusal was effective.
- Audit: HigherGov, govcontractfinder and samsearch mirrors again show Databricks Federal LLC, order 47QPCA26F0011, ceiling $1,824,000, five months, one bidder, posted 10 Feb 2026 by TTS.
- Amounts: $1,824,000; $18M; $1.8M; $10B; $62B
- Dates: 2026-02-10; Dec 2024; 10 Feb 2026
- Sources: [15](https://www.highergov.com/contract/47QTCA25D00D3-47QPCA26F0011/) [16](https://govcontractfinder.com/contracts/sole-source-justification-for-databricks-engineering-service-47qpca26f0011) [17](https://www.gunder.com/en/news-insights/client-news/gunderson-dettmer-represents-thrive-capital-in-leading-databricks-dollar10b-series-j) [18](https://www.alternativeswatch.com/2024/12/17/databricks-raises-10-billion-dollars-series-j-round-62-billion-valuation-thrive-capital-andreessen/) [10](https://cryptobriefing.com/thrive-capital-trump-adviser-contract-concerns/) [11](https://www.edgen.tech/fr/news/post/trump-advisers-thrive-capital-investment-draws-scrutiny-over-18m-contract)

## Denials and context

- Gruenbaum: disclosed the investment, received ethics approval, recused himself from the relevant matters; departure planned and voluntary.
- Thrive: neither Thrive nor Joshua Kushner discussed government contracts with Gruenbaum.
- Ramp: unaware of the investment (C66).
  - GSA (2025): rejects any suggestion of unfair or preferential contracting.
- OneGov deals at $1 or $0.25 per agency generate little or no direct revenue; their value to vendors is market access.
  - Many other vendors without Thrive ties (Google, Meta, xAI, Microsoft, AWS, Snowflake) got similar deals.
- A documented relationship is not proof of wrongdoing; no source alleges that the Databricks order was improperly justified or that Jared Kushner had any role.
- Disclosure: Anthropic, maker of the model that produced this report, is one of the OneGov vendors.
  - It is treated like every other company here.
- GSA official (per WSJ, relayed): Gruenbaum recused himself from the procurement and did not advocate for Ramp's business specifically.

## Full report

**Verdict: mostly-accurate (confidence: medium).** The procurement side is documented in contract records, congressional letters and several outlets. The personal-stake side rests on one WSJ report (late May 2026) that we saw only through aggregators. Some details in the claim are wrong or unsupported: the "$18M" figure, a 2026 "Connolly probe" and a Thrive stake in Anthropic.

**Disclosure.** Anthropic makes the model that wrote this report, and it is one of the companies with a GSA OneGov deal. It is treated here exactly like every other vendor. We found no primary source for a Thrive stake in Anthropic, and the report says so.

**Method note.** This report builds on C66 (sub-claim 11, the Thrive/GSA link) and C68 (the OpenAI and Musk/xAI nexus). The original pages at Washington Technology, SamSearch, distillintelligence and valorep.com were not read directly. The WSJ original could not be reached either. Findings therefore rely on search-result summaries from several outlets, and each one is graded below.

### Connections found

#### 1. Gruenbaum, Ramp and SmartPay (documented)
- ProPublica (2025-04-17) reported that senior GSA officials met Ramp executives at least four times before a public SmartPay opportunity opened, and that FAS commissioner Josh Gruenbaum organized some of those meetings.
  - The RFI was dated 2025-03-20.
  - The pilot was worth up to $25M, inside a program the coverage calls roughly $700B.
- Ramp's backers:
  - Founders Fund (Peter Thiel) is Ramp's first and largest investor.
  - Thrive took part in Ramp's June 2025 Series E ($16B valuation) and later rounds.
  - Bloomberg sources say Thrive and Dragoneer co-led a round of about $1.85B at about $60B in Oct 2026. Ramp has not confirmed it.
- GSA rejected any suggestion of preferential contracting.
- Gruenbaum's background: almost five years at KKR (director, credit team) and earlier at Moelis.
- We found no award to Ramp.

#### 2. Connolly's probe (documented, with a correction)
- On 2025-05-09 Rep. Gerald Connolly wrote to Acting GSA Administrator Stephen Ehikian. He asked for:
  - Ramp's bids
  - the RFI timeline
  - communications with officials tied to the President and DOGE
- Connolly died on 2025-05-21. We found no public GSA reply.
- Some 2026 aggregators describe Connolly as "probing" the Thrive investment. That is a mistake: his letter came a year before the WSJ disclosure and does not mention Thrive.

#### 3. Gruenbaum's Thrive investment (single-source)
- **What the WSJ reported** (as relayed by Crypto Briefing 2026-05-31, Edgen, EconoTimes and Wikipedia):
  - In March 2025 Gruenbaum put $100,001-$250,000 through a private wealth manager into a special-purpose vehicle tied to Databricks.
  - The vehicle is described as a $194M Thrive vehicle.
  - Some senior GSA officials did not know about the investment.
- **What Gruenbaum says:** he disclosed it, got ethics approval and recused himself. He gave the WSJ documentation.
- **What we could not check:** his OGE 278 form.
- Nobody disputes that the investment exists.

#### 4. Databricks sole-source order (award documented; the link to his stake is single-source)
- **The order:** GSA's Technology Transformation Service, which sits inside FAS, posted a sole-source justification on 2026-02-10.
  - Order 47QPCA26F0011, "Databricks Engineering Services," under MAS contract 47QTCA25D00D3
  - Legal basis cited: FAR 8.401(b) and GSAR 538.7104-3(b)(3)
  - Fixed price, five months, ceiling **$1,824,000** (HigherGov)
- **Thrive's stake:** Thrive led Databricks's $10B Series J in Dec 2024 at a $62B valuation.
- **What this does not show:**
  - No source says Gruenbaum signed or steered the order.
  - The amount is immaterial to Databricks.
  - The issue is the appearance of a conflict and whether the recusal worked.

#### 5. Gruenbaum's exit (departure documented; the reason is single-source)
- **Documented:** Federal News Network, Nextgov and MeriTalk reported in April 2026 that Gruenbaum was leaving FAS. Administrator Ed Forst named Laura Stanton acting commissioner.
- **Single-source** (WSJ via Edgen, citing people familiar): Forst blocked a ~$25M Ramp award in April and asked Gruenbaum to go. Gruenbaum says the exit was planned and voluntary.
- **Unresolved timing:** a SAM.gov mirror shows GSA cancelled the commercial-payments CSO (47QRAB25R0002) on 2026-02-11 and shifted to next-generation SmartPay. That makes the "April block" hard to place.
- GSA named a permanent FAS chief in Nov 2026.

#### 7. Family layer beyond Josh Kushner (roles documented; the overlap is inferred)

### Corrections
- The Databricks award ceiling is $1.824M, not $18M.
- Connolly's only action was the 2025-05-09 letter about Ramp. He died on 2025-05-21.
- The Databricks order was issued by TTS, within FAS. No source says Gruenbaum signed it.
- A Thrive stake in Anthropic is not supported by primary sources.
- Founders Fund, not Thrive, is Ramp's lead investor.

### Denials and context
- **Gruenbaum:** disclosed the investment, got ethics approval and recused himself. He says his departure was voluntary.
- **Thrive:** neither Thrive nor Josh Kushner discussed government contracts with him.
- **Ramp:** says it was unaware of the investment.
