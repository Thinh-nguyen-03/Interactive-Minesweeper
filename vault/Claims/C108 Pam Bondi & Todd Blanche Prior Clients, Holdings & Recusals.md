# C108: Pam Bondi & Todd Blanche: Prior Clients, Holdings & Recusals

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Type:** Check · Regulators & Enforcement

## The Claim

> Which Justice Department decisions touched the former clients or holdings of Pam Bondi and Todd Blanche, and were they recused?

## Bottom Line

The two officials' OGE filings document the core of this lead.

## What Holds Up

- Pam Bondi's nominee disclosure lists her Ballard Partners income and the Ballard clients she served, her Trump Media holdings and her other income.
- Bondi's ethics agreement set recusal commitments for her former employers and clients, and she received no waiver or authorization.
- Bondi sold her Trump Media shares and warrants on 2 April 2025, and Rep. Raskin asked the inspector general to investigate.
- Bondi, a former registered agent for Qatar, signed the Justice Department memo clearing acceptance of Qatar's 747 without recusing (mostly accurate)
- Todd Blanche's disclosure lists his representation of Donald Trump, his other law clients and his crypto holdings.
- Blanche signed the 7 April 2025 memo ending 'regulation by prosecution' and disbanding the crypto enforcement team while he still held crypto, and disposed of it only in late May and early June 2025, partly by gifts to relatives.
- Current positions and dates: Bondi left as Attorney General in April 2026; Blanche was confirmed as Attorney General on 8 August 2026 and sworn in on 10 August 2026.

## What Needs Correcting

- Bondi and Blanche narrowed FARA and FCPA enforcement in ways that benefit lobbyists and companies, including former clients (partly accurate)
- Blanche, Trump's former defence lawyer, was advised to recuse from Trump matters and did not fully do so (partly accurate)
- C65 (Blanche sub-claim) says 'ProPublica's range is $158K-$470K and the Senate letter's $159K-$485K'. The record shows the reverse. ProPublica (22 December 2025, read in full) gives $159,000-$485,000. The senators' 28 January 2026 letter gives $158,000-$470,000. By arithmetic from his nominee 278, the coins alone total $158,010-$470,000 and $159,011-$485,000 with his Coinbase shares.
- C65 says the divestment 'took place May 31-June 3, 2025'. His 278-T shows crypto dispositions dated 30 May to 2 June 2025 and the Coinbase sale on 29 May; 3 June is the date he signed the form. The senators' letter also uses 31 May-3 June.
- C65 says the June 2025 filing reports the crypto holdings 'were gifted in their entirety'. The filing's note names only bitcoin, solana, cardano and ether as gifted. The same form lists bitcoin, solana and ether as 'Sale' lines, and ethereum classic, polygon, polkadot, Quant and Coinbase stock as sales. Basic Attention Token and 'Decentralized' appear in no later filing reviewed.
- C65 says 'no waiver has been published, and no official finding was found'. Still true, with three additions. His 5 June 2025 compliance certification answers 'No' to receiving a section 208 waiver. In July 2026 written answers he said he did receive a written 208(b)(1) determination before the memo but would not disclose it. An OGE note of 2 September 2026 on his annual report says 'DOJ IG declined further action' on seven crypto dispositions.
- C65 and C102 say Bondi's TMTG sale was 'disclosed May 14, 2025' or 'made public in mid-May 2025' and that the form itself was not read. The 278-T (now read in full) was signed and agency-certified on 5 May 2025 and OGE-certified on 12 May 2025, with values and dates as already recorded. C102's 'confirmed in early Feb 2025' can be made precise: 4 February 2025.
- C102 describes Bondi's ethics agreement as requiring divestiture 'within 90 days of taking office'. The agreement says 'not later than 90 days after my confirmation', which made the deadline about 5 May 2025.
- C65, C73 and others date Bondi's removal to 2 April 2026. That is the date it was announced; her OGE termination report gives 1 April 2026 as the date of termination. Both dates should be stated where precision matters.
- The Senate Judiciary Democrats' 15 January 2025 release says Bondi was 'earning $115,000 per month' from Qatar. The $115,000 monthly fee was paid to Ballard Partners; her own share is not on record (already noted in C65).
- CLC's first complaint (22 January 2026) gave Blanche's holdings as $274,013-$785,000. Its 23 January correction gives $159,011-$485,000, but its conclusion still says 'up to $785,000'. The lower range is the one supported by his filing.

## What Is Unproven

- Bondi took part in other Justice Department matters involving former Ballard clients (Warner Bros. Discovery bidders, UnitedHealth in the Mangione case).
- Blanche's participation in the crypto memo was cleared by a waiver or ethics determination.
- Complaints about Blanche's crypto holdings led to an inspector-general or OGE finding.

## In More Detail

- Bondi earned $1.07M from Ballard Partners and was paid $2.97M in DWAC shares by Trump Media's predecessor.
- She sold her Trump Media shares and warrants on 2 April 2025, within her 90-day deadline.
- Blanche, Trump's former defence lawyer, signed the 7 April 2025 memo disbanding NCET while he held $158K-$470K of crypto.
- He disposed of it by sale and by gifts to relatives between 29 May and 2 June 2025.
- His 5 June 2025 certification answers 'No' to 'I received a waiver pursuant to 18 U.S.C. § 208', but in July 2026 he answered 'Yes' when senators asked whether he received a written 208(b)(1) determination before the memo; a 208(b)(1) determination is the statutory form of such a waiver, and the documents cannot be reconciled on their face.
- No authority has addressed the discrepancy.
- OGE notes that DOJ's inspector general 'declined further action'.
- The links between FARA, FCPA or other decisions and specific former clients (Qatar, Ballard clients) rest on timing, recusal requests and our own cross-referencing.
- No authority has found that either official breached 18 U.S.C.
- 208 or an ethics agreement.

- No public recusal memo listing specific matters was found.

### 9. Blanche's participation in the crypto memo was cleared by a waiver or ethics determination.

**Unverified**

- Grade: the two documents conflict on their face, no waiver or determination is public, and no authority has addressed the conflict. (1) Certification.
- His Certification of Ethics Agreement Compliance as Deputy AG, digitally signed 5 June 2025 (read in full, including the form's answer fields), asks at item 7a: 'I received a waiver pursuant to 18 U.S.C. § 208.'
- The box marked is 'No', and the follow-up 'If yes, indicate the date of the waiver and indicate the financial interest covered by the waiver' is blank.
- Item 7b ('I received an authorization pursuant to 5 C.F.R. § 2635.502(d)') and 7c (a 2635.503(c) waiver) are also 'No'.
- Item 5 is 'Yes': 'I have complied with my interim recusal obligations pending the divestitures required by my ethics agreement.'
- The appointee comment reads: 'Some assets were gifted to family members, whose financial interests are not imputed to me, and did not require a transaction report.'
- The section's heading adds: '(Consult with your agency's DAEO if uncertain under what authority a waiver or authorization was granted.)' The question is not limited by date or matter. (2) Senate answers.
- Sen. Hirono's written question 16(b) (questions dated 17 July 2026; DOJ's compiled responses dated 20 July 2026, read in excerpt) asks: 'Did you receive a written determination pursuant to 18 U.S.C. § 208(b)(1) in advance of issuing your April 7, 2025 memorandum regarding cryptocurrency enforcement?
- If so, please provide a copy.'
- His answer: 'Yes, but such determinations are not subject to disclosure.'
- To 16(f), why he did not recuse, he answered that the memo 'set general enforcement priorities and did not constitute a particular matter having a direct and predictable effect on my personal financial interests within the meaning of 18 U.S.C. § 208.
- The matter was reviewed with, and cleared by, the Department's ethics officials in advance.'
- To 16(a) he said the matter 'was appropriately flagged, addressed, and cleared in advance with the Department's ethics officials' and declined to itemise 'privileged internal ethics communications'. (3) Can they be reconciled? Not from the documents.
- A '208(b)(1) determination' and a 'section 208 waiver' are the same instrument: section 208(b)(1) exempts an employee who 'receives in advance a written determination' that the interest is not so substantial as to affect his services, and the White House's own posted 208(b)(1) instruments are titled 'Waiver' (Kennedy's of 18 February 2025 says 'This waiver reflects my determination...').
- On the dates, a determination received before 7 April 2025 would predate the 5 June 2025 certification, which answers 'No'.
- His other answer, that the memo was not a particular matter, would mean no waiver was needed, which also sits uneasily with having received one.

## Full Report

**Verdict:** mostly-accurate (confidence: medium)

**Question:** Which Justice Department decisions touched the former clients or holdings of Pam Bondi and Todd Blanche, and were they recused?

### Summary

Both officials' own OGE filings document the core of this lead. All of the filings listed below were read in full from extapps2.oge.gov:

- Bondi: nominee report, ethics agreement, compliance certification, 278-T and termination report.
- Blanche: two nominee reports, two ethics agreements, 278-T, 2025 compliance certification and 2026 annual report.
- Blanche's 2026 Attorney General compliance certification was read only in part.

**Bondi**
- Her nominee report lists $1,067,000 from Ballard Partners and $2,969,563 of consulting pay from Renatus LLC (Trump Media's predecessor), paid in DWAC shares.
- She held Trump Media shares and warrants worth $1M-$5M each on that report.
- Her ethics agreement required her to divest Trump Media within 90 days of her 4 February 2025 confirmation. It barred her for one year from matters in which Ballard, or any client she had served in the past year, was a party.
- She sold the shares ($1M-$5M) and warrants ($250K-$500K) on 2 April 2025, the day of the tariff announcement, and reported the sale on 5 May 2025.
- Her certification reports no waiver or authorization.

**Blanche**
- Blanche, Trump's former defence lawyer, held $158K-$470K in ten cryptocurrencies, plus Coinbase shares, when he signed the 7 April 2025 memo "Ending Regulation By Prosecution". The memo disbanded the National Cryptocurrency Enforcement Team (NCET).
- His 278-T shows the crypto and Coinbase shares disposed of between 29 May and 2 June 2025, within his deadline. A note says bitcoin, solana, cardano and ether were "gifted in their entirety" to his grandchild and adult children.
- His 5 June 2025 certification answers "No" to "I received a waiver pursuant to 18 U.S.C. § 208."
- In July 2026, asked in writing whether he received "a written determination pursuant to 18 U.S.C. § 208(b)(1)" before the memo, he answered: "Yes, but such determinations are not subject to disclosure." A 208(b)(1) determination is the statutory form of a section 208 waiver, so the two answers cannot be reconciled on the face of the documents. Neither he nor DOJ has explained the difference, and no authority has addressed it.
- An OGE note dated 2 September 2026 on his annual report reads: "DOJ IG declined further action" on seven of the crypto lines.

**What remains allegation or inference:** that the FARA and FCPA enforcement changes, the Qatar jet memo, the Warner Bros. review or the Mangione case were shaped by the officials' former clients. No inspector-general, OGE or court finding that either official breached 18 U.S.C. 208 or an ethics agreement was found as of 17 November 2026.

**Current posts:**
- Bondi was AG from 4 February 2025. Her OGE termination report gives 1 April 2026 as her termination date; the removal was announced on 2 April. She was named to PCAST in late May 2026.
- Blanche was confirmed as Deputy AG on 5 March 2025 and sworn in on 6 March 2025. He was acting AG from 2 April 2026 (his written Senate answers). The Senate confirmed him as AG 50-49 on 8 August 2026, a date his own OGE certification confirms, and he was sworn in on 10 August 2026.

**Evidence rule applied:** holding an asset, or a decision that benefits a former employer or client, is not by itself a violation. Each point states the rule (18 U.S.C. 208, the ethics agreement, the 5 C.F.R. 2635.502 recusal period), whether a waiver or recusal exists, and whether any authority has made a finding.

The matter was reviewed with, and cleared by, the Department's ethics officials in advance."
  - 16(a): it "was appropriately flagged, addressed, and cleared in advance with the Department's ethics officials"; he would not itemise "privileged internal ethics communications".
- **Can the two be reconciled? Not from the documents.**
  - A 208(b)(1) "determination" and a section 208 "waiver" are the same instrument. The statute exempts an employee who "receives in advance a written determination" that the interest is not so substantial as to affect his services. The White House's own posted 208(b)(1) instruments are titled "Waiver"; Kennedy's of 18 February 2025 says "This waiver reflects my determination..." [52].

That would fit the "No" but not his "Yes" to a question naming 208(b)(1). Neither he nor DOJ has offered this or any other explanation, and the document has not been produced.

- **Disclosure rule:** 18 U.S.C. 208(d)(1) says a copy of any determination granting a (b)(1) exemption "shall be made available to the public" on request, subject to FOIA exemptions (also 5 C.F.R. 2640.304). His answer that such determinations "are not subject to disclosure" does not match the rule as written.
