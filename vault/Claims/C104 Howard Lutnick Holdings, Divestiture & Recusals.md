# C104: Howard Lutnick: Holdings, Divestiture & Recusals

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Type:** Check · Regulators & Enforcement

## The Claim

> What did Howard Lutnick divest, to whom, and which of his department's decisions touch his former businesses?

## Bottom Line

Filings read in full document the divestiture. Lutnick signed agreements on 16 May 2025, inside his 90-day window, to sell his Cantor, BGC and Newmark interests at a fixed price, and gave up all economic benefit from that date.

## What Holds Up

- Lutnick's nominee disclosure and ethics agreement: what he held and what he promised.
- Lutnick sold his BGC and Newmark shares back to the companies in May 2025.
- His Cantor Fitzgerald interest was transferred to trusts for his children, with 26North and Glenn August as minority investors; the sale closed in October 2025.
- He received certificates of divestiture deferring capital-gains tax.
- A White House waiver let him take part in matters affecting Cantor, BGC and Newmark while he still owned them.
- Lutnick's 2025 income included a $192M Cantor distribution and more than $250M in total (mostly accurate)

## What Needs Correcting

- The Cantor divestiture missed the 90-day ethics deadline (misleading)
- Cantor's tariff-refund claim trading touches Commerce's tariff policy (partly accurate)
- Cantor's Tether and stablecoin business is a Lutnick conflict at Commerce (partly accurate)
- Cantor and affiliates have business before Commerce in chips, satellites, data centres and critical minerals (partly accurate)
- **Claimed:** The divestiture missed the 90-day deadline  
  **The record shows:** The ethics agreement required sale agreements within 90 days (signed 16 May 2025, deadline 19 May) and completion after regulatory approvals; closing on 6 Oct 2025 is within those terms, as his 19 June 2025 certification explains.
- C35: 'minority stakes went to 26North and Glenn August'. The White House waiver names the buyers as 26North, Oak Hill Advisors and Cantor Fitzgerald, L.P. (Glenn August founded Oak Hill). C35's 'Bloomberg: October 2025' closing is confirmed and narrowed by his own 278-T: consummated 6 Oct 2025. The assets sold also included CF Group Management, Inc. (Cantor's managing general partner) and his BGC Class B shares.
- C35/C50: 'Commerce: Lutnick fully complied with ... recusal requirements'. Add the primary record: a limited 18 U.S.C. 208(b)(1) waiver (White House Counsel, 8 Jul 2025) authorised his participation in matters of general applicability affecting Cantor, BGC and Newmark until closing. His 24 May and 19 June 2025 certifications show no waiver at those dates.
- C50 (tariff refunds): the ~$10M trade claim cited by Raskin comes from the Cantor sales letter reviewed by Wired, not from separate internal documents. Cantor's later explanation is that a salesman 'erroneously' believed the trade would be approved.
- C50 (Blumenthal letter): the $250M income and $192M distribution come from his 2025 annual OGE report as reported by WSJ and CNBC. The filing describes the $192M as a tax distribution under the ethics agreement. The report is not yet posted by OGE.
- C77: 'the NYT reported ... recusal from matters involving 106 corporate entities'. We could not match that number to the ethics agreement, whose Appendix A lists several hundred Cantor, BGC and Newmark subsidiaries. Treat the 106 figure as unverified. The specific-party bar on Cantor ran for one year from his resignation (to about Feb 2026), not indefinitely.
- C35: 'the divestiture package was announced 19 May 2025 and parts took effect 16 May 2025'. Precisely: all sale agreements were signed on 16 May 2025; BGC Class A and Newmark buybacks, Nasdaq, Disney and the trust sales are dated 16 May; Cantor, CF Group Management, BGC Class B and retirement-account shares are dated 6 Oct 2025.

## What Is Unproven

- Complaints and requests for investigation have produced a finding.

## In More Detail

- BGC and Newmark bought back about $278M of his shares.
- The Cantor sale, to buyers the White House names as 26North, Oak Hill Advisors and Cantor Fitzgerald (with Cantor saying his stake went to trusts for his children led by Brandon Lutnick), closed on 6 Oct 2025.
- His ethics agreement allowed that closing date, so the 'missed deadline' charge does not hold on its terms.
- From 8 Jul to 6 Oct 2025 a White House waiver let him work on broad policy affecting the three firms.
- OGE issued six certificates of divestiture, which defer tax.
- Cantor's tariff-refund pitch, Tether ties, Satellogic and data-centre work and its minerals deals all overlap with Commerce's remit, but no decision by Lutnick favouring them is documented.
- The sale price, the Tether loan's role and his 2025 annual report are not public.
- Eight requests from Democratic members of Congress and one watchdog complaint have produced no finding.
- Lutnick, Commerce and Cantor say he complied fully.

## Sub-Claims

### 1. Lutnick's nominee disclosure and ethics agreement: what he held and what he promised.

**Confirmed**

- Grade: documented (OGE Form 278e nominee report e-signed 30 Dec 2024 and ethics agreement dated 21 Jan 2025, both read in full).
- The 278e lists more than 800 positions and holdings.
- Part 2 reports Cantor Fitzgerald, L.P. partnership distributions of $198,984,693, a $16M salary/bonus from Cantor Fitzgerald Securities, $30M salary/bonus plus a $5M equity bonus from Newmark, and $8M salary/bonus, an $11M equity bonus and a $61.3M '2023 BGC unit' item from BGC.
- As a nominee report, its income period runs from 1 January 2023 to the December 2024 filing date, so the $198,984,693 covers about two years; endnotes say it aggregates all Cantor distributions attributed to him, including those on the Cantor interest then held by the Lutnick 1999 Descendants' Trust (a grantor trust at the time, value 'Over $50,000,000').
- No Tether holding is listed for him personally; the Tether convertible bond is Cantor's.
- The agreement commits him to: resign from Cantor, BGC, Newmark and their subsidiaries on confirmation; sign agreements to sell his and his trusts' interests in all three 'as soon as practicable but not later than 90 days after my confirmation, for a sum certain', with the buyer receiving all economic interest from signing and the divestiture completed 'after required regulatory approvals are obtained'; request a written 18 U.S.C.
- 208(b)(1) waiver for the ownership he would still hold between signing and closing; recuse from any particular matter with a direct and predictable effect on the three firms until divested; and, under 5 C.F.R.
- 2635.502, stay out of specific-party matters in which they are or represent a party for one year after resignation or as long as he holds an interest, whichever is later.
- He also had to sell GE Healthcare, GE Vernova, GE Aerospace, Nasdaq, Disney, Roivant, Kimberly Akimbo LLC and Kiajia LLC within 90 days.
- He kept trusteeships of family trusts, about 30 property and vehicle LLCs and promissory notes owed to him by the Lutnick 2020 and 1999 Descendants' Trusts, with a recusal on matters affecting the trusts' ability to repay.
- The nominee report values those notes at $1M-$5M (2020 trust) and $5M-$25M (1999 trust).
- For each firm the agreement also bars him, 'until the sale closes and I receive the final payment', from specific-party matters in which the buyer is a party.
- Appendix D shows he left the Satellogic board in Nov 2024 and Cantor Equity Partners and Cantor Equity Partners I in Dec 2024.
- Confirmed 18 Feb 2025, so the 90-day signing deadline fell on 19 May 2025.
- Amounts: $198,984,693; $16M; $30M; $5M; $8M
- Dates: 30 Dec 2024; 21 Jan 2025; 1 January 2023; December 2024; Nov 2024

### 4. The Cantor divestiture missed the 90-day ethics deadline.

**Misleading** · Evidence: Documented

- The agreement required him to sign sale agreements within 90 days of confirmation (by 19 May 2025) and to complete the sale after regulatory approvals, with no fixed outer date.
- He signed on 16 May 2025, within the window, and closed on 6 Oct 2025.
- On 24 May 2025 he certified to OGE that he had completed the divestitures 'within the time period specified' (278-T 'Forthcoming').
- An updated certification dated 19 June 2025 (both read in full) adds that on 19 May 2025 he and his trusts 'entered into agreements to divest' and that these 'are subject to regulatory approvals and will be completed once obtained'.
- The certification gives 19 May as the signing date, while both 278-Ts and the White House waiver give 16 May 2025.
- Either date is inside the window.
- Both certifications mark 'No' for any section 208 waiver as of those dates.
- The lawmakers' letter to the Commerce inspector general (17 Dec 2025) asks why the divestiture was not completed until 6 Oct 2025 and says he committed to divest by 19 May.
- That reads the agreement more strictly than its text, which separates signing from closing.
- The criticism that he kept legal ownership for almost eight months as Secretary is accurate.
- We found no OGE notice of non-compliance and no OGE or Senate committee statement that a deadline was missed or met.
- OGE certified both 278-Ts.
- A side point from the 17 June 278-T: three GE sales dated 7 Mar 2025 (GE HealthCare $1K-$15K, GE Vernova $1K-$15K, GE Aerospace $15K-$50K; at most $80K in total) were first reported on a report signed on 17 Jun 2025, 102 days later.
- The form says transactions must be disclosed 'within 30 days of receiving notification of a transaction but not later than 45 days after the transaction', which put the deadline at about 21 Apr 2025.
- Each line is marked 'Notification received over 30 days ago: No', and the form gives no explanation. Under 5 C.F.R.
- 2634.704 a $200 late-filing fee applies to a report filed more than 30 days after its deadline unless waived; whether one was charged is not public.
- The sales themselves met the ethics agreement's 90-day deadline, the report was certified by the Commerce ethics official (18 Jun) and OGE (14 Jul 2025), and we found no reporting on the lateness.
- Roivant Sciences, also on the agreement's 90-day list and held at $1M-$5M on the nominee report, appears on neither published 278-T; whether it was sold before he took office (when no 278-T is required) is not shown.
- Amounts: $1K-$15K; $15K-$50K; $80K; $200; $1M-$5M
- Dates: 19 May 2025; 16 May 2025; 6 Oct 2025; 24 May 2025; 19 June 2025
- Sources: [1](https://extapps2.oge.gov/201/Presiden.nsf/PAS+Index/436440318CFB67BE85258C1C003219C1/$FILE/Lutnick%2C%20Howard%20%20finalEA.pdf) [3](https://extapps2.oge.gov/201/Presiden.nsf/PAS+Index/4452B23B6A938EFE85258C98002C2FD5/$FILE/Lutnick%20EA%20Certification%20Portfolio%201%20of%201.pdf) [4](https://extapps2.oge.gov/201/Presiden.nsf/PAS+Index/66A69EB879848CF885258CC9002C8582/$FILE/Howard-Lutnick-06.17.2025-278T.pdf) [5](https://extapps2.oge.gov/201/Presiden.nsf/PAS+Index/8C12E5475578E84385258D5C00345940/$FILE/Howard-Lutnick-11.05.2025-278T.pdf) [12](https://www.warren.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/warren_dean_letter_to_commerce_ig.pdf)

### 6. A White House waiver let him take part in matters affecting Cantor, BGC and Newmark while he still owned them.

**Confirmed**

- Grade: documented (memo from White House Counsel David Warrington, 8 Jul 2025, read in full).
- It is a 'limited interim waiver' under 18 U.S.C. 208(b)(1).
- It lets him take part in particular matters of general applicability that may affect Cantor, Newmark and BGC until regulatory approvals arrive and the sales close.
- It does not cover specific-party matters in which they are or represent a party, which are screened by a 'gatekeeping agreement' with the Commerce ethics office.
- It also bars meetings with representatives of the three firms, covers no other asset, and lapses at closing.
- The rationale is that he had already forgone all economic gain or loss from 16 May 2025, so he had no remaining financial stake in outcomes. OGE was consulted.
- OGE's index lists a 'Conflict of Interest Waiver' for Lutnick but marks it 'Oops!
- We need to upload this file'.
- Whether that entry is this memo or a separate document is not shown.
- The waiver means participation in broad policy, such as tariff or chip-export rules, was authorised from 8 Jul to 6 Oct 2025 even where it affected the three firms.
- Lawmakers asked the Commerce inspector general (17 Dec 2025) how it was enforced. No answer was found.
- Dates: 8 Jul 2025; 16 May 2025; 8 Jul; 6 Oct 2025; 17 Dec 2025
- Sources: [7](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Howard-W.-Lutnick-Limited-Waiver-Pursuant-to-18-U.S.C.-208b1.pdf) [6](https://extapps2.oge.gov/201/Presiden.nsf/PAS+Index?OpenView&Start=1&Count=2000&Expand=121) [12](https://www.warren.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/warren_dean_letter_to_commerce_ig.pdf)

- Amounts: 20-30%; ~$10 million

## Full Report

**Verdict:** mostly-accurate (confidence: medium)

**Question:** What did Howard Lutnick divest, to whom, and which of his department's decisions touch his former businesses?

### Summary

Filings read in full document the divestiture step by step.

**What he agreed to (21 Jan 2025).**
- Resign from Cantor Fitzgerald, BGC and Newmark on confirmation.
- Sign agreements within 90 days of confirmation to sell all his and his trusts' interests in them "for a sum certain", and complete the sales after regulatory approvals.
- Recuse from matters affecting the three firms until he had divested.
- He was confirmed on 18 Feb 2025, so the 90-day signing deadline was 19 May 2025.

**What he did.**
- **16 May 2025:** he signed the sale agreements, within the window, and gave up all economic benefit from that date.
- **BGC and Newmark** bought back his shares for $151.5M and about $127M. His 278-T lists both sales on 16 May.
- **6 Oct 2025:** the Cantor sale closed. His second 278-T lists Cantor, its managing partner CF Group Management, BGC Class B shares and smaller interests as sold that day, "following regulatory approvals".
- **Buyers:** the White House waiver names 26North, Oak Hill Advisors and Cantor Fitzgerald, L.P. Cantor says his stake went to trusts for his adult children, with Brandon Lutnick as controlling trustee.
- **Price:** not public.

**Waivers and tax.**
- From 8 Jul to 6 Oct 2025, a limited White House waiver under 18 U.S.C. 208(b)(1) let him work on broad policy affecting the three firms. It did not cover specific deals or meetings with the firms.
- OGE issued six certificates of divestiture, which defer capital-gains tax if the proceeds are reinvested in Treasuries or diversified funds.

**Cantor's business before Commerce.** Cantor's tariff-refund pitch, its Tether ties, its Satellogic, data-centre and critical-minerals work all fall in or near Commerce's remit. No decision by Lutnick favouring Cantor or a Cantor client is documented.

**Investigations.** Eight requests for investigation from Democratic members of Congress, and one Campaign Legal Center complaint, have produced no finding.

**What is not on the public record:**
- the Cantor sale price;
- whether Tether's loan to "Dynasty Trust A" funded the purchase;
- his 2025 annual report (described in the press but not posted by OGE);
- an OGE-listed "Conflict of Interest Waiver" that has not been uploaded.

**Evidence rule applied:** holding an asset, or a decision that benefits a former firm, is not a violation by itself. Each point below states the rule (18 U.S.C. 208, the ethics agreement, 5 C.F.R. 2635.502), whether a waiver or recusal exists, and whether any authority has made a finding.

This builds on C35 (Cantor and Tether), C50 (chips, the Tether loan probe, tariff refunds), C65 (officials and former firms) and C77/C60 (critical minerals).

#### 4. "He missed the 90-day deadline": misleading
- **The terms.** The agreement required **signing** within 90 days (by 19 May 2025) and **closing** after regulatory approvals, with no outer date. He signed on 16 May and closed on 6 Oct 2025, which is within those terms.
- **His certifications** (both read in full):
  - On 24 May 2025 he certified that he had completed the divestitures within the time specified, with the 278-T "Forthcoming".
  - An updated certification dated 19 June 2025 explains that the agreements were signed and that closing awaited regulatory approvals. It gives 19 May as the signing date; the 278-Ts and the waiver say 16 May. Either date is inside the window.
  - Both certifications say no section 208 waiver had been received at those dates.
- **The criticism.** The 17 Dec 2025 letter to the Commerce inspector general asks why the divestiture took until 6 Oct. It reads the agreement more strictly than its text. It is accurate that he kept legal ownership for almost eight months as Secretary.
- **Official statements.** We found no OGE notice of non-compliance and no OGE or Senate committee statement either way. OGE certified both 278-Ts.
- **A side point: late-reported GE sales.**
  - Three GE sales dated 7 Mar 2025 (GE HealthCare $1K-$15K, GE Vernova $1K-$15K, GE Aerospace $15K-$50K; at most $80K in total) first appear on the report signed on 17 June, 102 days later.
  - The form says transactions must be disclosed "within 30 days of receiving notification of a transaction but not later than 45 days after the transaction", which put the deadline at about 21 Apr 2025. Each line is marked "Notification received over 30 days ago: No", and the form gives no explanation.
  - Under 5 C.F.R. 2634.704 a $200 late-filing fee applies to a report filed more than 30 days after its deadline, unless waived. Whether one was charged is not public.
  - The sales themselves met the ethics agreement's 90-day deadline. The Commerce ethics official (18 June) and OGE (14 July 2025) certified the report. We found no reporting on the lateness.
  - Roivant Sciences, also on the 90-day list and held at $1M-$5M on the nominee report, appears on neither published 278-T. Whether it was sold before he took office, when no 278-T is required, is not shown.
