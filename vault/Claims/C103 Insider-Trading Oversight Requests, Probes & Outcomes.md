# C103: Insider-Trading Oversight: Requests, Probes & Outcomes

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Type:** Check · Regulators & Enforcement

## The Claim

> What investigations of the insider-trading allegations exist, what did they find, and what law applies?

## Bottom Line

The requests are well documented: at least ten letters from members of Congress (April 2025 to July 2026) asked the SEC, its inspector general, GAO, the Pentagon inspector general, DOJ, state attorneys general and a House committee to investigate trading around the tariff pause, Iran announcements, a reported attempted trade by Defense Secretary Hegseth's broker and the paid Truth API.

## What Holds Up

- Members of Congress asked the SEC, its inspector general and GAO to investigate trading around the 9 April 2025 tariff pause.
- House Democrats asked the House Financial Services Committee to investigate the April 2025 trading itself.
- In 2026 senators asked the SEC and the Pentagon inspector general about trading before Iran and tariff announcements and about a broker for Defense Secretary Hegseth.
- The CFTC is investigating oil-futures trades placed before Trump's Iran posts (mostly accurate)
- Regulators have brought insider-trading cases over prediction-market bets on government information.
- Congress and the prediction-market platforms investigated insider trading on those platforms.
- Enforcement staffing and policy at the SEC, CFTC and DOJ changed in ways that bear on capacity to investigate.

## What Needs Correcting

- The executive branch and the Office of Government Ethics responded with rules or guidance (partly accurate)
- Insider-trading law reaches a president, officials, their families and friends (partly accurate)
- Claimed in the 10 April 2025 and 24 February 2026 House letters: Trump said 'he made $2.5 million today'. The record shows: the Oval Office remark was 'two-and-a-half billion' (Charles Schwab) and '$900 million' (Roger Penske), Trump's own claims about paper gains (C58). The letters cite a social-media post for the quotation.
- Claimed (House Oversight letter of 29 September 2026 and memo of 13 November 2026): a Hyperliquid short was established 'within minutes' of an October 2025 tariff announcement. The record shows: the article both documents cite (Bank Syz, republished by Investing.com) describes a build-up that began about 28-30 hours before the 20:50 UTC post (account funded 16:39 UTC on 9 October) and continued until about 20:49 UTC, and its summary says the position was 'opened 30 hours before'. 'Within minutes' fits the last additions, not the opening. An earlier version of this check called the memo's wording wrong; it is incomplete rather than wrong. No trader has been identified (see C101).
- The White House teleprompter operator's profit was first reported as 'close to $100,000'; the CFTC order puts it at $107,539.02, with a $65,000 penalty (about $172,500 in total).
- The DOJ memo disbanding the National Cryptocurrency Enforcement Team (7 April 2025) was written by Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, not Attorney General Bondi, though it is often attributed to her.
- 2026-11-16 (audit): the George Santos order is for manipulation of a contract on his own attendance, not insider trading; the Van Dyke indictment has five counts; the CFTC complaint and DOJ give slightly different profit figures (more than $404,000 versus about $409,881).

## What Is Unproven

- Senators asked state attorneys general to investigate, and state authorities acted.
- Lawmakers asked the SEC and DOJ to examine the paid Truth API, and the SEC investigated.
- Any authority has found that Trump, his family or a senior official traded on, or tipped others about, nonpublic information around these announcements.

## In More Detail

- Public responses are mostly silence: no SEC, inspector-general, state or House Financial Services investigation of these episodes has been announced, and House Democrats said in February 2026 they had no substantive SEC reply.
- Enforcement that did happen came through the CFTC and DOJ on prediction markets, mostly detected by the platforms: a soldier charged over Maduro bets, settled orders against a White House teleprompter operator and George Santos; the CFTC oil-futures probe is open with no charges after a reported referral retraction.
- No authority has identified a trader in the April 2025 or Iran-related trades or made any finding about Trump, his family or senior officials; that is not exoneration, and the letters are allegations, not findings.
- The STOCK Act names the President in the securities-law duty and tippees can be liable (our summary of the statute and case law), but the law has never been tested against a president and several gaps remain, while SEC enforcement staff fell 18% and CFTC staff about 21% below their ten-year average.

### 11. Insider-trading law reaches a president, officials, their families and friends.

**Partly accurate**

- Securities: STOCK Act section 9(b) (Pub. L. 112-105, read in full) affirms executive-branch employees are 'not exempt' from section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5 and adds Exchange Act section 21A(h), under which each executive-branch employee owes a duty of trust and confidence to the government and citizens for material nonpublic information 'derived from such person's position'; the Act's definition of executive-branch employee expressly includes the President and Vice President.
- Under Dirks v. SEC and Salman v. United States, a tipper is liable if he discloses for a personal benefit, which includes a gift of information to a relative or friend, and a tippee who knows of the breach is liable when trading.
- So, on our reading of the statute and case law (no court has applied it to these facts), officials, and family or friends who trade on a tip, are covered on paper.
- Commodities: CEA section 4c(a)(3)-(4) (7 U.S.C. 6c) bars any 'employee or agent of any department or agency of the Federal Government' from using government information that has not been made public, 'in his personal capacity and for personal gain', to trade futures, options on futures, off-exchange options or swaps, or from imparting it for personal gain to help another trade; makes a person who 'knowingly' uses such imparted information liable; and makes it unlawful for 'any person' to steal, convert or misappropriate such information and trade on it (section 4c(a)(4)(C), the provision the CFTC calls the 'Eddie Murphy Rule').
- The statute text was re-read in the audit.
- The CFTC treats event contracts as swaps; Van Dyke's pending motion to dismiss disputes that, and no court has ruled.
- CFTC Rule 180.1 is modelled on 10b-5.
- Gaps and limits: (a) a public post is not a tip, and several professors (Coffee, Pritchard, Seyhun; PolitiFact, excerpt) say the 9 April post itself is unlikely to violate insider-trading law, while aides who traded would face STOCK Act exposure; (b) whether the President is an 'employee or agent of any department or agency' under the CEA provision is untested.
- Our own reading, not a source's: section 4c(a)(3) borrows the STOCK Act's definitions only for Members and employees of Congress and judicial officers and employees, so the STOCK Act's express inclusion of the President does not carry over to the 'department or agency' phrase; the 'any person' theft provision in 4c(a)(4)(C) has no such limit; (c) the OGE rule on nonpublic information (5 CFR 2635.703) and 18 U.S.C.
- 208 do not apply to the President or Vice President (5 CFR 2635.102(h); 18 U.S.C. 202(c)); (d) after Kelly, the Second Circuit in Blaszczak (2022) held confidential agency regulatory information is not 'property' for Title 18 wire and securities fraud, narrowing criminal routes outside the Exchange Act and CEA; (e) DOJ policy (OLC, 2000) bars indicting a sitting president, and Trump v. United States (2024) gives immunity for official acts but not unofficial ones.
- John Coates, a former SEC general counsel under Biden, argues the STOCK Act makes the President a fiduciary and that selling early access to official announcements could breach it, with a five-year limitations period; TMTG rejects that theory.
- No court has applied any of these provisions to a president.
- Dates: 9 April
- Sources: [38](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-112publ105/html/PLAW-112publ105.htm) [39](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/7/6c) [44](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/463/646) [45](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/15-628) [40](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/5/2635.102) [41](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/5/2635.703) [42](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/202) [46](https://www.mofo.com/pdf/resources/insights/230123-second-circuit-nixes-theory) [43](https://www.justice.gov/olc/opinion/sitting-president%E2%80%99s-amenability-indictment-and-criminal-prosecution) [47](https://www.execfunctions.org/p/is-trump-medias-early-access-feed) [48](https://www.politifact.com/article/2025/apr/10/critics-donald-trump-of-insider-trading-tariffs/) [22](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/9217-26)

What has been done about the insider-trading allegations of this term (from 20 January 2025)? This covers requests from members of Congress and state officials; any SEC, CFTC, Justice Department, Office of Government Ethics (OGE), inspector-general or state investigation and its outcome; inquiries by exchanges and prediction-market platforms; changes in enforcement staffing and policy; and what law applies to a president, officials, family and friends. The verdict rates how well this record is documented. It does not rate whether anyone traded illegally.

### Summary

- **The requests are well documented.** Members of Congress sent at least ten letters between April 2025 and July 2026. They went to the SEC, its inspector general, GAO, the Pentagon inspector general, DOJ, state attorneys general and the House Financial Services chairman. Most of the letters were read in full.
- **Public responses are mostly silence.**
  - No investigation of the 9 April 2025 trading has been announced by the SEC, an inspector general, a state or the House Financial Services Committee.
  - In February 2026 House Democrats wrote that they had "received no substantive response" in nearly a year, and that GAO says it cannot compel information.
  - The SEC declined to comment on the 2026 letters.
- **Enforcement that did happen is in prediction markets.** It was brought by the CFTC and DOJ, and in each case the platform first detected the trades:
  - a soldier was charged over bets on the Maduro operation and has pleaded not guilty;
  - settled CFTC orders were made against a White House teleprompter operator and against George Santos.
- **The CFTC oil-futures probe is open.** It covers trades placed before the Iran posts. Its enforcement director confirmed it is open after Bloomberg reported, citing anonymous sources, that a referral had been retracted. No charges have been filed.
- **No finding about Trump, his family or senior officials.** No authority has identified a trader in the April 2025 or Iran-related trades or made such a finding. That is not exoneration, and the letters are allegations and questions, not findings.
- **Enforcement capacity shrank.** These figures are documented; no motive is asserted here.
  - SEC Enforcement lost 18% of its staff in FY2025, according to GAO.
  - CFTC staff ended 2025 about 21% below the ten-year average, with nearly 80% fewer enforcement actions (NPR, citing OPM data and agency reports).
  - DOJ disbanded its crypto enforcement team. NBC reported, citing anonymous sources, that the Public Integrity Section would be cut to about six people.

This builds on C58 (the market data and the first letters), C52 (the SEC Enforcement Director's exit) and C38 (the Polymarket probes closed in July 2025). The market data itself is covered in C101 and documented trades in C102.

### Sub-claims

#### 1. Congressional requests to the SEC, SEC IG and GAO over 9 April 2025: confirmed; no substantive response
- The letters of 10 and 11 April 2025 were read in full. The House letter asked for preliminary findings by 18 April. The Senate letter asked whether the White House had contacted the SEC, and what staff departures meant for its capacity.
- On 24 February 2026 Waters and Green wrote: "It has been nearly a year and we have received no substantive response to this request."
- They added that GAO "does not have the authority to compel necessary information."
- The SEC has announced no investigation, closure or finding.

#### 2. Request to state attorneys general: request confirmed, outcome unverified
- The 11 April 2025 letter to the National Association of Attorneys General asked states to investigate "whether President Trump, his family, members of his administration, or members of Congress engaged in insider trading, market manipulation, or any violations of the laws of your states."
- No state attorney general, comptroller or securities regulator was found to have opened such an inquiry. That includes New York's Martin Act, which Coates names as a possible route for the Truth API.
- State activity in this period concerns prediction markets as gambling. Search excerpts also report executive orders in New York and Maryland on state employees' use of nonpublic information.

- The Pentagon denies that Hegseth or any representative approached BlackRock, and the trade was never executed.

#### 9. Executive-branch and OGE response: partly-accurate

- No OGE guidance on prediction markets was found, and OGE has not responded to CREW in public.

#### 10. Enforcement staffing and policy: confirmed (documented; no motive asserted)
- **SEC staffing (GAO-26-107813).** Enforcement had 1,305 staff at the end of FY2024 and lost 235 (18%). Trading and Markets lost 22%. Agency-wide losses were 871 people, about 18%. The SEC had about 4,000 employees in January 2026, which GAO says is lower than its average full-time-equivalent workforce in any year since 2014. (Audit: Table 2 re-read; Enforcement 109 + 54 + 72 = 235 of 1,305.)
- **SEC policy.** The Commission rescinded the Enforcement Director's delegated authority to issue formal orders (March 2025, White & Case excerpt). It also sought comment on "foundational and existential aspects" of the Consolidated Audit Trail, according to Better Markets, an advocacy group.
- **CFTC.** Staff ended 2025 about 21% below the ten-year average, and enforcement actions fell nearly 80% (NPR). The CFTC says the departures were voluntary and that it is hiring. Selig is the sole commissioner.
- **DOJ.** The National Cryptocurrency Enforcement Team was disbanded. NBC reported (anonymous sources) cuts to the Public Integrity Section.

#### 11. The law: partly-accurate (reaches officials and tippees on paper; untested against a president; gaps)
- **STOCK Act (read in full).**
  - Section 2(3) defines "executive branch employee" to include the President and Vice President.
  - Section 9(b) affirms that such employees are "not exempt" from section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5.
  - It adds Exchange Act section 21A(h): a duty of trust and confidence to the government and citizens for material nonpublic information "derived from such person's position."
- **Tipper and tippee.** Under *Dirks v. SEC* (1983) and *Salman v. United States* (2016), an insider who tips for a personal benefit is liable, and a gift of information to a relative or friend counts as a benefit. A tippee who knows of the breach is liable when trading. On our reading (no court has applied this to these facts), family and friends who trade on a tip from an official are covered as tippees, not as officials.
- **Commodity Exchange Act section 4c(a)(3)-(4).**
  - Statute text re-read in the audit. Section 4c(a)(3) bars "any employee or agent of any department or agency of the Federal Government" from using government information that has not been made public, "in his personal capacity and for personal gain", to trade futures, options on futures, off-exchange options or swaps. Section 4c(a)(4)(A) bars imparting it for personal gain to help another trade.
  - Section 4c(a)(4)(B) makes a recipient who "knowingly" uses imparted information liable. Section 4c(a)(4)(C) makes it unlawful for "any person" to steal, convert or misappropriate such information and trade on it (the CFTC calls this the "Eddie Murphy Rule").
  - CFTC Rule 180.1 mirrors Rule 10b-5.
  - The CFTC treats event contracts as swaps. Van Dyke's pending motion disputes that, and no court has ruled. The Van Dyke and Perez cases are the first uses of these provisions on event contracts.
- **Gaps and limits.**
  1. A public post is not a tip. Professors Coffee, Pritchard and Seyhun (PolitiFact, excerpt) see no violation in the 9 April post itself; aides who traded would be exposed.
  2. Whether the President is an "employee or agent of any department or agency" under the CEA provision is untested. **Our own reading, not a source's:** section 4c(a)(3) borrows the STOCK Act's definitions only for Members and employees of Congress and judicial officers and employees, so the STOCK Act's express inclusion of the President does not carry over to the "department or agency" phrase. The "any person" theft provision has no such limit.
  3. The OGE rule on nonpublic information (5 CFR 2635.703) excludes the President and Vice President (5 CFR 2635.102(h)), as does 18 U.S.C. 208 (via 18 U.S.C. 202(c)).
  4. *Blaszczak* (2d Cir. 2022) held that confidential agency information is not "property" for Title 18 wire and securities fraud. That narrows criminal theories outside the Exchange Act and the CEA.
  5. A 2000 opinion of DOJ's Office of Legal Counsel bars indicting a sitting president. *Trump v. United States* (2024) gives immunity for official acts but not unofficial ones.
  6. Enforcement depends on agencies led by the President's appointees. Coates notes a five-year limitations period for SEC civil actions and criminal charges.
- **The competing view.** John Coates (Harvard, former SEC general counsel under Biden, who discloses that he is not neutral) argues that the STOCK Act makes the President a fiduciary and that selling early access to official announcements could breach that duty. Renée Jones (Boston College, a former senior SEC official) agrees that the Truth API "appears" to run afoul of the law but expects the defence that the posts are public. TMTG rejects the theory.
- No court has applied these provisions to a president.

#### 12. Any finding against Trump, his family or senior officials: unverified (none exists)
- No trader has been identified in the April 2025 options trading, the Iran oil trades or the Iran-strike prediction-market bets.
- No authority has made a finding about Trump, his family, Cabinet members or senior appointees.
- The absence of a finding is not exoneration, and the allegations are not findings.

### Corrections
- **Claimed:** the House letters of April 2025 and February 2026 quote Trump as saying "$2.5 million." **The record shows:** he said "two-and-a-half billion" (Schwab) and "$900 million" (Penske). Both are his own claims about paper gains (C58).
- **Claimed:** the House Oversight letter (29 September 2026) and memo (13 November 2026) say a Hyperliquid short was established "within minutes" of a tariff announcement. **The record shows:** the article both cite (Bank Syz, republished by Investing.com) describes a build-up from 16:39 UTC on 9 October, about 28-30 hours before the 20:50 UTC post, to about 20:49 UTC on 10 October, and its summary says "opened 30 hours before". "Within minutes" fits the last additions, not the opening. An earlier version of this check called the memo's wording wrong; it is incomplete rather than wrong. No trader has been identified (see C101, which now says the same).
- **Audit, 16 Nov 2026:** the Santos order is for manipulation, not insider trading. The Van Dyke indictment has five counts. The CFTC complaint and DOJ give slightly different profit figures (more than $404,000 versus about $409,881).
- **Claimed:** the teleprompter operator made "close to $100,000." **The record shows:** the CFTC order puts the profit at $107,539, plus a $65,000 penalty.
- **Claimed:** the April 2025 memo disbanding the National Cryptocurrency Enforcement Team was Bondi's. **The record shows:** Deputy AG Todd Blanche wrote it.

### Denials and context
- The White House says the 9 April post reassured markets and calls the insinuations that officials traded "baseless and irresponsible."
- The Pentagon denies the Hegseth approach.
- TMTG denies that the Truth API involves insider trading.
- The SEC says it refocused enforcement; Atkins disputed the decline before the SEC's own figures confirmed it.
- The CFTC says the oil probe is open and that staff departures were voluntary.
- Kalshi and Polymarket say they police insider trading.
- Experts disagree on the 9 April post. Some see no violation; others say an investigation is warranted.
- **Source limits.** The following could not be read directly, so the points that rely on them rest on excerpts or secondary coverage: the Warner-Schiff PDF, CRS report LSB11406 (cited in the Oversight memo), the CLS Blue Sky "Shadow SEC Statement No. 13", PolitiFact, White & Case, Axios and Bloomberg.

### What remains unanswered
1. Did the SEC open any matter under inquiry or investigation into the 9 April 2025 options trading or the "DJT" post? Did it ever reply to the April 2025, April 2026 and July 2026 letters?
2. Did the SEC inspector general or GAO take any step on the April 2025 request beyond GAO's statement that it cannot compel information?
3. Who placed the 9 April 2025 SPY and QQQ calls, the oil-futures positions of 23 March and 7 April 2026, and the Iran-strike Polymarket bets? Has the CFTC obtained Tag 50 identities, and will it charge anyone?
4. Who decided to retract the CFTC surveillance referral, and why? Has the CFTC inspector general looked at it?
5. Has the DoD inspector general answered Warner and Schiff, and has the SEC examined the Hegseth broker report?
6. Has any state attorney general or the New York AG (under the Martin Act) opened an inquiry, including into Truth API subscribers?
7. Will OGE issue guidance, or the White House a rule, on officials and event contracts? What did the November 2026 Oversight memo leave out about officials' securities and futures trades?
8. Does CEA section 4c(a)(3) reach the President? Would the STOCK Act duty be enforced against a president or his tippees after he leaves office, and how would immunity and pardons interact with civil liability?
9. Outcome of the Van Dyke trial, and whether wire-fraud counts survive *Blaszczak*-type challenges.
10. Findings of GAO's review of CFTC staffing (starting December 2026).
