# C15: Jared Kushner and Affinity Partners

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Origin:** Claim from the video · transcript lines 535-553

## Bottom line

The core facts hold. Trump publicly named Kushner an 'envoy of peace' (19 Feb 2026), and he negotiates alongside Witkoff with no formal title and no financial disclosure.

## What holds up

- Trump has called Kushner the 'peace envoy'.
- Kushner takes part in negotiations alongside Steve Witkoff in an unofficial capacity.
- Affinity is a multibillion-dollar fund.
- Per the NYT, Affinity has been trying to raise $5B, largely from Middle Eastern/Gulf sovereign funds.
- Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund put $2B into Affinity and is one of its biggest investors.

## What needs correcting

- Because he holds no official title, Kushner is not subject to divestment requirements and does not have to file financial disclosures (partly accurate)
- **Video said:** PIF is 'one of his biggest investors'  
  **Record shows:** The record shows it is Affinity's largest single investor ($2B, 2021). QIA and Lunate are also investors (about $1.5B, Dec 2024).
- **Video said:** He is 'not subject to divestment requirements' and need not file disclosures  
  **Record shows:** That is the White House's position, which CREW contests in litigation filed Oct 2026, and no court has ruled.
- The $5B was a fundraising effort reported by the NYT (March 2026), not a completed raise; headlines saying 'raised $5B' overstate it.

## What is unproven

- In closed-door meetings it is unknown whether he argues for the US government or for his own Gulf-backed financial interests.

## In more detail

- Saudi PIF committed $2B to Affinity in 2021, and the NYT reported in March 2026 that he was seeking $5B or more, largely from Gulf sovereign funds.
- Two points overreach: PIF is Affinity's largest investor, not just 'one of' them, and 'not subject to' disclosure or divestment is the White House's contested legal position, which CREW is challenging in court.
- The suggestion that he argues for his own interests in talks is unproven insinuation.

## What the video said

> Jared Kushner acts as an unofficial 'peace envoy' alongside Witkoff with no official title, so no divestment or financial disclosure; his fund Affinity Partners got $2B from Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund and, per the New York Times, has been trying to raise $5B more largely from Gulf sovereign funds.

## Full report

**Verdict: mostly-accurate** (confidence: medium-high)

### What the video says (transcript lines 535-553)

- Jared Kushner, the president's son-in-law, "has been called the peace envoy by Trump."
- He takes part in negotiations alongside Steve Witkoff "in an unofficial capacity."
- He runs a "multibillion dollar fund called Affinity," which according to the New York Times "has been trying to raise five billion dollars ... from a lot of these Middle Eastern countries."
- Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) "put in two billion dollars" and is "one of his biggest investors"; other Gulf sovereign funds "are expected to be asked as well."
- Because he "does not hold any official title, he is not subject to divestment requirements" and "does not have to file financial disclosures."
- Insinuation: in closed-door meetings "we don't necessarily know if he is arguing on behalf of the U.S. government or on behalf of his own financial interests."

### Evidence, sub-claim by sub-claim

1. **Trump called him "peace envoy." Confirmed.** At the first Board of Peace meeting on 19 Feb 2026, Trump said: "We're making Jared an envoy also, envoy of peace" (Just the News, AOL/Hill coverage). The White House then told NewsNation the role would not be official. Kushner also sits on the executive board of the Gaza "Board of Peace," announced in January 2026 (Al-Monitor).

2. **He works alongside Witkoff in an unofficial capacity. Confirmed.** The AP, NBC and Bloomberg Law all report that he has no formal title and no Senate confirmation. He has worked with Witkoff on Gaza and Iran talks (including Geneva) and on Russia-Ukraine. The White House says he is "a volunteer and not serving in any official capacity," pays his own expenses and draws no salary. Witkoff, by contrast, is a formal employee and filed a public financial disclosure.

3. **Affinity is a "multibillion dollar fund." Confirmed.** Its Form ADV dated 22 Mar 2026 reports about $6.16B in regulatory assets at end-2025 (Bloomberg rounds this to $6.2B). About 99% comes from non-US clients, mainly Saudi, Emirati and Qatari.

4. **The NYT reported he is trying to raise $5B, largely from Gulf sovereign funds. Confirmed, with a nuance.** The NYT reported in March 2026, citing five people familiar with the talks, that Kushner was seeking "$5 billion or more" for Affinity. Representatives had already met the PIF, and Gulf funds were expected to be approached. Axios (16 Mar 2026) said the fund was not formally in market yet, but talks with the PIF had started because of the PIF's contractual "first look" rights. The House Oversight Democrats' letter of 5 Oct 2026 says public reporting in March 2026 showed he was seeking at least $5B. Some headlines (Dealroom, a later Mediaite piece quoting a September 2026 New Yorker report) say the money was "raised." That has not been confirmed. The video's wording, "trying to raise," is the accurate one.

5. **Saudi PIF put in $2B. Confirmed.** The PIF committed $2B in mid-2021, a few months after Kushner left the White House. The NYT reported in 2022 that the PIF's screening panel objected on 30 Jun 2021, citing "public relations risks" and the "inexperience" of Affinity's management, and that the board chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman overruled it. Correction: the video calls the PIF "one of his biggest investors"; it is the **largest** (per Wyden's Senate Finance letter). Other Gulf money is already in: Qatar Investment Authority and Abu Dhabi's Lunate added about $1.5B in Dec 2024 (Bloomberg).

6. **No official title, so no divestment or financial disclosure. Partly accurate.** It is true that Kushner has filed no financial disclosure and has not divested. "Not subject to" those rules is the White House's legal position, not settled law. CREW argues that the "special envoy for peace" designation and his continuing duties make him at least a special government employee, which would require a public disclosure within 30 days. In October 2026 CREW sued to bar him from the role without Senate confirmation. The suit names Witkoff, Wiles, Rubio and Hegseth in their official capacities. Kushner's lawyer, Chad Mizelle, calls the suit "without merit." He says outside and government ethics attorneys reviewed Kushner's "volunteer government service" and found it compliant. No ruling was found.

7. **Conflict-of-interest insinuation. Unverified (opinion/insinuation).** The documented fact is that Kushner sought Gulf sovereign money while negotiating for the US in that region. Senator Wyden and Rep. Garcia opened a formal inquiry into this on 19 Mar 2026. No public evidence shows he argued for his own financial interests in any specific meeting. The video frames this as an open question, and that framing is fair. It is not a proven fact.

### Corrections

- PIF is Affinity's largest investor, not merely "one of" the biggest. Qatar's QIA and Abu Dhabi's Lunate are already investors too (about $1.5B, Dec 2024).
- "Not subject to divestment or disclosure" is the White House position. CREW disputes it in litigation filed in October 2026, and no court has ruled.
- The $5B was a solicitation reported by the NYT, not a completed raise. Headlines saying "raised $5B" overstate the record.

### Denials and context

- White House: Kushner is a volunteer with no official capacity, pays his own expenses and takes no salary.
- Mizelle (for Kushner/Affinity): his arrangements comply with ethics requirements; the CREW suit is without merit. He has called earlier Democratic referrals political.
- In Dec 2024 Kushner said: "We preemptively tried to avoid any conflicts, so we don't have to raise capital for the next four years." The 2026 fundraising effort cuts against that pledge.
- The Daily Beast (citing Bloomberg, around March 2026) reported that Affinity's chief legal officer later said the firm had dropped the planned new fundraising vehicle and would not take new capital while Kushner volunteers for the government. That statement is undated in the available coverage and was not independently confirmed. If accurate, it postdates the NYT report and partly defuses the "$5B" point.
- Earlier NYT reporting, relayed by The Media Line, said Qatar and the main Emirati funds had declined to invest in a round. This conflicts with the 2024 QIA/Lunate investment and may refer to a different round.
