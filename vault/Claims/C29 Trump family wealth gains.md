### Method note

Direct fetches of every primary outlet I tried failed in this environment: Bloomberg Law, Forbes (.com and .com.au), The Block, The Daily Beast and GV Wire. Everything below comes from search-result snippets that agree across several outlets: Reuters syndications, AP syndications, NYT-sourced reports, Forbes headlines and URLs, CoinDesk, Fortune and Decrypt. I did not read the OGE filing (Form 278e, 927 pages) itself.

### Sub-claims

#### 1. Trump's disclosure shows about $1.4B from crypto out of about $2.2B, and crypto was his biggest business. **Confirmed, with a caveat**
- Trump's annual OGE disclosure covering calendar 2025 was released on **June 30, 2026**. It is about 927 pages.
- **Reuters** and the NYT both report **more than $1.4B** in crypto income. Reuters breaks it down as:
  - nearly **$800M from World Liberty Financial**: more than $520M from token sales and more than $250M from selling interests in the WLF business;
  - about **$635M from TRUMP meme-coin licensing/royalties**, through CIC Digital and Celebration Coin.
- The **AP** counted it differently and got "**nearly $1.2B**" from crypto businesses.
- The **NYT** put total 2025 income at **at least $2.2B**, more than triple the minimum of $622M reported for 2024. **Forbes** estimated about $2.4B.
- Crypto was the largest category. Real estate, golf and resorts brought in about $500-575M.
- Caveat: these numbers are revenue/income as the form reports it. The form does not show profit.

#### 2. "This may have been one of his most profitable years ever." **Plausible but unverifiable as worded**
- Forbes said the year was about three times the roughly $760M Trump took in for 2024, and the Daily Beast called it his "most lucrative year." Several outlets noted the haul tops his entire first term's income.
- However, the AP and NYT both point out that the disclosure gives **revenue, not profit**. "Most profitable" therefore cannot be shown from the filing. The video's hedge ("may have been") is fair.

#### 3. Per Forbes, Eric Trump is about 10x richer since the election. **Confirmed (as of Dec 2025)**
- Forbes (Kyle Mullins, **Dec 4, 2025**) ran the headline "Eric Trump has gotten 10 times richer since dad's election." It put him at about **$400M**.
- His largest holding was his American Bitcoin stake, about $160M in early Dec 2025. The rest is mainly World Liberty tokens and USD1-related value.
- Eric disputed parts of Forbes' valuation but offered no alternative figure.
- The estimate is now dated. ABTC later fell 87-95% from its debut peak, so his current figure is probably lower.

#### 4. Per Forbes, Don Jr. is about 6x richer. **Confirmed (as of Dec 2025)**
- Forbes (Dec 8, 2025) ran the headline "How Donald Trump Jr.'s fortune jumped six-fold in a year." It put him at about **$300M**, six times his November 2024 level.
- Forbes noted he had been near $500M in September 2025, before American Bitcoin's stock fell.

#### 5. Per Forbes, Barron Trump is worth about $150M. **Confirmed (Forbes estimate, Sept/Oct 2025)**
- Forbes' family tally (Sept 22, 2025) and the article "How 19-Year-Old Barron Trump Is Worth $150 Million" (Oct 6, 2025) both give this figure.
- It assumes Barron holds about 10% of the roughly $1.5B that WLF added to the family fortune. Forbes heavily discounted his locked tokens.
- It is an estimate, not a disclosed figure.

#### 6. Most of the actual investors in these ventures lost money. **Mostly accurate for the main ventures; "American" is not established**
- **TRUMP coin:**
  - Chainalysis/NYT counted 813,294 wallets that lost about $2B in the first 19 days. A later Chainalysis/CNBC count found about 764K losing wallets, while 58 wallets made more than $10M each.
  - A Nansen analysis cited by the NYT found 988,905 wallets in loss, about $3.8B combined, by June 2026. The token was down about 97% from its peak.
- **WLFI:** the token hit record lows of about $0.055-0.077 in April-May 2026, more than 80% below its September 2025 peak of about $0.46.
- **American Bitcoin (ABTC):** down 75% by January 2026 and 87-95% from its Sept 2025 debut later in the year. A Forbes-cited estimate puts retail losses at about $500M.
- Wallet data does not reveal nationality, so the claim that these were "American" investors is not supported. Some ventures, such as USD1 and the family's licensing income, did not involve retail buyers losing money.

### Corrections
- The $1.4B crypto figure is the NYT/Reuters count. The AP counted about $1.2B. Both are revenue, not profit.
- The $2.2B is a minimum figure from the NYT tally. Forbes estimated about $2.4B.
- The Forbes figures for the sons date from Dec 2025 (Eric and Don Jr.) and for Barron from Sept/Oct 2025. They predate ABTC's deeper slide, so current values are probably lower.
- The investor-loss data covers wallets worldwide, not specifically "American" investors.

### Denials / other side
- White House spokesperson Anna Kelly said neither the president nor his family "has ever engaged, or will ever engage, in conflicts of interest."
- The White House also said Trump's assets are "in a trust managed by his children," or held in "fully discretionary accounts managed by independent third-party financial institutions."
- The Trump Organization called the filing "one of the most comprehensive financial disclosure reports ever submitted." World Liberty Financial declined to comment.
- Critics note that Trump remains the beneficiary of the trust. Former acting OGE head Don Fox said every post-Watergate president managed his finances "as though he were subject to conflicts of interest." Trump has not done so.
- Eric Trump disputed Forbes' valuation.
