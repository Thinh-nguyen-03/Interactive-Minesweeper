## Summary

The core claims hold up. New America Acquisition I Corp filed with the SEC on Aug. 4, 2025 for a $300M NYSE IPO, with Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump on its advisory board. The two hold 5 million founder shares, worth about $50M on paper at $10 a share. The original S-1 said the SPAC would target firms 'well-positioned to benefit from federal or state-level incentives, such as grants, tax credits, government contracts or preferential procurement programs', and an amendment filed later the same day removed that language after AP asked about it. The caveats: the IPO priced in December 2025 and closed at $345M with the over-allotment, the video's quote drops 'grants, tax credits', the 'identifiable economic or security interest' phrase could not be checked against the filing, and the idea that the sons would phone the Pentagon is the video's inference, not a documented act.

## Corrections

- The transcript says the SPAC 'has been raising three hundred million dollars'. It filed for $300M in August 2025, priced at $300M on Dec. 3, 2025, and closed at $345M after the over-allotment was exercised.
- The video's quote of the filing omits 'grants, tax credits'. The original reads 'federal or state-level incentives, such as grants, tax credits, government contracts or preferential procurement programs.'
- The sons' 'tens of millions' is about $50M in paper value from 5 million founder shares (3M Eric, 2M Don Jr.) at $10. It is not realized cash, and it depends on a completed merger.
- The auto-transcript's 'New American acquisition, one corp' is New America Acquisition I Corp (NYSE: NWAX).

## Denials and context

- The filing's law firm, Paul Hastings, called the incentives sentence a scrivener's 'mistake'. A company spokesperson called it a clerical error.
- The Trump Organization did not answer AP's question about whether New America still planned to benefit from government programs.
- The amended filing still talks about 'public policy tailwinds' and revitalizing domestic manufacturing and critical supply chains, so the government-policy angle was not dropped entirely.
- The Trump brothers are also large shareholders in Dominari Holdings, whose broker-dealer co-underwrote the IPO and whose president, Kyle Wool, sits on the advisory board. The same Dominari partnership is behind American Bitcoin.
- Founder shares bought for a nominal price and worth millions are standard in SPACs. What is unusual is the sitting president's sons as advisers combined with the government-incentives targeting language.
