# C74: Pentagon investment offices: Cerberus alumni, no-bid contracts and the Trump sons' SPAC underwriter

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Origin:** Connection hunt

## Bottom line

Every role, contract and loan in the brief is documented. Lorch (ex-Cerberus MD) runs OSC, and Kollitides (ex-Cerberus, ex-A&M Capital, a special government employee) runs Feinberg's Economic Defense Unit, which A&M Federal advises under no-bid deals worth up to about $350M. Kollitides sat on D. Boral Acquisition I's board from Feb 10 to Sep 23, 2026, alongside Kevin McGurn, CEO of the Trump sons' D. Boral-underwritten SPAC and interim CEO of Trump Media.

## What holds up

- Deputy Secretary Feinberg is linked to OSC Director David Lorch through Cerberus.
- Feinberg is linked to George Kollitides through Cerberus; Kollitides heads the Economic Defense Unit.
- Alvarez & Marsal received no-bid contracts worth up to about $350M to advise Kollitides's office.
- Kollitides took a board seat and founder shares at D. Boral Acquisition I while in his Pentagon post.
- On that board Kollitides sat with Kevin McGurn, CEO of the Trump sons' SPAC and interim CEO of Trump Media.
- D. Boral Capital, the SPAC's affiliate, co-underwrote the Trump sons' New America SPAC.
- Cerberus-linked firms won Golden Dome work and other large DoD contracts (mostly accurate)
- Feinberg's personal lawyer and Cerberus trust manager represented DoD in the MP Materials deal (mostly accurate)
- OSC lent to Vulcan (1789), PDW and Neros, and held talks with Unusual Machines (Don Jr.) (mostly accurate)

## What needs correcting

- Feinberg's Cerberus trust is exposed to a possible Ligado settlement with DoD (partly accurate)
- Recusal arrangements exist for Feinberg and the Cerberus alumni (partly accurate)
- C51 gave no figure for PDW's OSC deal: it is a conditional loan commitment of up to $820M (Jul 31, 2026).
- C65 says Kollitides 'later resigned' from D. Boral Acquisition I: the 8-K dates it Sep 23, 2026, citing 'personal reasons'.
- **Claim:** The brief puts Kollitides in OSC-related offices: he heads the separate Economic Defense Unit, which reports to Feinberg  
  **Record:** Lorch is the OSC director.

## What is unproven

- Cerberus alumni steered awards or loans toward Trump-family-linked companies.

## In more detail

- OSC lent to Vulcan, PDW and Neros, but not, so far, to Unusual Machines.
- The Pentagon says Feinberg is recused from Cerberus matters, a single-source report says Lutnick screens Cerberus deals, and no recusal or waiver for Kollitides or Lorch has been published.
- No reporting shows any Cerberus alum steering a decision toward a Trump-family interest.

## Sub-claims

### 1. Deputy Secretary Feinberg is linked to OSC Director David Lorch through Cerberus.

**Confirmed** · Evidence: Documented

- The official DoW bio says Lorch has directed OSC since Nov 2025 and is also a senior adviser to Feinberg.
- Lorch was a managing director on Cerberus's Supply Chain and Strategic Opportunities team.
- Feinberg co-founded Cerberus.
- No reporting ties Lorch personally to any specific loan decision.
- Dates: Nov 2025
- Sources: [13](https://www.war.gov/About/Biographies/Biography/Article/4506870/david-lorch/) <https://www.semafor.com/article/09/02/2026/trumps-venezuela-deal-draws-scrutiny-of-obscure-pentagon-office>

### 2. Feinberg is linked to George Kollitides through Cerberus; Kollitides heads the Economic Defense Unit.

**Confirmed**

- Evidence grade: documented for the role and Cerberus history; single-source (Bloomberg Government) for his status as a special government employee.
- Feinberg appointed Kollitides, a former Cerberus MD who headed its aerospace, government and defense group, to lead the EDU ('Deal Team Six') in Feb 2026 (appointed Feb 4 per C65).
- The EDU director reports to Feinberg.
- The unit got more than $266M in FY2026 RDT&E funding.
- Semafor's recruiting document floated $200B of deals over three years, an aspirational, unaudited figure.
- The brief's wording ('related offices') is right: Kollitides heads the EDU, not OSC.
- Amounts: more than $266M; $200B
- Dates: Feb 2026; Feb 4
- Sources: [10](https://axios.com/2026/05/06/economic-defense-unit-kollitides-pentagon) [11](https://www.militarytimes.com/news/pentagon-congress/2026/05/08/hegseth-aims-to-cut-through-the-bureaucracy-with-deal-team-six/) [12](https://www.semafor.com/article/03/11/2026/pentagon-headhunting-goldman-jpmorgan-bankers-for-economic-defense-unit) [1](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/pentagon-investment-arm-director-joined-firm-with-defense-ties)

### 3. Alvarez & Marsal received no-bid contracts worth up to about $350M to advise Kollitides's office.

**Confirmed**

- Evidence grade: documented (ProPublica, Oct 2026, via Defense One/GovExec syndication; contract records).
- DoD awarded A&M a roughly $67M deal shortly after Kollitides joined the Pentagon.
- Defense One dates the run of awards to 'since December' 2025, so he appears to have started there before his Feb 2026 EDU appointment; that start date is not otherwise documented, and no contract record found here confirms the $67M award.
- About two months later it finalised a $281M no-bid contract with Alvarez & Marsal Federal to advise his office, which Defense One calls the Business Operators for National Defense (BOND), for a combined ceiling of about $350M with about $31M paid.
- Kollitides was a partner at the legally distinct A&M Capital, which says he stopped working as a senior adviser there days before ProPublica's questions arrived.
- ProPublica did not establish that he chose the firm.
- The Pentagon says he followed all rules.
- Experts quoted by GovExec said taking part in a contract affecting one's own finances requires a waiver, and no waiver has been reported.
- A&M Federal has not published a full list of services under the $281M contract.
- Amounts: roughly $67M; $67M; $281M; about $350M; about $31M
- Dates: Oct 2026; Feb 2026
- Sources: [8](https://defenseone.com/policy/2026/10/pentagon-350-million-contracts-official/416543) [9](https://www.govexec.com/defense/2026/10/pentagon-gave-350-million-contracts-firm-closely-tied-senior-defense-department-official/416537/)

### 4. Kollitides took a board seat and founder shares at D. Boral Acquisition I while in his Pentagon post.

**Confirmed** · Evidence: Documented (Bloomberg Government; SEC 8-Ks seen via search snippets)

- He joined the board of the Cayman SPAC on Feb 10, 2026, the week of its IPO, which priced Feb 10-11 and closed at $287.5M with D. Boral Capital as sole book-runner.
- SEC filings show an indirect interest in 100,000 founder shares, which Bloomberg valued at up to about $1M and which become sellable only on a completed merger.
- An 8-K reports he resigned 'for personal reasons' effective Sep 23, 2026.
- The Pentagon declined comment to Bloomberg.
- Amounts: $287.5M; about $1M
- Dates: Feb 10, 2026; Feb 10-11; Sep 23, 2026
- Sources: [1](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/pentagon-investment-arm-director-joined-firm-with-defense-ties) [2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002095161/000121390026104218/ea0306801-8k_dboralacq1.htm) [4](https://finviz.com/news/309854/d-boral-acquisition-i-corp-announces-closing-of-287500000-initial-public-offering-including-full-exercise-of-underwriters-over-allotment-option)

### 5. On that board Kollitides sat with Kevin McGurn, CEO of the Trump sons' SPAC and interim CEO of Trump Media.

**Confirmed**

- Evidence grade: documented (Bloomberg Government; Nov 6, 2026 8-K naming McGurn and Luisa Ingargiola to the audit and compensation committees).
- McGurn is chairman, CEO and CFO of New America Acquisition I (C27) and became TMTG interim CEO on Apr 21, 2026.
- Kollitides and McGurn were co-directors from about Feb 10 to Sep 23, 2026.
- Bloomberg says McGurn and Ingargiola sit on boards of several defense-related companies with Trump sons as investors or advisers; we did not verify which boards.
- This is a shared board seat, not evidence of any transaction between them.
- Dates: Nov 6, 2026; Apr 21, 2026; Feb 10; Sep 23, 2026
- Sources: [1](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/pentagon-investment-arm-director-joined-firm-with-defense-ties) [3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002095161/000121390026116912/ea0309298-8k_dboralacq1.htm) [6](https://www.thewrap.com/media-platforms/journalism/trump-media-company-kevin-mcgurn-interim-ceo/) [7](https://be.marketscreener.com/insider/LUISA-INGARGIOLA-A0KWO9/)

- D. Boral Capital also received the House CCP committee's March 2026 letter on Chinese small-cap IPOs (C47).

- D. Boral did not respond to Bloomberg.
- Amounts: $345M
- Dates: Dec 2025; March 2026

### 7. Cerberus-linked firms won Golden Dome work and other large DoD contracts.

**Mostly accurate** · Evidence: Documented that the awards exist; their values are undisclosed

- Per Warren's Apr 27 and Jul 27, 2026 letters, the Pentagon acknowledged that at least four Cerberus-owned or partly owned companies hold Golden Dome contracts, and the letters name North Wind, Stratolaunch and Red River Technology.
- Eos Energy is also reported.
- Context: MDA's SHIELD vehicle ($151B ceiling) gave slots to about 2,100 firms, so a slot is not funding.
- Separately, M1 Support Services ('a Cerberus Capital Management company') won the Army's Flight School Next IDIQ (up to $10B, 26 years) on Aug 13, 2026, against 10 other bidders.
- DoD says Feinberg has no direct responsibility for Golden Dome acquisitions, although program head Gen. Guetlein reports to him.
- Amounts: $151B; up to $10B
- Dates: Apr 27; Jul 27, 2026; Aug 13, 2026
- Sources: [14](https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-questions-deputy-secretary-of-defense-on-conflicts-of-interest-involving-dods-golden-dome-missile-defense-program) [15](https://www.warren.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Follow-Up-Letter-from-Senator-Warren-to-Stephen-Feinberg-on-Golden-Dome.pdf) [16](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2025/12/mda-awards-1014-companies-slots-151b-golden-dome-contract-vehicle/409903/) [20](https://www.washingtontechnology.com/contracts/2026/08/army-picks-m1-lead-10b-helicopter-school-transformation/415463/)

### 8. Feinberg's personal lawyer and Cerberus trust manager represented DoD in the MP Materials deal.

**Mostly accurate**

- Evidence grade: single-source (American Prospect, Aug 21, 2026; Waldenberg confirmed it on the record).
- Alan Waldenberg, outside counsel to Cerberus and to Feinberg and manager of the trust holding Feinberg's Cerberus assets, represented DoD in the July 2025 MP Materials deal: a $400M equity stake, a $150M loan and a 15% government stake.
- He says Feinberg chose him and ethics staff were informed.
- DoD says the arrangement complied with the rules.
- Richard Painter criticised it.
- Amounts: $400M; $150M; 15%
- Dates: Aug 21, 2026; July 2025
- Sources: [17](https://prospect.org/2026/08/21/pentagon-minerals-deal-department-defense-cerberus-capital-alan-waldenberg-stephen-feinberg/)

### 9. Feinberg's Cerberus trust is exposed to a possible Ligado settlement with DoD.

**Partly accurate** · Evidence: Single-source (American Prospect, Jul 17, 2026)

- Ligado, which is Cerberus-backed, is suing DoD; the claim rose from $39-40B to about $50B. The Prospect says Feinberg's trust interest could rise if DoD settles.
- DoD says any Ligado profit to him would go to a veterans' charity, which it did not name.
- As of Nov 2, 2026, DOJ was seeking dismissal and no settlement had been reported, so the windfall remains hypothetical.
- Amounts: $39-40B; about $50B
- Dates: Jul 17, 2026; Nov 2, 2026
- Sources: [18](https://prospect.org/2026/07/17/top-defense-official-could-reap-windfall-from-dod-settlement/) [19](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/11/02/us-doj-wants-ligado-50-billion-lawsuit-dismissed/)

### 10. OSC lent to Vulcan (1789), PDW and Neros, and held talks with Unusual Machines (Don Jr.).

**Mostly accurate** · Evidence: Documented

- Vulcan got a $620M loan (Nov 3, 2025), initiated by Navarro (C26/C65).
- PDW got a conditional commitment of up to $820M (Jul 31, 2026).
- Neros got up to $450M (Nov 2, 2026) alongside $165M in equity; DroneXL says the OSC release does not mention a government stake.
- Unusual Machines was named in the WSJ-reported talks (May 28, 2026), but no OSC release naming it had appeared as of early Nov 2026.
- Trump-sons-linked Cove Kaz applied to OSC for $400M (FT, May 2026), not reported as approved.
- All of these came under Lorch's directorship, but nothing ties Lorch or Kollitides to the Vulcan decision; ProPublica attributes that push to Navarro.
- Amounts: $620M; up to $820M; up to $450M; $165M; $400M
- Dates: Nov 3, 2025; Jul 31, 2026; Nov 2, 2026; May 28, 2026; early Nov 2026
- Sources: [29](https://www.propublica.org/article/donald-trump-jr-vulcan-deal-white-house) [24](https://soldiersystems.net/2026/07/31/the-office-of-strategic-capital-signs-820-million-conditional-loan-commitment-with-performance-drone-works-to-expand-domestic-drone-component-manufacturing/) [25](https://www.suasnews.com/2026/11/department-of-wars-office-of-strategic-capital-signs-conditional-loan-commitment-of-up-to-450-million-with-neros-inc/) [26](https://dronexl.co/2026/11/02/neros-450m-osc-loan-taxpayer-ownership-stake/) [27](https://dronexl.co/2026/05/28/pentagon-drone-funding-equity-neros-unusual-machines-pdw/) [28](https://en.orda.kz/pentagon-may-add-400-million-to-kazakh-tungsten-project-linked-to-trumps-sons-10417/)

### 11. Recusal arrangements exist for Feinberg and the Cerberus alumni.

**Partly accurate** · Evidence: Partly documented, partly single-source

- DoD spokesman Parnell says Feinberg 'has completely divested' from Cerberus and is recused from executive-branch dealings with it.
- His OGE agreement actually placed his interests in trusts for his adult children (C65).
- Warren asked him to extend the recusal to his whole tenure and refuse waivers, and no public reply has been found.
- A US official told Bloomberg (Sep 4, 2026; seen via Briefs and CryptoBriefing) that Cerberus alumni must recuse from Cerberus matters and that such deals are routed to Commerce Secretary Lutnick.
- Whether Lutnick reviewed M1 or Stratolaunch is unknown.
- No recusal, waiver or financial disclosure for Kollitides (an SGE) or Lorch has been published.
- Reuters (Sep 5, 2026) reported that the White House reviewed possible Feinberg successors; Hegseth said he 'isn't going anywhere.'
- Dates: Sep 4, 2026; Sep 5, 2026
- Sources: [21](https://www.briefs.co/news/pentagon-taps-howard-lutnick-to-screen-pentagon-deals-linked/) [22](https://cryptobriefing.com/lutnick-review-pentagon-cerberus-capital-deals/) [15](https://www.warren.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Follow-Up-Letter-from-Senator-Warren-to-Stephen-Feinberg-on-Golden-Dome.pdf) [23](https://www.militarytimes.com/news/pentagon-congress/2026/09/05/white-house-reviews-candidates-to-replace-hegseth-deputy-sources-say/)

### 12. Cerberus alumni steered awards or loans toward Trump-family-linked companies.

**Unverified** · Evidence: None found

- No reporting shows Lorch, Kollitides or Feinberg acting on Vulcan, Unusual Machines, Cove Kaz or New America.
- The D. Boral and McGurn overlap is a shared board, not a transaction.
- Any steering is inferred only from co-occurrence.
- Sources: [1](https://news.bgov.com/bloomberg-government-news/pentagon-investment-arm-director-joined-firm-with-defense-ties) [29](https://www.propublica.org/article/donald-trump-jr-vulcan-deal-white-house)

## Denials and context

- Pentagon (Sean Parnell): 'There are no conflicts of interest between Deputy Secretary of War Steve Feinberg and Cerberus'; Kollitides and the department followed all applicable rules; OSC has a multi-layered ethics process.
- Cerberus says Feinberg is not involved in its operations.
- A&M Capital and Alvarez & Marsal Federal are legally distinct; the consulting arm did not answer ProPublica.
- D. Boral Capital and David Boral did not respond to Bloomberg Government.
- SHIELD gave slots to about 2,100 vendors and Flight School Next was competed against 10 other bidders, so winning one is not evidence of favouritism.
- Don Jr. and 1789 deny contacting officials about Vulcan or UMAC (C26/C51).
- No IG or OGE finding of wrongdoing has been reported.

## Full report

**Verdict: mostly-accurate (confidence: medium).** Every role, contract and loan in the brief is documented. The connections between them are a different matter. Some are documented: Feinberg to Lorch and Kollitides via Cerberus; Kollitides to Alvarez & Marsal; Kollitides to D. Boral Acquisition I. Others are inferred from shared names and timing: the D. Boral to Trump-sons chain, and OSC loans to Trump-linked firms. We found no evidence that any Cerberus alum steered a Pentagon decision toward a Trump-family interest, and the Pentagon says all ethics rules were followed. On recusals, the Pentagon says Feinberg is recused from Cerberus matters. A single-source report says Cerberus-related deals are routed to Commerce Secretary Lutnick. We found no published recusal or waiver for Kollitides or Lorch.

Builds on: C65 (sub-claim on Feinberg and Cerberus), C51 (drone ventures, OSC drone loans), C26 (1789 and Vulcan), and C47 and C27 (Dominari, D. Boral and the New America SPAC). This report does not repeat facts those files already establish. It extends them.

### What we found that the parent files did not have

1. **The D. Boral SPAC board puts Kollitides beside Kevin McGurn, the Trump sons' SPAC CEO.** *Documented: Bloomberg Government plus D. Boral Acquisition I 8-K filings.*
   - Bloomberg Government reported that D. Boral Acquisition I (Nasdaq: DBCA, a Cayman SPAC) appointed Kollitides, Kevin McGurn and Luisa Ingargiola as directors. Kollitides joined on Feb. 10, 2026, days after taking his Pentagon post. He took an indirect interest in 100,000 founder shares, which Bloomberg valued at up to about $1M.
   - The SPAC's IPO priced on Feb. 10-11, 2026. D. Boral Capital was sole book-runner, and the offering closed at $287.5M.
   - McGurn is chairman, CEO and CFO of the Trump sons' New America Acquisition I (C27). D. Boral Capital co-led that SPAC's $345M IPO in Dec. 2025. McGurn also became interim CEO of Trump Media on Apr. 21, 2026.
   - So for about seven months (Feb. 10 to Sep. 23, 2026) the Pentagon's Economic Defense Unit chief sat on a SPAC board with the man running both the Trump sons' SPAC and the president's media company.
   - Bloomberg says McGurn and Ingargiola serve on boards of several defense-related companies that count Don Jr. and Eric Trump as investors or advisers. That is Bloomberg's characterisation; we did not verify which boards.
   - An 8-K reports that Kollitides resigned as an independent director "for personal reasons" effective Sep. 23, 2026. A second 8-K (Nov. 6, 2026) puts McGurn and Ingargiola on its audit and compensation committees after another director left.
   - The board seat is documented. The co-directorship is documented. It shows a shared board, not a transaction.
2. **The Alvarez & Marsal timeline.** *Documented: ProPublica, Oct. 2026, via Defense One and GovExec syndication.*
   - DoD gave Alvarez & Marsal a roughly $67M deal shortly after Kollitides started at the Pentagon. Defense One dates the awards to "since December" 2025, which is before his Feb 2026 EDU appointment. No contract record found here confirms the $67M figure.
   - About two months later it finalised a $281M no-bid contract with Alvarez & Marsal Federal to advise Kollitides's own office, which Defense One calls BOND (Business Operators for National Defense). The combined ceiling is about $350M, of which about $31M had been paid.
   - Kollitides had been a partner at A&M Capital, a legally distinct affiliate. A&M Capital says he stopped working as a senior adviser to it "several days" before ProPublica's questions arrived.
   - ProPublica did not establish that he chose the firm. The Pentagon says he followed all rules. Ethics experts quoted by GovExec said taking part in a contract affecting one's own finances is generally illegal without a waiver. No waiver has been reported.
3. **Kollitides is a special government employee.** *Single-source: Bloomberg Government; DefenseScoop reports the EDU structure.*
   - Bloomberg reports he was brought in as an SGE. SGEs are temporary appointees under lighter rules. That status helps explain how he could hold an outside board seat. Bloomberg says it is unclear whether his status has changed.
   - The EDU ("Deal Team Six") director reports to Feinberg. The unit received more than $266M in FY2026 RDT&E funding. Semafor reported a recruiting document that floated $200B of deals over three years. Neither figure has been audited.
4. **More Cerberus links to Feinberg's Pentagon.** *Mostly single-outlet (American Prospect); Warren letters are primary documents.*
   - **Alan Waldenberg.** Waldenberg is outside counsel to Cerberus and to Feinberg personally, and he manages the trust holding Feinberg's Cerberus assets. He confirmed to the American Prospect (Aug. 21, 2026) that he represented DoD in the July 2025 MP Materials deal: a $400M equity stake plus a $150M loan. He said ethics staff were informed. DoD says the arrangement complied with ethics rules.
   - **Ligado Networks.** This Cerberus-backed company is suing DoD (the claim has grown from $39-40B to about $50B). The Prospect (Jul. 17, 2026) reported that Feinberg's trust interest could rise if DoD settles. DoD says any Ligado profit to Feinberg would go to a veterans' charity, which it did not name. As of Nov. 2, 2026, DOJ was seeking dismissal and no settlement had been reported.
   - **M1 Support Services.** This firm, which describes itself as "a Cerberus Capital Management company", won the Army's Flight School Next IDIQ on Aug. 13, 2026, worth up to $10B over up to 26 years, against 10 other bidders.
   - **Golden Dome.** Warren's Apr. 27 and Jul. 27, 2026 letters name North Wind, Stratolaunch and Red River Technology among at least four Cerberus-related Golden Dome awardees, and Eos Energy is also reported. Context cuts the other way here: the MDA's SHIELD vehicle (ceiling $151B) gave slots to about 2,100 companies, so a slot is not money. The award values have not been disclosed.
5. **The recusal picture.**
   - DoD spokesman Sean Parnell says Feinberg "has completely divested" from Cerberus and is recused from executive-branch dealings with it. His OGE agreement actually moved his interests into trusts for his adult children (C65). Warren asked him to extend his recusal to his whole tenure and to refuse waivers. No public answer has been found.
   - A US official told Bloomberg (Sep. 4, 2026; seen only via Briefs and CryptoBriefing) that Cerberus alumni must recuse from Cerberus matters and that such deals go to Commerce Secretary Lutnick for sign-off. *Single-source.* Briefs adds that public filings show Cantor Fitzgerald has backed Cerberus vehicles since at least 2012 and is prime broker to several Cerberus funds. If accurate, the official reviewer of Cerberus deals has his own family firm's commercial tie to Cerberus. That is our observation, not an outlet's allegation.
   - Reuters (Sep. 5, 2026) reported that the White House reviewed possible successors to Feinberg. Hegseth said he "isn't going anywhere." Feinberg was still in post at last report.
6. **OSC lending to Trump-linked and other drone firms.** *Documented.*
   - **Vulcan Elements** (1789 Capital, Don Jr.): a $620M loan, Nov. 3, 2025, initiated by Navarro (C26/C65).
   - **PDW:** a conditional commitment of up to $820M, Jul. 31, 2026. This corrects C51's unspecified figure.
   - **Neros:** a conditional commitment of up to $450M, Nov. 2, 2026, alongside $165M in equity. DroneXL says the OSC release does not mention a government stake.
   - **Unusual Machines** (Don Jr. adviser and shareholder): in the WSJ-reported talks (May 28, 2026), but DroneXL found no OSC release naming it as of early Nov. 2026.
   - **Cove Kaz** (Trump sons via Skyline/American Ventures, C47): applied to OSC for $400M (FT, May 2026). It is not reported as approved.
   - All of these loans were made while Lorch was director (since Nov. 2025). No reporting ties Lorch personally to any of them.
