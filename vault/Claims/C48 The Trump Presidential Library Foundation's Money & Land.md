# C48: The Trump Presidential Library Foundation's Money & Land

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Type:** Check · Family Ventures & Wealth

## The Claim

> What happened to the lawsuit-settlement money earmarked for Trump's presidential library after the first fund was dissolved, and did a successor foundation receive Miami land worth more than $67 million for $10?

## Bottom Line

Documented: the first Trump Presidential Library Fund (incorporated Dec 2024) was administratively dissolved in Sept 2025.

## What Holds Up

- A successor foundation received Miami Dade College land worth $67M+ for $10.
- The foundation got fast-track IRS approval during the government shutdown.
- The Qatari 747 is slated to go to the library foundation (mostly accurate)
- Foundation officers include Eric Trump, Michael Boulos and James Kiley.
- The land transfer has been challenged in court.

## What Needs Correcting

- The first library fund was dissolved in Sept 2025 and ~$60M+ of settlement money earmarked for the library is unaccounted for, per Warren, Blumenthal and Stansbury (partly accurate)
- The foundation, White House and payers have explained the money trail (false)
- The claim says ~$60M+ is unaccounted for; after Trump's 30 Jun 2026 disclosure, $56.5M is shown as paid to the foundation, and the lawmakers' current allegation is $6.5M-$14.5M missing plus $7M of unexplained expenditures.
- X's settlement (~$8M per the disclosure, not the ~$10M press figure) was paid to Trump personally, not to the library.
- $67M is the county assessed value; reported estimates range from a $60M appraisal to ~$300M market value.
- The 747 transfer to the library is the stated plan, not settled; Eric Trump says it is not planned for display in Miami.

## In More Detail

- A successor Donald J. Trump Presidential Library Foundation (May 2025; trustees Eric Trump, Michael Boulos and James Kiley) won IRS exemption in 10 days during the Oct 2025 shutdown and was deeded the Miami Dade College parcel (county-assessed at $67M+) for $10, with two lawsuits failing so far to stop it.
- The weakest element is '$60M+ unaccounted for': Trump's June 2026 disclosure shows $56.5M paid to the foundation and $8M from X paid to Trump personally, so lawmakers now allege $6.5M-$14.5M missing plus $7M unexplained.
- The 747 transfer to the foundation is the stated plan, but it is not settled.

## Sub-Claims

### 1. The first library fund was dissolved in Sept 2025 and ~$60M+ of settlement money earmarked for the library is unaccounted for, per Warren, Blumenthal and Stansbury.

**Partly accurate**

- The fund was incorporated in Florida on 20 Dec 2024 by Trump-entity lawyer Jacob Roth and administratively dissolved in late Sept 2025 for not filing an annual report.
- The lawmakers' March-April 2026 letters alleged up to $63M lacked any public accounting.
- Trump's OGE disclosure, released 30 Jun 2026, then showed $64.5M in total: $56.5M paid to the Foundation by Meta, ABC and Paramount, and $8M paid by X to Trump personally.
- The lawmakers' 16 Jul 2026 letter alleges $6.5M-$14.5M missing and $7M of unexplained spending against the Foundation's reported $50M of revenue (as of 10 Oct 2025).
- The dissolution and the allegation are documented, but 'unaccounted for' now refers to a much smaller gap, and that gap remains an allegation.
- Amounts: up to $63M; $64.5M; $56.5M; $8M; $6.5M-$14.5M
- Dates: 20 Dec 2024; late Sept 2025; April 2026; 30 Jun 2026; 16 Jul 2026
- Sources: [1](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2025-10-16/trump-presidential-library-nonprofit-miami) [2](https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-blumenthal-stansbury-press-big-tech-ceos-on-trump-settlements-to-now-dissolved-library-fund) [3](https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-blumenthal-stansbury-press-trump-on-missing-millions-donated-to-dissolved-trump-library-fund-new-big-tech-ceo-responses-raise-fresh-questions/) [4](https://www.warren.senate.gov/?p=39088) [5](https://www.warren.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Warren-Letter-to-Trump-Library-Foundation-re-Financial-Disclosures.pdf) [22](https://www.thedailybeast.com/donald-trumps-librarys-missing-millions-mystery-targeted-in-new-probe)

### 2. A successor foundation received Miami Dade College land worth $67M+ for $10.

**Confirmed**

- The MDC trustees voted on 23 Sep 2025 to give the ~2.6-acre downtown parcel to the state.
- DeSantis and the Cabinet (Board of Trustees of the Internal Improvement Trust Fund) voted unanimously on 30 Sep 2025 to deed it to the foundation for $10, on condition that construction starts within five years.
- The county appraiser's 2025 assessment is more than $67M; one report cites a $60M appraisal, and plaintiffs estimate ~$300M market value.
- The deed was recorded in Jan-Feb 2026.
- Amounts: $10; more than $67M; $60M; ~$300M
- Dates: 23 Sep 2025; 30 Sep 2025; Feb 2026
- Sources: [9](https://www.myfloridacfo.com/news/pressreleases/press-release-details/2025/09/30/in-case-you-missed-it--governor-ron-desantis-and-cabinet-deed-land-for-the-donald-j.-trump-presidential-library) [10](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/local/lawsuit-challenges-vote-to-gift-prime-miami-real-estate-for-trumps-presidential-library/3702519/) [14](https://keyt.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/05/13/locals-sue-to-block-prime-miami-land-from-becoming-trump-presidential-library/)

### 3. The foundation got fast-track IRS approval during the government shutdown.

**Confirmed**

- It applied on 10 Oct 2025 and received an IRS determination letter dated 20 Oct 2025, during the shutdown.
- Normal processing is 3-6 months, and lawyers called it 'virtually unheard of'.
- The request cited settlement money in limbo.
- The application projected ~$950M of contributions ($50M in 2025, $300M in 2026, $600M in 2027).
- The day after the approval, the IRS announced it would not process exemption applications for the rest of the shutdown (Miami Herald).
- The IRS declined comment, citing privacy.
- No evidence of political intervention was found; 'special treatment' is the Miami Herald's characterization.
- Amounts: ~$950M; $50M; $300M; $600M
- Dates: 10 Oct 2025; 20 Oct 2025
- Sources: [6](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/11/20/trumps-library-foundation-got-special-treatment-from-the-irs-during-the-government-shutdown/173612/) [7](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/11/19/tax-documents-show-trump-library-is-counting-on-a-1-billion-fundraising-haul/173534/) [8](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2025-11-20/trump-library-foundation-expects-to-raise-50-million-this-year)

### 4. The Qatari 747 is slated to go to the library foundation.

**Mostly accurate**

- Per C16, the jet is to transfer to the foundation by 1 Jan 2029, and Bondi and White House counsel called this legally permissible.
- It flew as Air Force One from mid-2026.
- A White House official said there was 'no final decision'.
- In Oct 2026 Eric Trump told NBC that displaying it in Miami is 'not the plan at the moment' and that an older retired Air Force One would be shown instead.
- Dates: 1 Jan 2029; mid-2026; Oct 2026
- Sources: [21](https://thepulseofnh.com/no-final-decision-on-future-of-qatar-donated-air-force-one-after-trumps-term-white-house-official-says/)

### 5. Foundation officers include Eric Trump, Michael Boulos and James Kiley.

**Confirmed**

- The May 2025 Florida incorporation lists Eric Trump (president and trustee), Michael Boulos (Tiffany Trump's husband) and James Kiley (an attorney who has represented Trump) as trustees.
- Tiffany Trump's inclusion is reported inconsistently and is unverified.
- The NYT found that a proposed budget allotted $400,000 to officers, while a Florida charity filing said none expected pay.
- The first fund's directors were not identified.
- Amounts: $400,000
- Dates: May 2025
- Sources: [20](https://cbsnews.com/news/trump-library-democratic-congress) [8](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2025-11-20/trump-library-foundation-expects-to-raise-50-million-this-year) [18](https://www.citizensforethics.org/reports-investigations/crew-investigations/the-trump-organization-is-developing-trumps-presidential-library-maybe-as-a-trump-hotel/)

### 6. The land transfer has been challenged in court.

**Confirmed**

- In the state case, Marvin Dunn's Sunshine Law suit won a pause and then a temporary injunction (Oct-Nov 2025, Judge Mavel Ruiz; Dunn posted his home as bond).
- It was dismissed on 18 Dec 2025 after the MDC board re-voted.
- In the federal case, Sistrunk Seeds (Dunn's Overtown Farm) et al. v. Trump et al. (S.D. Fla. 1:2026cv23365) was filed on 13 May 2026 by the Constitutional Accountability Center and Gelber Schachter & Greenberg under the Domestic Emoluments Clause, alleging the 'library' is a hotel venture.
- In Sept 2026 Judge Rodolfo Ruiz II stayed discovery and said the immunity and standing defenses could 'dispose of the entire case'.
- No final ruling was found as of Nov 2026.
- Dates: Nov 2025; 18 Dec 2025; 13 May 2026; Sept 2026; Nov 2026
- Sources: [11](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/donald-trump/trump-presidential-library-judge-blocks-miami-land-transfer-rcna237725) [12](https://www.local10.com/news/local/2025/11/03/judge-rules-to-temporarily-stop-transfer-of-miami-dade-college-land-for-trump-library) [13](https://www.wlrn.org/government-politics/2025-12-19/judge-dismisses-complaint-against-miami-land-transfer-for-trumps-presidential-library) [14](https://keyt.com/politics/cnn-us-politics/2026/05/13/locals-sue-to-block-prime-miami-land-from-becoming-trump-presidential-library/) [15](https://www.theusconstitution.org/litigation/sistrunk-seeds-v-trump/) [16](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/florida/flsdce/1:2026cv23365/714261) [17](https://www.nwaonline.com/news/2026/sep/13/dismissal-eyed-for-trump-library-suit/)

### 7. The foundation, White House and payers have explained the money trail.

**False**

- No on-record foundation response to reporters or to the lawmakers was found.
- The White House refers questions to the library.
- The companies told lawmakers they paid as their agreements required, without saying where the money went after the fund dissolved.
- The IRS declined comment.
- Sources: [3](https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-blumenthal-stansbury-press-trump-on-missing-millions-donated-to-dissolved-trump-library-fund-new-big-tech-ceo-responses-raise-fresh-questions/) [22](https://www.thedailybeast.com/donald-trumps-librarys-missing-millions-mystery-targeted-in-new-probe) [6](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/11/20/trumps-library-foundation-got-special-treatment-from-the-irs-during-the-government-shutdown/173612/)

## Denials & Context

- The foundation has not responded publicly; the White House refers questions to it; the IRS cites privacy law.
- Presidential library foundations are private nonprofits not required to disclose donors; no evidence that funds went to Trump family members has been shown, although the lawmakers ask.
- The land transfer passed two public MDC votes and a unanimous Cabinet vote, and survived the state lawsuit; DeSantis and the CFO championed it.
- The federal emoluments suit faces presidential-immunity and standing defenses that the judge signaled could end it.
- The money allegations come from Democratic lawmakers; the underlying records (Sunbiz, IRS letter, OGE disclosure) are official.

## Full Report

**Verdict: mostly-accurate** (confidence: medium)

This check builds on C33 (settlements to the library) and C16 (Qatari 747). C33 already established the four settlement amounts (ABC $15M, Meta ~$22-25M, Paramount $16M, X ~$10M) and the fund's dissolution; C16 established the 747's acceptance and the 2029 transfer plan. This file adds the entities, officers, the newer money trail, the lawsuits over the land, and the responses.

**Bottom line:** The structural facts in the claim hold up: a first library fund (Dec 2024) was administratively dissolved in Sept 2025; a successor foundation (May 2025) run by Eric Trump, Michael Boulos and attorney James Kiley got IRS exemption in 10 days during the shutdown and took title to the Miami Dade College parcel (county-assessed at $67M+) for $10. The weakest part is "~$60M+ unaccounted for". Warren, Blumenthal and Stansbury did say this in March-April 2026. But Trump's 2025 financial disclosure, released 30 Jun 2026, shows $56.5M paid to the foundation by Meta, ABC and Paramount, plus $8M paid to Trump personally by X. The lawmakers then narrowed the claim to $6.5M-$14.5M missing and $7M of unexplained spending. The 747 transfer is the stated plan, but it is not settled.

### Sub-claims

#### 1. The first library fund was dissolved in Sept 2025, and ~$60M+ of settlement money is alleged to be unaccounted for — *partly-accurate*
- Trump Presidential Library Fund, Inc. was incorporated in Florida on 20 Dec 2024, days after the ABC settlement. It was formed by Jacob Roth, a Florida attorney who forms Trump-related entities; Roth declined comment. It filed one document, a Feb 2025 amendment, and was administratively dissolved in late Sept 2025 for not filing its annual report (WLRN/WUSF, 16 Oct 2025). Search results did not identify its directors.
- On 11 Mar 2026 Warren, Blumenthal and Stansbury wrote to ABC, Meta, Paramount and X. They said up to $63M promised to the library had no public accounting. On 20 Apr 2026 they released the companies' replies; they said the replies did not explain where the money went. Per C33, ABC said its $15M remained in the settlement escrow.
- **Update that weakens the "missing" framing:** Trump's 2025 OGE financial disclosure, made public 30 Jun 2026, shows $64.5M from the four companies. Of that, $56.5M was paid to the *Foundation* by Meta, ABC and Paramount, and $8M was paid by X to *Trump personally*. In a 16 Jul 2026 letter to the Foundation, the lawmakers say at least $6.5M and up to $14.5M is missing and $7M of expenditures is unexplained. They also note that the Foundation reported $50M of revenue as of 10 Oct 2025.
- The $6.5M/$14.5M range appears to be $56.5M minus $50M, with or without X's $8M. That is my inference from the figures; the release text was not read directly.
- Verdict: the dissolution and the lawmakers' allegation are documented. But "unaccounted for" is now mostly a disclosure gap of $6.5M-$14.5M, not $60M+. The X money going to Trump rather than the library is itself a notable finding.

#### 2. A successor foundation received Miami Dade College land worth $67M+ for $10 — *confirmed*
- Donald J. Trump Presidential Library Foundation, Inc. was incorporated in Florida in May 2025.
- On 23 Sep 2025 the Miami Dade College trustees voted to give the ~2.6-acre downtown parking-lot parcel (by the Freedom Tower) to the state. On 30 Sep 2025 Gov. DeSantis and the Cabinet, sitting as the Board of Trustees of the Internal Improvement Trust Fund, voted unanimously to deed it to the foundation (Florida CFO release).
- Property and court records show a $10 price. The only reported deed restriction is that construction of a "Presidential library, museum, and/or center" must start within five years.
- The Miami-Dade property appraiser's 2025 assessment is "more than $67 million" (AP/NBC). The Inquirer cites a $60M appraisal. Plaintiffs and local experts put market value near $300M.
- The deed was recorded in Jan-Feb 2026 (CNN says January; earlier dataset work says February).

#### 3. The foundation got fast-track IRS approval during the government shutdown — *confirmed*
- The foundation applied on 10 Oct 2025 and received an IRS determination letter dated 20 Oct 2025, three weeks into the shutdown (Miami Herald, 20 Nov 2025). Normal processing takes 3-6 months unless the IRS grants expedited processing. Nonprofit lawyers called the timeline "virtually unheard of."
- The request said settlement money was in limbo until the foundation was an approved charity.
- One report says the IRS announced the next day that it would stop processing exemption applications for the rest of the shutdown.
- The IRS cited privacy laws and did not comment. The White House and the foundation did not respond.
- The application projected about $950M of contributions: $50M in 2025, $300M in 2026 and $600M in 2027. Whether this was improper favoritism is characterization; the approval is lawful on its face, and no evidence of intervention was found.

#### 4. The Qatari 747 is slated to go to the foundation — *mostly-accurate*
- Per C16, the Pentagon accepted the jet on 21 May 2025, with ownership to pass to the library foundation by 1 Jan 2029. Bondi and White House counsel deemed this legally permissible.
- The jet flew as Air Force One from mid-2026, and in Oct 2026 the administration reaffirmed the transfer.
- However, a White House official said there was "no final decision." In Oct 2026 Eric Trump told NBC that displaying it in Miami is "not the plan at the moment." He had earlier said the library would show an older, retired Air Force One.
- The foundation has not taken possession.

#### 5. Officers: Eric Trump, Michael Boulos, James Kiley — *confirmed*
- The May 2025 incorporation lists Eric Trump (president and trustee), Michael Boulos (Tiffany Trump's husband) and James Kiley (a Long Island attorney who has represented Trump) as trustees.
- Some reports also name Tiffany Trump as a trustee, but Fox does not; her role is unverified.
- The NYT reported that a proposed budget set aside $400,000 for officers, while a Florida charity filing said none of the four officers expected pay. The foundation did not explain the difference.
- Eric Trump, who is also a Trump Organization executive, has said the Trump Organization is involved in developing the project. Trump said in March 2026 that it will "most likely" include a hotel, in a roughly 47-story tower.

#### 6. Lawsuits over the land — *confirmed (none has stopped the transfer)*
- **State case (Dunn v. Miami Dade College):** historian Marvin Dunn alleged a Sunshine Law violation because of inadequate notice for the 23 Sep 2025 vote. Circuit Judge Mavel Ruiz paused the transfer in Oct 2025 and issued a temporary injunction in early Nov 2025. Dunn posted his home to cover the bond. After the college board re-voted, the judge dismissed the complaint on 18 Dec 2025. An earlier report of an "August trial" was overtaken by that dismissal. No appeal was found.
- **Federal case (Sistrunk Seeds Inc. d/b/a Dunn's Overtown Farm et al. v. Trump et al., S.D. Fla. 1:2026cv23365):** filed 13 May 2026 by the Constitutional Accountability Center and Gelber Schachter & Greenberg. Defendants are Trump, DeSantis, state officials, the foundation and the MDC trustees. It alleges the gift violates the Domestic Emoluments Clause and seeks to void the deal. Plaintiffs say the "library" is a hotel venture that will enrich the president.
- In Sept 2026, Judge Rodolfo A. Ruiz II stayed discovery and signaled that DOJ's presidential-immunity and standing defenses could "dispose of the entire case." No final ruling was found as of Nov 2026.

#### 7. Responses — *documented non-responses*
- The foundation has not answered reporters' or lawmakers' questions in any coverage found.
- The White House refers questions to the library.
- The companies say they paid as their settlement agreements required.
- The IRS says privacy law bars comment.
- Florida officials (DeSantis, CFO Ingoglia) publicly championed the land gift.
- Paramount (per C33) says no money went to Trump personally. The disclosure that X paid $8M to Trump personally is separate.

### Corrections
- Claimed: "$60M+ unaccounted for". After the June 2026 disclosure, $56.5M is shown as paid to the foundation. The lawmakers' current claim is $6.5M-$14.5M missing plus $7M of unexplained spending.
- Claimed: settlement money "earmarked for the library". X's ~$8M is disclosed as paid to Trump personally, not to the library.
- The $67M figure is the county assessed value; market estimates run to ~$300M, and one report cites a $60M appraisal.
- The 747 going to the library is the stated plan, not settled; Eric Trump says it won't be displayed in Miami for now.

### Denials and context
- No foundation response on the money; no evidence that money was diverted to Trump family members (the lawmakers ask, but allege nothing specific).
- Presidential library foundations are private nonprofits and are not legally required to disclose donors; the IRS approval was lawful on its face.
- The land transfer went through two public votes and survived the state lawsuit.
- The federal emoluments suit may be dismissed on immunity or standing grounds.
- The lawmakers' material is partisan (all Democrats), but the underlying documents (state filings, IRS letter, OGE disclosure) are official records.
