# C109: Other Senior Officials: Waivers, Late Divestitures & Outside Interests

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Type:** Check · Pardons, Lobbyists & Access

## The Claim

> Which other senior officials received ethics waivers or divested late, and what happened to the offices that oversee them?

## Bottom Line

Filings document a small set of published conflict-of-interest waivers (Kennedy's interim waiver for family-office funds, Wright's CERAWeek waiver, plus Sacks, Lutnick and one task-force official), retained interests approved in ethics agreements (Patel's Elite Depot units, Isaacman's Shift4 stake), a missed certification deadline flagged for McMahon by the Campaign Legal Center, and a gap between Kennedy's amended ethics agreement and how his Gardasil fee interest was actually assigned, according to HHS's own letter as quoted by senators.

## What Holds Up

- Elon Musk served as a special government employee without a public financial disclosure or a published conflict-of-interest waiver while his companies held federal contracts and faced federal regulators; he left in late May 2025.
- Kash Patel kept his Elite Depot (Shein parent) stock units as FBI director, and his ethics agreement records other outside interests.
- Robert F. Kennedy Jr. received an ethics waiver, and his Gardasil referral-fee interest was handled differently from his ethics agreement (mostly accurate)
- Chris Wright received an ethics waiver while divesting Liberty Energy.
- Linda McMahon missed her divestiture deadline (mostly accurate)
- Jared Isaacman kept a large stake in Shift4 at NASA.
- The White House has published only a handful of conflict-of-interest waivers, and no new ethics pledge replaced the one revoked on 20 Jan 2025 (mostly accurate)
- The OGE director was removed in February 2025 and the office has since been run by acting heads with other jobs or left without one.
- Inspectors general were dismissed in January 2025, a court found the removals unlawful, and they were not reinstated.

## What Needs Correcting

- Doug Burgum's divestitures or disclosures were late, and Interior has unresolved ethics questions (partly accurate)
- Kelly Loeffler kept outside interests of note while running the SBA (partly accurate)
- Frank Bisignano divested Fiserv late (false)
- C103 records CREW's 18 May 2026 letter as addressed to 'OGE Acting Director Eric Ueland'. CREW did address it that way, but senators wrote on the same day that Ueland's acting service had ended in early December 2025 and that OGE had no acting head; Keith Sonderling was named acting director on 23 June 2026. Suggested wording: 'addressed to Eric Ueland as acting director (senators said his acting service had ended in Dec 2025)'.
- C65 (Patel) can add from his ethics agreement (read in full): he keeps Elite Depot (Shein parent) stock units under an agency 'remote' determination, agreed to resign from the Trump Media board, and has a one-year covered relationship with Qatar after embassy consulting that ended in Nov 2024 (a 4 Mar 2025 waiver for a Qatar-related matter was later reported; C73). No change to C65's verdict.
- C68 (Musk) can add that no 208(b)(1) waiver for Musk appears on the White House waiver list and that his disclosure was confidential; no correction to its figures.
- C86: no overlap found; no correction.
- **Claimed:** Bisignano divested late  
  **The record shows:** His Fiserv sales (16 May to 1 July 2025) were inside the 120-day window; the dispute is about timing before the stock's fall, not lateness.
- **Claimed:** Isaacman divested his Shift4 stake  
  **The record shows:** His ethics agreement lets him keep it with reduced voting control and recusal.

## What Is Unproven

- Mehmet Oz divested late or incompletely.
- Pete Hegseth's broker approached BlackRock about a defence-fund investment before the Iran strikes.
- GAO or the courts have made findings about these officials' ethics compliance.
- Any authority has found that one of these officials violated 18 U.S.C. 208 or an ethics agreement.

## In More Detail

- Musk served without a public disclosure or published waiver.
- Bisignano's Fiserv sale met its deadline, and nothing late was found for Oz or Hegseth; nothing at all was found on record for Boulos.
- The oversight offices changed sharply: the OGE director was removed in Feb 2025 and the office has been run by acting heads with other jobs or left vacant; a court found the Jan 2025 IG removals unlawful but did not reinstate anyone; Biden's ethics pledge was revoked without replacement.
- No authority has found that any named official violated 18 U.S.C.
- 208 or an ethics agreement.

### 1. Elon Musk served as a special government employee without a public financial disclosure or a published conflict-of-interest waiver while his companies held federal contracts and faced federal regulators; he left in late May 2025.

**Confirmed** · Evidence: Documented status, no finding

- 208, which bars work on particular matters affecting his financial interests unless a 208(b) waiver or exemption applies.
- Filing: a White House official said in February 2025 that he would file a confidential (non-public) report, which SGEs may do (AP via Star-Advertiser, excerpt).
- Waiver: none appears on the White House list of 208(b)(1) waivers (read in full, Nov 2026: Kennedy, Leder, Lutnick, Sacks twice, Wright).
- Letters: Sen. Schiff asked OGE Director Huitema on 10 Feb 2025, the day Huitema was removed, whether any waiver existed (read in full); House Oversight Democrats asked White House Counsel Warrington on 1 May 2025 for the disclosure and any 208(b) determination (read in full).

- Departure: Musk announced on 28 May 2025 that his SGE time was ending (ABC, excerpt).
- The Campaign Legal Center asked OGE in June 2025 to check DOGE staff's disclosure compliance (read in full).
- Response: Press Secretary Leavitt said Musk would excuse himself from conflicting contracts (quoted in the Schiff letter).
- No OGE, inspector-general or court finding on his compliance was found.
- Builds on C68 (contracts, IPO).
- Amounts: at least $2.37B
- Dates: February 2025; Nov 2026; 10 Feb 2025; 1 May 2025; 27 Apr 2025

### 2. Kash Patel kept his Elite Depot (Shein parent) stock units as FBI director, and his ethics agreement records other outside interests.

- Letter: Sen. Schiff and colleagues objected on 12 Feb 2025 to the retained Shein stake.
- No certification-of-compliance problem, waiver or finding was found.
- Response: Patel told senators he would not take part in matters affecting Elite Depot.
- Amounts: $1M-$5M

### 14. Inspectors general were dismissed in January 2025, a court found the removals unlawful, and they were not reinstated.

**Confirmed** · Evidence: Documented

- On 24 Jan 2025 more than a dozen IGs (about 17) were removed by a two-sentence email citing 'changing priorities', without the 30-day notice to Congress and case-specific reasons the IG Act requires.
- Eight sued (Storch v. Hegseth, D.D.C.).

**Verdict:** mostly-accurate (confidence: medium)

**Question:** Which other senior officials received ethics waivers or divested late, and what happened to the offices that oversee them?

### Summary

Filings and official letters document:

- **A few published conflict-of-interest waivers.** The White House posts six waivers under 18 U.S.C. 208(b)(1). They cover Kennedy (an interim waiver for family-office funds), Wright (for a conference appearance while divesting), Sacks (two), Lutnick (one) and one task-force official.
- **Retained interests approved in ethics agreements.** Patel keeps his Elite Depot (Shein parent) stock units. Isaacman keeps his Shift4 stake with less voting control.
- **One missed certification deadline flagged in a complaint.** The Campaign Legal Center told OGE that McMahon had not certified her divestitures more than a month after her 1 June 2025 deadline.
- **A gap in Kennedy's case.** His amended ethics agreement said he would assign his Gardasil fee interest to his adult son. HHS's own letter, as quoted by senators, says he assigned it to the law firm Wisner Baum instead.
- **Musk** served as a special government employee. His disclosure was confidential, and no waiver for him has been published.

Nothing late was found for Bisignano, whose Fiserv sales met their deadline, or for Oz or Hegseth. Nothing at all was found on record for Massad Boulos (no public disclosure, ethics agreement, waiver, letter or finding), so he is not listed.

The oversight offices changed sharply:

- **OGE:** the director was removed in Feb 2025. Since then the office has been run by acting heads who held other jobs, or has had no head at all.
- **Inspectors general:** a court found the Jan 2025 removals unlawful but did not reinstate anyone.
- **Ethics pledge:** the Biden appointee pledge was revoked and not replaced.

**No authority has found that any official named here violated 18 U.S.C. 208 or an ethics agreement.**

**Evidence rule applied:** holding an asset is not a violation. A late transaction report is a reporting lapse that can carry a $200 per-report fee (waivable by the agency), not a late divestiture. An allegation without a finding is marked unverified. This check builds on C65 (Patel), C68 (Musk) and C103 (Hegseth, OGE). The waivers for Lutnick (C104) and Sacks (C106) are only listed here.

### Sub-claims

#### 1. Musk: confirmed (status); no finding
As a special government employee (SGE), limited to 130 days in any 365, Musk was subject to 18 U.S.C. 208. That law bars an official from working on particular matters that affect his own financial interests unless he has a 208(b) waiver or an exemption.

- **Disclosure:** a White House official said in Feb 2025 that Musk would file a confidential report (AP, excerpt).
- **Waiver:** none is on the White House waiver list (read in full).
- **Letters:** Sen. Schiff wrote to OGE Director Huitema on 10 Feb 2025, the day Huitema was removed, asking whether a waiver existed (read in full). House Oversight Democrats asked White House Counsel Warrington on 1 May 2025 for the disclosure and any 208(b) determination (read in full).
- **Exposure:** the PSI minority staff memo counted 65 actions by 11 agencies against his companies. It estimated at least $2.37B of potential liability across 40 of them (excerpt; a staff estimate, not a finding).
- **Departure:** Musk announced the end of his SGE term on 28 May 2025.
- **Requests and findings:** CLC asked OGE to review DOGE staff's disclosures (read in full). No OGE, inspector-general or court finding was found.

#### 2. Patel: confirmed; no finding
His ethics agreement (read in full) sets out the following:

- **Elite Depot:** he keeps his Elite Depot stock units, which vest quarterly to 1 Nov 2025.
