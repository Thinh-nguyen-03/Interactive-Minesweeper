Confirmed. The top 220 $TRUMP holders, ranked by average holdings from April 23 to May 12, 2025, were invited to a dinner with Trump on May 22, 2025 at Trump National Golf Club in Sterling, Virginia. ## Corrections

- The video does not identify the questioner. It was The Bulwark's Andrew Egger, at the May 9, 2025 briefing, about two weeks before the May 22 dinner.
- 'Top holders' means the top 220 by average holdings from April 23 to May 12, 2025, with a separate top-25 VIP tier. The White House tour originally promised to the top 25 was later removed from the promotion.
- Leavitt did not literally deny 'corruption.' She said Trump abides by conflict-of-interest laws and was attending in his personal time.

## Denials and context

- The White House position: the dinner was a private event on Trump's personal time, not a White House function, and Trump complies with all conflict-of-interest laws. It has also cited the trust that holds his assets. That trust is a family-managed revocable trust, not an independent blind trust.
- Critics: Sen. Elizabeth Warren ('an orgy of corruption'), Sen. Richard Blumenthal ('auctioning off access') and other Democrats. Analyses found that most top wallets used non-US exchanges, which raised foreign-influence concerns.
- About 80% of the 1B $TRUMP supply is held by Trump-linked CIC Digital LLC and Fight Fight Fight LLC, so buying pressure benefits the Trump side. Estimates of what the top 220 spent range from about $140-148M to about $394M.
- No court, regulator or ethics office has found that the dinner was illegal. Whether it was corrupt is a matter of characterization.

## Full report

**Verdict: confirmed (high confidence)**

### What the video says (transcript lines 233-241)
- Top holders of the $TRUMP meme coin got a dinner with the president.
- The White House denied it was corrupt. A reporter asked "Why is the president planning to attend a dinner for the top investors in his coin?" and the press secretary answered: "Look, the president is abiding by all conflict of interest laws. The president has been incredibly transparent with his own personal financial obligations throughout the years. The president is a successful businessman..."
- Coffeezilla adds a sarcastic aside ("we elected the president so he could hold private dinner parties with meme coin holders"). That is commentary, not a factual claim.

### What the evidence shows

1. **Top holders got a dinner with Trump: confirmed.** The contest was announced on April 23, 2025 (UPI, 2025-04-24). Holders were ranked by their average $TRUMP holdings from April 23 to May 12, 2025. The top 220 were invited, after registering on the leaderboard and passing a background check. The dinner took place on **May 22, 2025** at Trump National Golf Club in Sterling (Potomac Falls), Virginia (Axios, Washington Times, CNN via KRDO). The top 25 were promised a VIP reception with Trump before the dinner. The original announcement also promised them a White House tour, but that line was later removed from the site. Trump did attend. Attendees said he spoke for about 20-25 minutes, took no questions and left by helicopter (CNBC via newsio). Justin Sun, the top holder, attended (The Block, Cointelegraph).
2. **The White House defended the dinner and said it complied with conflict-of-interest laws: confirmed.** Karoline Leavitt said this at the briefing on **Friday, May 9, 2025**. The question came from Andrew Egger of The Bulwark, who had the "new media" seat (Mediaite, PBS, The Bulwark). The first two sentences in the video match the reported quote: "Look, the president is abiding by all conflict of interest laws. The president has been incredibly transparent with his own personal financial obligations throughout the years." Some outlets record her as saying "all applicable conflict of interest laws." At other briefings she said Trump was attending "in his personal time" and that "it is not a White House dinner." I found no written report with the exact words "successful businessman ... reelected him." The video plays it as a continuous clip, so it is probably the same answer, but I could not confirm that part word for word.

### Corrections / precision
- The video does not say who asked the question. It was The Bulwark's Andrew Egger, on May 9, 2025, about two weeks before the dinner.
- "Top holders" means the top 220 by time-weighted average holdings, plus a top-25 VIP tier. It was not a dinner for all holders.
- "The White House denies is corrupt": Leavitt did not use the word "corrupt." She said Trump abides by conflict-of-interest laws and is attending in his personal time. Her answer is a denial in substance.

### Denials and context
- The White House said the dinner was a private event on Trump's personal time, not a White House function, and that he obeys all conflict-of-interest laws. It would not commit to releasing the guest list. It has also said Trump's assets sit in a trust. The trust is family-managed and revocable, not an independent blind trust.
- Critics included Sen. Elizabeth Warren ("an orgy of corruption"), Sen. Richard Blumenthal ("auctioning off access") and other Democrats. They pointed to the anonymous and largely foreign wallets on the leaderboard. CNBC and Bloomberg-cited analyses found that most of the top wallets had used non-US exchanges. Estimates of what the top 220 spent run from about $140-148M to as much as $394M, depending on method.
- About 80% of the 1 billion token supply belongs to Trump-linked entities: CIC Digital LLC (owned by Trump's revocable trust) and Fight Fight Fight LLC. Trading fees and token value therefore benefit the Trump side. That is why critics call the dinner pay-for-access. Whether it broke any law has not been decided. I found no legal or ethics-office finding either way.
