# C46: The alleged Kushner Gulf accounts: Azerbaijan route, 'Beta' account, Gabbard complaint

**Verdict:** Unverified (confidence: medium)

## Summary

The lead accurately describes what Matthew Cole's book 'Princes and Thieves' (Vanity Fair excerpt, 22 Oct 2026) alleges, but the account and money claims, the ~$750M 'Alpha' account at First Abu Dhabi Bank, the routing via Azerbaijan and the ~$1B 'Beta' Bank of Israel account, rest on Cole's anonymous sources alone, with no documents published and no independent corroboration. The Beta account is actively disputed by Axios's Barak Ravid, a Jerusalem Post column, the Jerusalem Post's removal of its write-up and David Friedman's denial. The Gabbard whistleblower complaint about an NSA intercept of foreigners discussing Kushner is real but separate: its contents are classified and described as uncorroborated 'gossip', and the IG found it not credible or could not assess it. Kushner's legal threats are confirmed, but no lawsuit had been reported by mid-Nov 2026.

## Corrections

- The brief calls it an FAB account 'with Kushner as signatory'. Per the book, the holder is the Watani Al Emarat Foundation; Kushner is a signatory, not the owner.
- The Jerusalem Post retraction was of a news write-up; a JPost opinion column critiquing the excerpt remains published.
- The Gabbard complaint (filed May 2025, reported Feb 2026) predates the book and is not linked by any outlet to the alleged accounts; Ynet's 'Iran-linked' label is a characterization, since the WSJ could not identify the speakers.
- Headline totals vary ($700M+, $1.75B, $2B); the book's own phrase is 'at least $2 billion' across 'at least two' accounts.

## Denials and context

- Kushner, Mizelle, the White House (Anna Kelly) and Trump deny that any accounts existed; Kushner's team notes the absence of bank statements, wires, account numbers or dates.
- David Friedman denies knowledge of UAE money for Kushner and denies relaying messages.
- Cole stands by the reporting (50+ sources, fact-checked, legally vetted).
- Skepticism also comes from outside Kushner's camp: Barak Ravid (Axios), a Jerusalem Post column and the JPost newsroom.
- On the Gabbard complaint, ODNI says the delay reflected a classified legal review; Gabbard resigned on 22 May 2026 (effective 30 Jun), citing her husband's health.

## Full report

**Verdict: unverified** (confidence: medium)

The lead correctly describes what the sources allege. The allegations themselves are not substantiated. Every account and money claim (the roughly $750M "Alpha" account at First Abu Dhabi Bank, the routing via Azerbaijan, and the roughly $1B "Beta" account at the Bank of Israel) traces back to one source: Matthew Cole's book *Princes and Thieves*, excerpted by Vanity Fair on 22 Oct 2026. Cole relies on anonymous sources and has published no bank records. No outlet has independently corroborated any of it.

The most specific detail, the Bank of Israel "Beta" account, has drawn the most rebuttal:

- Axios's Barak Ravid publicly questioned it.

The Gabbard whistleblower complaint is real and well documented, but it is a **separate** story. ### Sub-claims

1. **Cole's book and the Vanity Fair excerpt make these allegations. Confirmed, as a fact of publication.**
   - Vanity Fair ran the excerpt on 22 Oct 2026.
   - The book's full title, as listed by the publisher: *Princes and Thieves: Jared Kushner and Mohammed bin Zayed's Secret Plot to Remake the Middle East*. It is published by Atria/One Signal (Simon & Schuster).
   - Per Political Wire, citing Status, Simon & Schuster released the book two days early, on 26 Oct, after Kushner's legal threats.
   - Cole told Democracy Now! (2 Nov) that the book "is a result of more than 50 sources" and was fact-checked and legally vetted. That quote comes via search summaries; the fetch was blocked.

2. **"Alpha": a ~$750M First Abu Dhabi Bank account with Kushner as signatory. Unverified, single source.** As reported from the excerpt:
   - The account was opened in Dubai at First Abu Dhabi Bank (FAB) from 2017.
   - It was held by the Watani Al Emarat Foundation, a UAE government-funded charity. Kushner is described as a **signatory, not owner**.
   - It was funded with Saudi and Emirati money and run as a "foreign policy fund" partly managed by Kushner and Mohamed bin Zayed.
   - Some coverage says the book has Tahnoon bin Zayed, FAB's chairman, overseeing it.

   No bank statement, account number, wire record or transaction date has been published. Kushner's team stresses this point. No statement from FAB, the foundation or the UAE government turned up in search. Asia Times reports that four former senior Trump officials said they knew of no presidential authorization that would have allowed Kushner access to a foreign government's account. That comes from a search summary and was not fetched.

3. **The money was routed via Azerbaijan. Unverified, single source.** The book has Saudi and UAE money moving through an Azerbaijani bank, and MBZ's roughly $1B reaching Israel "through Azerbaijan" by spring 2018. No outlet has identified the bank, the dates or the amounts. JAMnews (Azerbaijan/Caucasus) recalled the earlier OCCRP "Azerbaijani Laundromat" (about $1.4B through Baktelekom / International Bank of Azerbaijan) but said **no confirmed link** exists between that scheme and the alleged Kushner accounts. No Azerbaijani government response was found.

4. **"Beta": a ~$1B transfer to an Israeli government (Bank of Israel) account. Unverified, and actively disputed.** As reported by Ynet, i24 and Common Dreams from the book:
   - The account was set up "for Kushner's personal benefit," according to one source Cole cites.
   - Netanyahu encouraged placing the money at the Bank of Israel so it would look like a government account.
   - Netanyahu wanted to be told before transfers, with messages relayed through then-Ambassador David Friedman.

   The rebuttals:
   - **Barak Ravid (Axios), on X:** "There are many things in this book excerpt ... that don't add up, but two are extremely questionable." He doubts such an account "is even technically possible" at the Bank of Israel and doubts that Netanyahu could be a signatory.
   - **Jerusalem Post opinion column:** the Bank of Israel holds accounts only for the government, banks, certain financial entities and foreign central banks.    - **Jerusalem Post news report:** the paper pulled its write-up of the excerpt, citing "significant factual questions" (reported by Mediaite). Chad Mizelle praised the removal.
   - **David Friedman:** denied any knowledge of or involvement in UAE money for Kushner, and denied passing messages for him (via search summary of Ynet/i24 coverage).
   - No response from the Israeli Prime Minister's Office or the Bank of Israel was found.

   - The complaint was filed with the Intelligence Community Inspector General on 21 May 2025 by a whistleblower represented by Andrew Bakaj.

   - The complaint reached Congress, heavily redacted, about eight to nine months after filing (Feb 2026).
   - Gabbard called the claim that she hid it "a blatant lie." Her office says she acted "fully within her legal and statutory authority."

### Corrections and caveats

- The brief says "First Abu Dhabi Bank account with Kushner as signatory." In the book's account, the account holder is the Watani Al Emarat Foundation, and Kushner is a signatory, not the owner.
- The "Beta" account is described as at the Bank of Israel and "for Kushner's personal benefit." That is a single anonymous source quoted by Cole, and it is the most heavily disputed element.
- The Jerusalem Post retraction was of a **news write-up** of the excerpt. A JPost **opinion** column critiquing the excerpt remains published.
- The Gabbard complaint was filed in May 2025, before the book. Nothing in the public record links it to the alleged accounts, and its substance is unknown.

### Denials and other side

- Kushner, Mizelle, the White House and Trump deny that any accounts existed. Friedman denies his alleged role.
- Cole stands by the book, citing more than 50 sources, fact-checking and legal vetting.
- Skeptics who are not part of Kushner's camp (Ravid, the JPost column, the JPost newsroom) question specific mechanics.
