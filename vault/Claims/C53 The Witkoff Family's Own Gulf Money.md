# C53: The Witkoff Family's Own Gulf Money

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Type:** Check · Gulf Money

## The Claim

> Beyond World Liberty, does the Witkoff family have financial ties to Qatari, Emirati and Kuwaiti funds while Steve Witkoff serves as special envoy?

## Bottom Line

The core of the claim is documented. Qatar's sovereign fund (QIA) bought the Park Lane Hotel for about $623M in August 2023.

## What Holds Up

- Qatar's sovereign fund bought the Witkoff Group's Park Lane hotel stake in 2023 (mostly accurate)
- Steve Witkoff sold a stake in his firm for about $120M, with no buyer named.
- Witkoff's ethics agreement and disclosures leave the Gulf conflicts unresolved (mostly accurate)

## What Needs Correcting

- Alex and Zach Witkoff sought billions from Qatar, UAE and Kuwait sovereign funds for real-estate vehicles in 2025 (partly accurate)
- The claim says Qatar bought 'the Witkoff Group's Park Lane hotel stake'. In fact QIA bought the whole hotel (about $623M, Aug 2023) from a Witkoff–Mubadala JV. Witkoff's share is undisclosed, and Qatar says the hotel was not owned by Witkoff or his companies.
- The claim dates the sons' fundraising to 2025. QIA dates Alex's pitch to 2024, before Witkoff took office. The NYT reported it in Sept 2025, and QIA declined, so the fund was shelved.
- The claim names 'Alex/Zach'. Only Alex's approach to QIA is confirmed by the counterparty. Zach's role and the UAE and Kuwait approaches rest on NYT sourcing.
- The NYT's '$100M from an Apollo-run trust partly owned by Qatar' was corrected. Per the correction relayed by the Jerusalem Post, the lender on the Belgrove loan was Apollo Global Management, not the Qatar-invested subsidiary. No Qatari money in that loan is documented (see C73).
- 2026-11-17 (holdings audit, C104/C105): Claimed: his disclosures 'went uncertified'. The record shows: the White House DAEO certified the new-entrant report on 12 Dec 2025 (revised to add the State post, Jan-Jun 2025); the annual report signed 29 Jul 2026 shows no reviewer signature as published.
- 2026-11-17 (holdings audit, C104/C105): The Warren-Murphy letter is dated 17 Nov 2025 (press releases 18 Nov). The Meeks-Amo-Garcia release names the acting inspectors general of State and Commerce only, not OGE. On 12 Dec 2025 State's OIG referred the Warren-Slotkin request to its Office of Investigations; no result has been reported.
- 2026-11-17 (holdings audit, C104/C105): The $120M sale was disclosed in September 2025 but took place between January and 13 August 2025 (date undisclosed).

## What Is Unproven

- The family's Gulf money overlaps with Witkoff's Gaza, Qatar, Iran and Russia negotiations, including the Qatar jet and the Doha strike aftermath.

## In More Detail

- Alex Witkoff pitched QIA a planned $4B real-estate credit fund, and per the NYT the sons also approached UAE and Kuwaiti funds.
- Steve Witkoff disclosed a $120M sale of a Witkoff Group stake (made between January and 13 August 2025) with no buyer, date or percentage named.
- His new-entrant disclosure was certified only on 12 Dec 2025, after senators complained, and his later annual report is uncertified as published; Congress pressed for investigations, and State's inspector general referred one request to its Office of Investigations.
- Corrections: the Park Lane seller was a Witkoff–Mubadala joint venture, and Qatar says the hotel was not Witkoff's.
- The QIA pitch dates to 2024, not 2025, and QIA declined, so the fund was shelved.
- The NYT's separate claim of $100M from a Qatar-part-owned Apollo trust was corrected: the lender was Apollo itself.
- No source shows the family's business influenced Gaza, Qatar, Iran or Russia diplomacy.

## Sub-Claims

### 1. Qatar's sovereign fund bought the Witkoff Group's Park Lane hotel stake in 2023.

**Mostly accurate**

- In August 2023 QIA bought the Park Lane Hotel at 36 Central Park South for about $623M ($622.9M) in an entity-level deal, with $400M of financing from Athene, Apollo's annuity arm.
- The seller was a joint venture of Witkoff and Abu Dhabi's Mubadala.
- Mubadala had bought Jho Low's roughly 38% stake from the DOJ for $139.6M in 2018, and the 2013 purchase price was about $654M. Witkoff's share at the sale was not disclosed.
- Qatar's International Media Office says the hotel was not owned by Witkoff or his companies, and Witkoff says he partnered with Mubadala, never with QIA.
- Amounts: about $623M; $622.9M; $400M; roughly 38%; $139.6M
- Dates: August 2023
- Sources: [1](https://bisnow.com/new-york/news/hotel/qatari-fund-pays-623m-to-acquire-park-lane-hotel-120410) [2](https://www.pincusco.com/qatar-fund-acquires-park-lane-hotel-for-622-9m-in-midtown-west) [3](https://crenews.com/2023/08/28/qatar-fund-pays-623mln-for-park-lane-hotel-in-manhattan-athene-lends-400mln/) [4](https://commercialobserver.com/2023/08/qatar-sovereign-fund-acquires-park-lane-hotel-for-623m/) [5](https://therealdeal.com/2017/05/23/witkoff-and-mubadala-to-split-proceeds-from-park-lane-hotel-sale) [11](https://thepeninsulaqatar.com/article/27/09/2025/international-media-office-of-the-state-of-qatar-issues-statement-in-response-to-false-accusations-in-the-new-york-times)

### 2. Alex and Zach Witkoff sought billions from Qatar, UAE and Kuwait sovereign funds for real-estate vehicles in 2025.

**Partly accurate**

- The NYT (2025-09-26, Kamin and Hope) reported that the sons sought billions from sovereign funds in Qatar, the UAE and Kuwait.
- The vehicle was a proposed $4B 'Special Situations Real Estate Credit Fund' expected to earn about $80M a year in fees.
- QIA spokeswoman Melanie Dunn confirmed that Alex pitched QIA, in 2024, and said QIA declined.
- The UAE and Kuwait approaches rest on the NYT alone.
- The Witkoff Group called the plans preliminary and shelved.
- The NYT originally reported that the Witkoff Group received $100M (as recently as May 2025) from an Apollo-run trust partly owned by Qatar.
- That link has since been corrected: per a correction relayed by the Jerusalem Post, the lender on the Belgrove property loan was Apollo Global Management itself, not the subsidiary in which Qatar had invested.
- The family's lawyer (Parlatore) said Athene, Apollo's insurance arm, made the loan and has no QIA ownership or control.
- QIA told the NYT it was not invested in the Brook or Belgrove properties, and Apollo says its investors do not direct its investments.
- The Witkoff family and the Witkoff Group threatened the NYT with a defamation suit over the article.
- No filed suit was found. See C73.
- Amounts: $4B; about $80M; $100M
- Dates: 2025-09-26; May 2025
- Sources: [6](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/?p=3827497) [7](https://www.ynetnews.com/article/bkukver2ll) [9](https://www.jpost.com/american-politics/article-869981) [8](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/witkoff-family-threatens-nyt-defamation-212437336.html) [23](https://accountable.us/2025-09-22-research-on-steve-witkoff-special-government-employee-status) [8](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/witkoff-family-threatens-nyt-defamation-212437336.html) [24](https://dailycaller.com/2025/10/04/the-times-reporting-on-trumps-circle-draws-accusations-of-bias)

### 3. Steve Witkoff sold a stake in his firm for about $120M, with no buyer named.

**Confirmed**

- Bloomberg reported on 2025-09-15/16, from his OGE disclosure, that he sold a Witkoff Group stake for $120M. The filing gave no buyer, date or percentage; because the same line appears in his new-entrant report (period to 13 Aug 2025) and his annual report for calendar 2025, the sale took place between January and 13 August 2025.
- A spokesperson says he remains a minority shareholder with no operating role since 2024, and Alex runs the firm.
- Witkoff first said in March 2025 that he was divesting.
- No buyer identity surfaced through Nov 2026.
- Amounts: $120M
- Dates: 2025-09-15; 13 Aug 2025; 13 August 2025; March 2025; Nov 2026
- Sources: [13](https://therealdeal.com/national/2025/09/16/steve-witkoff-sells-stake-in-real-estate-firm-for-120m) [14](https://www.bisnow.com/national/news/commercial-real-estate/steve-witkoff-sells-120m-stake-in-his-real-estate-firm-130993) [15](https://dfnihf8fcqrzv.cloudfront.net/forbes/giacomo-tognini/how-peace-envoy-steve-witkoff-got-richer-thanks-to-trump-and-elon-musk/188276197) [12](https://therealdeal.com/national/2025/03/13/steve-witkoff-divesting-from-eponymous-real-estate-firm)

### 4. Witkoff's ethics agreement and disclosures leave the Gulf conflicts unresolved.

**Mostly accurate**

- No published ethics agreement surfaced.
- Warren and Murphy (letter dated 2025-11-17; press releases 18 Nov) said no ethics official had signed his disclosure (the White House DAEO, Scott F. Gast, certified it on 12 Dec 2025, in a version that adds his State Department post), and asked about divestment and waivers.
- Schiff and seven other senators (2025-10-22) pressed him on his WLF assets.
- Meeks, Amo and Garcia (2026-01-08) asked the acting inspectors general of State and Commerce to investigate him over the UAE AI-chip deal overlapping WLF's Emirati deal; their release names only the two IGs, not OGE.
- On 12 Dec 2025 State's OIG told Warren and Slotkin it had 'referred your request to our Office of Investigations for review'; no result has been reported and no authority has made a finding.
- The WLF income and divestment questions are covered in C14 and C42.
- Dates: 2025-11-17; 18 Nov; 12 Dec 2025; 2025-10-22; 2026-01-08
- Sources: [16](https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-murphy-investigate-ethics-conflicts-related-to-trumps-envoy-to-the-middle-east) [17](https://fortune.com/img-assets/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025.10.22-Sen.-Schiff-Colleagues-Letter-to-Witkoff-on-WLF-Assets.pdf) [18](https://democrats-foreignaffairs.house.gov/2026/1/meeks-amo-garcia-demand-investigation-into-trump-despot-whisperer-witkoff-s-sketchy-business-dealings) [25](https://www.warren.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/warren_murphy_letter_to_white_house_oge_on_witkoff_financial_disclosures.pdf) [26](https://www.banking.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2025_09_23_sens_slotkin_and_warren_requesting_oig_investigation_into_uae_chips_corruption_response1.pdf)

### 5. The family's Gulf money overlaps with Witkoff's Gaza, Qatar, Iran and Russia negotiations, including the Qatar jet and the Doha strike aftermath.

**Unverified**

- The overlap in time is real.
- He led the January and October 2025 Gaza ceasefire talks with Qatar as mediator, and the NYT story ran in between.
- On the 2025-09-09 Israeli strike on Doha, the White House said Witkoff warned Qatar in advance, while Qatar said the call came about 10 minutes after the strike began.
- No source shows any business tie influenced policy.
- Qatar says investment is kept separate from diplomacy.
- On Russia, Byline Times reports an undisclosed real-estate tie to Leonard Blavatnik, and Witkoff's spokeswoman denies any financial stake in the Russian asset talks.
- Dates: October 2025; 2025-09-09
- Sources: [19](https://app.al-monitor.com/originals/2025/09/trump-directed-top-aide-warn-qatar-israel-attack-was-coming-white-house-says) [20](https://www.newarab.com/news/qatar-says-us-warning-came-after-israeli-strike-started) [10](https://www.gulf-times.com/article/711590/qatar/qatar-debunks-nyt-claims-about-witkoff) [22](https://bylinetimes.com/2025/12/01/trump-envoy-has-financial-ties-with-former-adviser-to-putins-money-man-now-leading-kremlin-peace-talks/)

## Denials & Context

- Qatar's International Media Office (2025-09-27) called the NYT's claim that Qatar cultivated Witkoff false and 'absurd', and said Qatari entities had dealt with him for years before Trump's election.
- The Witkoff family's lawyer, Timothy Parlatore, threatened the NYT with a defamation suit on 2025-10-02 and demanded a retraction. No suit was found.
  - The NYT corrected a lender name on a separate (Belgrove) loan.
- Apollo says its investors do not direct its investments.
  - The White House counsel says Witkoff takes ethics compliance seriously.
- Against the family: his new-entrant disclosure was certified only in December 2025 and his annual report is uncertified as published, the $120M buyer is unnamed, and his son runs a firm that takes Qatar-linked capital.
  - For the family: the QIA pitch predates his appointment and was rejected, and the Park Lane sale predates his government role.
- The Witkoff family and Witkoff Group threatened the NYT with a defamation suit over the 26 Sep 2025 article and demanded corrections.
  - QIA denies investing in the Brook or Belgrove, and the White House called the story a 'bogus smear'.

## Full Report

**Verdict: mostly-accurate (confidence: medium)**

This check builds on C14, C42 and C16. The main elements are documented:

- Qatar's sovereign fund bought the Park Lane Hotel in 2023.
- The Witkoff sons pitched a multibillion-dollar real-estate fund to Gulf sovereign funds.
- Steve Witkoff disclosed a $120M stake sale with no buyer named. The sale took place between January and 13 August 2025.
- His new-entrant disclosure was certified only on 12 December 2025, after senators complained. His later annual report is uncertified as published. Congress challenged both.

Several details need correcting:

- **Park Lane counterparty.** The 2023 seller was a Witkoff–Mubadala joint venture, not a clean "Witkoff Group stake." Qatar says the hotel was not owned by Witkoff or his companies at the time of the sale.
- **Timing of the fund pitch.** QIA says the pitch dates to 2024, before Witkoff became envoy. It was not a 2025 event.
- **Who did the pitching.** Only the QIA approach is confirmed by the counterparty. The UAE and Kuwait approaches rest on the NYT's unnamed sourcing.
- **Outcome.** QIA declined and the fund was shelved.
- **The $100M 'Qatar trust' money.** It was corrected: the lender was Apollo Global Management itself, not its Qatar-part-owned trust.
- **Timing versus negotiations.** No source shows the family's business influenced any negotiation. The links to the Doha strike, the jet and Iran are timing-based insinuation.

Note on method: the original pages at ynetnews.com and en.wikipedia.org were not read directly. The findings below rest on search-result excerpts from multiple outlets: Bisnow, The Real Deal, CRE News, Commercial Observer, PincusCo, Gulf Times, The Peninsula, JPost, Yahoo, Washington Examiner, Forbes, AP via regional outlets, and congressional press releases. The NYT article (Sept 26, 2025, by Debra Kamin and Bradley Hope) was read only through secondary coverage.

### Sub-claims

#### 1. Qatar's sovereign fund bought the Witkoff Group's Park Lane hotel stake in 2023: mostly-accurate
- In August 2023, the Qatar Investment Authority (QIA) bought the Park Lane Hotel at 36 Central Park South for about $623M ($622.9M per PincusCo). PincusCo described it as an entity-level exchange.
- Athene, Apollo's annuity arm, lent $400M toward the purchase.
- The seller was a joint venture of Witkoff and Abu Dhabi's Mubadala, per Bisnow. Witkoff and Jho Low had bought the hotel in 2013 for about $654M. After the 1MDB forfeiture, Mubadala bought Low's roughly 38% stake from the DOJ for $139.6M in December 2018.
- PincusCo and Bisnow said it was unclear how much of the property Witkoff still held at the sale, or whether he kept any stake afterwards.
- The 2023 price was below the 2013 price, and an attempt to sell for $1B around 2017 failed. Secondary accounts of the NYT story say the sale let Witkoff exit a long-stuck asset without a large loss.
- Qatar's International Media Office (Sept 27, 2025) said "the hotel in question was not owned by Witkoff or his companies." Witkoff has said he was partnered with Mubadala, "never" with QIA. One account says QIA had foreclosed on a mezzanine position.
- **Gulf money touches both sides of this deal:** an Abu Dhabi fund was co-owner, and Qatar's fund was the buyer.

#### 2. Alex and Zach Witkoff sought billions from Qatar, UAE and Kuwait sovereign funds: partly-accurate
- The NYT (Sept 26, 2025) reported that the sons worked to raise billions from sovereign funds in Qatar, the UAE and Kuwait. The vehicle was a proposed $4B "Special Situations Real Estate Credit Fund" targeting US regions short of affordable housing.
- It was projected to earn about $80M a year in management fees.
- QIA spokeswoman Melanie Dunn confirmed that Alex Witkoff pitched QIA, and said QIA declined after internal discussions.
- Reporting dates the QIA pitch to **2024**, before Steve Witkoff took office. It was not a 2025 event.
- No UAE or Kuwaiti response surfaced, and those approaches are attributed to the NYT alone.
- Witkoff Group spokeswoman Anna LaPorte called the plans "preliminary" and said they were shelved. The family says talks never went beyond preliminary discussions.
- The NYT originally reported that the Witkoff Group received $100M (as recently as May 2025) from an Apollo-run trust partly owned by Qatar. **Corrected:** a correction relayed by the Jerusalem Post says the lender on the Belgrove property loan was Apollo Global Management itself, not the subsidiary in which Qatar had invested. The family's lawyer said Athene made the loan, and QIA told the NYT it was not invested in the Brook or Belgrove. Apollo says its investors do not direct its investments. The family threatened the NYT with a defamation suit, and no filed suit was found (see C73).

#### 3. Steve Witkoff sold a stake in his firm for about $120M with no buyer named: confirmed
- Bloomberg reported on Sept 15–16, 2025, from his financial disclosure, that he sold a stake in the Witkoff Group for $120M. The Real Deal and Bisnow carried the report.
- Forbes noted the filing named no buyer, no sale date, and did not say how much of his stake was sold.
- A Witkoff Group spokesperson said he remains a minority shareholder with no operating role since 2024.
- Alex Witkoff runs the firm day to day. Witkoff told Bloomberg in March 2025 that he was divesting from the Witkoff Group and WLF.
- Whether the buyer is a family member, a family trust or an outside, possibly foreign, investor is **unverified**. No later reporting identifying the buyer surfaced through Nov 2026.

#### 4. Ethics agreement and disclosures: mostly-accurate (the gaps are documented)
- No published ethics agreement surfaced.
- Sens. Warren and Murphy (letter dated Nov 17, 2025; press releases Nov 18) said no ethics official had signed his disclosure. The White House ethics official certified it on Dec 12, 2025, in a version that adds his State Department post. They also said it "appears to be" unreliable. They asked the White House counsel and OGE whether he had fully divested or received waivers.
- Eight Democratic senators led by Schiff (Oct 22, 2025) pressed him on his remaining WLF assets.
- Reps. Meeks, Amo and Garcia (Jan 8, 2026) asked the acting State and Commerce inspectors general to investigate him (and David Sacks) over the UAE AI-chip deal overlapping WLF's Emirati deal. Their release names only the two inspectors general, not OGE.
- State's OIG told Warren and Slotkin on Dec 12, 2025 that it had "referred your request to our Office of Investigations for review". No result has been reported, and no authority has made a finding.
- The WLF side (about $107M of 2025 distributions from Witkoff Holdings LLC, the holding company that owns his WLF interest, the Aryam stake, and divestment claims) is covered in C14 and C42 and is not repeated here.

#### 5. Timing versus Gaza, Qatar, Iran and Russia negotiations: unverified as to influence; overlap confirmed
- Witkoff was the lead negotiator on the January 2025 and October 2025 Gaza ceasefires, both with Qatar as mediator. The NYT story ran during the run-up to the October deal.
- On the Sept 9, 2025 Israeli strike on Doha, the White House said Trump told Witkoff to warn Qatar beforehand. Qatar said the call came about 10 minutes after the strike began.
- The Qatar jet is covered in C16.
- No source links the family's business to any of these decisions. Qatar said "investment decisions are kept entirely separate from diplomacy" and called the cultivation narrative "absurd." Witkoff's lawyer, Timothy Parlatore, threatened the NYT with a defamation suit on Oct 2, 2025. No suit was found.
- Russia: reporting covers Witkoff's undisclosed real-estate tie to Leonard Blavatnik and the NYT's report on Russian asset and Lukoil deal talks. Witkoff's spokeswoman says he has "no financial stake." These are logged as leads.

### Corrections
- The claim says Qatar bought "the Witkoff Group's Park Lane hotel stake." In the record, QIA bought the whole hotel (about $623M, Aug 2023) from a Witkoff–Mubadala JV. Witkoff's share is undisclosed, and Qatar disputes that Witkoff owned it.
- The claim puts the sons' fundraising in 2025. QIA dates Alex's pitch to 2024, and the NYT reported it in Sept 2025. QIA declined and the fund was shelved.
- The claim names "Alex/Zach." QIA confirms only Alex's approach. Zach's role and the UAE and Kuwait approaches rest on the NYT alone.

### Denials and context
- Qatar's International Media Office called the cultivation claims false and "absurd," and said Qatari entities have dealt with Witkoff for years, long before Trump's election.
- The Witkoff family, through Parlatore, called the NYT story a "baseless narrative" and demanded a correction. The NYT corrected the name of a lender on a separate (Belgrove) loan.
- The White House counsel said Witkoff "takes seriously his compliance with the government ethics rules."
- Context cutting against the family: the envoy's new-entrant disclosure was certified only in December 2025 and his annual report is uncertified as published, the $120M buyer is unnamed, and his son runs a firm that takes Qatar-linked capital.
- Context cutting the other way: the QIA pitch predates his appointment and was rejected, and the Park Lane sale predates his government role by over a year.
