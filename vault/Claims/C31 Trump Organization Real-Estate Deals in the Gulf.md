# C31: Trump Organization Real-Estate Deals in the Gulf

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Type:** Check · Gulf Money

## The Claim

> Since the 2024 election, has the Trump Organization signed branded tower, hotel and golf licensing deals across the Gulf, largely through Saudi-linked developer Dar Global, and what are they worth to the family?

## Bottom Line

The Trump Organization does have a large and growing set of Trump-branded tower, hotel and golf licensing deals in the Gulf, mostly with Dar Global, the London-listed international arm of Saudi developer Dar Al Arkan.

## What Holds Up

- Since the 2024 election the Trump Organization has signed Trump-branded tower/hotel/golf licensing deals across the Gulf (mostly accurate)
- The deals span Dubai, Saudi Arabia (Jeddah/Riyadh), Oman, Qatar and possibly others (e.g., Abu Dhabi) (mostly accurate)
- The deals are largely via Saudi-linked developer Dar Global.
- Project values.
- Fees paid to the Trumps (mostly accurate)

## What Needs Correcting

- State-linked counterparties are involved (partly accurate)
- These deals come despite a pledge of no new foreign deals (misleading)
- The claim says the deals came 'since the 2024 election'. In fact Oman (March 2022), Trump Tower Jeddah (July 2024) and the first Trump Tower Dubai announcement (July 2024) predate it. Post-election deals are Riyadh/Diriyah, the Dubai formal launch, Qatar and Trump Plaza Jeddah.
- The claim says 'despite a pledge of no new foreign deals'. That pledge applied to the first term only. The January 2025 policy allows private foreign deals and bars only direct deals with foreign governments.
- Claimed: 'a brand new hotel going up in Dubai or Abu Dhabi'. It is Dubai (Trump International Hotel & Tower, Sheikh Zayed Road). No Abu Dhabi project has been announced; one was only talked about.
- Qatar's '$5.5 billion' is the value of the whole Simaisma masterplan, not the Trump golf/villa component.
- 16 Nov 2026: Claimed: about $21.9M from Dar Al Arkan in 2025, 'nearly identical' to 2024, and a Damac figure of about $12.5M that is only an estimate. The record shows: $21.9M was the 2024 total. For 2025 the three Dar Al Arkan lines sum to $20.55M, and with the $5.25M Doha fee from Dar Global Holdings the Dar-linked total is $25.80M. Damac's four fee lines are exact and sum to $12,589,884. The Gulf total is $38.39M.
- 16 Nov 2026: Claimed: no Abu Dhabi deal exists. The record shows: no Dar Global or launched Abu Dhabi project, but two Abu Dhabi licence entities each received $5,000,000 from Damac subsidiaries in 2025 (OGE filing).

## In More Detail

- They are in Oman, Jeddah (two), Dubai, Qatar and Riyadh/Diriyah, and the licensing income runs to tens of millions of dollars a year.
- The claim overreaches on timing: Oman (2022), Trump Tower Jeddah (July 2024) and the first Dubai announcement (July 2024) came before the 2024 election.
- It also blurs the pledge.
- The 'no new foreign deals' promise belonged to the first term only.
- The January 2025 second-term policy bars deals with foreign governments but explicitly allows deals with private foreign companies.
- No Abu Dhabi project has been announced, though two Damac-licensed Abu Dhabi entities received $5M each in 2025.
- The Riyadh golf project sits inside the PIF-owned Diriyah Company's giga-project master plan, though the licence counterparty is Dar Global.

## Sub-Claims

### 1. Since the 2024 election the Trump Organization has signed Trump-branded tower/hotel/golf licensing deals across the Gulf.

**Mostly accurate**

- Deals announced or formally launched after November 2024: two Riyadh projects with Dar Global, first announced December 2024 and launched 11 Jan 2026 as a Trump National Golf Course, Trump International Hotel and ~500 residences in Diriyah.
- The formal launch of the 80-storey, ~350 m, ~$1B Trump International Hotel & Tower Dubai on 29 Apr 2025.
- The Trump International Golf Club and Trump Villas at Simaisma, Qatar, signed 30 Apr 2025 (see C16).
- Trump Plaza Jeddah, ~$1B, announced 29 Sep 2025.
- Earlier deals predate the election: Trump International Golf Club/Resort Oman at Aida, Muscat (~$500M, first signed March 2022), and Trump Tower Jeddah (~SAR 2B/$530M, announced 1-2 July 2024).
- The Dubai tower was also first announced in July 2024, as Dar Global's 'third collaboration' with the Trump Organization.
- So the pattern is real, but it began in 2022 rather than after the 2024 election.
- Amounts: ~$1B; ~$500M; $530M
- Dates: November 2024; December 2024; 11 Jan 2026; 29 Apr 2025; 30 Apr 2025
- Sources: [19](https://www.financemiddleeast.com/news/dar-global-the-trump-organization-partner-to-launch-two-new-projects-in-riyadh/) [20](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/dar-global-and-trump-organization-launch-10-billion-saudi-developments-ce7e58dad98ef32d) [12](https://meconstructionnews.com/63211/trump-international-hotel-tower-launched-by-dar-global-and-the-trump-organisation/amp) [11](https://www.prnewswire.com/ae/news-releases/dar-global-announces-third-collaboration-with-trump-organization-to-launch-iconic-trump-tower-dubai-in-2025-302200483.html) [8](https://www.meed.com/dar-global-launches-trump-tower-in-jeddah) [9](https://gulfconstructiononline.com/Article/387230/Dar_Global_unveils_$531m_Trump_Tower_in_Jeddah) [15](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2025/09/29/dar-global-plans-to-launch-1bn-trump-tower-in-jeddah/) [1](https://www.forbes.com/sites/giacomotognini/2025/05/17/the-saudi-real-estate-tycoon-whos-a-member-of-the-trump-family/)

### 2. The deals span Dubai, Saudi Arabia (Jeddah/Riyadh), Oman, Qatar and possibly others (e.g., Abu Dhabi).

**Mostly accurate**

- Confirmed in Dubai (UAE), Jeddah x2, Riyadh/Diriyah (Saudi Arabia), Muscat (Oman) and Simaisma (Qatar).
- The National's September 2026 overview counts seven projects in four countries.
- Abu Dhabi: no Dar Global deal and no launched project.
- In December 2024 Eric Trump said 'We'll probably be in Abu Dhabi in the next year or so', and Gulf News in 2025 reported talks were 'pretty serious'.
- The 2026 OGE filing does show two Abu Dhabi licence entities, DT Marks Abu Dhabi LLC and DT Marks Al Raha Beach LLC, each paid $5,000,000 in 2025 by Damac subsidiaries (see C88), so licence agreements exist even though no project has been announced.
- The 'hotel going up in Dubai or Abu Dhabi' that is sometimes mentioned is the Dubai tower.
- A separate, older Dubai golf club with Damac also earns the Trumps licensing fees.
- Amounts: $5,000,000
- Dates: September 2026; December 2024
- Sources: [6](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/property/2026/09/03/where-is-trumps-property-empire-expanding-in-the-gulf/) [16](https://www.semafor.com/article/12/16/2024/trump-organization-eyes-gulf-expansion) [17](https://thearabweekly.com/trump-organisation-plans-new-projects-riyadh-abu-dhabi) [18](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/after-dubai-how-soon-will-a-trump-tower-come-to-abu-dhabi-1.500115945) <https://extapps2.oge.gov/201/Presiden.nsf/PAS+Index/69AEAA9D7455ACD585258E27002DDEE1/$FILE/Donald-J-Trump-2026-278ANNUAL.pdf>

### 3. The deals are largely via Saudi-linked developer Dar Global.

**Confirmed**

- Every Gulf project listed above is a Dar Global licensing deal.
- Dar Global is the London-listed international arm of Riyadh-based Dar Al Arkan, which Forbes profiled as controlled by the Saudi tycoon partnering with the Trump family.
- Qatar is the partial exception: Dar Global develops the Trump component, but the agreement is with Qatari Diar, the real-estate arm of Qatar's sovereign fund QIA.

### 5. Fees paid to the Trumps.

- Trump's 2025 financial disclosure (covering calendar 2024) showed $21.9M in licence fees from Dar Al Arkan licensees: $5,000,000 (Dubai), $15,882,352.94 (DT Marks KSA) and $1,005,145 (Oman), read from the filing, and more than $26-27M from the company since 2021-2023 (Forbes, CREW).

- Damac's four fee lines are exact amounts, not range estimates, and total $12,589,884 (see C88).
- Only asset values on the form are ranges.
- CREW projects foreign property income of more than $400M over the second term; that is a projection, not reported income.

### 6. State-linked counterparties are involved.

**Partly accurate**

- Documented for Qatar: Qatari Diar is owned by the QIA sovereign fund.
- Forbes also reports that state-owned companies previously owned the land in Oman and Qatar, which it says appears to conflict with Trump's first-term pledge.
- Dar Global and Dar Al Arkan themselves are private and listed companies, not state entities.
- Audit update: the Riyadh project (Trump International Golf Club, Wadi Safar, about 2.6M sq m) sits inside the Diriyah master plan developed by Diriyah Company, a wholly owned PIF subsidiary and one of PIF's giga-projects (about $63B).
- In Nov. 2025 the NYT reported the Trump Organization was in talks over joining the Saudi government-owned development, and Diriyah Company CEO Jerry Inzerillo said a deal was 'just a matter of time'.
- The licence itself is with Dar Global, which the company statement calls 'owner and developer of the property'; who holds title to the land parcel was not established.
- Amounts: about $63B
- Dates: Nov. 2025

### 7. These deals come despite a pledge of no new foreign deals.

**Misleading**

- The first-term (2017) pledge barred new foreign deals of any kind.
- The second-term ethics white paper, released 10 Jan 2025, deliberately dropped that.
- It bars deals directly with foreign governments but allows deals with private foreign companies, adds an outside ethics adviser, and bars Trump from day-to-day management.
- The Dar Global deals are therefore permitted under the current policy rather than a breach of it.
- The Qatar deal is the tension point, because its counterparty is owned by the QIA sovereign fund.
- Ethics experts called the 2025 agreement remarkably weak.
- Dates: 10 Jan 2025
- Sources: [23](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/2025/01/10/the-trump-company-is-not-banning-private-foreign-deals-a-break-with-its-first-term-policy/) [24](https://newser.com/story/362321/trump-organization-to-allow-deals-with-foreign-companies.html) [25](https://popular.info/p/trump-promised-no-new-business-deals) [26](https://www.timesofisrael.com/trumps-mideast-visit-comes-as-his-family-deepens-business-crypto-ties-in-region/)

## Denials & Context

- The Trump Organization says its 2025 ethics policy, with no deals with foreign governments and an outside ethics adviser, governs these deals.
  - It says Trump has no day-to-day role and that Eric Trump and Donald Trump Jr. run the business.
- Dar Global and Eric Trump present the deals as ordinary brand-licensing arrangements.
  - The Trumps license the name and management, and the developer finances and builds.
- Critics (CREW, ethics lawyers such as Kathleen Clark, Forbes) argue that the Qatar and Oman land links to state entities, and the timing alongside Gulf diplomacy (2025 Gulf trip, Saudi nuclear talks per Forbes July 2026), create conflicts of interest.
  - No regulator or court has found a violation.
  - Emoluments-style objections remain arguments, not findings.
- Many fee figures come from coded ranges on OGE forms, so per-project amounts are estimates.

## Full Report

**Verdict: mostly-accurate** (confidence: medium)

The pattern described in the claim is real and well documented. The Trump Organization has a string of Trump-branded tower, hotel and golf licensing deals in the Gulf, nearly all with Dar Global, the London-listed international arm of Saudi developer Dar Al Arkan. They are worth tens of millions of dollars a year in fees. The claim overreaches on two points:

- **Timing.** Several deals predate the 2024 election.
- **The pledge.** The "no new foreign deals" pledge was a first-term policy. The January 2025 second-term policy explicitly allows deals with private foreign companies.

No Abu Dhabi project has been announced, though two Damac-licensed Abu Dhabi entities received $5M each in 2025.

*Method note:* the original pages at thenationalnews.com, citizensforethics.org, forbes.com, pbs.org and wikipedia.org were not read directly. The research ended before I could check the Diriyah landowner and the Oman land question further. Findings rely on search-result summaries across several outlets.

### Sub-claims

1. **Deals signed "since the 2024 election."** Verdict: *mostly-accurate*.
   - Riyadh/Diriyah, the Dubai launch, Qatar and Trump Plaza Jeddah all fall after the election.
   - Oman (2022), Trump Tower Jeddah (July 2024) and the first Dubai announcement (July 2024) do not.
2. **Dubai, Jeddah/Riyadh, Oman, Qatar, possibly others.** Verdict: *mostly-accurate*.
   - All five locations are confirmed.
   - Abu Dhabi: no Dar Global deal and no launched project. Eric Trump said in December 2024 "We'll probably be in Abu Dhabi in the next year or so", and Gulf News reported "pretty serious" talks in 2025. The 2026 OGE filing does show two Abu Dhabi licence entities (DT Marks Abu Dhabi LLC and DT Marks Al Raha Beach LLC), each paid $5,000,000 by Damac subsidiaries in 2025 (see C88).
   - The "Dubai or Abu Dhabi" hotel in the claim is the Dubai tower.
3. **Largely via Dar Global.** Verdict: *confirmed*. Dar Global is the developer or co-developer on every Gulf project above.
4. **Values.** Verdict: *confirmed*, with a caveat. These are developer-stated headline figures, not money paid to the Trumps.
5. **Fees to the Trumps.** Verdict: *mostly-accurate*.
   - Trump's disclosure covering 2024 shows $21.9M from Dar Al Arkan licensees: $5,000,000 (Dubai), $15,882,352.94 (DT Marks KSA) and $1,005,145 (Oman), read from the filing (Forbes reported the same total, 16 Jun 2025). CREW and MPA put the total from the company since 2021-23 at more than $26-27M.
   - The disclosure covering 2025, read line by line, shows $38.39M of Gulf licence and management fees. Dar-linked fees are $25.80M: Dar Al Arkan licensees paid $10,361,458.68 (Dubai tower), $9,243,574.15 (Saudi Arabia) and $949,642 (Oman), and Dar Global Holdings paid $5,250,000 for Doha. CNBC reports the same $25.8M. The "$21.9M again" relayed by The National repeats the 2024 figure.
   - Damac's 2025 fee lines are exact, not estimates: $12,589,884 in total (see C88). Only asset values on the form are ranges.
   - CREW's figure of more than $400M over the second term is a projection.
6. **State-linked counterparties.** Verdict: *partly-accurate*.
   - Qatar: Qatari Diar is owned by the QIA sovereign fund.
   - Forbes reports that state-owned companies previously owned the land in Oman and Qatar.
   - Dar Global and Dar Al Arkan are private and listed companies, not state entities.
   - Audit update: the Riyadh project (Trump International Golf Club, Wadi Safar, about 2.6M sq m) lies inside the Diriyah master plan developed by Diriyah Company, a wholly owned PIF subsidiary and PIF giga-project (about $63B). The NYT reported in Nov 2025 that the Trump Organization was in talks over joining the Saudi government-owned development, and Diriyah Company CEO Jerry Inzerillo called a deal "just a matter of time". The licence is with Dar Global, described as owner and developer of the property. Who holds title to the specific parcel is still **unverified**.
7. **"Despite a pledge of no new foreign deals."** Verdict: *misleading*.
   - That pledge applied to 2017-2021.
   - The 10 Jan 2025 ethics white paper bars deals directly with foreign governments but allows private foreign deals. It also adds an outside ethics adviser and bars Trump from day-to-day management.
   - The Qatar deal, whose counterparty is owned by the QIA sovereign fund, is where the new rule is most strained.

### Corrections
- Not all the deals are post-election: Oman (2022), Trump Tower Jeddah (Jul 2024) and the first Dubai announcement (Jul 2024) predate it.
- The first-term pledge does not govern the second term. The current policy permits these deals.
- The "Dubai or Abu Dhabi" hotel in the claim is Dubai. No Abu Dhabi project has been announced, though Damac paid two $5M Abu Dhabi licence fees in 2025.
- Claimed: about $21.9M from Dar Al Arkan in 2025. The record shows: $21.9M was the 2024 total; the 2025 Dar-linked total is $25.80M including the $5.25M Doha fee. Damac's 2025 fees are an exact $12,589,884 (correction of 16 Nov 2026).
- The Qatar $5.5B is the masterplan value.

### Denials and other side
- The Trump Organization points to its 2025 ethics policy: no foreign-government deals, an outside ethics adviser, and Trump's sons running the business.
- Dar Global and Eric Trump describe the deals as standard brand licensing, in which the developer finances and builds.
- Critics (CREW, Forbes, ethics lawyer Kathleen Clark) cite the state land links and the timing against Gulf diplomacy, including Forbes' July 2026 report on the Saudi deal during nuclear talks.
- No regulator, court or congressional body has found a violation. The conflict-of-interest charge remains argument and insinuation.
