# C06: Secret $500M / 49% UAE stake in WLF

**Verdict:** Confirmed (confidence: medium)

## What the video said

> Per a Wall Street Journal investigation (reporter Sam Kessler), four days before the inauguration the Trump family secretly signed a $500M deal selling 49% of World Liberty Financial to an entity tied to Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed, UAE national security adviser and brother of the UAE president ('spy sheikh'); it was not disclosed.

## Summary

The WSJ reported on 2026-02-01 that Aryam Investment 1, an Abu Dhabi vehicle backed by UAE national security adviser Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed (brother of President Mohamed bin Zayed), agreed to pay $500M for 49% of World Liberty Financial. Eric Trump signed the deal about 2025-01-16, four days before the inauguration, and it was not publicly disclosed. The figures, timing, roles and secrecy in the video all match the reporting, as relayed by multiple outlets; the WSJ original could not be accessed directly. Trump says he knew nothing about it, and WLF and the White House deny wrongdoing.

## Denials and context

- Trump said in the Oval Office in early February 2026: 'I don't know about it ... My sons are handling that.' He later told CBS he knows 'nothing' about the firm.
- WLF and the White House deny wrongdoing. WLF says Trump and Steve Witkoff were not involved, that the deal gave no influence over US policy, and that it takes its ethical obligations seriously.
- Some outlets link the deal to the May 2025 US agreement allowing the UAE to buy up to 500,000 advanced AI chips a year, with a share for G42. Any causal connection is insinuation and unproven, and reports say the money did not come from G42.
- In August 2026 the WSJ reported that Tahnoon and co-investors also hold 49% of WLF's bank-charter holding company.

### What the video says (transcript lines 305-331)
- Justin Sun was the largest *public* investor, but there "turned out to be a bigger one": Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed, the UAE "spy sheikh". (The auto-transcription renders the name as "Tahoon".)
- WSJ reporter Sam Kessler says his team found that, four days before the inauguration, the president's family signed a deal with Tahnoon, who is the UAE's national security adviser and the brother of the UAE president.

### Evidence by sub-claim
1. **A WSJ investigation exists, and Sam Kessler worked on it.** Confirmed. The WSJ published it on 2026-02-01 under the headline "Abu Dhabi Royal Secretly Bought Stake in Trump Crypto Firm". Secondary reports credit Sam Kessler alongside Rebecca Ballhaus, Eliot Brown and Angus Berwick. Kessler saying "just last week" fits an interview recorded in early February 2026.
2. **$500M for 49%.** Confirmed as reported. The buyer was Aryam Investment 1, an Abu Dhabi vehicle backed by Tahnoon. It agreed to pay $500M for 49% of WLF. Half ($250M) was paid upfront. Of that half, about $187M went to Trump-family entities and at least $31M to entities linked to the Witkoff family. The WSJ said the second $250M was due by 2025-07-15 and that it could not trace where that money went.
3. **Signed four days before the inauguration.** Confirmed. Eric Trump signed the deal in January 2025. Several outlets give the date as 2025-01-16, four days before the 2025-01-20 inauguration.
4. **"An entity tied to" Tahnoon, who is national security adviser and brother of the president.** Confirmed. Tahnoon bin Zayed Al Nahyan is the UAE's national security adviser and a brother of President Mohamed bin Zayed. The "spy sheikh" nickname is widely used in the press. Two Aryam executives who also hold senior roles at G42 (Peng Xiao and Martin Edelman) joined WLF's five-member board.
5. **Bigger than Sun.** Confirmed. Sun's publicly announced purchase was $75M (2024-11), against $500M here.
6. **Kept secret / not disclosed.** Confirmed, with a nuance. The deal and the buyer's identity were never publicly disclosed before the WSJ story. Reports say WLF later revealed that the Trump family's stake had fallen sharply, but it did not say who had bought in. The WLF spokesperson acknowledged that the company had never named the outside party. Separately, Justin Sun's lawyers have asked a court to sanction WLF, alleging it delayed disclosing UAE ownership in their litigation. That is an allegation, not a finding.

### Context and the other side
- **Trump.** He told reporters in the Oval Office in early February 2026: "I don't know about it ... My sons are handling that — my family is handling it." In a later CBS interview he said he knows "nothing" about the firm.
- **WLF and the White House.** Both denied wrongdoing. WLF said Trump and Steve Witkoff were not involved in the deal, that it gave no one influence over US policy, and that the company "takes its ethical obligations extremely seriously".
- **Chips (not part of this claim).** Some outlets point out that in May 2025 the US agreed to let the UAE buy up to 500,000 advanced AI chips a year, with a share for Tahnoon's G42. That timing is insinuation and is not established as cause and effect. Reports also say the money did not come from G42.
- **Later developments.** In August 2026 the WSJ reported that Tahnoon and co-investors hold 49% of the holding company WLF set up to seek a bank charter.
