# C94: The Sale of Lukoil's Foreign Assets & the Boehly Consortium

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Type:** Connection check · Foreign Deals

## The Claim

> Who is bidding for sanctioned Lukoil's foreign assets, who is in the consortium led by Trump donor Todd Boehly, and what role does the US government play in the sale?

## Bottom Line

The bidder sequence is documented: Gunvor was killed by Treasury (Nov 2025), the Boehly/Allied bid fronted by Xtellus, a former VTB Capital unit, was rejected (Dec 2025), Carlyle's Jan 2026 deal lapsed, and a Boehly-led consortium now leads.

## What Holds Up

- Gunvor's ~$22B agreement to buy Lukoil International was killed by a public US Treasury rebuke and withdrawn.
- Boehly's first attempt came in Dec 2025, fronted by Xtellus Partners (a former VTB Capital US unit) with UAE-based Allied Investment Partners, and Treasury rejected it.
- Lukoil hired former Trump 2024 campaign senior adviser Bryan Lanza (Mercury Public Affairs) to deal with Treasury over the sale.
- Carlyle signed a deal for most of Lukoil's foreign assets in Jan 2026 and later dropped out.
- The leading bidder is a Boehly-led consortium of IHC, Allied Investment Partners, Al Hail Holding, the Al-Khayyat brothers and the US DFC, structured as an escrowed share swap (mostly accurate)
- Boehly is a Trump donor.
- Consortium partner Tahnoon/IHC is a business partner of the Trump family (WLF) and of Kushner (Affinity).
- The Al-Khayyat brothers are partners of Kushner and Ivanka Trump in the Sazan, Albania resort.
- OFAC licences: negotiations are authorised only under time-limited general licences, the latest running to 21 Nov 2026, and any closing needs a separate licence.
- Outcome through Nov 2026.

## What Needs Correcting

- C76 graded the Al-Khayyat-Kushner/Ivanka Albania partnership single-source. It is documented: the brothers confirmed it to the NYT, CBS and OCCRP cite filings, and SPAK froze the brothers' company's accounts.
- The claim lists Gunvor's withdrawn bid. That happened in Nov 2025, after a public Treasury rebuke, not during the 2026 bidding.
- Boehly's interest is not a product of the Sep 2026 Kremlin talks. Reuters reported him behind the rejected Xtellus swap bid in Dec 2025.
- Some relays cite a '13 December' Lukoil deadline. That was the 2025 deadline; the current one is 21 Nov 2026.
- C86 uses entity id al-khayyat-family; C76 and C94 use al-khayyat-brothers for the same people.

## What Is Unproven

- Putin raised the Lukoil sale with Witkoff and Kushner, and the two envoys helped negotiate the terms of the DFC's stake.
- Inferred: US envoy Tom Barrack presided over Al-Khayyat (UCC) deals in Syria, and the same family is in the Lukoil consortium.

## In More Detail

- The consortium's reported members are IHC, Allied Investment Partners, Al Hail, the Al-Khayyat brothers and the DFC, which together tie Gulf partners of the Trump family (WLF), Kushner (Affinity, Albania) and envoy Barrack (UCC) to a deal the administration must licence.
- The weightiest points are single-source NYT reporting: that Putin raised the sale with Witkoff and Kushner, and that the envoys helped negotiate the DFC's stake.
- Witkoff denies any stake, and no source documents personal benefit.
- As of 14 Nov 2026 nothing had been signed, and the OFAC negotiation licence ran to 21 Nov 2026.

## Sub-Claims

### 1. Gunvor's ~$22B agreement to buy Lukoil International was killed by a public US Treasury rebuke and withdrawn.

**Confirmed** · Evidence: Documented (multiple outlets)

- Gunvor agreed in late Oct 2025 to buy Lukoil International GmbH for about $22B. On 6 Nov 2025 the US Treasury posted on X that 'the Kremlin's puppet, Gunvor, will never get a licence to operate and profit'.
- Gunvor called the statement 'fundamentally misinformed and false' and withdrew the bid.
- Co-founder Gennady Timchenko sold his stake to Torbjorn Tornqvist in 2014; Tornqvist's holding is reported as 85% or 88%.
- No Trump-family or administration tie to Gunvor was found.
- The withdrawal happened in Nov 2025, not 2026.
- Amounts: about $22B; 85%; 88%
- Dates: late Oct 2025; 6 Nov 2025; Nov 2025
- Sources: [20](https://kyivindependent.com/gunvor-withdraws-offer-for-russias-lukoil-assets-after-us-calls-it-kremlins-puppet) [21](https://www.euronews.com/2025/11/07/swiss-firm-gunvor-drops-plan-to-buy-lukoil-assets-after-us-resistance)

### 2. Boehly's first attempt came in Dec 2025, fronted by Xtellus Partners (a former VTB Capital US unit) with UAE-based Allied Investment Partners, and Treasury rejected it.

**Confirmed** · Evidence: Documented AS REUTERS REPORTING (multiple relays, anonymous sources)

- Xtellus is a New York bank formed in 2018 through a management buyout of VTB Capital's US arm.
- In Dec 2025 it pitched Treasury a cashless swap: frozen Lukoil shares held by US investors would be exchanged for LIG's assets.
- Per Reuters, Xtellus was advising Todd Boehly and Allied Investment Partners.
- Reuters reported on 10 Dec that Lukoil favoured the bid, and on 15-16 Dec (four sources) that Treasury had rejected it because Lukoil must not profit in cash or shares.
- The Nov 2026 Boehly plan reuses the swap structure, with the shares held in escrow.
- Boehly's interest therefore predates the Sep-Oct 2026 peace-talk reporting by about nine months.
- Dates: Dec 2025; 10 Dec; 16 Dec; Nov 2026; Oct 2026
- Sources: [24](https://www.oilandgas360.com/exclusive-us-bank-floats-plan-to-repay-us-investors-via-lukoil-asset-sale-sources-say/) [25](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/lukoil-leans-towards-xtellus-cashless-bid-for-its-foreign-assets-ce7d50dbd88df327) [26](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/u-s-treasury-rejects-xtellus-led-bid-for-lukoil-assets-sources-say-ce7d50d9dc8af222) [27](https://tass.com/economy/2058893)

### 4. Carlyle signed a deal for most of Lukoil's foreign assets in Jan 2026 and later dropped out.

**Confirmed** · Evidence: Documented

- No price was disclosed; the assets are estimated at up to $22B. Carlyle explored UAE partners in Feb 2026.
- The agreement lapsed without OFAC approval, according to LIG's annual report.
- Bloomberg reported on 8 Oct 2026 that Carlyle had confirmed the expiry, which relays describe as a withdrawal.
- No Trump-family or administration tie to Carlyle surfaced in our searches.
- Amounts: up to $22B

### 5. The leading bidder is a Boehly-led consortium of IHC, Allied Investment Partners, Al Hail Holding, the Al-Khayyat brothers and the US DFC, structured as an escrowed share swap.

**Mostly accurate** · Evidence: Documented AS PRESS REPORTING (FT Sep 2026; Reuters 3 Nov 2026; NYT relays)

- Terms are unconfirmed. Per the FT, the DFC would take a 'mid-teens' equity stake (other relays say 10-20%).
- Boehly and the DFC would control a majority of the board, and IHC and Allied would lead on the Gulf side.
- Per Reuters on 3 Nov 2026, the deal is a cashless swap of frozen Lukoil securities held by Western funds, including Capital Group.
- An unnamed foreign bank had already bought Lukoil paper.
- The shares would go into escrow rather than to Lukoil, and a deal was 'expected within weeks'.
- Lukoil and Treasury did not comment; Al Hail, the Al-Khayyats and the DFC did not respond.
- IHC's interest is documented on its own: it publicly declared interest and notified Treasury in Nov 2025.
- We could not establish Al Hail Holding's owners.
- Its CEO is Hamad Jassim Al Darwish, and no source ties it to Tahnoon.
- Audit: both the FT (Sep 2026) and Reuters (3 Nov 2026) rest on anonymous sources; one says the final structure is not settled.
- IHC's spokesperson said it does not comment on potential investments.
- No price for the Boehly bid has been disclosed; Reuters says the assets' value has fallen from the ~$20-22B first estimated.
- Amounts: 10-20%; ~$20-22B
- Dates: Sep 2026; 3 Nov 2026; Nov 2025

### 10. OFAC licences: negotiations are authorised only under time-limited general licences, the latest running to 21 Nov 2026, and any closing needs a separate licence.

**Confirmed** · Evidence: Documented

- The GL 131 series covers negotiations and conditional agreements for Lukoil International GmbH.
- A final transfer needs a separate OFAC authorisation, and proceeds must be blocked (C76 audit).
- Reported deadline chain: 13 Dec 2025, 17 Jan 2026, 28 Feb, 1 Apr, 30 May, 27 Jun, 25 Jul, 22 Oct, then extended on 21 Oct 2026 to 21 Nov 2026 (GL 131K per C76's audit).
- OFAC may revoke early if the parties do not negotiate in good faith.
- Licences for LIG's filling stations and Bulgarian assets run to 24 Apr 2027.
- Commentators note the structural conflict: the DFC would invest in the buyer while Treasury/OFAC licenses the sale.
- Audit: GL 131K (issued 21 Oct 2026) keeps the condition that Lukoil may receive no benefit beforehand, for example through an exchange of assets or securities, and requires a buyer to seek OFAC review before reselling significant assets (FesenkoLaw).
- The consortium's escrow arrangement is reported as its answer to that condition (Reuters, two people).
- No further extension beyond 21 Nov 2026 had been reported as of 14 Nov 2026.
- Dates: 13 Dec 2025; 17 Jan 2026; 28 Feb; 1 Apr; 30 May
- Sources: [14](https://interfax.com/newsroom/top-stories/119637/) [15](https://kurs.com.ua/en/novost/1485213-vashington-prodovzhiv-termin-prodazhu-zakordonnogo-biznesu-lukoila) [16](https://shipandbunker.com/news/world/708441-us-again-extends-deadline-for-lukoil-foreign-assets-sale) [41](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/export-controls-trade-investment-sanctions/1786274/ofac-widens-the-door-on-lukoil-divestments-navigating-gls-131e-and-128c-for-lukoil-divestments) [42](https://globalsanctions.com/2026/06/us-renews-general-license-for-lukoil-international-sale-until-25-july-2026/) <https://fesenkolaw.com/blog/ofac-gl-131k-one-more-month-for-the-lukoil-deal/>

### Background

- **Sanctions.** The US sanctioned Lukoil and Rosneft in Oct 2025, effective 21 Nov 2025. Lukoil put Lukoil International GmbH (LIG) up for sale. LIG is its Austrian holding company for its foreign assets, which include:
  - West Qurna-2 in Iraq (75%);
  - the Burgas refinery in Bulgaria and the Petrotel refinery in Romania;
  - filling-station networks.
- **Valuation.** Initial estimates were about $20-22B. Reuters reports the value has fallen as some assets stopped operating.
- **Iraq.** Iraq's cabinet moved to nationalise operations at West Qurna-2.

### Connections, each graded

#### 11. OFAC licences and deadlines (documented)
- **The GL 131 series** authorises negotiations and *conditional* agreements for LIG only. Any closing needs a separate OFAC licence, and proceeds must go to a blocked account (C76 audit).
- **Deadline history, as reported:**
  - 13 Dec 2025, extended to 17 Jan 2026;
  - then 28 Feb, 1 Apr and 30 May;
  - then 27 Jun and 25 Jul;
  - then 22 Oct;
  - on 21 Oct 2026 (Interfax; GL 131K per C76's audit), extended to **21 Nov 2026**.
- OFAC can revoke early if the parties do not work in good faith.
- Separate licences for LIG's filling stations and Bulgarian assets run to **24 Apr 2027**.
- Some relays cite a "13 December" date. That is the 2025 deadline, not a 2026 one.
- **Structural conflict, noted by commentators:** a US agency (the DFC) would invest in the buyer while another arm of the same administration (Treasury/OFAC) must licence the sale.

- No signed sale, no OFAC transfer licence and no announced DFC commitment.

- The 21 Nov negotiation licence expiry was pending at the time of writing.
