# C49: The Market for Pardon Lobbying

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · medium confidence  
**Type:** Check · Pardons, Lobbyists & Access

## The Claim

> Is there a paid lobbying market around clemency in Trump's second term, what fees have been reported, and which pardon recipients were donors or had SEC cases dismissed afterwards?

## Bottom Line

A paid clemency-lobbying market is well documented. The WSJ (Dec 2025) reported a $1M going rate and success-fee offers of up to $5-6M. NOTUS counted more than $2.1M in 2025 lobbying disclosures that name pardons.

## What Holds Up

- A paid pardon-lobbying industry has grown around Trump's second term.
- Asking prices of $1M or more were reported.
- Trump pardoned donors or their relatives: Paul Walczak, Trevor Milton and Changpeng Zhao (mostly accurate)
- The SEC dropped civil cases after criminal pardons.
- Pardon attorney Liz Oyer was fired, and Ed Martin became pardon attorney.
- House Judiciary Democrats estimate total restitution wiped out at about $1.3B or more (mostly accurate)
- Official justifications given for the grants and the process.

## What Needs Correcting

- Roger Stone, Ches McDowell/Checkmate and Teresa Goody Guillén charged large fees to seek clemency (partly accurate)
- Carlos Watson's commutation belongs among donor-linked grants (misleading)
- The claim lists Carlos Watson among donor-linked grants; no Watson donation was found. His case ran through Alice Marie Johnson and reform advocates.
- The House Judiciary Democrats' $1.3B figure (Jun 2025) was raised to about $1.7B in their Aug 21, 2026 'Pardons, Inc.' report, which also counts forfeitures.
- Roger Stone's $600K from Roger Ver was disclosed as exit-tax lobbying, not a pardon, and Ver received a DOJ deferred prosecution deal, not clemency (C36).
- Ed Martin is no longer pardon attorney: Trump announced his departure on Aug 21, 2026.
- Changpeng Zhao is not a documented personal donor; his link is the Binance lobbying fees and the WLF/USD1 business ties (C09).
- Todd Blanche, who fired Oyer as Deputy AG, is now Attorney General; Pam Bondi is described as the former AG.

## What Is Unproven

- Implied: pardons were bought with fees or donations.

## In More Detail

- NBC reported Fred Daibes paid Keith Schiller's firm $1M, and 60 Minutes filmed Burkman and Wohl pitching a $300K pardon.
- The SEC dropped its civil cases against Milton, Watson, Ozy and Archer after clemency, and Liz Oyer was fired and replaced by Ed Martin.
- Several details in the claim need correcting: Watson is not a documented donor, the House Democrats' estimate is now about $1.7B, Stone's $600K from Ver was disclosed as exit-tax lobbying, and Martin left the post in Aug 2026.
- No grant has been shown to be bought, and the White House denies any pay-to-play.

## Sub-Claims

### 1. A paid pardon-lobbying industry has grown around Trump's second term.

**Confirmed**

- NOTUS: federal lobbyists disclosed more than $2.1M in 2025 from clients seeking a 'pardon' or 'clemency', likely an undercount because many pardon lawyers do not register.
- NBC (Jun 2025) and NPR/NOTUS (Jul 19, 2026) describe a growing business that runs on access to White House aides rather than the DOJ Pardon Office.
- Paying for advocacy is legal.
- Amounts: more than $2.1M
- Dates: Jun 2025; Jul 19, 2026
- Sources: [5](https://www.notus.org/money/pardon-lobbyists-president-donald-trump) [8](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/donald-trump/trump-pardons-drive-big-burgeoning-business-lobbyists-rcna209801) [17](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5896394) [9](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/trump-administration/white-house-clemency-process-trump-pardons-rcna239549)

### 2. Asking prices of $1M or more were reported.

**Confirmed**

- Bloomberg Law (May 2025) found lawyers quoting $1M fees.
- The WSJ (about Dec 23, 2025; read secondhand) reported $1M as the lobbyists' standard rate, with success fees of up to $6M offered, and said Binance offered up to $5M. NBC: Fred Daibes, convicted of bribing Sen. Bob Menendez, paid $1M to Keith Schiller's Javelin Advisors for 'executive relief' (Q1 2025).
- He got no pardon and dropped the firm in Jun 2025.
- The House Judiciary Democrats' Aug 2026 report says intermediaries charged up to $2M per engagement.
- These are reported fees, not audited prices, and many came from anonymous sources.
- Amounts: $1M; up to $6M; up to $5M; up to $2M
- Dates: May 2025; Dec 23, 2025; Jun 2025; Aug 2026
- Sources: [1](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/us-law-week/lawyers-are-quoting-1-million-in-fees-to-get-pardons-to-trump) [2](https://boingboing.net/2025/12/27/trump-pardons-start-at-1m-reports-wsj.html) [3](https://politicalwire.com/2025/12/23/inside-the-new-fast-track-to-a-presidential-pardon/) [4](https://www.rawstory.com/trump-pardons-money-ceo) [7](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/politics-news/new-jersey-businessman-paid-1m-lobbying-firm-try-get-pardon-trump-rcna239278)

### 3. Roger Stone, Ches McDowell/Checkmate and Teresa Goody Guillén charged large fees to seek clemency.

**Partly accurate**

- McDowell/Checkmate ($450K) and Goody Guillén (about $290K) for Binance's CZ pardon are confirmed (see C36).
- Stone's $600K from Roger Ver was disclosed as lobbying on the exit tax, so NOTUS left it out of its pardon total.
- Ver dropped Stone in Dec 2025, and Stone denies lobbying the executive branch on the case.
- No other named Checkmate pardon client was confirmed.
- Other documented players: Jack Burkman and Jacob Wohl, paid just under $1M by Joseph Schwartz (Washington Post, via the Daily Beast) and $600K by Boosie BadAzz, who has filed for arbitration to recover $300K; and Keith Schiller (Daibes).
- Amounts: $450K; about $290K; $600K; under $1M; over $300K
- Dates: Dec 2025
- Sources: [5](https://www.notus.org/money/pardon-lobbyists-president-donald-trump) [6](https://www.notus.org/money/donald-trump-pardons-clemency-lobbyists-roger-stone-boosie-badazz) [10](https://decrypt.co/316387/bitcoin-jesus-pays-trump-ally-roger-stone-600000-to-lobby-against-exit-tax) [11](https://www.floridabulldog.org/2026/02/fired-couldnt-deliver-pardon-roger-stone-hired-aid-nigeria-christians/) [12](https://www.thedailybeast.com/nursing-home-fraudster-joseph-schwartz-paid-fraudster-lobbyists-for-donald-trump-pardon) [16](https://www.wafb.com/2026/09/14/boosie-takes-600k-pardon-fight-60-minutes-after-failed-trump-clemency-push/) [14](https://cbsnews.com/news/trump-pardon-lobbyists-60-minutes)

### 4. Trump pardoned donors or their relatives: Paul Walczak, Trevor Milton and Changpeng Zhao.

**Mostly accurate**

- Milton and his wife gave more than $1.8M in Oct 2024, and he was pardoned Mar 28, 2025.
- Walczak was pardoned Apr 23, 2025, weeks after his mother Elizabeth Fago attended a $1M-per-head Mar-a-Lago dinner (NYT, May 2025).
- His application cited her fundraising, and the pardon voided about $4.4M in restitution; whether she paid the $1M is unclear.
- Zhao was pardoned Oct 2025.
- He is not a documented personal donor: the link runs through Binance's lobbying and its business ties to WLF (see C09).
- Amounts: more than $1.8M; $1M; about $4.4M
- Dates: Oct 2024; Mar 28, 2025; Apr 23, 2025; May 2025; Oct 2025
- Sources: [18](https://www.thedailybeast.com/trump-pardoned-tax-cheat-after-mom-attended-1m-mar-a-lago-dinner) [19](https://www.mediaite.com/media/news/tax-evader-pardoned-by-trump-after-mom-attended-1m-per-plate-mar-a-lago-dinner-report) [26](https://gizmodo.com/trump-pardoned-him-now-he-wont-have-to-pay-back-660-million-2000661542)

### 5. Carlos Watson's commutation belongs among donor-linked grants.

**Misleading**

- Trump commuted Watson's roughly 116-month sentence on Mar 28, 2025, hours before he was due to report, which voided about $36M in restitution and about $60M in forfeiture.
- Watson credited pardon czar Alice Marie Johnson and advocate Glenn Martin.
- No donation by Watson was found, and the White House gave no justification.
- Amounts: about $36M; about $60M
- Dates: Mar 28, 2025
- Sources: [20](https://www.axios.com/2025/03/28/trump-ozy-media-carlos-watson-commute) [21](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/trump-commutes-sentence-ozy-media-founder-carlos-watson-rcna198667) [22](https://www.thewrap.com/carlos-watson-prison-sentence-commuted-trump)

### 6. The SEC dropped civil cases after criminal pardons.

**Confirmed**

- The SEC dismissed its civil cases against Milton (Sep 2025), Carlos Watson and Ozy Media (filed about Sep 18-19, 2025) and Devon Archer after clemency, though a pardon does not legally erase civil liability.
- It later waived penalties for ex-Ozy executives Samir Rao and Suzee Han, citing cooperation.
- Bloomberg Law (Apr 9, 2026) counts $176M in restitution and fines that pardoned securities-fraud defendants were originally due to pay.
- The SEC declined comment.
- Milton's spokesperson, quoted by Bloomberg Law, said the SEC itself initiated contact with defence counsel to dismiss the Milton case, in which Milton was represented by Brad Bondi, brother of then-AG Pam Bondi; that article was paywalled and read only through excerpts.
- Amounts: $176M
- Dates: Sep 2025; Sep 18-19, 2025; Apr 9, 2026
- Sources: [23](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/securities-law/trumps-criminal-pardons-have-sec-enforcers-following-suit) [24](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-19/sec-drops-carlos-watson-fraud-case-after-trump-axed-prison-term) [25](https://www.law360.com/bankruptcy-authority/articles/2387230)

### 7. Pardon attorney Liz Oyer was fired, and Ed Martin became pardon attorney.

**Confirmed**

- Then-Deputy AG Todd Blanche fired Oyer on Mar 7, 2025, the morning after she declined to recommend restoring Mel Gibson's gun rights.
- Then-AG Pam Bondi restored those rights in Apr 2025.
- Oyer sued in federal court in Washington on Sep 3, 2026 for reinstatement and back pay, alleging due-process, First Amendment and civil-service violations; the allegations are untested.
- Blanche, confirmed by the Senate 50-49 on Aug 8, 2026 and sworn in as Attorney General on Aug 10, 2026 after Trump removed Bondi in April 2026, says he knew nothing of the Gibson matter.
- Trump named Ed Martin pardon attorney on May 8, 2025, after Martin's DC US Attorney nomination collapsed.
- Martin posted 'No MAGA left behind' after the Scott Jenkins pardon.
- He lost the Weaponization Working Group in Feb 2026, and Trump announced on Aug 21, 2026 that he was leaving to fight election 'Legal Battles'. No successor was found.
- Dates: Mar 7, 2025; Apr 2025; Sep 3, 2026; Aug 8, 2026; Aug 10, 2026
- Sources: [27](https://www.goodmorningamerica.com/news/story/doj-pardon-attorney-removed-after-dispute-mel-gibsons-119677857) [28](https://abcnews.com/Politics/wireStory/justice-department-pardon-lawyer-fired-dispute-mel-gibsons-136185902) [29](https://www.govexec.com/workforce/2026/09/former-pardon-atty-calls-mspb-dead-end-sues-reinstatement/415817/) [30](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/us-law-week/ed-martin-takes-doj-pardon-job-as-trump-upends-clemency-process) [31](https://www.rawstory.com/no-maga-left-behind-outrage-as-trump-pardon-chief-issues-new-vow/) [32](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/justice-department/ed-martin-dojs-weaponization-czar-sources-say-rcna257093) [34](https://www.local10.com/news/politics/2026/08/21/trump-loyalist-ed-martin-is-leaving-the-justice-department-to-focus-on-election-legal-battles/)

### 8. House Judiciary Democrats estimate total restitution wiped out at about $1.3B or more.

**Mostly accurate**

- A Jun 17, 2025 staff memo under Ranking Member Raskin put fines and restitution erased at about $1.3B, roughly $680M of it tied to Milton, whose restitution had not been set.
- Its caveats: some sums may already have been paid, and collectability is unknown.
- The Aug 21, 2026 'Pardons, Inc.' report raised the figure to about $1.7B, now including forfeitures.
- Both are partisan estimates.
- The White House (Harrison Fields) dismissed the 2025 memo.
- Amounts: about $1.3B; roughly $680M; about $1.7B
- Dates: Jun 17, 2025; Aug 21, 2026
- Sources: [35](https://democrats-judiciary.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/new-judiciary-democrats-analysis-reveals-trump-s-corrupt-pardon-spree-cheated-crime-victims-of-13-billion) [36](https://www.axios.com/2025/06/17/trump-pardons-cost-democrats-report) [37](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trumps-pardons-cost-victims-and-taxpayers-1-3-billion-house-judiciary-committee-democrats-review) [38](https://kvia.com/news/2025/06/17/trumps-pardons-could-have-erased-more-than-1-3-billion-in-fines-and-restitution-democrats-say/) [39](https://democrats-judiciary.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/new-judiciary-democrats-report-exposes-maga-s-cash-for-clemency-racket-which-gives-unrepentant-criminals-a-new-lease-on-life-and-leaves-victims-in-the-dust) [40](https://democrats-judiciary.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/democrats-judiciary.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/2026-08-hjc-dems-staff-report-pardons-inc.pdf) [41](https://newrepublic.com/post/214599/trump-pardons-fraudsters-cost-victims-billions)

### 9. Official justifications given for the grants and the process.

**Confirmed**

- The White House says the president acts on his constitutional authority after vetting by the White House Counsel and the DOJ.
- Leavitt says Trump 'finds it detestable that anyone would even attempt to profit off pardons'.
- The line 'anyone spending money to lobby for pardons is foolishly wasting their money' is attributed to a spokesperson, variously Lauren Bis or Leavitt.
- For Schwartz, the White House cited his $5M restitution paid, his poor health and a harsh sentence.
- For Milton, Trump said he was targeted for supporting him.
- No justification was given for Watson or Walczak.
- Amounts: $5M
- Sources: [14](https://cbsnews.com/news/trump-pardon-lobbyists-60-minutes) [15](https://www.foxnews.com/media/white-house-denies-trump-influenced-pardon-brokers-after-60-minutes-investigation) [9](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/trump-administration/white-house-clemency-process-trump-pardons-rcna239549)

### 10. Implied: pardons were bought with fees or donations.

**Unverified**

- Money flows to lobbyists and donations ahead of grants are documented, but no evidence shows Trump granted clemency in exchange for payment.
- Several big spenders (Daibes, Boosie, Ver on clemency) got nothing.
- Democrats Rep. Dave Min and Sen. Peter Welch opened a probe on May 7, 2026 with letters to 17 recipients, including David Gentile (commuted; about $15.5M restitution), and escalated on Jun 15, 2026.
- They lack subpoena power.
- Amounts: about $15.5M
- Dates: May 7, 2026; Jun 15, 2026
- Sources: [42](https://www.welch.senate.gov/welch-min-launch-bicameral-oversight-investigation-into-trump-pardons-amid-corruption-concerns/) [43](https://keyt.com/news/crime/2026/05/07/bicameral-congressional-investigation-launched-over-multiple-trump-administration-pardons) [44](https://min.house.gov/media/press-releases/representative-dave-min-and-senator-peter-welch-escalate-oversight) [7](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/politics-news/new-jersey-businessman-paid-1m-lobbying-firm-try-get-pardon-trump-rcna239278)

## Denials & Context

- White House: clemency is vetted by the White House Counsel and the DOJ and decided by the president.
  - It calls profiting off pardons 'detestable' and says lobbying money is 'foolishly' wasted.
- Trump rejected nearly 6,000 clemency applications (Reason, Jul 26, 2026), which argues against a simple pay-for-pardon market.
- Paid pardon advocacy is legal and has long existed; the new elements are the scale, the fees and the bypassing of the DOJ Pardon Office.
- Many large payments bought no clemency (Daibes $1M, Boosie $600K), and Boosie says he was scammed.
- Burkman and Wohl deny wrongdoing.
  - Stone denies lobbying the executive branch on Ver's case.
  - Blanche denies the Gibson link to Oyer's firing.
- The restitution totals are partisan estimates with acknowledged caveats (some sums already paid, unclear collectability), and Milton's restitution was never set.
- The SEC gave no public rationale for the post-pardon dismissals; critics such as Richard Painter call them political.

## Full Report

**Verdict: mostly-accurate** (confidence: medium)

This check builds on C36 and C09. The record confirms the core of the claim:
- A large, paid clemency-lobbying market exists.
- Reported fees of $1M or more.
- Pardons of donors or their relatives.
- The SEC dropping civil cases after clemency.
- The firing of Liz Oyer and Ed Martin's appointment.

Several details in the claim are outdated or wrong (see Corrections). No evidence shows a pardon was bought, and the White House denies pay-to-play.

**Method note:** Many primary pages could not be read here. The WSJ, Bloomberg Law and NYT are paywalled. The House Judiciary Democrats' site (democrats-judiciary.house.gov) could not be read directly. Figures from those outlets come from search-result excerpts and secondary coverage across several outlets. They are flagged where relevant.

### The claim
The claim describes a paid pardon-lobbying industry (Roger Stone, Ches McDowell/Checkmate, Teresa Goody Guillén), asking prices above $1M, and donor-linked grants (Walczak, Milton, CZ, Watson). It also covers SEC civil cases dropped after pardons, Oyer's firing and Ed Martin's role, and about $1.3B in restitution wiped out.

### Sub-claims

1. **A paid pardon-lobbying industry: confirmed.**
   - NOTUS counted more than $2.1M in 2025 lobbying disclosures that name a "pardon" or "clemency". This is likely an undercount, because many pardon lawyers do not register.
   - NBC (Jun 2025) and NPR/NOTUS (Jul 19, 2026) describe a business that runs on access to White House aides. The DOJ Pardon Office is largely bypassed.
   - Paying for advocacy is legal.

2. **Asking prices of $1M or more: confirmed (as reported).**
   - Bloomberg Law (May 2025) found lawyers quoting $1M fees.
   - The WSJ (Dec 2025; read via Political Wire, Boing Boing and Raw Story) reported $1M as the standard rate and success fees of up to $6M. It also reported that Binance offered up to $5M.
   - NBC: Fred Daibes, who bribed Sen. Menendez, paid $1M to Keith Schiller's Javelin Advisors in Q1 2025. He got no pardon and dropped the firm in Jun 2025.
   - The House Judiciary Democrats' Aug 2026 report says intermediaries charged up to $2M per engagement.
   - Many of these figures come from anonymous sources.

3. **Stone, McDowell and Goody Guillén charged large fees: partly-accurate.**
   - The Binance payments are confirmed (C36): $450K to Checkmate and about $290K to Goody Guillén.
   - Stone's $600K from Roger Ver was disclosed as exit-tax lobbying, so NOTUS kept it out of its pardon total. Ver fired Stone in Dec 2025.
   - Other documented players: Jack Burkman and Jacob Wohl, who were paid just under $1M by Joseph Schwartz (Washington Post, via the Daily Beast). Schwartz was pardoned Nov 14, 2025. Boosie BadAzz paid them $600K, got no pardon and is seeking $300K back in arbitration.
   - 60 Minutes (Sep 2026) filmed Burkman and Wohl pitching a $300K pardon.

4. **Donor or relative pardons (Walczak, Milton, CZ): mostly-accurate.**
   - Milton and his wife gave more than $1.8M in Oct 2024. He was pardoned Mar 28, 2025.
   - Walczak was pardoned Apr 23, 2025, weeks after his mother Elizabeth Fago attended a $1M-per-head Mar-a-Lago dinner (NYT, May 27, 2025). His application cited her fundraising, and about $4.4M in restitution was voided. Whether she paid the $1M is unclear.
   - Zhao is not a documented personal donor. His link runs through Binance's lobbying and WLF business (C09).

5. **Watson's commutation as donor-linked: misleading.**
   - Trump commuted Watson's sentence on Mar 28, 2025, voiding about $36M in restitution and about $60M in forfeiture.
   - Watson credited pardon czar Alice Marie Johnson. No donation was found, and the White House gave no justification.

6. **SEC drops civil cases after pardons: confirmed.**
   - The SEC dismissed its cases against Milton (Sep 2025), Watson and Ozy Media (about Sep 18-19, 2025), and Devon Archer. A pardon does not legally erase civil liability.
   - The SEC later waived penalties for ex-Ozy executives Samir Rao and Suzee Han.
   - Bloomberg Law (Apr 9, 2026) counted $176M in restitution and fines originally owed by pardoned securities-fraud defendants. The SEC declined comment.
   - Milton's own spokesperson (not the SEC) told Bloomberg Law the SEC initiated contact with defence counsel to dismiss; Milton's SEC lawyer was Brad Bondi, brother of then-AG Pam Bondi.

7. **Oyer fired; Martin as pardon attorney: confirmed, with an update.**
   - Then-Deputy AG Todd Blanche fired Oyer on Mar 7, 2025, after she declined to recommend restoring Mel Gibson's gun rights. Bondi restored them in Apr 2025.
   - Oyer sued in federal court in Washington on Sep 3, 2026 for reinstatement and back pay, alleging due-process, First Amendment and civil-service violations (untested). Blanche, confirmed by the Senate 50-49 on Aug 8, 2026 and sworn in as AG on Aug 10, 2026 after Trump removed Bondi in April, denies knowing about the Gibson matter.
   - Martin was named pardon attorney on May 8, 2025, and posted "No MAGA left behind" after the Scott Jenkins pardon.
   - He lost the Weaponization Working Group in Feb 2026. Trump announced his departure on Aug 21, 2026. No successor was found.

8. **About $1.3B+ in restitution wiped out: mostly-accurate.**
   - The Jun 17, 2025 staff memo estimated about $1.3B, with roughly $680M tied to Milton, whose restitution was never set. The memo itself warns that some sums were already paid and that collectability is unknown.
   - The Aug 21, 2026 "Pardons, Inc." report raised the estimate to about $1.7B, now including forfeitures.
   - Both are partisan estimates. The White House dismissed the 2025 memo.

9. **Official justifications: confirmed.**
   - The White House says clemency is the president's constitutional power, vetted by the White House Counsel and the DOJ.
   - Leavitt says Trump "finds it detestable that anyone would even attempt to profit off pardons".
   - A spokesperson said lobbying money is "foolishly" wasted; attribution varies between Bis and Leavitt.
   - Schwartz: the White House cited $5M already paid in restitution, his health and a harsh sentence.
   - Milton: Trump said he was targeted for supporting Trump.

10. **Implied: pardons were bought: unverified.**
    - The money flows are documented, but a quid pro quo is not.
    - Big spenders such as Daibes and Boosie got nothing, and about 6,000 applications were rejected (Reason, Jul 2026).
    - Rep. Dave Min and Sen. Peter Welch opened a probe on May 7, 2026, with letters to 17 recipients including David Gentile, and escalated on Jun 15. They have no subpoena power.

### Corrections
- Watson is not a documented donor.
- The $1.3B estimate has been updated to about $1.7B (Aug 2026).
- Stone's $600K from Ver was disclosed as exit-tax lobbying, and Ver got a DOJ deferred prosecution deal, not clemency.
- Ed Martin left the pardon attorney post (announced Aug 21, 2026).
- CZ is not a personal donor.
- Blanche is now Attorney General (confirmed Aug 8, sworn in Aug 10, 2026).

### Denials and context
- The White House denies pay-to-play.
- Burkman and Wohl deny wrongdoing. Stone denies lobbying the executive branch on Ver's case. Blanche denies the Gibson link to Oyer's firing.
- Paid advocacy is legal. What is new is the scale and the bypassing of the DOJ office.
- Critics such as Richard Painter call the SEC dismissals political.
