# C34: David Sacks: AI & crypto czar with investments

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate (confidence: medium)

## Lead investigated

> Follow-up to the UAE chips claim: White House AI and crypto czar David Sacks, who helped drive the UAE chip deals and crypto policy, kept financial interests in AI/crypto companies via Craft Ventures under ethics waivers; a New York Times investigation (late 2025) detailed hundreds of AI-related holdings; verify the holdings, waivers, his role in the UAE/G42 chip approvals and GENIUS Act, and his denials.

## Summary

Well documented: Sacks served as White House AI and crypto czar under ethics waivers (crypto, March 5, 2025; AI, June/July 2025) while he and Craft Ventures kept 400+ AI-linked investments. The NYT's Nov 30, 2025 investigation counted 708 tech holdings, 449 AI-related and 20 crypto-related, and he coordinated the White House push for the GENIUS Act. His role in the UAE/G42 chip approvals rests on secondary summaries of NYT reporting that he pushed for Emirati chip access; the formal licences came from Commerce/BIS, and no stake in a UAE counterparty is documented. Sacks and the White House deny any conflict, no violation has been found, and the NYT did not allege illegality.

## Corrections

- There were two waivers: crypto (March 5, 2025) and AI (June or July 2025; outlets differ).
- The NYT investigation is dated Nov 30, 2025 and counted 449 AI-related holdings out of 708 tech investments.
- Sacks left the czar post in March 2026 and now co-chairs PCAST.
- Formal UAE/G42 chip licences were issued by Commerce/BIS; Sacks's documented role was in policy and negotiation.

## Denials and context

- Sacks: the NYT story is a 'nothing burger'; OGE approved his waivers; he divested at substantial cost to his net worth.
- White House: Sacks acted appropriately; OGE required divestment only of certain AI holdings.
- The NYT did not allege illegality, and no ethics agency has found a violation.
- Critics, including Warren, Slotkin, Stansbury and ethics scholar Kathleen Clark, call the waivers inadequate and question his 130-day SGE count.

## Full report

**Verdict: mostly-accurate** (confidence: medium)

**Lead as briefed.** This is a follow-up to the UAE chips claim (C08) and the GENIUS Act claim (C11). The brief says David Sacks was the White House AI and crypto czar and helped drive the UAE chip deals and crypto policy. It says he kept financial interests in AI and crypto companies through Craft Ventures under ethics waivers. It says a late-2025 New York Times investigation detailed hundreds of AI-related holdings.

**Bottom line.** The holdings, the waivers, the NYT findings, his GENIUS Act role and his denials are well documented across many outlets. His role in the UAE/G42 chip deal is less firm. The claim that he was "central" in pushing for Emirati chip access rests on secondary summaries of the NYT's September 2025 reporting. The formal export licences came from the Commerce Department, and no source names Sacks as an approver. No source shows that Sacks or Craft held a stake in G42, MGX or any other part of the UAE deal. The idea that his holdings shaped his policy work is the NYT's framing. It is not a documented finding of wrongdoing, and the NYT did not allege illegality.

**Method note.** Direct fetches of npr.org and banking.senate.gov failed: those hosts are blocked in this session. The New York Times articles themselves were not reached. Everything below comes from search results across NPR affiliates, TechCrunch, The Block, Cointelegraph, The Lever, Jacobin, Nextgov, Senate press releases, The National and other outlets. The shared web-search budget ran out before two planned follow-ups: BitGo IPO details and 2026 developments after Sacks left the czar post. Those points are marked as gaps.

### Sub-claims

1. **Sacks was the White House AI and crypto czar, serving as a special government employee (SGE). Confirmed.**
   - He was named in December 2024 and served as an SGE, a status capped at 130 days a year. He remained a partner at Craft Ventures while in the role.
   - He stepped down as czar in late March 2026 after reaching the 130-day limit. Reports give March 26 or 27. He moved to co-chair PCAST, the President's science and technology advisory council.
   - Outlets disagree on whether a successor will be named.

2. **He kept AI and crypto interests through Craft Ventures under ethics waivers. Confirmed.**
   - On March 5, 2025, White House Counsel David Warrington issued a waiver memo covering digital-asset matters.
     - The memo acknowledged that Craft still held crypto stakes but called them "a small fraction" of its holdings, about 3.7% of Craft's total assets (The Lever, Jacobin).
   - A second waiver covered AI. Outlets date it to June (NPR) or July 2025 (Nextgov).
   - Sacks and Craft divested more than $200M in crypto and crypto-linked stock, at least $85M of it his own. That figure was first reported by CNBC on March 14, 2025, citing an ethics memo, and later repeated by the NYT.
   - NPR reports he sold out of Amazon, Meta and xAI. He and Craft still hold more than 400 AI-linked investments.
   - Gap: the public filings give neither the timing of the sales nor the value of what remains, so his "over 99%" divestment claim cannot be checked independently.
   - Ethics scholar Kathleen Clark called these "sham ethics waivers."

3. **A late-2025 NYT investigation detailed hundreds of AI-related holdings. Confirmed.**
   - The story ran on November 30, 2025, under the headline "Silicon Valley's Man in the White House Is Benefiting Himself and His Friends." Five reporters worked on it for about five months.
   - The NYT counted 708 tech investments in his disclosures: 449 AI-related and 20 crypto-related.
   - That count is the NYT's own classification. Sacks's filings label 438 holdings as "software" or "hardware" rather than as "AI interests."

4. **Sacks helped drive the UAE/G42 chip approvals. Partly accurate.**
   - The NYT's September 15, 2025 piece reportedly described Sacks as central in pushing for broad chip access for the Emirates, alongside Witkoff and Sheikh Tahnoon. This is per summaries by The Decoder, Business Standard and others. The NYT text itself was not read.
   - C08 already records that he negotiated the May 2025 framework.
   - The formal licences were issued by Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS):
     - The first Nvidia licences for the UAE came in October 2025.
     - Authorisations for up to 35,000 GB300-class chips each for G42 and Saudi Arabia's Humain came in November 2025.
   - None of these reports names Sacks as a decision-maker.
   - No source found shows that Sacks or Craft held a stake in G42, MGX or another UAE counterparty.
   - The White House said Sacks "acted appropriately."

5. **Sacks drove the GENIUS Act. Confirmed for his policy role; the conflict angle is unverified.**
   - He coordinated the White House position on the bill and promoted it publicly, saying it could create "trillions" in demand for US Treasurys (The Block, CNBC via NBC DFW).
   - He spoke at the July 18, 2025 signing. Trump signed the bill.
   - The NYT cited Craft's 7.8% stake in BitGo, a crypto infrastructure firm, as of BitGo's IPO filing.
   - Separately, C11 records that BitGo custodies the reserves of World Liberty Financial's USD1 stablecoin. That overlap is documented, but no source shows Sacks acted on it.

6. **Sacks and the White House deny any conflict. Confirmed.**
   - Sacks called the story a "nothing burger" made of "a bunch of anecdotes that don't support the headline." He said the Office of Government Ethics (OGE) approved his waivers and that he had divested "hundreds of millions of dollars" at a cost to his net worth.
   - A White House spokesperson said OGE required him to sell only certain kinds of AI holdings, not all of them.
   - Before publication, his defamation lawyers at Clare Locke sent the NYT a letter, which Sacks posted publicly.
     - The letter disputed the story's claims about the All-In podcast's role in a White House AI event.
     - It also said the Times dropped a reference to a dinner with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang that, it said, never happened.
   - The NYT stood by its story.
   - Some outlets said he sued the paper. No filed lawsuit could be confirmed, so that claim is unverified.

### Other side and context

- **Sacks and his defenders** argue that the waivers were approved and that he divested what the rules required. They say Craft's remaining crypto exposure is small, and that the NYT alleged no illegality. Commentary at Nonzero and elsewhere notes that the piece accuses him of nothing illegal.
- **Critics:**
  - Senators Warren and Slotkin and Representative Stansbury question the waivers and the 130-day SGE count. Warren wrote to Sacks on March 6, 2025, and to OGE on May 5, 2025. Warren and Stansbury opened an investigation on September 17, 2025.
  - Steve Bannon criticised Sacks's judgment rather than the conflicts themselves.
- **Ethics enforcement:** no ethics agency is known to have found a violation.

### Corrections to the brief

- The NYT investigation is dated November 30, 2025, and counted 449 AI-related holdings out of 708 tech investments. "Hundreds" is accurate.
- There were two waivers: one for crypto (March 2025) and one for AI (June or July 2025).
- Sacks no longer holds the czar title. He left in March 2026 and now co-chairs PCAST.
- His role in the UAE chips deal was policy and negotiation, as reported. The formal approvals were Commerce/BIS licences.
