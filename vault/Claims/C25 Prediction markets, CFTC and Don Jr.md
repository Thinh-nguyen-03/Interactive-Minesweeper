# C25: Prediction markets, CFTC and Don Jr.

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate (confidence: medium)

## What the video said

> TMTG announced 'Truth Predict', which flopped; prediction markets on sports depend on a legal theory backed by Trump's CFTC chairman Mike Selig; Don Jr. is a strategic adviser to Kalshi and on the advisory board of/an investor in Polymarket; Selig was asked in Congress whether the White House asked him to drop the CFTC probe into Polymarket; Trump posted that prediction markets are a major industry to protect; Don Jr. reportedly told Republican state attorneys general not to fight prediction markets (he says it was a passing comment).

## Summary

Most of the core facts hold up. TMTG announced Truth Predict in Oct 2025 and shelved it in Aug 2026 before a full launch. Don Jr. is a Kalshi strategic adviser and sits on Polymarket's advisory board, and his firm 1789 Capital invested in Polymarket. Rep. McGovern asked Selig on Apr 16, 2026 whether the White House had sought to drop a Polymarket probe. Trump posted 'It is a major industry, and we must protect it' on May 26, 2026, and the NYT reported Don Jr.'s March 2026 pitch to GOP attorneys general. The weak points: the sports-as-swaps theory predates Selig, 'on the board of both' is loose (both roles are advisory), the earlier DOJ and CFTC Polymarket probes had closed in July 2025, and no source has Don Jr. calling his remarks a 'passing comment'.

## Corrections

- Transcript says 'Mike selling' and 'Seelig'; the CFTC chairman is Michael (Mike) Selig, confirmed 2025-12-18.
- Transcript says 'CallSheet'; the company is Kalshi.
- Transcript says Don Jr. is 'on the board of both'; he is a strategic adviser to Kalshi and on Polymarket's advisory board. Neither is a board-of-directors seat. His firm 1789 Capital invested in Polymarket.
- Transcript implies Truth Predict launched and failed; it was announced in Oct 2025 and shelved in Aug 2026 before a full launch.
- Transcript says sports prediction markets are 'only possible thanks to' Selig; Kalshi's sports contracts began in Jan 2025 under Acting Chair Pham. Selig is the theory's leading federal defender, not its origin.
- The earlier DOJ and CFTC probes into Polymarket were closed in July 2025, before Selig became chair.
- The 'passing comment' wording of Don Jr.'s denial was not found; the public denials came from his spokesperson and from Kalshi.

## Denials and context

- Selig: the CFTC treats all market participants alike and does not bring politics into matters; he called McGovern's insinuation insulting.
- Don Jr.'s spokesperson: he does not trade on prediction platforms and does not engage with federal officials on behalf of companies he advises or invests in.
- Kalshi: Don Jr. advises on marketing, not regulation. Mansour says Kalshi never asked him for regulatory favors and points to politically diverse advisers.
- TMTG: Truth Predict was dropped for competitive reasons (a crowded market), not regulatory ones.
- Context cutting against the video: the CFTC's jurisdiction claim is contested in the courts, and 44 AGs (including Republicans) told the CFTC it lacks authority over sports wagering, so the policy outcome Don Jr. favors is not assured.
- Context supporting the video: Sen. John Curtis (R-Utah) has called for a Senate Judiciary probe of presidential family members profiting from access, naming Don Jr.'s prediction-market ties.

### Evidence by sub-claim

**1. Truth Predict announced, then flopped: confirmed (the word "flopped" is a characterization).**
TMTG announced Truth Predict on Oct 28, 2025. The plan was to run it through Crypto.com Derivatives North America, a CFTC-registered exchange, and let users wager on elections, interest rates and sports [1][2]. A full launch never happened.

On the Q2 2026 earnings call (around Aug 7-10, 2026), interim CEO Kevin McGurn said TMTG was dropping the plan to run its own prediction market on Truth Social. TMTG will instead promote Crypto.com's existing prediction products under a marketing agreement [3][4]. McGurn said the market had become too crowded and pointed to competition rather than regulation. TMTG ended its planned CRO-treasury deal with Crypto.com and Yorkville at the same time [4][5].

"Flopped" is fair in the sense that the product was shelved before it launched. It was not a product that launched and then failed.

**2. The legal theory depends on Selig: partly accurate.**
The theory is real: the CFTC treats sports event contracts as "swaps" under the Commodity Exchange Act, which would put them under exclusive federal jurisdiction and preempt state gambling laws. Selig has championed it. Under Selig, the CFTC sued Arizona, Connecticut and Illinois in April 2026, later took action against New York and other states, and filed amicus briefs supporting the platforms [6][7][8].

Selig was confirmed as CFTC chair on Dec 18, 2025 by a 53-43 vote [9], so he did not create the theory. Kalshi's sports contracts launched in January 2025 under Acting Chair Caroline Pham, after Kalshi's 2024 court win on election contracts. The video's framing ("only possible thanks to") overstates Selig's role: he is the theory's chief federal defender now, not its source. Courts are split on it, including a Nevada ruling against the platforms, so it is not settled law [8].

**3. Don Jr. is linked to both companies: confirmed. "On the board of both" is imprecise.**
- **Kalshi:** Kalshi named Don Jr. a strategic adviser in January 2025 [10][11]. Later reporting, citing the NYT, says his pay included more than $300,000 in Kalshi shares [12].
- **Polymarket:** Don Jr. joined Polymarket's advisory board on Aug 26, 2025. On the same day, 1789 Capital, the firm where he is a partner, made a strategic investment reported as "double-digit millions" or "eight figures" [13][14][15].

Neither role is a corporate board-of-directors seat. Both are adviser roles, so "on the board of both" is loose wording, though Polymarket does call it an advisory board. Both companies have been valued in the billions, so "multibillion-dollar" is consistent with reporting. The App Store finance-chart ranking was not independently verified.

**4. Selig asked about the White House and the Polymarket probe: confirmed.**
At a House Agriculture Committee hearing on April 16, 2026, Rep. Jim McGovern (D-Mass.) asked Selig whether anyone in the White House had asked the CFTC to drop an investigation into Polymarket [16][17][18]. Selig replied "we treat all market participants alike" and called the insinuation "insulting". McGovern answered: "It smells like corruption." The quotes match the transcript.

Context the video omits: the DOJ and the CFTC had already closed their earlier probes into Polymarket (over US users getting around its geoblock) in July 2025, under Acting Chair Pham and before Selig took over [19][20]. Nothing found shows that the White House asked for any probe to be dropped. The exchange documents a question and a non-answer, not interference.

**5. Kalshi CEO evasive on Don Jr.: partly verified.**
Kalshi co-founder and CEO Tarek Mansour has been questioned repeatedly about Don Jr., including on The Axios Show in April 2026 [21]. His reported answers stress a politically diverse adviser roster, say that Kalshi "never asked for any favors", and describe Don Jr. as giving marketing advice [21][22]. The exact clip ("We have a lot of advisors") was not located in text form, so the wording is unverified, but the substance fits his reported answers.

**6. Trump's "major industry" post: confirmed.**
On May 26, 2026, Trump posted on Truth Social that the CFTC's "exclusive authority over Prediction Markets" must be maintained and wrote: "It is a major industry, and we must protect it." In the same post he attacked Walz, Pritzker, Letitia James and Chris Christie [23][24][25]. The post followed Minnesota's ban on prediction markets.

**7. Don Jr. lobbied Republican attorneys general: confirmed as reported. The "passing comment" denial is unverified as worded.**
On Aug 27, 2026 the NYT reported, citing four people familiar with the remarks, that Don Jr. spoke at an early-March 2026 closed-door retreat of Republican attorneys general in New Orleans. According to the report, he took part in a Q&A alongside Montana AG Austin Knudsen, argued that prediction markets should be overseen federally, and said opposition came from gambling firms protecting "monopolies" [12][26][27][28]. Sen. Elizabeth Warren asked the Republican Attorneys General Association to release the transcript [29].

The responses found are:
- Don Jr.'s spokesperson said he "does not engage/interface with the federal government on behalf of any company he invests in or advises" [12][26].
- Kalshi published a point-by-point rebuttal saying he "advises on marketing strategy" and "does not advise on regulatory matters" [30].

No source found quotes Don Jr. calling it a "passing comment". The gist of a downplaying denial is supported, but that exact phrasing is unverified. The same reporting says the White House also directly contacted state officials about prediction markets [26][27].

### Corrections
- "Mike selling" / "Seelig" should be Mike Selig. "CallSheet" should be Kalshi.
- Don Jr. is not on either company's board of directors. He is a strategic adviser to Kalshi and sits on Polymarket's advisory board, and his firm 1789 Capital invested in Polymarket.
- Truth Predict was shelved before launch (Aug 2026). It did not launch and then fail.
- The sports-as-swaps theory predates Selig: Kalshi's sports contracts launched in January 2025 under Acting Chair Pham. Selig became chair on Dec 18, 2025 and has been its leading enforcer since.
- The CFTC and DOJ probes into Polymarket had closed in July 2025, before Selig. No evidence shows White House interference.
- The "passing comment" wording of the denial was not found. The public denials came from his spokesperson and from Kalshi.

### Denials and other side
- Selig says the CFTC treats all participants alike and called the insinuation insulting [16].
- Don Jr.'s spokesperson says he does not trade on the platforms and does not lobby federal officials for companies he advises [12][17].
- Kalshi says he advises only on marketing, and Mansour says Kalshi never asked him for regulatory favors [22][30].
- TMTG says it shelved Truth Predict for competitive reasons, not regulatory ones [4].
