# C54: The crypto revolving door: officials who moved to firms they shaped policy for

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate (confidence: medium)

## Lead investigated

> Leads from C35/C25/C12: administration crypto officials moving to industry (and vice versa) — Bo Hines (White House crypto council director → CEO of Tether's US arm/USAT), Brian Quintenz (Kalshi board; withdrawn CFTC nomination), Mike Selig's and Paul Atkins's prior crypto clients, Jonathan Gould (OCC; ex-Bitfury), David Sacks, SEC/CFTC staff departures to Coinbase, a16z, etc.; plus Trump family business partners placed in government roles. Verify each move with dates, cooling-off/ethics-waiver facts and responses.

## Summary

Most of the named moves are documented. Bo Hines left the White House crypto council on 9 Aug 2025, joined Tether on 19 Aug and became CEO of its US arm (USAT) on 12 Sep 2025. Per the WSJ (14 Oct 2026), a firm he co-owned, Nxum, had a $415K-a-month Tether deal from May 2025 and received about $6.3M, and Sen. Blumenthal opened an inquiry on 22 Oct 2026. Caroline Pham (CFTC to MoonPay), Summer Mersinger (CFTC to Blockchain Association) and Brian Quintenz (a16z/Kalshi to a withdrawn CFTC nomination to SUI Group's board) are also documented. So are the industry ties Mike Selig, Paul Atkins and Jonathan Gould brought into office, and Steve Witkoff's retained World Liberty holdings as special envoy. Gaps: I found no waiver or cooling-off record for Hines or Gould, no named 2025-26 SEC staff move to Coinbase or a16z, and no finding by any authority that a move broke ethics rules.

## Corrections

- The brief calls Quintenz part of an official-to-industry move, but he never took office in this administration. His nomination was withdrawn on 30 Sep 2025, and his Kalshi board seat predated the nomination.
- Hines was first a Tether 'strategic adviser' (19 Aug 2025). He became CEO of the US arm/USAT on 12 Sep 2025, not on leaving the White House.
- The '200+ officials' revolving-door count (Tech Transparency Project) dates from 2022 and does not measure this administration.
- No 2025-26 SEC staff move to Coinbase or a16z was confirmed. The best-documented departures are CFTC leaders (Pham to MoonPay, Mersinger to Blockchain Association).

## Denials and context

- Nxum's representative said Hines did not know of the Tether deal and received no Tether funds while at the White House. Tether says an external-counsel investigation found no wrongdoing.
- The SEC says Atkins complies with his ethics agreement, and Trump Media called the Patomak subpoena inquiry 'meritless'.
- Witkoff's spokesperson says he does not take part in matters that could affect his financial interests. WLF says neither Trump nor Witkoff was involved in the UAE-linked $2B MGX deal.
- Selig's and Atkins's one-year recusals are the standard federal ethics pledge, not special treatment. Critics argue it is too short for officials writing rules for the whole industry.
- Industry-to-regulator hiring (Lindman, Selig) is defended as bringing technical expertise. Revolving-door hiring in crypto also predates this administration, as the TTP 2022 study shows.
- Searches did not show any of these moves violating 18 U.S.C. 207 or a signed ethics pledge. The concerns rest on timing, payments and congressional letters.

### Sub-claims

#### 1. Hines: White House crypto council to Tether/USAT CEO (confirmed)

- **In government.** Hines was executive director of the President's Council of Advisers on Digital Assets from January 2025. He announced on 9 Aug 2025 that he was leaving for the private sector.
- **Tether, 19 Aug 2025.** Tether named him strategic adviser for digital assets and US strategy, 10 days after he left. Some outlets called him "CEO of Tether USA" at this point; Bloomberg and others said adviser.
- **USAT, 12 Sep 2025.** Tether unveiled USAT and named him CEO of the US business, based in Charlotte. Anchorage Digital is the issuer, and Cantor Fitzgerald is reserve custodian and primary dealer (see C35).
- **Launch.** USAT launched on 27 Jan 2026.

#### 2. Hines's ethics and cooling-off issues (partly-accurate)

The payments, per the WSJ of 14 Oct 2026 as summarised by IBTimes and AMBCrypto:

- Tether agreed in May 2025 to pay Nxum $415,000 a month for lobbying, PR and advisory work.
- Hines co-founded Nxum and kept a one-third stake while in government.
- Total payments were about $6.3M. Early payments went through a marketing firm, Notorious. Later ones, including two of $2.49M in December 2025 and April 2026, went directly to Nxum after Hines left.

The policy link: the WSJ says Hines pushed for the GENIUS Act's three-year transition period, which favoured Tether. Coverage stresses that the reporting does not show the money changed any provision.

The Senate inquiry:

- On 22 Oct 2026 Sen. Blumenthal, ranking member of the Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, wrote to Paolo Ardoino at Tether and to Todd Hines, Bo's father and Nxum's CEO.
- He asked for contracts, payment records and communications by 3 Nov 2026. One outlet gives 6 Nov.
- He says the arrangement implicates ethics, anticorruption and lobbying laws, and that the lobbying was never registered.

The other side:

- Nxum says Hines did not know about the deal and got no Tether money while in the White House.
- Tether says an external-counsel review found no wrongdoing.

No published waiver or 18 U.S.C. 207 determination for Hines was found.

#### 3. Quintenz: Kalshi board and withdrawn CFTC nomination (confirmed)

- **Background.** He was a CFTC commissioner in Trump's first term, then a16z crypto's global head of policy. Reports put him on Kalshi's board since about 2021.
- **Nomination.** Trump nominated him to chair the CFTC in early 2025. He pledged to leave the Kalshi board if confirmed, and his Kalshi ties drew gaming-industry opposition.
- **Withdrawal.** The nomination stalled through a feud with the Winklevoss twins, who lobbied Trump against him; Quintenz published their texts. The White House withdrew it on 30 Sep 2025, reported 1 Oct. An official told NOTUS he "remains a trusted ally." Selig was nominated instead.
- **Since then.** He became an independent director and audit-committee member of SUI Group (Nasdaq: SUIG) effective 5 Jan 2026. He is also reported as a senior adviser to a prediction-markets coalition backing Kalshi; I could not confirm the date.
- **Caveat.** He never held office in this administration. This is an industry-to-nominee-to-industry path, not an official leaving for industry.

#### 4. Selig's prior crypto clients (confirmed; client list rests on one outlet)

- **Clients.** As a Willkie Farr partner he represented eToro and Paradigm. The Intercept (26 Nov 2025), citing his ethics records, adds venture firms, a Cayman Islands blockchain company, Architect Financial Technologies and the Blockchain Association.
- **Path into office.** He was chief counsel of the SEC Crypto Task Force in 2025, was confirmed CFTC chair on 18 Dec 2025 (53-43) and was sworn in on 22 Dec 2025.
- **Ethics terms.** Per The Intercept, his ethics agreement requires only the standard one-year step-back from former clients.

#### 5. Atkins's prior crypto clients and holdings (confirmed)

- **Patomak's clients.** They reportedly included banks, crypto exchanges and DeFi platforms. FTX signed on in early 2022.
- **Holdings.** His OGE disclosure, public on 25 Mar 2025, showed:
  - up to about $327M in family assets
  - up to about $6M in crypto-related assets: Anchorage Digital equity (about $1M), Securitize call options ($2.5-5M) and a small Pontoro stake
  - no direct crypto
- **Ethics pledge.** He committed to divest Patomak within 90 days of confirmation and to stay out of matters involving Patomak or its clients for one year.
- **The sale.** Warren asked on 6 Aug 2025 who bought his stake and for how much. No disclosure of either was found. The SEC says he complies with his ethics agreement.

### Denials and context

- Nxum says Hines did not know about the deal and got no money while in office. Tether says a counsel review found no wrongdoing.
