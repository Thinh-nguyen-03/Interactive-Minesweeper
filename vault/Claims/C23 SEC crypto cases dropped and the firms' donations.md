# C23: SEC crypto cases dropped and the firms' donations

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate · high confidence  
**Type:** Check · Regulators and enforcement

## The claim

> Did the SEC drop more than a dozen enforcement actions against crypto companies after Trump took office, and did several of those firms donate to his campaign, inauguration, military parade or White House ballroom?

## Bottom line

The SEC did drop or close its cases against Coinbase (Feb 27, 2025), Kraken (Mar 27, 2025), Binance (May 29, 2025) and Gemini (investigation closed Feb 2025; Earn suit dismissed Jan 2026), plus many other crypto matters.

## What holds up

- Coinbase, Kraken, Binance and Gemini all faced SEC enforcement.
- The cases were dropped one by one, very shortly after Trump took office (mostly accurate)
- Multiple of these exchanges gave $1M or more to Trump's campaign or inauguration fund (mostly accurate)
- They sponsored the White House ballroom.

## What needs correcting

- Over a dozen lawsuits against cryptocurrency exchanges were dropped (partly accurate)
- They sponsored Trump's military parade (partly accurate)
- **Claimed:** The cases were dropped 'very shortly after Trump took office'  
  **The record shows:** Coinbase, Kraken and the Gemini investigation fell within about 10 weeks, but Binance's dismissal came in May 2025, Ripple's in August 2025 (with the $125M penalty upheld), and the Gemini Earn suit's in January 2026.
- **Claimed:** 'over a dozen lawsuits against cryptocurrency exchanges' were dropped  
  **The record shows:** The SEC formally dismissed about 7-8 crypto lawsuits. Counts above a dozen (Decrypt: at least 17) include closed investigations and non-exchange firms.
- Gemini's $1M gifts were personal 2024 campaign contributions from the Winklevoss twins, partly refunded as over the limit, not donations from the company to the inauguration.
- No Binance donation to Trump's campaign or inauguration was found; Binance's Trump links run through the MGX/USD1 deal and the CZ pardon.
- Of the exchanges, only Coinbase is documented as a sponsor of the military parade.

## What is unproven

- The dismissals coincide with what looks like quid-pro-quo deals.

## In more detail

- Coinbase, Kraken, Crypto.com and Ripple each gave $1M or more to the inauguration, the Winklevoss twins gave about $1M each to the 2024 campaign (the excess was refunded), Coinbase sponsored the Army parade, and Coinbase, Ripple, Tether and the Winklevosses gave to the ballroom.
- The claim is overstated in three ways: some dismissals came months later; formal lawsuit dismissals number about 7-8 (over a dozen only if closed probes are counted); and no Binance donation was found.
- The quid-pro-quo framing rests on timing, not on proof, and the SEC denies any political favoritism.

## Sub-claims

### 1. Coinbase, Kraken, Binance and Gemini all faced SEC enforcement.

**Confirmed**

- The SEC sued Coinbase (Jun 2023), Kraken/Payward (Nov 2023), Binance and Zhao (Jun 2023), and Gemini over Gemini Earn (Jan 2023).
- Gemini also faced a separate investigation, with a Wells notice in 2024.
- Dates: Jun 2023; Nov 2023; Jan 2023
- Sources: [2](https://www.theblock.co/post/343835/coinbase-case-officially-dropped-by-sec) [3](https://www.theblock.co/post/348606/sec-formally-dismisses-enforcement-action-against-kraken-consensys-cumberland-drw) [4](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26316) [7](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-01-24-sec-to-dismiss-gemini-earn-lawsuit-with-prejudice-after-full-investor-recovery-ending-three-year-battle-386931) [6](https://theblock.co/news/regulation/2025-02-26-sec-closes-investigation-into-crypto-exchange-gemini-cameron-winklevoss-says-343643)

### 2. The cases were dropped one by one, very shortly after Trump took office.

**Mostly accurate**

- Coinbase was dismissed with prejudice on Feb 27, 2025.
- Gemini's investigation was closed on Feb 26, 2025.
- Kraken was dismissed with prejudice on Mar 27, 2025, together with Consensys and Cumberland.
- Binance was stayed in Feb 2025 and dismissed with prejudice on May 29, 2025.
- Ripple's appeals were dropped on Aug 7, 2025, with the $125M judgment intact.
- The Gemini Earn suit was dismissed by stipulation on Jan 23, 2026.
- The first wave came within weeks; other cases took months, up to a year.
- Amounts: $125M
- Dates: Feb 27, 2025; Feb 26, 2025; Mar 27, 2025; Feb 2025; May 29, 2025
- Sources: [1](https://sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-47) [3](https://www.theblock.co/post/348606/sec-formally-dismisses-enforcement-action-against-kraken-consensys-cumberland-drw) [4](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26316) [6](https://theblock.co/news/regulation/2025-02-26-sec-closes-investigation-into-crypto-exchange-gemini-cameron-winklevoss-says-343643) [7](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-01-24-sec-to-dismiss-gemini-earn-lawsuit-with-prejudice-after-full-investor-recovery-ending-three-year-battle-386931) [9](https://cointelegraph.com/news/sec-ripple-drop-appeals-enforcement-action)

### 3. Over a dozen lawsuits against cryptocurrency exchanges were dropped.

**Partly accurate**

- The SEC's own FY2025 accounting lists 7 crypto enforcement actions dismissed between Feb and May 2025: Coinbase, Binance, Cumberland, Consensys, Kraken, Dragonchain and Balina.
- Gemini Earn was added in Jan 2026.
- Decrypt counts at least 17 firms once closed probes are included (Robinhood, Uniswap, OpenSea, Crypto.com, Yuga, Gemini).
- The NYT, as reported secondhand, found about 60% of inherited crypto cases paused, dropped or dismissed.
- Over a dozen holds for crypto companies and probes combined, but not for lawsuits against exchanges specifically.
- Amounts: about 60%
- Dates: May 2025; Jan 2026
- Sources: [10](https://decrypt.co/356030/how-the-sec-is-handling-crypto-cases-12-months-into-trumps-presidency) [11](https://cointelegraph.com/news/sec-crypto-enforcement-donald-trump-dropped)

### 4. Multiple of these exchanges gave $1M or more to Trump's campaign or inauguration fund.

**Mostly accurate**

- Coinbase, Kraken and Crypto.com each gave $1M to the inaugural committee, and Ripple gave about $4.9-5M in XRP.
- Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss (Gemini) each gave about $1M in BTC to the Trump campaign in Jun 2024; about $155K each over the $844,600 limit was refunded.
- No Binance contribution to the campaign or inauguration was found.
- Amounts: $1M; about $4.9-5M; about $1M; about $155K; $844,600
- Dates: Jun 2024
- Sources: [12](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-01-31/trump-raised-millions-from-crypto-firms-for-his-inauguration) [13](https://decrypt.co/300274/every-crypto-firm-donated-donald-trump-inauguration) [14](https://bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2024/12/20/crypto-companies-kraken-ripple-ondo-donate-to-trumps-inauguration) [15](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-06-21/winklevoss-twins-refunded-after-trump-crypto-gift-exceeded-limit)

### 5. They sponsored Trump's military parade.

**Partly accurate**

- Coinbase was a named sponsor of the June 14, 2025 Army 250th parade and festival, via a donation to the nonpartisan America250.
- No other exchange was found among the sponsors.
- Total sponsorship was reported at about $885K.
- Amounts: about $885K
- Dates: June 14, 2025
- Sources: [16](https://www.mediaite.com/media/tv/trumps-military-parade-gives-shoutouts-to-corporate-donors-special-thanks-to-our-sponsor-coinbase) [17](https://dc.fortune.com/crypto/2025/06/16/coinbase-faces-crypto-backlash-over-sponsoring-military-parade-in-d-c)

### 6. They sponsored the White House ballroom.

**Confirmed**

- The White House's Oct 2025 list of 37 ballroom donors includes Coinbase, Ripple, Tether America, and Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss.
- Kraken and Binance were not found on it.
- Dates: Oct 2025
- Sources: [18](https://www.theblock.co/post/376062/crypto-donors-trump-white-house-ballroom) [19](https://fortune.com/2025/10/26/37-white-house-ballroom-donors-funding-300-million-build-tech-ceos-trump/)

### 7. The dismissals coincide with what looks like quid-pro-quo deals.

**Unverified**

- The donations and the timing are documented.
- No evidence was found that any dismissal was exchanged for a donation.
- The SEC cites policy and discretion, and firms with no known Trump donations (Uniswap, OpenSea) also had their probes dropped.
- Sources: [11](https://cointelegraph.com/news/sec-crypto-enforcement-donald-trump-dropped) [21](https://www.citizen.org/news/enforcement-actions-against-over-100-corporations-stopped-by-trump/)

## Denials and context

- The SEC says political favoritism played no role and that its decisions rest on legal and policy considerations; its dismissals cite 'the exercise of its discretion and as a policy matter.'
- The SEC later acknowledged 'flaws' in its earlier crypto enforcement.
  - The industry calls the Gensler-era cases 'regulation by enforcement', and courts had split on the legal theories (e.g., the Ripple programmatic-sales ruling).
- The Gemini Earn dismissal followed full return of investor assets through the Genesis bankruptcy, and Gemini had already paid NYAG settlements.
- The parade sponsorship went through the nonpartisan America250, and the ballroom donor list includes many non-crypto firms.
- Public Citizen and Democrats argue that crypto's political spending 'paid off'; that is advocacy, not proof of a quid pro quo.

## Full report

**Verdict: mostly accurate (confidence: high on the facts, low on any causal link)**

### The claim
Crypto researcher Molly White has argued that "basically every major cryptocurrency exchange," including Coinbase, Kraken, Binance and Gemini, faced SEC enforcement. She says "over a dozen lawsuits" against exchanges "were one by one dropped very shortly after Trump took office." She also says several of these exchanges gave "a million dollars or more" to Trump's campaign or inauguration fund, sponsored the military parade, and sponsored the White House ballroom. She frames this as something that "looks an awful lot like" a quid pro quo. A broader version of the claim is that SEC cases were "getting dropped left and right."

### Evidence by sub-claim

1. **Coinbase, Kraken, Binance and Gemini faced SEC enforcement. Confirmed.**
   - Coinbase: sued June 2023.
   - Kraken (Payward): sued November 2023.
   - Binance and Changpeng Zhao: sued June 2023.
   - Gemini: sued January 2023 over the Gemini Earn lending program, alongside Genesis. A separate SEC investigation of Gemini included a Wells notice in 2024.
2. **The cases were dropped one by one after Trump took office. Confirmed, with nuance on timing.**
   - Coinbase: the SEC dismissed the case with prejudice by joint stipulation on Feb 27, 2025 (SEC press release 2025-47).
   - Gemini: Cameron Winklevoss said the SEC closed its separate investigation of Gemini with no action on Feb 26, 2025.
   - Kraken: agreement in principle on Mar 3, 2025. The case was formally dismissed with prejudice on Mar 27, 2025, together with the Consensys and Cumberland DRW cases.
   - Binance: the case was stayed in February 2025 and dismissed with prejudice on May 29, 2025 (SEC litigation release LR-26316).
   - Ripple: the case was not dismissed. Both sides dropped their cross-appeals on Aug 7, 2025, and the $125M penalty and the injunction stand.
   - Gemini Earn: the case was stayed in April 2025. A resolution in principle came in September 2025, and a joint stipulation to dismiss with prejudice followed on Jan 23, 2026. The SEC cited the full return of investors' assets through the Genesis bankruptcy.
   - So the first wave came within about five to ten weeks of the inauguration, but some cases took up to a year. "Very shortly" fits Coinbase, Kraken and Gemini's investigation better than Binance, Ripple or Gemini Earn.
3. **"Over a dozen" cases. Mostly accurate, depending on what counts.**
   - The SEC's own FY2025 accounting lists 7 crypto enforcement actions formally dismissed between Feb 27 and May 29, 2025: Coinbase, Binance, Cumberland, Consensys, Kraken, Dragonchain and Balina. The Gemini Earn dismissal (January 2026) came later.
   - Decrypt counted at least 17 firms whose actions or investigations were ended, including closed probes: Robinhood, Uniswap, OpenSea, Crypto.com, Yuga Labs and Gemini.
   - A New York Times analysis, reported in secondary coverage, found that the SEC paused, dropped or dismissed about 60% of the crypto cases it inherited. I could not open the Times article.
   - "Over a dozen *lawsuits against exchanges*" overstates it: fewer than a dozen were lawsuits against exchanges specifically. "Over a dozen cases and probes against crypto companies" holds up.
4. **Several of these firms gave $1M or more to the campaign or inauguration. Confirmed for Coinbase, Kraken and Crypto.com, and for Gemini's founders. Not found for Binance.**
   - Coinbase: $1M to the inaugural committee.
   - Kraken: $1M to the inaugural committee, confirmed by a company representative.
   - Crypto.com: $1M to the inaugural committee (Bloomberg).
   - Ripple: about $4.9M to $5M in XRP to the inaugural committee.
   - Gemini: the company itself was not found among inaugural donors. Its founders, Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss, each gave about $1M in bitcoin to the Trump campaign in June 2024. The amount over the $844,600 limit (about $155,400 each) was refunded.
   - Binance: no direct donation to the campaign or inauguration found. Its Trump ties run through the MGX $2B investment settled in USD1 and the pardon of Changpeng Zhao (see C07 and C09).
5. **They sponsored the military parade. Confirmed for Coinbase only.**
   - Coinbase was a named sponsor of the June 14, 2025 Army 250th anniversary parade and festival, through a donation to America250, which is nonpartisan.
   - No other exchange was found among the parade sponsors.
   - Total sponsorship was reported at about $885K.
6. **They sponsored the ballroom. Confirmed.**
   - The White House's October 2025 list of 37 ballroom donors includes Coinbase, Ripple, Tether America, and Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss.
   - Kraken and Binance were not found on the list.
7. **The insinuation of a quid pro quo. Unproven.**
   - The timing and the donations are documented.
   - No evidence was found that any dismissal was a reward for a donation.

### Corrections
- Not all of these cases were "very shortly after" the inauguration. The Binance dismissal came in May 2025, the end of the Ripple case in August 2025, and the Gemini Earn dismissal in January 2026.
- Ripple's case was not dropped outright. The $125M judgment stands.
- Gemini's $1M gifts were personal campaign contributions from the Winklevoss twins in 2024, before the election, and part of them was refunded.
- No Binance donation to the campaign or inauguration was found.
- Of the exchanges, only Coinbase is documented as a sponsor of the parade.
- Formal SEC dismissals of crypto lawsuits number about 7 to 8. "Over a dozen" is only true if closed investigations are included.

### Denials and context
- The SEC said that political favoritism played no role and that its decisions rested on legal and policy considerations (as reported in coverage of the NYT analysis). Its dismissals said they were "in the exercise of its discretion and as a policy matter."
- The SEC later said its earlier crypto enforcement had "flaws."
- The industry argues that the Gensler-era cases were "regulation by enforcement," and that several courts had already split on the legal theories. In Ripple, the Torres ruling found that programmatic sales were not securities transactions.
- Coinbase says it supported the transition.
- Coinbase's parade sponsorship went through America250, which is nonpartisan.
- The ballroom donors include many non-crypto companies, such as Apple, Amazon and Google.
- Other donors on the same list did not have cases before the SEC. Some firms, such as Uniswap and OpenSea, had their probes dropped without any known Trump donation. This weakens a simple pay-to-play reading.
- On the other side, Public Citizen and Democrats argue that the industry's political spending "paid off."
