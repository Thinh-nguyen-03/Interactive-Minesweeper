# C51: Trump sons' drone ventures and the Pentagon's Drone Dominance programme

**Verdict: mostly-accurate** (confidence: medium)

The deals and the oversight letters in this lead are documented. Eric Trump and Donald Trump Jr. hold an indirect stake in the Powerus / Aureus Greenway drone merger, which was announced on March 9, 2026 and closed around October 1, 2026. Powerus then won a US Air Force counter-drone IDIQ with a ceiling of up to $90M and an $82M order under it.

In May 2026 the Wall Street Journal reported that the Pentagon was negotiating debt-and-equity financing with Don Jr.-advised Unusual Machines (UMAC). No deal had been announced by mid-November 2026, even though two peer companies have signed conditional Office of Strategic Capital (OSC) loans. Unusual Machines co-led a $10M investment in Draganfly in September 2026.

The policy tailwinds are real: Executive Order 14307 (June 2025) and the FCC's December 2025 Covered List action against foreign-made drones, including DJI. The oversight letters and the Senate Democrats' "$70M+" tally are all real.

What remains unproven is any causal link. No reporting shows a Trump family member intervening in any award, financing talk or policy step. All parties deny it, and the Pentagon Inspector General has not said it opened an inquiry.

**Background:** this check builds on C28 (Eric Trump / Foundation), C32 (Don Jr. / Unusual Machines / 1789 portfolio) and C26 (1789 Capital / Vulcan). Those earlier checks already establish Don Jr.'s Nov 2024 Unusual Machines advisory role and 331,580 shares, the Oct 2025 Army motor order, the Vulcan $620M OSC loan, and denials by Don Jr. and 1789. This report does not repeat them.

**Research note:** the original pages at breakingdefense.com, warren.senate.gov and oversightdemocrats.house.gov were not read directly. The findings rest on search-result summaries from several outlets (Reuters syndications via BNN Bloomberg/CTV/Yahoo, CNBC, Quartz, DroneXL, DefenseScoop, Inside Unmanned Systems, Tectonic Defense, Bloomberg Government, IBTimes, Inquirer, Manufacturing Dive, Al Jazeera, MoFo, TechCrunch). I could not read the letters themselves, only the coverage of them.

## Sub-claims

1. **Eric Trump and Don Jr. back the Powerus / Aureus Greenway drone merger.** *Confirmed.*
   - Aureus Greenway Holdings is a Florida golf-course company. On March 9, 2026 it announced a reverse merger with Powerus, a drone maker based in Rock Hill, SC, to take Powerus public on Nasdaq as PUSA [1][2]. The deal value was not disclosed.
   - The brothers' stake is indirect, held through American Ventures. Reuters/LSEG data put American Ventures at about 3% of Aureus at the time of the announcement. Filings indicate a 9.9% beneficial stake in the combined company [2].
   - Dominari Securities arranged about $9M of financing, with investors including Unusual Machines [2]. Each brother reportedly owns about 6% of Dominari (see C39).
   - The merger completed around October 1, 2026, with Andrew Fox as CEO and chairman [3][4].
   - Correction: one Manufacturing Dive headline describes the merger as "closed" in March. It was only announced then.

2. **Powerus has won federal drone business since the merger.** *Mostly accurate.*
   - In August 2026 its subsidiary Tandem Defense received a US Air Force IDIQ with a ceiling of up to $90M, managed by the Air Force Life Cycle Management Center. That figure is a ceiling, not committed money [5].
   - In October 2026 Powerus announced an $82M order under that contract [6].
   - In November 2026 it reported $130M in signed orders [3].
   - In the Drone Dominance Program it qualified for Phase II but was not among the 11 Phase I production winners or the Gauntlet II winners [7][8].
   - Powerus also placed a $16.9M components order with Unusual Machines [9], so the two Trump-linked firms trade with each other.
   - Retired Lt. Gen. Keith Kellogg sits on the Powerus advisory board and said "it never hurts to have their name on something" [3].
   - Powerus and the brothers' spokespeople say the brothers are passive investors with no operational role [2].

3. **The Pentagon is negotiating an equity stake in Unusual Machines under the $1.1B Drone Dominance programme.** *Mostly accurate, with nuance.*
   - On May 28, 2026 the WSJ reported months of talks with Unusual Machines, Performance Drone Works (PDW) and Neros on funding that could combine debt and equity [10][11]. Coverage tied the talks to the roughly $1.1B Drone Dominance push, but the vehicle is OSC lending rather than the Drone Dominance procurement budget itself.
   - UMAC shares rose about 57-65% on the report [10].
   - The other two firms have since closed financing: PDW signed a conditional OSC loan commitment of up to $820M on July 31 (see C74), and on November 2, 2026 Neros announced a conditional OSC loan of up to $450M with a small, non-voting stake [12].
   - No OSC release or company filing naming Unusual Machines was found as of mid-November 2026, so its talks remain unconsummated.

4. **Unusual Machines invested in Draganfly.** *Confirmed.*
   - Reuters reported the deal on Sept 28, 2026. Unusual Machines and an unnamed US asset manager are buying 1,869,159 Draganfly shares at $5.35 in a registered direct offering, for about $10M in total [13][14].
   - The investment is more than half of Draganfly's market cap of about $17.9M. It came weeks after Draganfly won a Canadian Armed Forces drone contract.
   - The reported split, $5M from each investor, comes from secondary summaries and is unconfirmed.

5. **Trump executive orders on drones and restrictions on Chinese DJI benefit domestic makers.** *Mostly accurate.*
   - EO 14307, "Unleashing American Drone Dominance" (June 6, 2025), directs agencies to prioritize US-made drones and calls for a supply-chain risk list. The order itself did not ban DJI [15][16].
   - Effective Dec 22, 2025, the FCC added foreign-made UAS and critical components, including DJI's, to its Covered List. Citing the EO, this bars new equipment authorizations [17][18].
   - The process was driven largely by the FY25 NDAA's mandated security review, a congressional act and not only Trump's. The FCC later exempted Blue UAS-listed and domestic-end-product items [16].
   - NDAA-compliant domestic suppliers such as Unusual Machines are structural beneficiaries [19].
   - That Don Jr. specifically lobbied for the DJI ban is unverified. The quote attributed to him comes from forum and blog posts.

6. **Warren and Garcia wrote to the Pentagon Inspector General.** *Confirmed.*
   - On May 8, 2026, Rep. Robert Garcia, the House Oversight ranking member, asked DoD IG Platte B. Moring III to probe safeguards against Trump-family self-dealing. The letter cites Unusual Machines and the Powerus Air Force interceptor award [20][21].
   - On Aug 4, 2026, Sen. Warren, Rep. Garcia and Sens. Blumenthal and Duckworth asked the IG to review about $3.2B in awards, plus $3.1B in options, to at least 15 companies tied to Trump-son-linked investment vehicles. The letter draws on a Washington Post analysis [22][23].
   - Separately, on May 6, 2026, Warren wrote to Hegseth (not the IG) about Unusual Machines' contracts [24].
   - No IG decision to open an inquiry has been reported.

7. **Senate Democrats tallied $70M+ in federal contracts to 1789 Capital portfolio companies.** *Confirmed as an attributed claim.*
   - On Jan 22-23, 2026, Sens. Warren, Blumenthal and Andy Kim wrote to Hegseth. Citing PitchBook, they said 1789 portfolio companies received more than $70M in administration contracts between April and November 2025, including $45M to Cerebras in April 2025 [25][26].
   - The $620M Vulcan loan was cited separately (see C26).
   - The figure has not been independently audited. The senators did not allege that any law was broken. 1789 spokesman Arthur Schwartz said the firm has "absolutely zero involvement" in its companies' operations [26].

8. **Any of this was obtained through Trump family influence.** *Unverified.*
   - No document or reporting shows a Trump son contacting officials about Powerus, Unusual Machines, Draganfly or the OSC talks.
   - Don Jr.'s spokesperson says he never communicated with the administration on Unusual Machines' behalf [24]. Unusual Machines CEO Allan Evans said Don Jr. "did not advise or do anything else" on the Army deal (C32).

## Corrections

- Warren's letters describe a "$12.8M DoD contract (Sept 2025)" to Unusual Machines. The company's Sept 30, 2025 release describes a $12.8M order from defense supplier Strategic Logix for the Rapid Reconfigurable Systems Line, which is a subcontract and not a direct Pentagon award [24][27].
- The Pentagon equity stake in Unusual Machines is reported as negotiated, not agreed. Peers PDW and Neros, not UMAC, have closed OSC financing.
- The Powerus merger was announced in March 2026 and closed in October 2026.
- The DJI restrictions flow from the FY25 NDAA review and the FCC, not from a Trump executive order alone.

## Denials and context

- Powerus and the brothers' spokespeople say the brothers are passive investors. Unusual Machines and Don Jr. deny he helped win contracts. 1789 says it has no operational involvement.
- The demand for drones without Chinese parts is a bipartisan, NDAA-codified policy. The Drone Dominance Gauntlet winners (Skycutter, Neros, Perennial Autonomy and others) are mostly not Trump-linked [8].
- The Trump sons' drone portfolio is wider than this lead: Eric Trump also backs the $1.5B XTEND / JFB Construction merger, in which Unusual Machines also invests [28].
