# C26: 1789 Capital and Vulcan Elements

**Verdict: mostly accurate** (confidence: medium-high)

The core facts hold up: Don Jr.'s partnership, the asset and return figures, the August 2025 investment followed by the November 2025 government package, and ProPublica's "call came from the White House" reporting. The claim as usually stated gets a few details loose. It says "right before," when the gap was about three months. It misquotes ProPublica slightly and calls the loan a "contract." Its insinuation that Don Jr. "anticipated" or arranged the deal is not backed by any documented evidence that he was involved.

Research note: propublica.org and ncnewsline.com could not be fetched from this environment (DNS blocked). The findings below rely on search results that summarize or syndicate the ProPublica story (WRAL/AP, NC Newsline, WUNC/NPR, Salon, The New Republic, Cato). They also draw on congressional press releases and letters, and on coverage by Reuters, Bloomberg, Axios, TechCrunch and others. The New York Times "200%" story was also known only through secondary coverage.

## The claim
- Don Jr. is a partner at 1789 Capital. The firm "now has over one billion dollars in assets" and reportedly returns of "over 200 percent".
- 1789 invests in companies that get government contracts. Example: rare-earth magnet maker Vulcan Elements. "Shortly afterwards" Vulcan announced a "billion dollar, ten thousand metric ton" magnet facility "enabled by our partnership with the United States government". "Trump Jr.'s company managed to buy right before the government got involved."
- 1789 says these are "common sense" investments, that "we stay totally out of that", and that "we don't have to call the government".
- ProPublica: "the call came from the White House. We have to get this deal done", and "Vulcan's was the only deal initiated by a top aide to the president".
- 1789 says it takes "minority positions in growth equity", has no strategic control, and is "simply economic investors".

## Evidence by sub-claim

1. **Don Jr. is a partner at 1789 Capital: confirmed.** Bloomberg reported on November 12, 2024 that he would join 1789 Capital as a partner instead of taking a White House role. The firm was co-founded by Omeed Malik (2022), with Chris Buskirk as co-founder and partner [1]. Axios reports that he does not sit on the investment committee of the growth-equity fund [6].

2. **"Over $1 billion in assets": confirmed, but outdated and understated.** Reuters reported on September 8, 2025 that 1789 had crossed $1 billion in assets [2]. Later reporting puts the figure much higher. Fox Business said the growth fund went from about $200M to about $2B in a year. Secondary summaries of the FT put total assets near $3.5B by 2026, and 1789 closed a $1.2B real-estate fund in August 2026 [5][6][7].

3. **"Over 200 percent" returns: confirmed (approximately).** The New York Times (Maureen Farrell, July 22, 2026) reported that the main fund showed returns of roughly or nearly 200% as of June 30, 2026, helped largely by its Polymarket stake [3][4]. The PitchBook comparison is about 21% for 2023-vintage VC funds. The figure is "nearly/about 200%", not clearly "over". These are paper returns early in the fund's life.

4. **1789 invested in Vulcan Elements, then the government deal followed: confirmed, but "right before" is loose.** 1789 participated in Vulcan's $65M Series A in August 2025, reportedly at about a $200M valuation [8][10]. On November 3, 2025, Vulcan and ReElement announced a $1.4B partnership with the Department of War/Pentagon and Commerce. It included a $620M Office of Strategic Capital (OSC) direct loan to Vulcan, $50M in Commerce CHIPS incentives, an $80M OSC loan to ReElement, about $550M in private capital, and government warrants or equity. Vulcan would build a 10,000-metric-ton magnet plant [9][10][11]. On November 18, 2025, NC Gov. Josh Stein announced the plant's site in Benson (Johnston County), described as a roughly $918M-$1B facility [12]. The quoted "billion dollar ten thousand metric ton... facility enabled by our partnership with the United States government" matches Vulcan's announcement. The investment came about **three months** before the government package, so it was not literally "right before". The OSC loan was the largest that office had made [10].

5. **"Investing in companies that get government contracts", and Don Jr.'s "remarkable skill in anticipating": insinuation.** Several 1789 portfolio companies have since won federal funding. ProPublica also noted that Unusual Machines, a company Don Jr. advises and holds a stake in, was under review for its own Pentagon loan [13]. However, no reporting found documents that Don Jr. had advance knowledge. Vulcan's award was a **loan** with warrants, not a "contract". The claim also speaks of "telling the Pentagon, we need a contract", which is the wrong instrument.

6. **ProPublica: "the call came from the White House" and "only deal initiated by a top aide": confirmed, with a small misquote.** ProPublica (Robert Faturechi, May 28, 2026) reported that Peter Navarro personally pushed OSC to fund Vulcan. Navarro is a White House trade adviser and described as a friend of Don Jr. A Pentagon official is quoted: "The call came from the White House: We have to get this done." The claimed version says "this deal done". ProPublica also reported that, of the dozens of companies OSC was considering, Vulcan's was the only deal initiated by a top presidential aide. Staff were pushed to close it in weeks rather than months [13][14][15]. ProPublica says the aide was Navarro. It does **not** report that Don Jr. or 1789 made the call.

7. **1789 says it is a passive minority investor and does not call the government: confirmed as the firm's position.** I found the substance in reporting but could not verify the exact quoted wording or who said it (likely Malik or Buskirk in an interview). 1789 told ProPublica it played no role in Vulcan getting the loan and did not know of the deal before it was public. A Trump Jr. spokesperson said he does not discuss portfolio companies with federal officials and did not speak to Navarro about Vulcan. NYT secondary coverage says Trump Jr. claimed he had never met or spoken with anyone at Vulcan [4][13][14]. Profiles describe 1789 as typically taking minority growth-equity stakes [6].

## Corrections
- Claimed: 1789 bought "right before" the government got involved. The record shows: it invested in August 2025, about three months before the November 3, 2025 announcement of the $1.4B package. That package included a $620M Pentagon OSC loan to Vulcan, conditional at first.
- Claimed: it was a Pentagon "contract". The record shows: it was a direct loan, plus Commerce CHIPS incentives and government warrants or equity.
- Claimed: ProPublica's quote was "We have to get this deal done". The record shows: the reported quote is "We have to get this done."
- Claimed: "over one billion dollars in assets". The record shows: that was true in September 2025, but by 2026 reporting put 1789 at about $2B-$3.5B+.
- Claimed: returns were "over 200%". The record shows: the NYT reported roughly or nearly 200% (as of June 30, 2026), and these are unrealized.
- The person ProPublica says initiated the deal is White House adviser Peter Navarro, not Don Jr. or 1789.

## Denials and context
- The Pentagon said "outside affiliations, investors, or political connections play absolutely no role in the Department's funding decisions" [13].
- 1789 says it played no role. A Trump Jr. spokesperson denies contact with Navarro about Vulcan. Vulcan CEO John Maslin had told reporters and House staff (April 16, 2026) that there was no political favoritism. House Democrats later said ProPublica's account contradicted him [16].
- Navarro reportedly called the reporting "fake news" and defended Vulcan and ReElement on supply-chain-resilience grounds. The White House cited national interest [16].
- Supporting context: rare-earth magnets are a genuine national-security bottleneck, given China's dominance and its 2025 export controls. The deal had bipartisan-style industrial-policy logic, and it included about $550M of private capital.
- Oversight: Sens. Warren, Blumenthal and Kim wrote letters (January and June 2026), and Sen. Schiff wrote to DoD (July 31, 2026). Reps. Huffman and Garcia demanded documents from Vulcan (August 11, 2026). Rep. Raskin opened an inquiry into 1789 (August 26, 2026), to which 1789 "fired back". House Democrats forced a failed vote to subpoena Trump Jr. [16][17][18][19][20][21]. ReElement withdrew from its $80M conditional loan in July 2026 [16].

## Audit (second-opinion pass, 2026-11-14)
**Result: upheld.**
- **ProPublica findings:** NPR's syndication of ProPublica (May 28, 2026) and House Democrats' letters confirm Navarro's role and the quote "The call came from the White House: We have to get this done" (no word "deal"). They also confirm the weeks-not-months timeline, the Aug 2025 Series A, which came about three months before the $620M OSC loan (the largest in OSC history), and the denials.
- **Returns:** the roughly 200% figure is still known only from secondhand summaries of the NYT article.
- **Added context:** in late Aug 2026, 1789 confirmed about $300M more for Polymarket, bringing its reported total to about $500M.
