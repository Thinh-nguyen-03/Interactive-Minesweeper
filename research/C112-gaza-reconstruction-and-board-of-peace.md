# C112: Gaza reconstruction, the Board of Peace and who is involved

**Verdict:** partly-accurate (confidence: medium)

**Question:** What are the documented plans for Gaza's reconstruction and governance since 2025, which named people and firms connected to the Trump family or administration have a documented role or financial interest, and what contracts or commitments exist? Plans and proposals are kept apart from signed commitments and from money actually committed.

Builds on C15 (Kushner and Affinity Partners) and C105 (Witkoff's holdings); those findings are not repeated here. Everyone named denies wrongdoing; nothing below is a finding by any authority.

## Summary

- **Plans are documented; signed commitments are almost absent.** The sequence is Trump's 4 Feb 2025 "Riviera" remarks, the 29 Sep 2025 20-point plan, the 10 Oct 2025 ceasefire, UN Security Council resolution 2803 (17 Nov 2025) and the Board of Peace (charter ratified 22 Jan 2026).
- **Roles are documented.** Kushner and Witkoff sit on both executive boards, with Tony Blair, Marc Rowan (Apollo), and on the Gaza board the developer Yakir Gabay; Aryeh Lightstone and Josh Gruenbaum are senior advisers.
- **No Trump-family firm has a documented Gaza contract, commitment or stated financial interest.** Searches covering the Trump Organization, the sons, Trump Media, WLF and American Bitcoin found nothing. Concerns about Kushner's Affinity Partners, Rowan and others concern indirect holdings and Gulf-investor overlap, are reported chiefly by the Telegraph, and are denied.
- **Money:** about $17B was pledged on 19 Feb 2026 (US $10B; nine states about $7B). The FT reported in May 2026 that the World Bank-administered account was empty while some donations went to a JPMorgan account controlled by the board. No public accounting exists.
- **Contracts:** one provisional construction award (Arkel International, Aug 2026, a small outpost), frozen because Israel has not admitted contractors.

## 1. The Riviera remarks (4 Feb 2025)

**Grade: documented (official text of the remarks).** At the White House press conference with Netanyahu, Trump said the US would "take over the Gaza Strip", "own it" and "level the site", create "economic development", and described "a long-term ownership position". Asked where residents would go meanwhile, he said "the Riviera of the Middle East. This could be so magnificent." This was a spoken proposal. No document, budget or contract followed. Press reports say Press Secretary Leavitt then said Trump was not committing US money for reconstruction and no US troops (search excerpt only; Fortune page not retrievable).

## 2. The 20-point plan, ceasefire and UN resolution 2803

**Grade: documented.** The plan was released on 29 Sep 2025. As reproduced by outlets (the whitehouse.gov page could not be retrieved), its governance point puts Gaza under a technocratic Palestinian committee supervised by a Board of Peace chaired by Trump with Tony Blair named, which "will set the framework and handle the funding for the redevelopment of Gaza"; an economic development plan by a panel of experts and a special economic zone are separate points; the plan says no one will be forced to leave. The ceasefire began 10 Oct 2025. On 17 Nov 2025 the Security Council adopted resolution 2803 by 13-0 with China and Russia abstaining. UN press coverage says the Board and the stabilisation force are authorised to 31 Dec 2027. I could not open the resolution text (docs.un.org returned only a viewer shell), so the operative wording ("transitional administration", funding role) comes from press summaries and from the White House statement of 16 Jan 2026, which says the resolution "endorsed" the plan and "welcomed the establishment of the Board of Peace". Carnegie notes that the charter, unpublished at the time of the vote, does not mention Gaza and sets a broader mandate.

## 3. The Board of Peace: chair, members, mandate

**Grade: documented (White House statements read in full).**

- **Chair:** Trump, ratifying the charter at Davos on 22 Jan 2026. Carnegie reads the charter as giving the chairman, in his personal capacity, an indefinite term and authority over agenda, subsidiary bodies, selection of the Executive Board and budgets; the UK, France, Germany, Italy and Sen. Markey raised concerns. Permanent membership for states contributing $1B in the first year was reported by AP citing an unnamed US official.
- **Founding Executive Board (16 Jan 2026):** Marco Rubio, Steve Witkoff, Jared Kushner, Tony Blair, Marc Rowan, Ajay Banga, Robert Gabriel. Senior advisers: Aryeh Lightstone and Josh Gruenbaum. High Representative for Gaza: Nickolay Mladenov. Stabilisation force commander: Maj. Gen. Jasper Jeffers.
- **Gaza Executive Board:** Witkoff, Kushner, Hakan Fidan, Ali Al-Thawadi, Hassan Rashad, Blair, Rowan, Reem Al-Hashimy, Mladenov, Yakir Gabay, Sigrid Kaag. The Palestinian committee (NCAG) is led by Dr Ali Shaath, who the Israeli authorities have reportedly barred from entering Gaza (The Week, Oct 2026).
- **Blair:** named in the plan; the FT reported he was reconsidered after criticism in Sept 2025 (search excerpt), but the January 2026 statements list him on both boards.

## 4. Pledges, custody and who holds the money

Separate three things:

1. **Announced pledges (19 Feb 2026):** US $10B (use unspecified) and about $7B from Kazakhstan, Azerbaijan, UAE, Morocco, Bahrain, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Uzbekistan and Kuwait, plus troop pledges from Indonesia, Morocco, Kazakhstan, Kosovo and Albania. The Intercept says Board documents show only eight formal state pledges (single source).
2. **Money in an account:** the FT (27 May 2026, five sources) reported the World Bank-administered fund had received nothing. NPR's interview with the FT reporter (read in full) adds that UAE and Morocco donations, and possibly private ones, went to a JPMorgan account to which the board has direct access, with no donor or spending reporting requirement. The board's reply (Arab News, read in full): the fund is "one of many funding mechanisms" not yet used because Gaza has not reached the reconstruction phase; it said it is "not hoarding money in a bank account".
3. **Money spent in Gaza:** none reported; the FT says funds are earmarked for police training and board salaries.

A State Department plan to redirect about $1.2B of aid toward the board's goals appears in one search summary (unverified). A $2.45B six-month roadmap was proposed in Sept 2026 against a Board estimate of about $35B medium-term reconstruction need (search excerpt).

## 5. Proposals circulated by advisers and consultants

**GREAT Trust (Aug 2025).** I read the 38-page deck from a public mirror. It proposes a US-led custodianship, $70B-$100B public investment plus $35B-$65B private, a land-trust and tokenisation funding model, a "$5K/person" relocation package, and named projects including "Gaza Trump Riviera & Islands", "The Elon Musk Smart Manufacturing Zone" and "The American Data Safe Haven". Those labels are concept names; none is a Trump Organization or Musk commitment. Reporting citing the Washington Post says it was prepared by Israeli businessmen including Michael Eisenberg and Liran Tancman, who helped conceive the GHF, with modelling by BCG staff. BCG said it dismissed two partners on 4 June 2025 for unauthorised work, was not paid and would not be, and later said the modelling was unauthorised. The Tony Blair Institute said its staff joined calls but it never authored, developed or endorsed relocation. The White House has not adopted the deck; it described the 27 Aug 2025 meeting as routine.

**Project Sunrise (Dec 2025) and New Gaza (Jan 2026).** WSJ: a 32-slide deck by Kushner, Witkoff and Gruenbaum, about $112B over ten years with nearly $60B of US-anchored grants and guarantees, pitched to Egypt, Turkey and Gulf states, with no named investors. At Davos Kushner showed a phased master plan (New Rafah, coastal towers, data centres, an airport; over $25B for utilities per the Board) and said developer Yakir Gabay volunteered to design it without profit motive. By April 2026 the plan was reported cut back to a Rafah pilot. These are proposals, not commitments.

**Gothams LLC.** The Guardian (via Democracy Now, search excerpt) reported that a US disaster-response firm pitched a Gaza logistics system with a seven-year monopoly and about 300% profit. Its CEO said in Dec 2025 he was halting it; a partner kept talking to White House officials and denied discussing returns; State said no procurement existed. Status: proposal only.

## 6. The Gaza Humanitarian Foundation

**Grade: partly documented.** GHF operated from late May 2025 and announced closure on 24 Nov 2025 (AP). Funding: the State Department approved $30M in June 2025; Reuters reported Witkoff aides Lightstone and Leith told Congress Israel agreed to match it, which Reuters could not verify and Israel did not confirm; GHF says it is a private charity and does not disclose donors, and said an unnamed Western European country gave over $100M (search excerpt). Contractors: Safe Reach Solutions (logistics; McNally Capital holds an economic interest, per Reuters) and UG Solutions (armed security). Witkoff and Huckabee toured a site on 1 Aug 2025. Casualty allegations near the sites are disputed by GHF and the Israeli military. The same planners overlap with later governance bodies: Lightstone is a Board of Peace senior adviser; Eisenberg and Tancman are reported by the FT (secondary summaries) as shaping the new administration, which I could not confirm from the FT itself. No Trump-family firm appears among GHF's funders or contractors.

## 8. Contracts and commitments

- **Arkel International (Louisiana):** provisionally awarded in early Aug 2026 to build a 100 m x 100 m outpost at a former GHF site for about 150 of a planned 500 Moroccan and Kosovar troops. A board official said several awards were near but none was final. No value reported. As of 15 Oct 2026 Israel had not admitted contractors, machinery or materials, so the project was frozen; the board expected movement after the Israeli and US elections.
- **UAE "New Rafah" $1.5B:** a single-source claim (Prospect); no agreement found.
- **Donor agreements, reconstruction contracts, procurement procedure:** none found.

## Denials and context

- Board of Peace: conflict reports are baseless ("no Board of Peace involvement, no Board of Peace decision, and zero demonstrated personal benefit"); a source told the Telegraph business interests were never discussed.
- Affinity: no control over Phoenix's investment decisions. Kushner's lawyer: ethics review found his volunteer service compliant (C15).
- TBI and BCG: see section 5. Israel: no confirmation of GHF funding. White House: Aug 2025 meeting was routine; Feb 2025 no US dollars for reconstruction.
- On the ground: Israel reportedly controls about 60% of Gaza; aid trucks average about 227 a day against a plan minimum of 600; no civilian administration operates inside Gaza.

## Corrections for older checks

- C15: add that the 16 Jan 2026 White House statement lists Kushner on both Board of Peace boards (and a Getty caption relayed by the Jerusalem Post calls him "US Special Envoy for Peace"); the volunteer-status dispute remains.
- C105: add Witkoff's two Board of Peace seats.
- General: "$17B fund" should read "$17B announced pledges"; nothing in the record shows the Riviera remark or the GREAT Trust became policy.
