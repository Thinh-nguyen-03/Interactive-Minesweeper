# C15 — Jared Kushner and Affinity Partners

**Verdict: mostly-accurate** (confidence: medium-high)

## The claim

- Jared Kushner, the president's son-in-law, "has been called the peace envoy by Trump."
- He takes part in negotiations alongside Steve Witkoff "in an unofficial capacity."
- He runs a "multibillion dollar fund called Affinity," which according to the New York Times "has been trying to raise five billion dollars ... from a lot of these Middle Eastern countries."
- Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) "put in two billion dollars" and is "one of his biggest investors"; other Gulf sovereign funds "are expected to be asked as well."
- Because he "does not hold any official title, he is not subject to divestment requirements" and "does not have to file financial disclosures."
- Insinuation: in closed-door meetings "we don't necessarily know if he is arguing on behalf of the U.S. government or on behalf of his own financial interests."

## Evidence, sub-claim by sub-claim

1. **Trump called him "peace envoy." Confirmed.** At the first Board of Peace meeting on 19 Feb 2026, Trump said: "We're making Jared an envoy also, envoy of peace" (Just the News, AOL/Hill coverage). The White House then told NewsNation the role would not be official. Kushner also sits on both executive boards of the Gaza "Board of Peace" (the founding Executive Board and the Gaza Executive Board), announced by the White House on 16 Jan 2026.

2. **He works alongside Witkoff in an unofficial capacity. Confirmed.** The AP, NBC and Bloomberg Law all report that he has no formal title and no Senate confirmation. He has worked with Witkoff on Gaza and Iran talks (including Geneva) and on Russia-Ukraine. The White House says he is "a volunteer and not serving in any official capacity," pays his own expenses and draws no salary. Witkoff, by contrast, is a formal employee and filed a public financial disclosure.

3. **Affinity is a "multibillion dollar fund." Confirmed.** Its Form ADV dated 22 Mar 2026 reports about $6.16B in regulatory assets at end-2025 (Bloomberg rounds this to $6.2B). About 99% comes from non-US clients, mainly Saudi, Emirati and Qatari.

4. **The NYT reported he is trying to raise $5B, largely from Gulf sovereign funds. Confirmed, with a nuance.** The NYT reported in March 2026, citing five people familiar with the talks, that Kushner was seeking "$5 billion or more" for Affinity. Representatives had already met the PIF, and Gulf funds were expected to be approached. Axios (16 Mar 2026) said the fund was not formally in market yet, but talks with the PIF had started because of the PIF's contractual "first look" rights. The House Oversight Democrats' letter of 5 Oct 2026 says public reporting in March 2026 showed he was seeking at least $5B. Some headlines (Dealroom, a later Mediaite piece quoting a September 2026 New Yorker report) say the money was "raised." That has not been confirmed. The wording "trying to raise" is the accurate one.

5. **Saudi PIF put in $2B. Confirmed.** The PIF committed $2B in mid-2021, a few months after Kushner left the White House. The NYT reported in 2022 that the PIF's screening panel objected on 30 Jun 2021, citing "public relations risks" and the "inexperience" of Affinity's management, and that the board chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman overruled it. Correction: the PIF is often called "one of his biggest investors"; it is the **largest** (per Wyden's Senate Finance letter). Other Gulf money is already in: Qatar Investment Authority and Abu Dhabi's Lunate added about $1.5B in Dec 2024 (Bloomberg).

6. **No official title, so no divestment or financial disclosure. Partly accurate.** It is true that Kushner has filed no financial disclosure and has not divested. "Not subject to" those rules is the White House's legal position, not settled law. In October 2026 CREW sued, arguing under the Appointments Clause that he exercises government power without Senate confirmation (with an alternative claim that the power was unlawfully delegated to a private citizen) and asking the court to order disclosure filings and, if it finds a violation, to bar him from continued service. Coverage of the complaint does not use the term "special government employee"; we did not read the complaint itself. The suit names Witkoff, Wiles, Rubio and Hegseth in their official capacities. Kushner's lawyer, Chad Mizelle, calls the suit "without merit." He says outside and government ethics attorneys reviewed Kushner's "volunteer government service" and found it compliant. No ruling was found.

7. **Conflict-of-interest insinuation. Unverified (opinion/insinuation).** The documented fact is that Kushner sought Gulf sovereign money while negotiating for the US in that region. Senator Wyden and Rep. Garcia opened a formal inquiry into this on 19 Mar 2026. No public evidence shows he argued for his own financial interests in any specific meeting. Framing this as an open question is fair. It is not a proven fact.

## Corrections

- PIF is Affinity's largest investor, not merely "one of" the biggest. Qatar's QIA and Abu Dhabi's Lunate are already investors too (about $1.5B, Dec 2024).
- "Not subject to divestment or disclosure" is the White House position. CREW disputes it in litigation filed in October 2026, and no court has ruled.
- The $5B was a solicitation reported by the NYT, not a completed raise. Headlines saying "raised $5B" overstate the record.

## Denials and context

- White House: Kushner is a volunteer with no official capacity, pays his own expenses and takes no salary.
- Mizelle (for Kushner/Affinity): his arrangements comply with ethics requirements; the CREW suit is without merit. He has called earlier Democratic referrals political.
- In Dec 2024 Kushner said: "We preemptively tried to avoid any conflicts, so we don't have to raise capital for the next four years." The 2026 fundraising effort cuts against that pledge.
- The Daily Beast (citing Bloomberg, around March 2026) reported that Affinity's chief legal officer later said the firm had dropped the planned new fundraising vehicle and would not take new capital while Kushner volunteers for the government. That statement is undated in the available coverage and was not independently confirmed. If accurate, it postdates the NYT report and partly defuses the "$5B" point.
- Earlier NYT reporting, relayed by The Media Line, said Qatar and the main Emirati funds had declined to invest in a round. This conflicts with the 2024 QIA/Lunate investment and may refer to a different round.
