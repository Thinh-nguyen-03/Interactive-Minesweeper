# C114: Trump family and associates: business in Israel

**Verdict:** partly-accurate (confidence: medium)

**Question:** What business ties do the Trump family and associates have in Israel or with Israeli companies, and where is there none?

## Summary

Three things are documented; most of the rest is "nothing found".

- **Past licensing project (never built):** in 2006-08 the Trump name was licensed to Crescent Heights for a 70-storey "Trump Plaza" tower in Ramat Gan, with Trump to receive 25% of unit sales. The developer flipped the site and Trump sued. Nothing was built.
- **Hotel talks, nothing signed:** Eric Trump held talks on a Jerusalem hotel and the Sarona tower in Tel Aviv in 2023, and preliminary talks with Nitsba on Sarona in spring 2025. He told the Times in June 2025: "we have no plans at this time". No later news was found as of 17 Nov 2026.
- **An Israeli company with a Trump son as investor:** on 17 Feb 2026 Eric Trump was named a strategic investor in the merger of Tel Aviv-founded drone maker XTEND with JFB Construction Holdings. Unusual Machines, which Donald Trump Jr. advises, and American Ventures (backers include both sons) were co-investors. The merger closed on 3 Sep 2026 and the company trades on the NYSE as XTND. XTEND won a Pentagon contract in Nov 2025, before Eric Trump invested. Nothing documents any influence on an award.

Not found: any Israeli entity or income in Trump's 2025 disclosure (only six Israeli trademark registrations); Israeli investors in World Liberty Financial, Trump Media (including the TAE merger) or American Bitcoin; Israeli dealings by Steve Witkoff or his family; any visit or business statement on Israel by Donald Trump Jr.; Israeli deals tied to Cantor, 1789 Capital or Dominari.

These are private-sector activities of the president's family. No source found shows a deal with the Israeli government, and no authority has made any finding.

## Sub-claims

### 1. Past projects: confirmed (abandoned)
- Globes (2008, read in full): Trump licensed the "Trump Plaza" name to Crescent Heights (Sonny Kahan), 25% of each unit sold plus royalties. Crescent Heights paid about $44M for the Elite lot and sold it to Azorim (Boymelgreen) for about $80M. Trump sued in Manhattan; Crescent Heights' lawyer called it meritless. Ynet (excerpt) says the court rejected the roughly $45M claim.
- Ynet (2016, excerpt): a Netanya hotel and an Ashkelon golf park stayed on paper; a Trump sales office opened in Israel under Ivanka Trump.

### 2. Hotel talks 2023-25: confirmed (talks only)
- Times, Oct 2024 (reproduced on Lockwood's page, excerpt): Jerusalem hotel on the former Foreign Ministry site and the Sarona tower in Tel Aviv; Eric Trump scouted in Feb 2023. His quote: "The deal absolutely would have gotten done if not for Oct. 7." Lockwood said the Trump campaign called off talks weeks before the war.
- Times, June 2025 (via Yeshiva World News, read in full): spring-2025 preliminary talks with Nitsba; Nitsba chairman Haim Tsuff said the brand could help win 12 extra storeys; Nitsba did not respond. Eric Trump: "no plans at this time". He toured Eilat's Princess Hotel and passed.

### 4. XTEND: confirmed (investment); contract link inferred only

Capital at closing is reported differently ($152M Globes; $100M IntelliNews; $110M one trade report), so treat it as unreconciled. The company release names Eric Trump a strategic investor, but XTEND's CEO told DroneXL (3 Sep 2026) that he is "not a direct investor in XTEND and holds no board seat" and his exposure runs through American Ventures.

- Contract: Nov 2025 undisclosed multi-million DoD award; Drone Dominance participant; XTEND was not in Gauntlet I (Bloomberg via DroneXL). Reported Israeli Ministry of Defense partnership and a March 2026 fabrication request.
- Responses: Unusual Machines and Don Jr.'s representatives said he played no role in its Army award; XTEND did not respond to Al Jazeera.
