# C14 — Steve and Zach Witkoff

**Verdict: mostly-accurate (confidence: medium)**

## What the video says (transcript lines 523-533)

The Witkoffs are "much more than co-founders of World Liberty Financial": Zach Witkoff's father, Steve Witkoff, is the US Special Envoy to the Middle East. WLF's largest shareholder is from the Middle East, which raises a possible conflict of interest. "World Liberty Financial now says Steve Witkoff has divested himself of that stake," which the narrator reads as a move to avoid those questions.

## Sub-claims

1. **The Witkoffs co-founded WLF, and Zach is a co-founder.** *Confirmed.* Steve Witkoff co-founded World Liberty Financial with his sons Zach and Alex in 2024. Zach is a co-founder and has been described as CEO. Steve was later listed on WLF's site as "co-founder emeritus."
2. **Steve Witkoff is the US Special Envoy to the Middle East.** *Confirmed, with a nuance.* Trump named him Special Envoy to the Middle East days after the November 2024 election. By 2026 the White House was also calling him "Special Envoy for Peace Missions," a title that covers his Russia–Ukraine work too.
3. **WLF's largest shareholder is from the Middle East.** *Mostly accurate.* The Wall Street Journal reported on 2026-02-01 that Aryam Investment 1 agreed to buy 49% of WLF for $500M. Aryam is an Abu Dhabi vehicle backed by UAE national security adviser Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed. Eric Trump signed the deal in January 2025, about four days before the inauguration. Of the $250M paid upfront, about $187M went to Trump-family entities and at least $31M went to entities tied to the Witkoff family. Aryam got two of the five board seats (Peng Xiao and Martin Edelman of G42). Reporting calls Aryam WLF's largest *outside* shareholder. WLF's site showed the Trump family's equity falling from 75% to 38%, which leaves Aryam's 49% as the largest single block. The buyer's identity was never disclosed by WLF; it came out through the WSJ's reporting.
4. **This is a possible conflict of interest.** *Opinion, but documented concern.* Several Senate Democrats (letters led by Schiff and Slotkin, October 2025) asked about Witkoff's failure to divest. The WSJ also reported that months after the investment the administration agreed to give the UAE access to roughly 500,000 advanced AI chips a year, and that Witkoff was involved in UAE talks. No source establishes that the investment caused any policy outcome.
5. **"WLF now says Steve Witkoff has divested."** *Partly accurate. The attribution is off and the status is disputed.*
   - In May 2025, WLF posted on X that Witkoff was *in the process* of divesting and had "no operational role, no financial interest in WLFI deals."
   - In September 2025, Bloomberg reported, citing the White House, that he had *not yet* divested his crypto assets, including the WLF stake.
   - In October 2025, Senate Democrats said his disclosure showed he still held WLF crypto assets.
   - In February 2026, *White House counsel David Warrington* (not WLF) told CNN that Witkoff "has also divested from World Liberty Financial." WLF's spokesman, David Wachsman, said neither Trump nor Witkoff was involved in the Aryam deal.
   - In September 2026, ABC News reported a disclosure, obtained by a watchdog, showing Witkoff took in about $107M in 2025 from a holding company tied to his WLF stake, up from $34M in 2024. White House spokeswoman Anna Kelly said he had "fully divested."
   - No public divestiture certificate was found, and it is not documented who received his interest. Whether it went to family members is unverified.
6. **The divestment was meant to avoid these questions.** *Insinuation.* Divestment was announced in May 2025, before the Aryam stake became public in February 2026. Avoiding conflict-of-interest questions is the normal reason ethics rules require divestment, so the reading is plausible. The White House says Witkoff recuses himself and does not discuss WLF in official work.

## Corrections
- The divestment claim came mainly from the White House (Warrington to CNN, February 2026; Anna Kelly to ABC, September 2026). WLF's own statement, in May 2025, said only that divestment was "in process."
- Divestment was reported as incomplete through at least September–October 2025. Witkoff also reported about $107M in 2025 income from the WLF-linked holding company.
- Aryam held 49% and has been described as WLF's largest outside shareholder. It is an Abu Dhabi/UAE vehicle linked to Tahnoon, so "the Middle East" means the UAE.
- Witkoff's title by 2026 was also "Special Envoy for Peace Missions."

## Denials and other side
- WLF spokesman David Wachsman said neither Trump nor Steve Witkoff had "any involvement whatsoever" in the Aryam transaction.
- Trump said he was unaware of the investment.
- The White House says Witkoff takes no part in official matters affecting his finances and has fully divested.
- A Witkoff spokesman said the envoy does not discuss WLF while doing his official duties.
