# C71: Who actually bought World Liberty tokens: Zhou, Web3Port, Aqua 1 and foreign wallets

**Verdict: mostly accurate (confidence: medium).** The brief's main facts hold up across several outlets:

- the Web3Port and Aqua 1 purchases;
- the New York Times identification of Guren "Bobby" Zhou;
- the Binance finding that Web3Port was the market maker behind the MOVE dump;
- WLF's own MOVE purchases;
- Reuters' foreign-wallet tally;
- the 75% revenue share that sent most of each purchase to a Trump-family company.

Three parts stay weak:

- **Source of funds.** Nobody has established who funded Aqua 1. The furthest anyone has traced it is about $90M arriving from an account at the OKX exchange.
- **US review.** No US agency is known to have reviewed any of these purchases. Four Senate Democratic letters have gone unanswered in public.
- **"Blacklisted".** Caixin's description of Zhou as "blacklisted" remains unexplained.

This report extends C67 (repeat investors), C42 (insider extraction) and C04 (WLF founding) rather than repeating them. Method note: the NYT, Reuters, Caixin, Daily Maverick, the Senate Banking Committee site and the radio-station copies of Reuters could not be fetched from this environment. Every finding below therefore rests on search-result summaries from several outlets, which are named for each point.

## The chain in one picture

```
Zhou (UK laundering suspect, uncharged)
 ├─ Caduceus (crypto project, ~$7.6M raised, near-worthless by 2024; claimed Bin Zayed Group backing, denied)
 ├─ Web3Port (crypto fund; MOVE market maker, offboarded by Binance Mar 2025)
 │    ├─ $38M MOVE dump via Rentech (Dec 2024)
 │    └─ $10M WLFI (200M tokens), announced 2025-01-27
 └─ BVI Web3Port entity renamed "Aqua 1 GP Limited" (about 2 weeks before 2025-06-26)
      └─ Aqua 1 Foundation ("UAE", not registered with ADGM)
           ├─ funded by about $90M from an OKX account (Mar-Jun 2025)
           └─ $100M WLFI (about $80M seen on-chain) → WLF → up to $75M to DT Marks DEFI (Trump & sons), Witkoff family also benefiting
```

## Connections (sub-claims)

### 1. Web3Port bought WLFI the week after the inauguration (documented)
On 2025-01-27, Web3Port Foundation announced it had bought 200M WLFI for $10M. It called the deal a "long-term partnership" and said more purchases were planned. CB Insights logs it as a round alongside Comma3 Ventures, KuCoin and Sui. Arkham, as cited by the NYT, traced about $20M from a Web3Port-controlled wallet that month (relayed in C67). The $10M figure is Web3Port's own claim.

### 2. Web3Port was the MOVE market maker behind the $38M dump, and Binance offboarded it (documented)
- **The dump:** MOVE listed on 2024-12-09/10. Within about a day, wallets tied to Web3Port and an intermediary called Rentech sold roughly $38M of MOVE (about 66M tokens). The price fell by about 47%.
- **Binance's action:** On 2025-03-18, Binance offboarded the unnamed market maker and froze the proceeds. Movement turned the recovered funds into a $38M USDT buyback.
- **Naming Web3Port:** ZachXBT (via Blockworks) and then CoinDesk named Web3Port as the market maker. Web3Port did not respond to requests for comment.
- **The Rentech contract:** CoinDesk reported that the deal effectively lent about half of MOVE's circulating supply to one counterparty. Movement said it was "led to believe" Rentech was a Web3Port subsidiary. Rentech denies misleading anyone.
- **Fallout:** Co-founder Rushi Manche was suspended, then ousted. Coinbase suspended MOVE trading on 2025-05-15.
- **Correction:** One relayed summary says Web3Port was "expelled in 2023". The documented exchange action is from March 2025. Treat "2023" as an error unless a source shows otherwise.

### 3. WLF bought MOVE the day after Web3Port's WLFI buy (purchases documented; link inferred)
- **WLF's MOVE buys:** On 2025-01-28, Arkham recorded WLF buying about 2.4M MOVE (about $1.85-2M) through CoW Swap, about two hours before Movement announced its developer mainnet. WLF bought more MOVE in February 2025. Arkham later tracked MOVE moving to the custodian Ceffu at a 60-70% loss.
- **Movement's denials:** Manche denied any coordination or insider trading.
- **The swap-deal report:** In February 2025, Blockworks reported that WLF representatives pitched protocols a deal: the protocol buys $10M of WLFI plus a 10% fee, and WLF buys the same amount of the protocol's token. Tron and Movement denied any such swap.
- **Our cross-reference (inferred):** MOVE's market maker bought exactly $10M of WLFI on the 27th, and WLF bought MOVE on the 28th. This matches the reported swap template. **No source asserts that Web3Port's WLFI purchase and WLF's MOVE purchase were linked.** Movement denies a swap, and no one has shown Web3Port acting for Movement in the WLFI purchase.

### 4. Aqua 1: a "UAE fund" with no UAE registration, funded through OKX (documented, mostly Reuters)
Reuters ("Trump's $100 million crypto mystery man", 2025-07-11) reported the following:
- Abu Dhabi Global Market said Aqua 1 "is not registered, licensed, or affiliated" with it.
- The website was created in late May 2025.
- The founder, "Dave Lee", had little public footprint.
- The wallet tagged aqua1.eth received about $90M from an OKX account between March and June 2025. Its only large outflows were two transfers totalling about $80M to WLF.

Cryptopolitan found no UAE Securities and Commodities Authority licence for Aqua 1. The $100M headline figure versus about $80M seen on-chain has not been reconciled publicly. The September 2026 Senate letter repeats the OKX funding point and notes that OKX pleaded guilty to US anti-money-laundering violations.

### 5. Aqua 1 is Web3Port under a new name, run by Zhou (documented reporting; identification rests on the NYT)
The NYT story ran on 2026-08-09, headlined "Trump Crypto Took $100 Million From a Businessman With Red Flags". It was relayed by The Block, Cointelegraph, Unchained, crypto.news, IBTimes UK and Bitcoin.com. Its findings:
- **The rename:** A BVI Web3Port entity renamed itself Aqua 1 GP Limited about two weeks before the 2025-06-26 announcement.
- **Zhou's arrest:** Zhou was arrested in Britain in 2021 on suspicion of money laundering. He has not been charged, and UK officials said the case was still open in late July 2026.
- **Court records:** A February 2024 Court of Appeal judgment confirms that he was released under investigation. A filing in November 2025 accuses him, with five others, of a laundering scheme dating from 2019.
- **Employees charged:** Two longtime employees were reportedly charged in September 2025 (single-source).
- **Earlier career:** Zhou was a director of about 13 UK flooring companies between 2008 and 2019, most now dissolved. His Caduceus project advertised backing from China Merchants Securities UK and the "Bin Zayed Group". Both told the Times those claims were false and unauthorized.
- **Source of funds:** The NYT could not determine where the $100M came from.

Denials: Aqua 1 and Lee denied any Web3Port tie in July 2025. Zhou did not respond to the NYT's many messages. Caixin (2026-08-26) calls Zhou "blacklisted", but its text and the basis for that word remain unverified. Searches of Caixin turned up only an unrelated London bitcoin-laundering case, the Qian Zhimin case.

### 6. How much of each purchase reached Trump-family entities (rule documented; per-purchase amounts mostly our arithmetic)
**The rule:** The Gold Paper (C42) gives DT Marks DEFI LLC 75% of net revenue, including token sales, after a $30M reserve and expenses. Sun's November 2024 purchase pushed sales past that threshold (C04).

**Aqua 1:** The NYT reports up to **$75M** of Aqua 1's $100M went to the Trump-and-sons company, with the Witkoff family also benefiting. This is single-source as relayed. One outlet folds the Witkoff affiliates into the $75M, so the split is unclear.

**Other purchases (our arithmetic, inferred):** Applying the same 75% to the other post-threshold purchases gives these gross ceilings before expenses:

| Buyer | Purchase | Up to (to DT Marks DEFI) |
|---|---|---|
| Web3Port | $10M | about $7.5M |
| DWF Labs | $25M | about $18.75M |
| Justin Sun | $45M (Jan 2025 tranche) | about $33.75M |

No source has published per-buyer payouts for these. Trump's 2025 disclosure and C42 give only the totals.

### 7. Reuters: 36 of the 50 largest WLFI wallets are likely foreign (single-source analysis, relayed)
Reuters' special report "Inside the Trump family's global crypto cash machine" (2025-10-28) included a wallet review, as relayed by Cointelegraph and Cryptopolitan:
- **Foreign wallets:** 36 of the 50 largest WLFI wallets were likely controlled by overseas buyers, holding about **$804M**.
- **US wallets:** Only 4 were US-linked. These include ALT5 Sigma (about $781M) and a WLF security adviser (about $35M).
- **Unknown:** 10 could not be classified.
- **Wider findings:** Reuters put Trump-family crypto income at more than $800M in the first half of 2025. More than a dozen experts reviewed the analysis and called it reasonable.

WLF's lawyer called the valuation and income analysis "inaccurate and misleading". Attribution of wallets is probabilistic. Foreign predominance is also built into the sale design (see 9).

### 8. DWF Labs: another Dubai-based buyer with a manipulation record (documented)
- **The purchase:** DWF bought $25M of WLFI on 2025-04-16 and committed USD1 liquidity (C67).
- **The WSJ report:** In May 2024, the WSJ reported that Binance surveillance staff concluded DWF had done about $300M of wash trading in 2023. Binance found the evidence insufficient, and the lead investigator was let go.
- **Denials:** DWF and Andrei Grachev call the allegations unfounded. Binance says the claims were not "fully substantiated".
- **Inferred overlap:** Web3Port and DWF were both market makers that had been in trouble at Binance, and both bought WLFI in early 2025. No source links the two firms.

### 9. Has any US agency reviewed these purchases? None publicly (documented absence; requests documented)
**WLF's sale design:** The sale ran under Reg D for accredited US buyers and Reg S for non-US buyers. US sales were capped, and roughly 90% of tokens were aimed abroad. WLF says all buyers passed KYC, and more than 85,000 participants were screened. WLF's spokesman David Wachsman told the NYT that the company "maintains a compliance program that meets or exceeds industry standards" and follows all laws.

**Senate Democratic letters:**

| Date | Senators | Addressed to | Subject | Answer due |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2025-11-18 | Warren and Reed | DOJ and Treasury | Accountable.US findings on token sales to illicit-linked wallets | 2025-12-01 |
| 2026-02-13 | Warren and Kim | Bessent, as CFIUS chair | CFIUS review of Aryam's 49% stake (C06) | 2026-03-05 |
| 2026-05-13/14 | Warren and Reed | Bessent and Blanche | WLF partner AB, whose Timor-Leste project involved people later sanctioned in the October 2025 Prince Group action (OCCRP and Guardian) | 2026-05-26 |
| about 2026-09-15 | Banking Committee Democrats | Bessent and Blanche | Aqua 1, Zhou and the OKX funding | 2026-09-29 |

**Outcome:** No public response or opened inquiry was found for any of the four letters. One low-reliability dossier site says no CFIUS review had begun by March 2026 (unverified).

**Our observation (inferred, analysis):** CFIUS reviews foreign investment in US businesses. WLFI tokens are governance tokens, not equity, so token purchases such as Aqua 1's may fall outside CFIUS's reach. Aryam's equity stake would not. No source makes this jurisdictional point about Aqua 1.

### 10. Illicit-actor wallet claims are partly disputed (documented claims; partly rebutted)
Accountable.US (September 2025) said WLF sold WLFI to wallets that had touched:
- a Lazarus-labelled address;
- Nobitex (Iran);
- a ruble platform;
- Tornado Cash (at least 62 users).

Researcher Nick Bax showed that the "Lazarus" link came from a joke "Dream Cash" airdrop contract. One user who was falsely flagged had about $95K of WLFI frozen. The Iran-exchange and Tornado Cash items were not rebutted in what we found. Accountable.US is a progressive watchdog.

## Not connected in the coverage we found

These are our own cross-references. No source asserts any of them.

- **Web3Port's $10M WLFI buy (2025-01-27) and WLF's MOVE buy (2025-01-28) as a reciprocal swap.** Inferred from timing, and from the match with Blockworks' $10M swap template. Movement denies any swap.
- **DWF and Web3Port as linked buyers.** Inferred co-occurrence: both are Dubai or UAE-presenting market makers with Binance manipulation histories. No source ties them.
- **Caduceus's false "Bin Zayed Group" claim and the real Tahnoon-linked Aryam stake (C06).** This is a name coincidence only. No link between Zhou and the Tahnoon network is reported.
- **Aqua 1's OKX funding and OKX's guilty plea.** This is a documented juxtaposition made by the Senate letter. It does not show that OKX knew the source of the funds.
- **Per-purchase Trump-family payouts for Web3Port, DWF and Sun.** Our 75% arithmetic, labelled inferred.
- **CFIUS lacking jurisdiction over token purchases.** Our analysis.

## What this does and does not show

- **What it does show:** WLF's largest single disclosed token buyer was presented as an Emirati fund. Multi-outlet reporting traces it to a Chinese businessman under an active UK laundering probe, using a renamed vehicle of a market maker that Binance had already offboarded. That market maker had already bought WLFI under its own name. Up to three-quarters of the money went to a company owned by the President and his sons.
- **What it does not show:**
  - that WLF knew who stood behind Aqua 1;
  - that the funds were illicit;
  - that Zhou sought or received any US government favour. No one alleges that.

## Denials and context

- **WLF:** says it complies with all laws and runs industry-standard KYC. It disputed Reuters' valuation analysis.
- **Aqua 1 and Lee:** denied any Web3Port tie in 2025. They did not name specific errors.
- **Zhou:** did not respond. He is uncharged. C67 records that his representative disputed the NYT's portrayal.
- **Movement and Tron:** deny any token-swap deal. Movement says it did not know of the Rentech/Web3Port dump. Rentech denies misrepresentation.
- **DWF:** denies the wash-trading allegations.
- **Wallet attribution:** largely probabilistic. Reg S made foreign buyers lawful and expected.
