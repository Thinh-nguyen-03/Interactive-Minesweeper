# C52: Trump-tied lobbyists hired by crypto firms and the SEC enforcement director's exit

**Verdict: mostly-accurate** (confidence: medium)

Each factual element of the brief is documented:
- crypto firms hiring Trump-connected lobbyists;
- the Winklevoss bitcoin gift after the CFTC changed course on Gemini;
- the SEC enforcement director's abrupt resignation and Blumenthal's letter;
- Fairshake's spending.

Three things remain unverified:
- most per-client lobbying fees (Crypto.com's and Gemini's payments to Miller Strategies);
- the reason Margaret Ryan resigned, which rests on anonymous Reuters sources;
- any causal link between lobbying or donations and regulatory outcomes.

## Lead as briefed
This is a follow-up from C43, C22 and C23. The video did not cover these specifics. The parent files already established:
- the SEC closing Crypto.com's probe (2025-03-27);
- the dismissals of Kraken, Coinbase and Binance (C23);
- AP's report on Crypto.com and Jeff Miller (C43);
- Gemini's 1.5M USDC to MAGA Inc. (C43).

The Binance lobbying and pardon story is covered in C36. This file builds on those findings rather than repeating them.

**Method note:** legis1.com, lda.senate.gov and lda.gov could not be fetched from this environment (DNS blocked, proxy 403). The underlying LDA filings could therefore not be read. Findings rest on search-result excerpts from several outlets: Reuters syndications, AP, The Block, Cointelegraph, Decrypt, Bloomberg Law, ThinkAdvisor, Fortune and Axios, plus Senate letter URLs seen in search results.

## Sub-claims

### 1. Crypto.com hired Miller Strategies (Jeff Miller). Verdict: confirmed
- **Established in C43:** AP (Dec 2025) reports that Crypto.com ramped up payments to Miller from late 2024.
- **What Miller lobbied:** Per the disclosures, he lobbied the White House and the SEC.
- **Who Miller is:** A GOP fundraiser and finance chair of Trump's inaugural festivities.
- **Response:** Crypto.com says Miller had no involvement in the SEC probe.
- **Fee:** The total Crypto.com paid Miller is unverified.

### 2. Gemini hired Miller Strategies. Verdict: mostly-accurate
- **The hire:** Legis1 reports that Gemini Space Station Inc., a first-time lobbying client, retained Miller Strategies on "issues around cryptocurrency regulation". The filing names no specific bill.
- **Lobbyists:** Jeffrey Miller and Jessica Anne Mandel. Mandel previously lobbied for Crypto.com.
- **Filing record:** A Senate LDA client record (ID 69134) reads "Baughman Kroup Bosse PLLC on behalf of Gemini Space Station, Inc.", with Miller Strategies as registrant.
- **Not available:** The registration date and fees could not be read.
- **Other tie:** Miller is reported as a member of the Winklevoss-funded Digital Freedom Fund PAC, alongside Donald Trump Jr. and David Sacks.
- **No link to the CFTC reversal:** No source ties Miller's work to the CFTC's change of position on Gemini.

### 3. Kraken and Blockchain.com hired Ballard Partners. Verdict: confirmed
- **The hire:** Reuters (Douglas Gillison, Jan 24, 2025) reports that Payward (Kraken) and Blockchain (US) Inc. hired Ballard in Dec 2024. Together they paid $60,000 for the final weeks of 2024.
- **Blockchain.com:** It had not used a DC lobbying firm in six years.
- **Ballard's growth:** The firm signed 40 new clients after the election.
- **Ballard's Trump ties:**
  - Brian Ballard is a longtime Trump fundraiser.
  - Susie Wiles and Pam Bondi are former Ballard colleagues.
- **Kraken's response:** "we work with a number of lobbying firms in our key markets."
- **Later spending:** Legis1 puts Kraken's 2025 lobbying at about $2.3M, spread across in-house staff and four outside firms, Ballard among them. A Ballard-specific 2025 figure was not found.
- **Outcome:** The SEC dismissed its Kraken suit with prejudice on 2025-03-27 (C23). No link between that dismissal and the hire is documented.

### 4. Binance hired Trump-connected lobbyists. Verdict: confirmed (see C36)
- **Ches McDowell (Checkmate Government Relations):** About $450K for Sept 2025 "executive relief" lobbying. McDowell is a friend of Trump Jr.
- **Teresa Goody Guillén:** About $290K, from roughly Feb 2025.
- **Outcome:** CZ was pardoned in Oct 2025.
- **Response:** McDowell says Trump Jr. did not help.

### 5. Fees and outcomes are documented. Verdict: partly-accurate
- **Documented fees:**
  - $60K to Ballard (Kraken and Blockchain.com combined);
  - about $450K and $290K for Binance's lobbyists.
- **Missing fees:** The Crypto.com and Gemini payments to Miller were not found.
- **Firm-level scale:**
  - Miller Strategies reported about $12.9M firm-wide in Q2 2025 (NOTUS).
  - Ballard led K Street with about $88M in 2025 (Bloomberg Government, Legis1).
- **Outcomes:** These are favorable to the firms (dismissals, closures, the pardon). They are not shown to result from the lobbying.

### 6. The Winklevoss twins gave $10M+ in BTC to MAGA Inc. shortly after the CFTC joined Gemini's vacatur bid. Verdict: mostly-accurate
- **The motion:** On May 27-28, 2026, the CFTC under Mike Selig and Gemini jointly moved in SDNY (1:22-cv-04563-AKH) to vacate the Jan 2025 $5M consent order.
  - The CFTC said the complaint "should not have been filed".
  - Selig said the twins were "politically targeted".
- **The gift:** On June 19, 2026, 23 days after the motion, each twin gave 79.35 BTC (more than $5M each) to MAGA Inc., which liquidated it.
  - The total is about $10.02M, so "about $10M" is more accurate than "$10M+".
  - Some outlets double-counted the liquidation entries.
- **Criticism:**
  - Warren (June letter) said the motion showed a CFTC "beholden to political pressures".
  - Ex-chair Tim Massad called it "extraordinarily unusual".
  - Texts released by Brian Quintenz show Tyler Winklevoss raising the litigation while Quintenz was being considered to lead the CFTC.
- **Outcome:** Judge Alvin Hellerstein denied the motion on **Oct 15, 2026**. He held that an agency's change of view is not grounds for relief under Rule 60. The paid penalty and the injunction stand.
- **No documented link:** Nothing ties the gift to the CFTC decision.

### 7. The SEC enforcement director resigned suddenly in March 2026. Verdict: confirmed
- **Who:** Margaret "Meg" Ryan, a former senior judge on the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Armed Forces.
- **Tenure:** Named on Aug 21, 2025; started on Sept 2, 2025; resigned on **Mar 16, 2026**.
- **No stated reason:** Neither the SEC nor her letter gave one.
- **Succession:**
  - Sam Waldon became acting director again.
  - David Woodcock (Gibson Dunn, former head of the SEC's Fort Worth office) took over on May 4, 2026.
- **Timing:** About 11 days before her exit, the SEC ended its Justin Sun case. Rainberry paid $10M and the claims were dismissed.

### 8. She left over crypto or Trump-linked cases. Verdict: unverified
- **Reuters account:** Citing anonymous sources, Reuters reported that Ryan wanted tougher action in fraud matters, including ones tied to Justin Sun and Elon Musk. Atkins and other Republican appointees resisted.
- **Counter-view:** An SEC enforcement official said shifting crypto guidance and pending legislation complicated the Sun case.
- **SEC statement:** Decisions are made "based on facts, the law, and policy — not politics." The SEC also credited Ryan with a "critical course correction".
- **Ryan:** She has given no public reason.

### 9. Sen. Blumenthal's inquiry. Verdict: confirmed
- **The letter:** On Mar 30, 2026, Blumenthal, Ranking Member of the Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations (PSI), wrote to Atkins. He cited reports that leadership blocked Enforcement from pursuing parties close to Trump, and possible "preferential treatment for financial partners of President Trump."
- **What he asked for, by Apr 13:**
  - records on crypto settlements since Jan 20, 2025, including companies tied to Justin Sun and Changpeng Zhao;
  - a list of matters where the director's recommendations were overruled.
- **Warren's letters:** She wrote the same day and again on Apr 15, with an Apr 28 deadline.
- **Responses:**
  - The SEC declined to comment.
  - Binance says it has not discussed matters with Atkins.
- **No reply found:** No SEC reply to either senator was found.

### 10. Fairshake's spending. Verdict: mostly-accurate
- **War chest:** About $193M cash on hand in Jan 2026, counting affiliates Defend American Jobs and Protect Progress.
  - It included $25M from Coinbase in 2025, plus money from Ripple and a16z.
- **Spending:**
  - about $150.8M by Oct 20, 2026 (Bitcoin Magazine);
  - more than $170M for crypto PACs overall (Fortune, Nov 4, 2026, citing Tech Influence Watch).
- **Primaries:** Every Republican primary winner it backed was Trump-endorsed, and its ads touted Trump rather than crypto (Decrypt).
- **General election:** Results were mixed. Juliana Stratton won in Illinois despite about $10M in crypto PAC spending against her, and the Ohio Senate spending also failed.
- **Trump link:** Fairshake is bipartisan, and no Fairshake money to Trump was found.
- **Final totals:** Unverified until post-election FEC reports.

### 11. Implied quid pro quo. Verdict: unverified
- **What is documented:** Timing and access.
- **What is not:** No record shows that a lobbying contact or donation was exchanged for a regulatory outcome.
- **Cutting against it:** The Gemini vacatur failed in court despite the CFTC's support.

## Corrections
- **Gift size:** It was about $10.02M (2 × 79.35 BTC), not clearly "more than $10M".
- **Vacatur outcome:** The motion was denied on Oct 15, 2026. One outlet's Feb 2026 date is wrong.
- **Ryan's reason:** The "clash" explanation for her exit comes from anonymous sources only.
- **Fairshake:** It is bipartisan and gave no money to Trump. Its Trump connection runs through the Trump-endorsed candidates it backed.

## Denials and other side
- **Crypto.com:** Miller had no role in the SEC probe. Miller did not comment.
- **Kraken:** It uses many lobbying firms.
- **CFTC (Selig):** The Biden-era case politically targeted the twins.
- **SEC:** Its decisions are not political. It declined to comment on the senators' letters.
- **Binance:** It has had no recent engagement with SEC commissioners.
- **Sector-wide context (C43):** The SEC's easing extended to firms with no Trump ties.
