# C75: Congo minerals diplomacy (Boulos, Gentry Beach and the firms that benefited)

**Verdict: partly-accurate (confidence: medium)**

This is a second-layer hunt built on C66, C65 and C60. Those files already establish three things:
- Massad Boulos holds his role.
- His business is SCOA Nigeria.
- KoBold credited him for its Manono deal.

This file asks a narrower question: who got, or sought, which Congolese asset under the US minerals-for-security framework, and what ties each party has to the Trump family.

## Bottom line

**The diplomacy and Beach's pursuit are documented.**
- Boulos fronted the talks that produced the June 27, 2025 DRC-Rwanda peace agreement and the December 4, 2025 Washington Accords.
- Gentry Beach is Don Jr.'s friend of more than 20 years and was Trump's 2016 finance co-chair. Through America First Global, he led a consortium with Mercuria that sought the M23-held Rubaya coltan mine.

**The assets that actually changed hands went to other firms.** None of them has a Trump-family tie that we could document:
- **Chemaf** went to Virtus Minerals. Reuters found Virtus overstated its mining experience.
- **Manono** went to KoBold Metals.
- **A 40% stake in Glencore's DRC mines** is covered by an MOU with the DFC/ADQ-backed Orion consortium.

**Three parts of the brief do not hold up as written:**
- **Beach and Chemaf:** we found no source linking Beach to Chemaf.
- **UBA:** the bank financed a rival bidder, Buenassa, not a winner.
- **Boulos:** no source shows a Boulos-family stake in any DRC asset.

## Connections, with evidence grades

### 1. Boulos and the minerals-for-security architecture. Grade: documented. Verdict: confirmed
- **The agreements:** Rwanda and the DRC signed a peace agreement in Washington in late June 2025 (Reuters). On Dec 4, 2025, Trump hosted the signing of five documents: the peace agreement, a regional framework, a US-DRC Strategic Partnership, a US-Rwanda economic framework and a security MoU (Fox News, AP).
- **The asset reserve:** Under the partnership, Kinshasa names assets for a "Strategic Asset Reserve". Qualifying projects must guarantee control by a US person and send offtake to the US (Egmont).
- **Beach's billing:** Africa Confidential calls Boulos and Beach the "prime movers" of the business-infused talks. This is a single-source characterisation.

### 2. Gentry Beach and Rubaya. Grade: documented. Verdict: confirmed
- **The proposal:** Reuters reported in June-July 2025 that Beach's America First Global and Mercuria sought the rights to Rubaya. They proposed a majority stake, with Kinshasa keeping about 30%, output routed via a Kigali smelter, and possibly more than $500M of investment.
- **Beach's response:** He confirmed his interest but gave no details. He has run Congo mining ventures since 2004.
- **The mine:** It is held by AFC/M23, which the UN says takes at least $800K a month from it.
- **The US shortlist:** Kinshasa put Rubaya on the asset shortlist presented in Washington on Feb 5, 2026 (Reuters, Feb 19, 2026).
- **Status:** We found no signed deal through Nov 2026.
- **The family:** Sources told Africa.com the Trump family has no direct involvement.

### 3. Beach's use of the Don Jr. relationship, and the cease-and-desist. Grade: single-source (WSJ, about Oct 2025, via the Daily Beast, Raw Story and Ground News; not fetched)
- **In Congo:** Beach toured Central Africa seeking concessions as "economic diplomacy" that he credited to Trump. Congolese officials say he showed them text messages with Don Jr.
- **The letter:** Don Jr.'s lawyer sent a cease-and-desist letter in August 2025. Beach denies receiving it. The WSJ says texts show he confirmed receipt.
- **Pakistan:** Officials there asked Don Jr. whether Beach spoke for the administration, and Don Jr. said he does not do business with him.
- **Documented separately:**
  - Beach led an investor delegation to Islamabad on Jan 30, 2025, as White-Bridge Global, pitching minerals and real estate (Arab News, Dawn).
  - Beach and Don Jr. had earlier business ties, which the Trump Organization called "strictly personal" (AP, 2018).
  - Beach met Tshisekedi and US Ambassador Mike Hammer in Kinshasa (NZ Herald).

### 4. Beach and Chemaf. Grade: not found. Verdict: unverified
The Chemaf bidders on record are:
- Virtus with Orion and India's Lloyds Metals (the winner)
- Buenassa, financed by UBA
- Gecamines, with its own counter-offer
- Norinco, earlier (blocked in 2024)

None of the coverage names Beach.

### 5. Virtus Minerals and Chemaf: the firm Reuters says overstated its experience. Grade: documented. Verdict: confirmed
- **Who Virtus is:** A Delaware company founded by US special-forces and intelligence veterans.
- **What Reuters found (Apr 21, 2026):** Virtus claimed a Congo track record from running a processing plant. The plant was idle, and Virtus never owned it.
- **How the deal unfolded:**
  - **Jul 2025:** the Orion/Virtus bid was reported.
  - **Feb 2026:** an MOU set out about $600M in Orion financing, plus $200M from Virtus and Lloyds (Mongabay).
  - **Feb 23-24, 2026:** Tshisekedi replaced Gecamines' chair and CEO. Three Reuters sources said their opposition to Virtus was part of the reason. Reuters said any link to the US talks was unclear.
  - **Apr 1, 2026:** Under Secretary Jacob Helberg announced the approval: about $30M for the equity, plus assumed debt (partly owed to Trafigura) and a $750M capex commitment.
- **US position:** State "fully supports" Virtus. Africa Intelligence (single-source, via Africa Is a Country) describes an "aggressive pressure campaign" by Trump officials on Virtus's behalf.
- **Family ties:** We found no Trump-family, Boulos or Beach tie to Virtus. This is state favouritism toward a chosen US firm, not a documented family benefit.

### 6. UBA's financing. Grade: single-source (Semafor, Jan 26, 2026). Verdict: confirmed, but not as briefed
- **What it was:** UBA drafted a $50M working-capital facility for Buenassa's bid for Chemaf. Buenassa was a rival to the US-favoured Virtus. There is no report that the facility closed.
- **Boulos:** No source ties UBA or Elumelu to Boulos. The fact that both have a Nigerian base is our own weak inference.

### 7. KoBold and Manono. Grade: documented. Verdict: confirmed
- **The deal:** KoBold signed a framework with AVZ on May 7, 2025, then deals with Kinshasa, pledging more than $1B. KoBold thanked Rubio, Boulos and Tshisekedi.
- **White House pressure:** White House officials met AVZ's managing director on Jan 21, 2026, and urged a sale to "an American company" without naming one (Bloomberg).
- **The dispute:** AVZ says the deals breach ICSID interim orders.
- **Family ties:** None found.

### 8. Orion CMC, ADQ and Glencore. Grade: documented, with an inferred cross-link
- **The consortium:** Orion CMC launched in Oct 2025 with $600M each from DFC and Abu Dhabi's ADQ ($1.8B in total).
- **The Glencore MOU (Feb 3, 2026):** It covers 40% of Mutanda and KCC, with an enterprise value of about $9B. Deputy Secretary Landau praised it.
- **The Gertler snag:** Sanctioned billionaire Dan Gertler's royalty claims are holding the deal up. A US official said his royalties must go to Congo in any deal easing his sanctions (Bloomberg).
- **The ADQ cross-link (inferred):** C57 documents ADQ as formerly chaired by Tahnoon bin Zayed, the figure behind the 49% WLF stake (C06). So the same Abu Dhabi power centre co-invests beside the US government in the flagship US Congo vehicle. No source connects this to WLF.

### 9. Erik Prince and Vectus. Grade: documented. Verdict: confirmed
- **The contract:** Trump ally Prince signed a five-year contract with Kinshasa in Dec 2024 (UN Group of Experts). It runs through companies partly based in the UAE and is worth $100-200M (GI-TOC).
- **What it covers:** It began with mining tax collection in Katanga (Reuters, Apr 2025) and shifted to security, including drone support around Uvira.
- **The ambush:** In Sept 2026, one contractor was killed and Tim Kennedy was wounded (Reuters, Oct 2026).
- **Positions:** State says it has no DRC contract with Prince. Prince and Vectus did not respond.
- **Link to Boulos:** We found none beyond working for the same client in the same war.

### 10. Trump-linked lobbyists for Kinshasa. Grade: documented (FARA) plus single-source
- **Ballard Partners:** Registered for the DRC (Florida Politics reports a one-year, $900K contract framed around climate). A 2026 FARA amendment shows April 2025 consulting for the Ministry of Communication via Asterix LLC.
- **Ballard's ties:** Brian Ballard is a Trump fundraiser, and Susie Wiles formerly worked at the firm (C64).
- **Tshisekedi's lobbying deal:** Africa Confidential reports he signed one aimed at a minerals deal with Trump.
- **Global Witness:** It lists both the DRC and Rwanda among countries paying Trump-linked firms.

## Not connected in the coverage we found
- **Beach and Chemaf:** not found. The brief's pairing appears to be an error.
- **Boulos family and any DRC asset (Rubaya, Chemaf, Manono, Glencore):** not found, consistent with C65 and C66.
- **UBA or Buenassa and Boulos's Nigerian network:** inferred only from a shared country. No source asserts it.
- **ADQ in Orion CMC and Tahnoon's WLF stake as one channel:** inferred from co-occurrence across C06, C57 and this file.
- **Prince's Vectus contract and US diplomacy:** inferred overlap of client and timing. State denies any contract.
- **Rwanda's Trump-linked lobbying and Beach's Kigali-smelter routing:** inferred. Each element has its own source, but no source links them.
- **Virtus, KoBold or Orion and the Trump family:** none found.

## Corrections to the brief
- The brief pairs Beach with Chemaf. In the record, Beach is tied to Rubaya, and Chemaf went to Virtus/Orion.
- UBA's $50M was a draft facility for a losing rival, Buenassa.
- Beach's firm is America First Global in the Congo coverage and White-Bridge Global in the Pakistan coverage.
- The "Washington Accords" date to Dec 4, 2025. The June 2025 document was the initial peace agreement.

## Denials and context
- **Beach:** denies claiming a Trump-family partnership and denies receiving the letter.
- **Don Jr.:** says he is not in business with Beach.
- **The Trump family:** sources say it is not involved in Rubaya.
- **State Department:** backs Virtus, KoBold and Orion as open industrial policy, and denies any Prince contract.
- **KoBold:** declined to comment on the White House talks with AVZ.
- **M23:** calls the US-DRC pact unconstitutional.
- **Analysts (Egmont, RANE):** note that the reserve mostly concerns Katanga.
- **What this does not show:** steering assets to named US firms is not evidence of personal gain by officials.
