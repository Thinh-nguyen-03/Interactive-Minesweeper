# C107: Scott Bessent and Paul Atkins: holdings and divestiture deadlines

**Verdict:** partly-accurate (confidence: high)

**Question:** Did Scott Bessent and Paul Atkins divest on time, and which of their decisions touch former clients or holdings?

## Summary

- **Bessent missed his deadline. That is documented.** The Office of Government Ethics (OGE) told Senate Finance Chairman Mike Crapo on 11 Aug 2025 that Bessent "has failed to timely comply with certain terms" of his ethics agreement. The deadline was 28 April 2025. His first certification (signed 2 May, submitted 5 May 2025) ticked "No" on divestitures and said they had "been initiated but not yet completed for all assets".
- **How it was resolved:** there were three amendments. In May 2025 he added two omitted resignations. In June 2025 Treasury's ethics official found that three private holdings (Presight, Half Acre WSW, Cambrian Biopharma) posed no conflict risk, so he kept them under recusal. In December 2025 he admitted he "was unable to divest ... within 90 days ... due to their illiquid nature" and set a new date of 15 Dec 2025. Under that amendment his partnership exchanged its North Dakota farmland for a **non-recourse promissory note from Glacier Ridge, LLP valued at $5M-$25M**. He signed his final certification on 5 Dec 2025. His 2026 annual report (OGE-certified 22 Sep 2026) shows the note.
- **Atkins met his deadlines. That is also documented.** He was confirmed on 9 April 2025 and signed his certification on 18 July 2025, before the 14 Aug due date. It confirms all resignations and divestitures were done on time, including Patomak Global Partners, Securitize, Anchorage (Anchor Labs) and Off the Chain. It records no waivers and no authorizations, and two OGE Certificates of Divestiture exist. Who bought Patomak, and for how much, is still not public.
- **Decisions touching holdings, former clients or friends:**
  - Bessent's China soybean deal and farm aid
  - the Argentina swap and investors who are his former Soros colleagues
  - SEC dismissals under Atkins, including the Devon Archer case, from which Atkins reportedly recused

  The decisions themselves are documented. **That any of them served the official's own interests is an allegation.** No authority has made such a finding. The one review on record went the other way: the Treasury Inspector General found "no knowing violation" over Bessent's spouse's mis-reported JPMorgan stock.

This extends C52, C54 and C65, and corrects them in a few places (see Corrections).

**Evidence rule applied:** OGE's documentation makes the missed deadline a fact. A conflict counts only as an allegation unless an authority has found one. Holding an asset, or making a decision that helps a former employer, client or friend, is not by itself a violation.

## The rules that apply

- **18 U.S.C. 208:** no personal and substantial participation in a *particular matter* that directly and predictably affects the official's own or imputed financial interests, unless a waiver or exemption applies. Broad policy, such as trade strategy, is usually not a particular matter.
- **5 C.F.R. 2635.502:** a one-year bar on specific-party matters involving a former employer or a client served in the past year, unless the official is authorized to take part. It does not cover friends or former colleagues.
- **Ethics agreements:** divestiture within 90 days of confirmation (5 C.F.R. 2634.802). OGE may grant an extension only for "unusual hardship". OGE notifies the Senate committee when an official misses a deadline (Program Advisory PA-19-09).

## Sub-claims

### 1. Bessent's disclosure and ethics agreement: confirmed
- **Nominee 278e** (signed 28 Dec 2024, read in full):
  - Key Square Group LP valued at over $50M, with $2,787,000 in fees and incentive allocations and a $1,041,134 guaranteed payment.
  - Key Square fund positions in SPY, QQQ, RSP, Treasury bills and open currency positions, each "over $50,000,000", plus index put options worth $1M-$25M.
  - iShares Bitcoin Trust: $250K-$500K.
  - **High Plains Acres LLP**, holding farmland in Burleigh, Kidder, Eddy, Benson and Wells counties, North Dakota, valued at **$5,000,001-$25,000,000**, with $100,001-$1,000,000 of rent tied to corn and soybean prices.
- **Ethics agreement** (9 Jan 2025):
  - Resign from Key Square on confirmation and divest within 90 days, with a one-year specific-party bar.
  - Divest 25 holdings within 90 days, including the farmland partnership, the Key Square funds, IBIT, Verizon, ADM, GLD, Greengage, Aperion, Presight, Half Acre and Cambrian.
  - Redeem three hedge-fund interests quarterly by September 2025.
  - Keep family LLCs and trusts under recusal.
- He was confirmed on 27 Jan 2025.

### 2. OGE's letter to the Senate on the missed deadline: confirmed
- **11 Aug 2025:** Deputy Director for Compliance Dale Christopher wrote to Chairman Crapo. Bessent had to comply by 28 Apr 2025 and certify by 5 May 2025, and his certification indicated "partial compliance". Amendments of 2 May and 5 Jun had been sent to the committee, and another was needed, with "no timeline for resolution". OGE also told Treasury to stress that avoiding conflicts with his holdings was his "personal responsibility".
- **13 Aug 2025:** Treasury said he would finish by 15 Dec 2025. It said the assets were "illiquid and ... not readily marketable" and that he was recused from matters affecting them, with a screening memorandum given to his front office.
- **25 Jul 2025:** OGE had already posted a statement next to his certification. It said he still had to submit a certification confirming divestiture.
- The letters document the missed deadline. They do not find that he took part in any conflicting matter.

### 3. What happened next: confirmed (filings)
- **278-T signed 14 Jul 2025:** 24 sales from 28 Jan to 30 Jun 2025.
  - Key Square Group and Key Square Partners II: 11 Feb-25 Mar 2025.
  - GLD and Verizon: 4 Mar 2025.
  - Roxo Energy Funding IV: 25 Mar 2025.
  - Old Farm Partners, Totem Macro, Castle Hook and others.
- **Amendment of 5 Jun 2025 and DAEO letter of 15 Jul 2025:** he keeps Presight RV Co-Invest, Half Acre WSW (Waterloo Sparkling Water) and Cambrian Biopharma because there is no liquid market and they pose no conflict risk. The 2026 report still shows them.
- **Certification signed 3 Oct 2025:** the Totem holdings were finalised.
- **278-T signed 5 Dec 2025:** Totem Management Seed sold 23 Sep 2025 ($250K-$500K); Greengage sold 27 Nov 2025.
- **Amendment signed in early December 2025** (forwarded by Treasury on 8 Dec):
  - Greengage and Aperion to be divested, and the farmland sold for a promissory note, by 15 Dec 2025.
  - No equity to be taken back if the buyer defaults.
  - Recusal from matters affecting the buyer's ability to repay, and from specific-party matters involving the buyer, until the note is paid.
- **Final certification signed 5 Dec 2025:** "All required divestitures are now completed." No waivers.
- **2026 annual report** (signed 9 Jun 2026, OGE-certified 22 Sep 2026):
  - Aperion sold 2 Dec 2025; ADM sold 4 Mar 2025.
  - High Plains Acres now holds bank accounts and a **"non-recourse note exchanged for farmland" from Glacier Ridge, LLP ($5M-$25M)**.
  - Bessent is listed as a partner of High Plains Acres from 11/2025.
- **Result:** the farmland was exchanged about 7.5 months after the original deadline. He still has an economic interest in it through a seller-financed note, and the filing calls that note non-recourse. Who is behind Glacier Ridge is not public in what we found.

### 4. "I'm actually a soybean farmer" and the December announcement: confirmed
- **October 2025:** he told ABC News "I'm actually a soybean farmer" (AP).
- **30 Oct 2025:** he announced that China would buy 12 million metric tons of US soybeans this season and 25 million tons a year for three years.
- **7 Dec 2025:** on CBS's "Face the Nation" he said "I actually just divested it this week as part of my ethics agreement". A $12B farm aid package was announced the next day.
- At the time, The New York Times (quoted by Tri-State Livestock News) found no record of a divestment. It also reported that North Dakota records listed his husband, John Freeman, as managing partner of High Plains Acres. The OGE filings now show the note exchange.

### 5. Conflicts with China, farm or tariff decisions: unverified (allegation)
- **The complaints:** Campaign Legal Center (9 Jul 2025), then Democracy Defenders Fund and CLC (13 and 20 Aug 2025), asked OGE and the Treasury Inspector General to investigate a possible 18 U.S.C. 208 violation. They argued that talks with China and Canada while he held the farmland created a "serious risk" of conflict.
- **Context:** the July complaint said Verizon and ADM were undivested. Filings posted later show both were sold on 4 Mar 2025, though they were not public when CLC wrote.
- **Findings:** no OGE, Inspector General or court finding of a violation was found. The only finding on record is narrower. His spouse's JPMorgan stock had been reported as a bank deposit; it was sold in July 2025 once noticed, and a $200 late fee was paid. The Treasury OIG "determined that there was no knowing violation" of the Ethics in Government Act or 18 U.S.C. 208, and Treasury's ethics official concurred.

### 6. Argentina package and former colleagues' investments: unverified (allegation)
- **The package:** Treasury announced support in September 2025 and in mid-October signed a $20B exchange stabilization agreement with Argentina's central bank. Argentina drew $2.5B and repaid it in December 2025. Treasury's 20 Jan 2026 letter says the Exchange Stabilization Fund holds no pesos. Sen. Warren (4 Feb 2026, read in full) notes that the agreement has not been shown to be terminated.
- **The investors:**
  - Mother Jones and Popular Information reported that Rob Citrone (Discovery Capital), a friend and former Soros colleague of Bessent, held large Argentine positions. Argentine press reported that he lobbied Bessent.
  - The New York Times reported that Stanley Druckenmiller was also invested. Druckenmiller said he had no communication with Bessent about Argentina, and a Citrone spokesman declined to comment (seen only in a search summary).
  - Neither man is a documented former client. The one-year bar covers Key Square, not former colleagues.
- **Oversight:** Warren and Shaheen (17 Dec 2025) and Warren (6 Nov 2025, 4 Feb 2026) asked for the terms and the list of interested investors, and Treasury did not provide the agreement. No IG or GAO review of the swap was found. The Treasury OIG's January 2026 report is a routine audit of the fund's financial statements.

### 7. Atkins's disclosure and ethics agreement: confirmed
- **Ethics agreement** (20 Mar 2025, read in full):
  - Resign as Patomak CEO, forfeit post-confirmation distributions and divest within 90 days.
  - One-year specific-party bar on Patomak, and on each former client for one year after his last service to it.
  - Divest Securitize options (he had resigned from Securitize in Feb 2025) and Pontoro equity within 90 days.
  - Divest 30 listed holdings (Attachment A, including Anchor Labs, Inc.) and 18 sector funds within 90 days, and 92 private funds (Attachment B, including Off the Chain LP) within 120 days.
- **Kept, under recusal:** TAMKO Holdings stock; his spouse is a TAMKO Building Products employee with profit-sharing. He also stays trustee of seven family trusts.
- **Earlier resignations:** The Digital Chamber and its Token Alliance in December 2024.
- **Reported values** (his nominee report is released only on request):
  - Securitize options $250K-$500K, Anchorage $250K-$500K, Off the Chain $1M-$5M; about $6M in crypto-related assets (Fortune, The Block).
  - He and his wife are worth at least $327M, and the Patomak stake is worth at least $25M (Bloomberg Law).
- **Disclosed Patomak clients:** FalconX, Virtu, Temasek, the Options Clearing Corporation, Bank of America, Barclays and Exxon Mobil. Four clients were withheld as confidential.

### 8. Atkins's deadlines: confirmed met
- **Deadlines:** confirmed 9 Apr 2025, so the 90-day deadline fell on 8 Jul 2025 and the 120-day deadline on 7 Aug 2025.
- **Certification** (signed 18 Jul 2025, read in full):
  - All resignations completed before he took office, and all divestitures completed within the time specified.
  - 278-Ts filed 28 Jun and 18 Jul 2025.
  - Patomak distributions forfeited.
  - No waiver and no 2635.502(d) authorization.
- **Other OGE records:** Certificates of Divestiture OGE-2025-099 (posted 20 May 2025) and OGE-2025-160 (posted 25 Jul 2025), a 278-T posted 18 Nov 2025 and a 2026 annual report. All are released only on request and were not read.
- **Open questions:** Warren (5 Aug 2025) asked for the buyer and price of each divested asset. She also asked how he could certify before the 120-day date. No reply was found. A reported $25M-$50M sale price was seen only in a search summary and is unverified.

### 9. SEC decisions touching former clients or holdings: unverified (allegation); one recusal reported
- **Devon Archer:** the SEC stipulated to dismiss its civil case on or about 18 Sep 2025, after Trump's pardon. The NYT, via Political Wire and Cryptonews, reported two things: Archer's lawyers paid Atkins $1,450 an hour as an expert witness in 2018, and an Atkins spokesperson said he recused from the decision. The NYT article itself was not read. Because the 2018 work falls outside the one-year former-client bar, this recusal goes beyond the agreement.
- **Dealer cases:** in mid-2025 the SEC dismissed seven dealer-registration cases, including the one against John Fife (White & Case, read in full). One search summary called Fife a disclosed Atkins client; that is unconfirmed.
- **Securitize:** its SPAC registration was declared effective on 5 Jun 2026, after the one-year bar lapsed. See C65.
- **Crypto dismissals** (C23, C43, C52): none of the firms is a documented Patomak client. FalconX is a client, and no FalconX action was found.
- **Trump Media litigation:** defendants sought Patomak records (Barron's). Trump Media denies engaging Patomak.
- **Bottom line:** the SEC publishes no recusal list. No waiver is on record, and no authority has found that Atkins took part in a matter he should have avoided.

## Corrections

- **Claimed (C65):** Bessent said in October 2025 that he had divested the farmland. **The record shows:** in October he said "I'm actually a soybean farmer". He announced the divestment on 7 Dec 2025, and the land was exchanged for a $5M-$25M note under a December amendment.
- **Claimed (C65):** "about $25M" of farmland. **The record shows:** the value is reported as a range of $5,000,001-$25,000,000.
- **Claimed (C54):** about $1M of Anchorage equity and $2.5M-$5M of Securitize options. **The record shows (as reported):** $250K-$500K each, plus $1M-$5M in Off the Chain. C65 already uses these figures.
- **Claimed (C54):** "up to ~$327M" in family assets. **The record shows:** Bloomberg Law reported "at least $327 million".
- **C54 and C65:** both are right that the Patomak buyer is not public. Add that Atkins certified full compliance on 18 Jul 2025 with no waivers, and that OGE issued him two Certificates of Divestiture.
- **CLC (July 2025):** it said Verizon and ADM were undivested. **The record shows:** both were sold on 4 Mar 2025, which became public later.
- **Minor date points:** Atkins's 90-day mark was 8 Jul 2025, not 9 Jul as in Warren's letter. OGE gives Bessent's deadline as 28 Apr 2025, and CLC used 27 Apr.
- **C52:** no correction needed. Its Atkins items (Ryan's exit, Blumenthal's letter) are unaffected.

## Denials and context

- **Treasury:** Bessent worked to divest from the start, the remaining assets were illiquid, and he was recused from matters affecting them. In August 2025 Bessent said he had sold about 96% of the required assets (NYT, cited by DDF/CLC). Treasury's ethics official certified each of his filings as compliant.
- **Sen. Wyden:** said the Secretary was picking and choosing which ethics requirements to follow (Banking Dive). No statement from Sen. Crapo was found.
- **Argentina:** Bessent says the package was not a bailout and that Treasury profited, and the draw was repaid. Druckenmiller denies any contact with Bessent on Argentina.
- **SEC:** Atkins is upholding his ethics commitments. His spokesperson says he recused in the Archer case. Trump Media calls the Patomak subpoena effort meritless.
- **Not read:** Atkins's 278 filings are released only on request, and the NYT full texts were not read.
