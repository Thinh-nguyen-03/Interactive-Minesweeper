# C106: David Sacks: holdings, ethics waivers, and AI and crypto policy

**Verdict:** mostly-accurate (confidence: medium)

**Question:** What did David Sacks's ethics waivers let him keep, and which of the policies he shaped affect those holdings?

## Summary

The two White House waivers (both read in full) document the core of this brief:

- **Status.** Sacks served as an unpaid special government employee (SGE), "expected to work ... for 130 days or less in a one-year period". He remained co-founder and general partner of Craft Ventures. Both March 2025 waivers already say he agreed to co-chair PCAST.
- **Crypto waiver (5 March 2025).** Signed by White House Counsel David Warrington under 18 U.S.C. 208(b)(1).
  - Sold: more than $200M of digital-asset positions, at least $85M of them his own. That includes all liquid crypto before 20 January 2025 and his Bitwise 10 fund (BITW) on 22 January 2025.
  - Kept: Craft-fund stakes in 20 digital-asset companies, worth 3.714% of *his* total assets. The largest are BitGo (2.468%) and Lightning Labs (1.131%).
- **AI waiver.** Also signed by Warrington, but undated on its face. Its text places it in March 2025, and the NYT says both waivers came "in March". It was posted on whitehouse.gov in a June 2025 folder.
  - Pending sale: Meta, Amazon, TSMC and xAI.
  - Kept: tiny AI stakes (Groq, Etched, Runway and others) plus hundreds of software and hardware holdings. The waiver covers those too, "out of an abundance of caution"; Palantir is listed as a direct SaaS holding at 1.819%.
- **What the reporting adds.** The New York Times (30 Nov 2025) counted 708 tech investments, 449 AI-related and 20 crypto. It links his work on the GENIUS Act, chip exports and the AI Action Plan to holdings such as BitGo and Anduril, and to his All-In podcast.
- **What is not established.** No authority has found a violation. Whether he exceeded the 130-day limit was never answered with a day log. He left the czar post on 26 March 2026, saying his non-consecutive 130 days were used up. The waivers give no dollar values or sale dates, so no gain or loss from his service can be computed.
- **Responses.** Sacks, his spokeswoman and the White House deny any conflict. Sacks calls the Times story a "nothing burger" from a "hoax factory". The Times stands by it.

**Evidence rule applied.** Holding an asset, or working on a policy that helps a company in one's portfolio, is not by itself a violation. Section 208 bars participation in particular matters affecting one's financial interest unless a 208(b)(1) waiver finds the interest "not so substantial". Both waivers make that finding for matters of general applicability only, not for matters involving specific parties.

This extends C34 (the original Sacks check). C65 and C68 contain no substantive Sacks findings. Related checks: C11 (GENIUS and CLARITY), C53 (Witkoff and Sacks oversight requests), C54 and C62 (BitGo).

## Sub-claims

### 1. SGE status, unpaid, still a Craft partner: confirmed
- Both waivers say he serves as an SGE, chairs the President's Working Group on Digital Asset Markets (crypto waiver) and "agreed to co-chair" PCAST [1][2].
- The NYT says he is unpaid [9]. Warren notes that SGE status lets him keep being paid by Craft (18 U.S.C. 209) [5].
- Appointment was announced on 5 December 2024, and he started on or around 20 January 2025 [5].

### 2. Exceeded the 130-day limit: unverified
- The 17 September 2025 Warren–Stansbury letter (read in full) [5][6] set out the arithmetic: if he worked every calendar day, his 130th day was 29 May 2025; if every business day, 25 July 2025.
- The letter cites OGE guidance that any day with more than de minimis work counts. It quotes the White House as counting only days he "engage[d] in work as the AI and crypto czar".
- It asked for his SF-50 and a list of days worked by 1 October 2025. No reply was found.
- On 26 March 2026 he said his "non-consecutive 130-day stint" was over and moved to PCAST co-chair [14][15][16].
- No day count, SF-50 or finding has been published. How the 365-day window was applied in 2026 is unknown.

### 3. The crypto waiver: confirmed
From the 8-page memo and Schedules A–C [1]:

**Sold**
- All liquid crypto (BTC, ETH, SOL) before 20 January 2025, and his BITW position on 22 January 2025.
- His Coinbase and Robinhood stock.
- His limited-partner stakes in Multicoin and Blockchain Capital. Craft sold its Multicoin and Bitwise Asset Management interests.
- Total: more than $200M, of which at least $85M was his.
- He also began selling limited-partner stakes in Sequoia funds (1.2% of his assets, set to close on 31 March 2025) and about 90 other VC funds.

**Kept**
- Schedule A: Craft-fund stakes in 20 companies, 3.714% of his total assets, including BitGo, Lightning Labs, dYdX, Chia, Dapper Labs, Fold, River and Talos.
- Schedule B: Beldore Capital Fund (an S&P 500 exchange fund, 5.07%) and AL Venture (0.03%).
- Schedule C (sale pending): Animoca, Republic, CoinList and the Sequoia funds.

**Reasoning, limits and process**
- Reasoning: the interests are "not so substantial" relative to his assets, and the job needs industry expertise.
- Limits: no new digital-asset holdings, and Craft is to be instructed likewise. He must consult an ethics official if the funds' digital-asset holdings pass 5% of his assets.
- The memo states that SGEs cannot get certificates of divestiture, so his sales were taxable.
- OGE was "consulted"; the decision is "within the sound discretion of the agency".

### 4. The AI waiver: mostly-accurate (date corrected)
The 39-page PDF [2]: the memo was read in full, the schedules were skimmed.

**Date**
- The memo is undated on its face. Its text says TSMC and Amazon will be sold "by March 31, 2025", Meta by the end of Q2, and the Sequoia stakes "by the end of this month". That points to March 2025.

**What it covers**
- Schedule A, direct AI interests (all pending sale): Meta 1.762%, Amazon 0.034%, TSMC 0.035%.
- Schedule B, Craft-fund AI interests: 0.045% in total, mostly xAI (pending sale), plus Groq, Etched, Runway and Stability AI at about 0%.
- Schedules C–F, SaaS interests ("just over 13%") and hardware interests (under 2%). Palantir is listed as a direct SaaS interest at 1.819%. The waiver says many SaaS companies "do not currently use" AI materially but "many ... are likely to at some point in the future".

**Claims and limits**
- He started or finished divesting "over 99%" of potentially conflicting holdings, and the Craft funds "over 97%".
- Craft must consult the White House Counsel's office before closing new investments.
- The waiver does not cover specific-party matters or matters affecting Craft Ventures beyond the listed fund interests.

### 5. Hundreds of AI and crypto-linked holdings retained: mostly-accurate
- The NYT's count is 708 tech investments, 449 AI-related and 20 crypto. It says 11 were designated "A.I. Interests" and 438 were classed as software or hardware [9]. This is the NYT's own classification; the underlying lists are in the waivers.
- The waivers give percentages, not values or sale dates. His OGE 278e is not public [4].
- **BitGo:**
  - The S-1 footnote (excerpt) [19] shows 7,421,851 Class A shares in three Craft I funds, and says Sacks makes their investment and voting decisions.
  - The NYT gives 7.8%; a tracker gives about 6.4% after the IPO [20]. Neither was checked against the filing.
  - At the $18 IPO price, about $134M (our arithmetic) [21].

### 6. GENIUS Act and BitGo: mostly-accurate (overlap documented, benefit not shown)
- He promoted the GENIUS Act (signed 18 July 2025) and called it "momentous" [9].
- BitGo is his largest retained digital-asset interest [1]. The NYT says BitGo marketed its services as fitting the new law.
- His spokeswoman says the Act "contained no specific benefit for BitGo".
- Legislation is a general-applicability matter that the waiver covers. The CLARITY Act stalled in the Senate (see C11).

### 7. Profit from the strategic bitcoin reserve: unverified
- Warren's 6 March 2025 letter [3] tied Craft's former Bitwise stake to the five tokens Trump named on 2 March, and asked when the sales happened.
- The waiver [1] answers part of this: crypto was sold before 20 January 2025, BITW on 22 January, and Craft's Bitwise interest is listed as sold.
- No trading by Sacks or his associates around the reserve announcements is documented.

### 8. Chip exports and Nvidia: unverified (allegation; role single-source)
**What the NYT reports (anonymous sources) [9]:**
- He worked to remove Biden-era export limits.
- In May 2025 he struck a UAE deal for about 500,000 chips; analysts put Nvidia's potential sales at up to $200B.
- He joined Jensen Huang in a July 2025 Oval Office meeting before Trump cleared sales to China.

**Other reporting:** Bloomberg reports he backed the December 2025 decision on H200 sales to China [22][23].

**What his holdings show:**
- Nvidia is not listed in either waiver.
- The AI waiver lists about 0% stakes in the chip start-ups Groq and Etched, and he held TSMC (0.035%, pending sale).

**Responses:**
- His spokeswoman: "None of his holdings benefited from the Emirates deal."
- Nvidia says Commerce Secretary Lutnick was its main contact. Formal licences came from Commerce/BIS (C34).

**Oversight:** State OIG referred a Warren–Slotkin request to its Office of Investigations on 12 December 2025 [7][8]. The reply speaks of "senior administration officials" and does not name Sacks, and says the OIG does not comment on criminal allegations. No outcome has been found.

**Grade:** no Nvidia holding is documented and no source shows a chip decision benefiting a company he holds, so the benefit claim is an allegation without a finding.

### 9. AI Action Plan and defence start-ups: unverified
- The plan (23 July 2025), drafted in part by Sacks, promoted military AI. The NYT says his filings show stakes in Anduril, Firestorm Labs and Swarm Aero.
- Anduril won a $159M Army contract in September 2025. Anduril says the relationship pre-dated the plan. Sacks's spokeswoman called military AI an "obvious idea" [9].
- He also helped shape the December 2025 order against state AI laws [13].
- All of these are general-applicability matters, which the AI waiver covers.

### 10. All-In podcast and the July 2025 summit: unverified (allegation; largely single-source, disputed)
**What the NYT reports [9][10]:**
- All-In proposed $1M sponsorships including access to a private reception (from a proposal the NYT viewed).
- Susie Wiles required a co-host (two anonymous people), and the Hill and Valley Forum co-hosted.
- Visa and the NYSE sponsored; amounts were not disclosed.
- About $21M in 2025 conference ticket sales (the NYT's estimate), and a $1,200 tequila.

**Response from Sacks's side [10][11]:**
- All-In lost money on the summit, no VIP reception took place, and no access to Trump was offered.
- Sacks gives up AI and crypto sponsorship revenue but shares in ticket and tequila sales.

### 11. MAGA Inc. dinner co-hosted while czar: mostly-accurate
- Several outlets report a 5 May 2025 $1.5M-per-plate "Crypto & AI Innovators Dinner" for MAGA Inc., with Sacks co-headlining, based on an invitation obtained by Axios [24][25]. Warren's letter of the same day says he was "reportedly co-hosting" it [4].

## Corrections to older checks
- **C34, Craft's crypto stakes.** C34 says "about 3.7% of its assets". The waiver figure, 3.714%, is a share of Sacks's own total investment assets, not of Craft's assets.
- **C34 and C54, AI waiver date.** They date it to "June or July 2025" or "mid-2025". The memo is undated; its text points to March 2025, and it was posted in a June 2025 folder.
- **C34, PCAST.** C34 says he "became PCAST co-chair" in March 2026. He was designated co-chair in 2025 (both waivers, and Warren's March 2025 letter). In March 2026 PCAST became his sole role and its members were named.
- **C34, the 130-day limit.** C34 treats his departure "at the 130-day limit" as settled. It rests on Sacks's own statement; no day count has been published.
- **C34, "OGE approved".** This is Sacks's characterization. The documents say OGE was only consulted.
- **C34, BitGo 7.8%.** This is the NYT's figure. The S-1 shows 7,421,851 shares in Craft I funds, voted by Sacks, and the waiver puts BitGo at 2.468% of his assets.
- **C34, divestment date.** The relationship dated "2025-01" should be refined: liquid crypto was sold before 20 January, BITW on 22 January, and some private holdings were still being sold in March 2025.
- **C65 and C68.** No substantive Sacks content, so no correction is needed. C68's Nvidia section could cross-reference this check.

## Denials and other side
- **Sacks:** he divested hundreds of millions at a cost to himself; OGE approved; the story is a "nothing burger".
- **Jessica Hoffman (spokeswoman):** the narrative is false, and he complied with SGE rules.
- **Liz Huston (White House):** he addressed his conflicts and was recused until he divested or received a waiver.
- **Clare Locke:** All-In lost money on the summit, and the Huang dinner never happened.
- **Critics:** Kathleen Clark calls them "sham ethics waivers". Warren and others question his day count and the breadth of the waivers.

## Audit (second review, 17 Nov 2026)

**Result: upheld.** Both White House waivers were re-downloaded and re-read from the page images.
- **Crypto waiver:** signed by David Warrington on 5 March 2025. It records "over $200 million" divested, "at least $85 million" of it his own. Schedule A totals 3.714% of his total assets, with BitGo at 2.468% and Lightning Labs at 1.131%. It sets the "130 days or less in a one-year period" expectation, and it says OGE was "consulted".
- **AI waiver:** also signed by Warrington, but undated. Its text points to March 2025: "by March 31, 2025", and the Sequoia stakes divested "by the end of this month". SaaS interests are "just over 13%", and Palantir is 1.819%. OGE was consulted.
- **Other checks:**
  - Warren's 6 March 2025 letter addresses him as PCAST chair.
  - His 26 March 2026 departure and the move to PCAST co-chair (with Michael Kratsios) are confirmed. Some outlets say the White House announced it on 25 March.
  - No finding by any authority was found through November 2026.

**Changes:**
- Sub-claims 8 (chip exports) and 10 (All-In) were regraded to unverified under the evidence rule for officials' holdings. Each alleges a benefit for which no authority has made a finding.
- Added that the State OIG reply does not name Sacks.
