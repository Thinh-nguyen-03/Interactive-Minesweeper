# C33: Corporate settlements and donations to Trump's library and ballroom

**Verdict: mostly accurate (confidence: high on amounts and recipients; low on any documented causal regulatory benefit)**

This is a follow-up lead, not a claim made in the video. The video touches it once (transcript line 655), when Molly White says crypto exchanges "have sponsored the ballroom that Trump wants to build." The brief asks whether companies with business before the government paid entities linked to Trump. It lists the settlement amounts, the recipients, the ballroom donors, and any regulatory outcome that followed.

**Method note:** Direct fetches failed. Wikipedia would not resolve from this session, and many news sites block fetching. The findings below come from search-result summaries that agree across several outlets, including AP syndications, CBS News, NPR, Al Jazeera, Variety, Bloomberg Law and The Block. They also draw on primary documents from Senator Elizabeth Warren's office and Public Citizen. The run hit its web-search budget before two lines of inquiry were finished:
- any settlements made in 2026;
- the regulatory outcomes for Tether and the Winklevoss twins.

## Evidence by sub-claim

1. **ABC News/Disney paid $15M toward the Trump library. Confirmed.**
   - The settlement was signed on 13-14 Dec 2024, before the inauguration. It ended Trump's defamation suit over George Stephanopoulos saying Trump had been found "liable for rape."
   - The $15M was described as a "charitable contribution" to a future presidential foundation or museum. It was to be paid into an escrow account run by the law firm of Trump's lawyer, Alejandro Brito. ABC also paid $1M in legal fees and posted an editor's note of regret.
   - In 2026, ABC told Democratic lawmakers the $15M was **still in that escrow account**, where it had been since December 2024, awaiting release to the foundation.

2. **Meta paid $25M. Confirmed.**
   - The deal was reported on 29 Jan 2025. It settled Trump's lawsuit over his account suspension after January 6.
   - About $22M is earmarked for the presidential library. The rest covers legal fees and other plaintiffs.
   - Meta had also given $1M to the inaugural committee.

3. **Paramount paid $16M. Confirmed.**
   - The settlement was announced 1-2 July 2025. It ended the "60 Minutes" lawsuit over a Kamala Harris interview, in which Trump had sought $20B.
   - The money goes to the future library after the plaintiffs' fees and costs are paid. Paramount said "no amount will be paid directly or indirectly to President Trump." There was no apology. Paramount agreed to release transcripts of future interviews with presidential candidates.
   - Trump claimed there would be a further roughly $20M in PSAs or "advertisements." Paramount says the settlement includes no PSAs. **That extra payment is unverified.**

4. **X paid about $10M. Mostly confirmed. The amount comes from press reports.**
   - The payment was reported in February 2025, first by the WSJ and then by CNBC/NBC. Trump's lawyer John Kelly confirmed the matter was "resolved." I found no court filing giving the figure.
   - The recipient is not clearly stated in the search results I have. Warren, Blumenthal and Stansbury sent a letter to X "re Trump library fund donation," and they count X among companies that sent "up to $63M" to the library fund. That points to the library fund, but the route is not independently confirmed.

5. **YouTube/Alphabet paid $24.5M, with $22M for the White House ballroom. Confirmed.**
   - The court filing is dated 29 Sep 2025. Trump's $22M share goes to the nonprofit **Trust for the National Mall**, earmarked for building the ballroom. The other $2.5M goes to co-plaintiffs, including the American Conservative Union and Naomi Wolf. The filing says the deal is not an admission of liability.
   - Correction to the brief: this money went to the ballroom, not the library.

6. **The settlement money reached entities linked to Trump personally. Confirmed in substance. The details are murky.**
   - The original library fund was incorporated in Florida on 20 Dec 2024 by a lawyer named Jacob Roth. It was dissolved in September 2025 after failing to file an annual report.
   - A second nonprofit, the Donald J. Trump Presidential Library Foundation Inc., was incorporated in May 2025.
   - In April 2026, Warren, Blumenthal and Stansbury said up to $63M in settlement money was sent to the dissolved fund. They said at least three companies could not or would not say where it went. A follow-up probe of the Foundation is dated 16 Jul 2026.
   - The Miami Herald reported that the Foundation's IRS filing projected $50M, then $300M, then $600M in contributions. It also reported $50M received in December 2025.
   - The Foundation took title to Miami Dade College land in February 2026. The land was valued at more than $67M and was deeded for $10. That transfer is in active litigation.
   - Lawmakers allege the money is "missing." That remains an allegation. I found no response from the Foundation.

7. **The ballroom donor list includes Coinbase, Ripple, Tether America and both Winklevoss twins. Confirmed.**
   - The White House released a list of about 37 donors on 23-24 Oct 2025. It includes Amazon, Apple, Google, Meta, Microsoft, Micron and Lockheed Martin, plus Coinbase, Ripple, Tether America, and Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss listed separately.
   - **No amounts were disclosed.** The White House says the list is incomplete. The funding contract with the Trust for the National Mall lets donors stay anonymous (Reuters, April 2026).
   - The project's estimated cost rose from $200M to $300M and then $400M. Reported amounts raised are between about $300M (Trump) and nearly $400M (Campaign Legal Center).
   - The National Trust for Historic Preservation sued in December 2025. It has refused DOJ's April 2026 demand to drop the suit. I found no ruling on the merits.

8. **Paramount obtained regulatory approval from the FCC for its Skydance merger. Confirmed as a sequence of events. Causation is not established.**
   - The FCC approved the roughly $8B Skydance-Paramount deal on 24 Jul 2025. The vote was 2-1, with Chair Brendan Carr in the majority and Commissioner Anna Gomez dissenting. Approval came about three weeks after the settlement and more than 250 days into a review that normally takes 180.
   - The conditions included Skydance's written commitments to have a CBS ombudsman for at least two years, to adopt no DEI programs, and to ensure "viewpoint diversity."
   - Gomez called the conditions "unprecedented government intrusion." Sen. Ron Wyden called the settlement a "bribe." Sen. Warren called for a criminal investigation.
   - Paramount says the lawsuit was "completely separate from, and unrelated to" the merger review.

9. **Other donors and payers obtained regulatory outcomes. Partly accurate, mostly from advocacy-group analysis.**
   - Coinbase: the SEC dismissed its case with prejudice on 27 Feb 2025. See C23.
   - Ripple: the SEC and Ripple dropped their cross-appeals on 7 Aug 2025. The judgment against Ripple, a $125M penalty plus an injunction, still stands, because Judge Torres refused to cut it. That is a partial benefit only.
   - Gemini: the SEC closed its investigation in 2025. See C23.
   - Tether: I did not verify any specific regulatory benefit in this run. Its link to the GENIUS Act is covered in C11.
   - *Audit, Meta, Google and Disney:* the record shows continued or adverse federal action, not favors.
     - **Meta.** Trump's FTC took its Instagram/WhatsApp case to trial. Judge Boasberg ruled for Meta on November 18, 2025, and the FTC said in January 2026 that it would appeal [A1][A2].
     - **Google.** Judge Mehta's September 2025 search remedies rejected a Chrome divestiture, and DOJ and the states cross-appealed in February 2026 seeking it [A3]. Judge Brinkema's September 2026 ad-tech remedy declined to order an AdX sale [A4]. These are court rulings.
     - **Disney/ABC.** The FCC under Carr opened a DEI probe in March 2025, ordered early renewal filings for ABC's eight owned stations in April 2026, and opened an equal-time probe of The View. Disney calls these actions unconstitutional, and no ruling was found as of late July 2026 [A5][A6].
   - Public Citizen's "Ballroom Billions" report (June 2026) says 19 of 27 identified corporate donors received $338B in federal contracts over about 5.5 years. It says 14 face federal enforcement actions, or had them suspended under the Trump administration. Those are an advocacy group's figures and I did not check them against USASpending.gov.
   - The Meta, Google and Disney outcomes cited by critics, such as the antitrust cases, were not verified in this run.

## Corrections
- The brief lumps the YouTube money in with the library. YouTube's $22M went to the ballroom through the Trust for the National Mall.
- The headline settlement figures are larger than the amounts earmarked: Meta's $25M includes about $22M for the library, and YouTube's $24.5M includes $22M for the ballroom. Lawyers' fees and co-plaintiffs took the rest.
- No source says the money was paid to Trump personally. It went to a nonprofit or an escrow account. The "personally linked" framing rests on Trump controlling the library foundation and the ballroom project.
- ABC's $15M had reportedly still not been released from escrow as of 2026.

## Denials and other side
- Paramount denies any link between the settlement and the merger approval. It says it settled to avoid legal costs.
- Carr's FCC treated the merger review and the "60 Minutes" settlement as separate matters.
- None of the settlements admits liability. Paramount and ABC say Trump received nothing personally.
- The White House says the ballroom is privately funded at no cost to taxpayers.
- The donors deny the donations are tied to regulatory outcomes. No company I found has acknowledged any such link.
- No quid pro quo is documented. The claim of regulatory benefit rests on timing, on critics such as Wyden, Warren, Gomez and Public Citizen, and on the pattern across donors.

## Audit (second review, Nov 2026)
Result: **upheld**.
- The settlement amounts, the recipients and the ballroom donor list were re-confirmed by AP, Fortune and The Block.
- **Meta, Google and Disney gap.** It is now filled (see sub-claim 9), and the finding cuts against an implied quid pro quo for those three payers.
- **2026 settlements.** No new corporate settlement routed to the library or ballroom was found. The notable 2026 settlement is a different kind. On May 18, 2026 Trump dropped his $10B suit against the IRS. In return, DOJ created a $1.776B "anti-weaponization fund" from the Treasury Judgment Fund, run by a commission the attorney general appoints. Trump, his sons and the Trump Organization get an apology but no payment [A7][A8]. It is added as a lead.
- Tether-specific outcomes were not re-checked.
