# C13: ALT5 Sigma $1.5B WLFI treasury deal

**Verdict: mostly accurate (medium confidence)**

The video's core facts hold up: the $1.5B deal, the reported ~$500M and ~$90M payouts, the ~94% stock collapse and the rename to AI Financial Corporation. Two parts go further than the record. "Run by the same people" overstates the overlap, which is at board level only. And the payout figures are press estimates of what the families were entitled to, not documented payments.

## What the video says (transcript lines 501-519)
- When "World Liberty went to the stock markets," the SEC is the regulator, and the president appoints its leadership.
- WLF announced a $1.5B deal with ALT5 Sigma. It reportedly made $500M for a Trump-family-owned entity and $90M for the Witkoff family.
- Molly White: ALT5 "is run by the same people who run World Liberty Financial," which creates a dual incentive to pump WLFI with retail money.
- ALT5 stock "got crushed down about 94% in one year." It rebranded from crypto to AI and is now "AI Financial Corporation."

## Evidence by sub-claim

### 1. The $1.5B deal: confirmed
- Announced Aug 11, 2025 [1][2][3].
- Structure: 200M ALT5 shares at $7.50, split between a registered direct offering and a private placement.
- WLF was lead investor. It contributed about $750M of WLFI tokens (priced at $0.20) in exchange for shares and warrants. The other ~$750M was cash from outside investors.
- The cash went mostly to buying more WLFI from WLF. The treasury was to hold about 7.5% of WLFI supply.
- Wording note: WLF did not itself list on the stock market. It went through an already-listed Nasdaq shell-like company.

### 2. About $500M to a Trump-family entity: mostly accurate
- The WSJ (Aug 31, 2025) said the deal "stands to earn over half a billion dollars" for the Trump family [4].
- CNBC (June 9, 2026) said company disclosures show the family was "entitled to roughly $500 million" [13].
- The entity is DT Marks DEFI LLC, which holds a contractual right to 75% of WLF's net token-sale proceeds.
- The number is an estimate after fees. No filing itemizes the actual cash paid.
- A Reuters-based tally gives a higher figure (~$987M) for total token-sale proceeds across all sales, so estimates differ by scope.

### 3. About $90M to the Witkoff family: partly confirmed
- Coverage of a February 2026 WSJ feature says "more than $500 million flowed to Trump entities and $90 million to Witkoffs through this structure" [8][9][19].
- I could not access the WSJ text itself, so this rests on secondary reporting.
- The same WSJ reporting puts the Witkoffs' total earnings from WLF at $200M or more.

### 4. "Run by the same people": partly accurate (overstated)
- On the board, WLF CEO Zach Witkoff became ALT5 chairman, and Eric Trump was announced as a director [1].
- WLF co-founder Zak Folkman later joined as a director. Some outlets describe Trump and Folkman as board observers.
- Day-to-day management did not come from WLF:
  - CEO Peter Tassiopoulos was suspended on Oct 16, 2025 [6].
  - Acting CEO Jonathan Hugh and COO Ron Pitters were removed in Nov 2025 [7].
  - President Tony Isaac then became acting CEO.
- "Controlled at board level by WLF insiders" is accurate. "Run by the same people" is a stretch.
- The dual-incentive point is analysis, but it is grounded: the same insiders sit on both sides of the WLFI token sale.

### 5. Stock down ~94% in a year: confirmed
- Reported declines range from 90% to 96%, depending on the dates used:
  - Protos: $8.97 before the deal to $0.35, about 96% [16].
  - Bloomberg Law (July 30, 2026): a "90% rout" [15].
  - Other outlets: about 92-95%.

### 6. Rebrand to AI Financial Corporation: confirmed
- The name changed on Apr 28, 2026, through a short-form merger (8-K) [10].
- The stock began trading as Nasdaq: AIFC on Apr 29, 2026 [11].
- The filing says the company does not currently offer AI-enabled services. The "crypto to AI" characterization is fair.
- Q1 2026 results: a $271M net loss, driven by a ~$348M markdown on its WLFI holdings, plus a going-concern warning [12]. The company later said that risk had been "substantially mitigated."

### 7. SEC conflict insinuation: unverified (opinion)
- POGO reports the SEC has not opened a probe, despite a Nevada shareholder suit alleging "illegality" and "unjust enrichment" [17].
- Gizmodo reports the suit was later withdrawn with prejudice [18].
- Former regulators quoted by the WSJ said the deal likely complies with securities law if fully disclosed, although such deals once drew scrutiny [4].
- No evidence of interference was found. This part is insinuation, not a documented fact.

## Corrections
- WLF did not "go to the stock markets." Already-listed ALT5 raised the money, and WLF was the lead investor, paying in tokens.
- The overlap between ALT5 and WLF is at board level: Zach Witkoff is chairman, and Eric Trump and Zak Folkman are directors or observers. The CEOs were not WLF people.
- The $500M and $90M are journalists' estimates of entitlements, not audited payments.
- The ~94% drop is consistent with reported declines of 90-96%.

## Denials and other side
- **Trump Organization:** Eric Trump and Don Jr. have "no involvement in ALT5" and no visibility into the company.
- **White House:** denies conflicts of interest. It says the president's assets are in a trust run by his children and that the companies operate independently.
- **WLF:** rejects allegations of impropriety and says it followed the required procedures.
- **ALT5 (Aug 2025):** denied reports of an SEC probe into investor Jon Isaac.

## Audit (second review, Nov 2026)
Result: **upheld**. Re-checked the AIFC rename and ticker dates, the $500M and $90M figures, and the stock decline. The company release confirms the rename (2026-04-28) and the ticker change (2026-04-29). The Daily Beast and IBTimes describe the $500M as an entitlement or estimate, and the stock at about 66 cents in mid-2026 is consistent with a drop of more than 90%. The $90M Witkoff figure still traces only to secondary relays of the WSJ, and other Witkoff totals (≥$200M; ABC's ~$107M for Steve Witkoff from a WLF-affiliated holding company in one year) measure different things. A filing-summary site mentions a Nasdaq listing deficiency (cure date 2026-12-28); I did not verify it, so it is not added.
