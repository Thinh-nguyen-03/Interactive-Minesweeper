# C103: Insider-trading oversight: requests, probes and outcomes

**Verdict:** mostly-accurate (confidence: medium)

## The claim

What has been done about the insider-trading allegations of this term (from 20 January 2025)? This covers requests from members of Congress and state officials; any SEC, CFTC, Justice Department, Office of Government Ethics (OGE), inspector-general or state investigation and its outcome; inquiries by exchanges and prediction-market platforms; changes in enforcement staffing and policy; and what law applies to a president, officials, family and friends. The verdict rates how well this record is documented. It does not rate whether anyone traded illegally.

## Summary

- **The requests are well documented.** Members of Congress sent at least ten letters between April 2025 and July 2026. They went to the SEC, its inspector general, GAO, the Pentagon inspector general, DOJ, state attorneys general and the House Financial Services chairman. Most of the letters were read in full.
- **Public responses are mostly silence.**
  - No investigation of the 9 April 2025 trading has been announced by the SEC, an inspector general, a state or the House Financial Services Committee.
  - In February 2026 House Democrats wrote that they had "received no substantive response" in nearly a year, and that GAO says it cannot compel information.
  - The SEC declined to comment on the 2026 letters.
- **Enforcement that did happen is in prediction markets.** It was brought by the CFTC and DOJ, and in each case the platform first detected the trades:
  - a soldier was charged over bets on the Maduro operation and has pleaded not guilty;
  - settled CFTC orders were made against a White House teleprompter operator and against George Santos.
- **The CFTC oil-futures probe is open.** It covers trades placed before the Iran posts. Its enforcement director confirmed it is open after Bloomberg reported, citing anonymous sources, that a referral had been retracted. No charges have been filed.
- **No finding about Trump, his family or senior officials.** No authority has identified a trader in the April 2025 or Iran-related trades or made such a finding. That is not exoneration, and the letters are allegations and questions, not findings.
- **Enforcement capacity shrank.** These figures are documented; no motive is asserted here.
  - SEC Enforcement lost 18% of its staff in FY2025, according to GAO.
  - CFTC staff ended 2025 about 21% below the ten-year average, with nearly 80% fewer enforcement actions (NPR, citing OPM data and agency reports).
  - DOJ disbanded its crypto enforcement team. NBC reported, citing anonymous sources, that the Public Integrity Section would be cut to about six people.

This builds on C58 (the market data and the first letters), C52 (the SEC Enforcement Director's exit) and C38 (the Polymarket probes closed in July 2025). The market data itself is covered in C101 and documented trades in C102.

## Sub-claims

### 1. Congressional requests to the SEC, SEC IG and GAO over 9 April 2025: confirmed; no substantive response
- The letters of 10 and 11 April 2025 were read in full. The House letter asked for preliminary findings by 18 April. The Senate letter asked whether the White House had contacted the SEC, and what staff departures meant for its capacity.
- On 24 February 2026 Waters and Green wrote: "It has been nearly a year and we have received no substantive response to this request."
- They added that GAO "does not have the authority to compel necessary information."
- The SEC has announced no investigation, closure or finding.

### 2. Request to state attorneys general: request confirmed, outcome unverified
- The 11 April 2025 letter to the National Association of Attorneys General asked states to investigate "whether President Trump, his family, members of his administration, or members of Congress engaged in insider trading, market manipulation, or any violations of the laws of your states."
- No state attorney general, comptroller or securities regulator was found to have opened such an inquiry. That includes New York's Martin Act, which Coates names as a possible route for the Truth API.
- State activity in this period concerns prediction markets as gambling. Search excerpts also report executive orders in New York and Maryland on state employees' use of nonpublic information.

### 3. House Financial Services investigation request (February 2026): confirmed; no action found

### 4. Requests in 2026 on Iran trading and Hegseth's broker: confirmed; no replies found
- The Warner-Schiff press release was read in full; the PDF could not be parsed. The Warren letter of 20 April was read in full.
- The Pentagon denies that Hegseth or any representative approached BlackRock, and the trade was never executed.
- The SEC declined to comment.

### 5. Truth API: requests confirmed; no SEC investigation announced (unverified)
- The SEC declined to comment to NPR.
- TMTG says the feed is "publicly available Truth Social data".
- The only lawsuit is the constitutional suit filed by The Intercept and FPF (C58).

### 6. CFTC oil-futures probe: mostly-accurate (open, no charges)
- The enforcement director confirmed on 22 October 2026 that the probe is open.
- The account that a referral was retracted rests on anonymous sources quoted by Bloomberg.
- No trader has been named.

### 7. Prediction-market enforcement: confirmed
- Van Dyke (charged), Perez (settled) and Santos (settled), plus a Google employee who is not a government employee.
- All were detected by the platforms.
- None involves Trump, his family or a policy-level official.

### 8. House Oversight and platform inquiries: confirmed
- The Comer memo (read in full) credits the platforms with detecting most cases.
- Hyperliquid told the committee it did not "possess the types of records and information" requested.
- The memo recommends restricting federal personnel from event contracts. It does not examine securities or futures trading around presidential announcements.

### 9. Executive-branch and OGE response: partly-accurate
- Guidance exists: the White House email, the CFTC advisory and the DoD memo.
- No OGE guidance on prediction markets was found, and OGE has not responded to CREW in public.

### 10. Enforcement staffing and policy: confirmed (documented; no motive asserted)
- **SEC staffing (GAO-26-107813).** Enforcement had 1,305 staff at the end of FY2024 and lost 235 (18%). Trading and Markets lost 22%. Agency-wide losses were about 18%, and the SEC had about 4,000 employees in January 2026, its lowest number since 2014.
- **SEC policy.** The Commission rescinded the Enforcement Director's delegated authority to issue formal orders (March 2025, White & Case excerpt). It also sought comment on "foundational and existential aspects" of the Consolidated Audit Trail, according to Better Markets, an advocacy group.
- **CFTC.** Staff ended 2025 about 21% below the ten-year average, and enforcement actions fell nearly 80% (NPR). The CFTC says the departures were voluntary and that it is hiring. Selig is the sole commissioner.
- **DOJ.** The National Cryptocurrency Enforcement Team was disbanded. NBC reported (anonymous sources) cuts to the Public Integrity Section.

### 11. The law: partly-accurate (reaches officials and tippees on paper; untested against a president; gaps)
- **STOCK Act (read in full).**
  - Section 2(3) defines "executive branch employee" to include the President and Vice President.
  - Section 9(b) affirms that such employees are "not exempt" from section 10(b) and Rule 10b-5.
  - It adds Exchange Act section 21A(h): a duty of trust and confidence to the government and citizens for material nonpublic information "derived from such person's position."
- **Tipper and tippee.** Under *Dirks v. SEC* (1983) and *Salman v. United States* (2016), an insider who tips for a personal benefit is liable, and a gift of information to a relative or friend counts as a benefit. A tippee who knows of the breach is liable when trading. So family and friends who trade on a tip from an official are covered as tippees, not as officials.
- **Commodity Exchange Act section 4c(a)(3)-(4).**
  - It bars "any employee or agent of any department or agency of the Federal Government" from trading futures, options or swaps on government information, or from imparting that information for gain.
  - Knowing recipients of such information are also liable, and anyone who misappropriates government information is liable under 4c(a)(4)(C).
  - CFTC Rule 180.1 mirrors Rule 10b-5.
  - Event contracts are treated as swaps. The Van Dyke and Perez cases are the first uses of these provisions on event contracts.
- **Gaps and limits.**
  1. A public post is not a tip. Professors Coffee, Pritchard and Seyhun (PolitiFact, excerpt) see no violation in the 9 April post itself; aides who traded would be exposed.
  2. Whether the President is an "employee or agent of any department or agency" under the CEA provision is untested. This is our reading of the text; no source found decides it.
  3. The OGE rule on nonpublic information (5 CFR 2635.703) excludes the President and Vice President (5 CFR 2635.102(h)), as does 18 U.S.C. 208 (via 18 U.S.C. 202(c)).
  4. *Blaszczak* (2d Cir. 2022) held that confidential agency information is not "property" for Title 18 wire and securities fraud. That narrows criminal theories outside the Exchange Act and the CEA.
  5. A 2000 opinion of DOJ's Office of Legal Counsel bars indicting a sitting president. *Trump v. United States* (2024) gives immunity for official acts but not unofficial ones.
  6. Enforcement depends on agencies led by the President's appointees. Coates notes a five-year limitations period for SEC civil actions and criminal charges.
- **The competing view.** John Coates (Harvard, former SEC general counsel under Biden, who discloses that he is not neutral) argues that the STOCK Act makes the President a fiduciary and that selling early access to official announcements could breach that duty. Renée Jones (Boston College, a former senior SEC official) agrees that the Truth API "appears" to run afoul of the law but expects the defence that the posts are public. TMTG rejects the theory.
- No court has applied these provisions to a president.

### 12. Any finding against Trump, his family or senior officials: unverified (none exists)
- No trader has been identified in the April 2025 options trading, the Iran oil trades or the Iran-strike prediction-market bets.
- No authority has made a finding about Trump, his family, Cabinet members or senior appointees.
- The absence of a finding is not exoneration, and the allegations are not findings.

## Corrections
- **Claimed:** the House letters of April 2025 and February 2026 quote Trump as saying "$2.5 million." **The record shows:** he said "two-and-a-half billion" (Schwab) and "$900 million" (Penske). Both are his own claims about paper gains (C58).
- **Claimed:** the House Oversight memo says a Hyperliquid short was opened "within minutes" of a tariff announcement. **The record shows:** the article the memo cites says the position was opened about 30 hours before the post.
- **Claimed:** the teleprompter operator made "close to $100,000." **The record shows:** the CFTC order puts the profit at $107,539, plus a $65,000 penalty.
- **Claimed:** the April 2025 memo disbanding the National Cryptocurrency Enforcement Team was Bondi's. **The record shows:** Deputy AG Todd Blanche wrote it.

## Denials and context
- The White House says the 9 April post reassured markets and calls the insinuations that officials traded "baseless and irresponsible."
- The Pentagon denies the Hegseth approach.
- TMTG denies that the Truth API involves insider trading.
- The SEC says it refocused enforcement; Atkins disputed the decline before the SEC's own figures confirmed it.
- The CFTC says the oil probe is open and that staff departures were voluntary.
- Kalshi and Polymarket say they police insider trading.
- Experts disagree on the 9 April post. Some see no violation; others say an investigation is warranted.
- **Source limits.** The following could not be read directly, so the points that rely on them rest on excerpts or secondary coverage: the Warner-Schiff PDF, CRS report LSB11406 (cited in the Oversight memo), the CLS Blue Sky "Shadow SEC Statement No. 13", PolitiFact, White & Case, Axios and Bloomberg.

## What remains unanswered
1. Did the SEC open any matter under inquiry or investigation into the 9 April 2025 options trading or the "DJT" post? Did it ever reply to the April 2025, April 2026 and July 2026 letters?
2. Did the SEC inspector general or GAO take any step on the April 2025 request beyond GAO's statement that it cannot compel information?
3. Who placed the 9 April 2025 SPY and QQQ calls, the oil-futures positions of 23 March and 7 April 2026, and the Iran-strike Polymarket bets? Has the CFTC obtained Tag 50 identities, and will it charge anyone?
4. Who decided to retract the CFTC surveillance referral, and why? Has the CFTC inspector general looked at it?
5. Has the DoD inspector general answered Warner and Schiff, and has the SEC examined the Hegseth broker report?
6. Has any state attorney general or the New York AG (under the Martin Act) opened an inquiry, including into Truth API subscribers?
7. Will OGE issue guidance, or the White House a rule, on officials and event contracts? What did the November 2026 Oversight memo leave out about officials' securities and futures trades?
8. Does CEA section 4c(a)(3) reach the President? Would the STOCK Act duty be enforced against a president or his tippees after he leaves office, and how would immunity and pardons interact with civil liability?
9. Outcome of the Van Dyke trial, and whether wire-fraud counts survive *Blaszczak*-type challenges.
10. Findings of GAO's review of CFTC staffing (starting December 2026).
