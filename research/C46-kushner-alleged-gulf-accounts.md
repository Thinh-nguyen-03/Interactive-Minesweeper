# C46: The alleged Kushner Gulf accounts (Azerbaijan route, "Beta" account, Gabbard complaint)

**Verdict: unverified** (confidence: medium)

The claim correctly describes what the sources allege. The allegations themselves are not substantiated. Every account and money claim (the roughly $750M "Alpha" account at First Abu Dhabi Bank, the routing via Azerbaijan, and the roughly $1B "Beta" account at the Bank of Israel) traces back to one source: Matthew Cole's book *Princes and Thieves*, excerpted by Vanity Fair on 22 Oct 2026. Cole relies on anonymous sources and has published no bank records. No outlet has independently corroborated any of it.

The most specific detail, the Bank of Israel "Beta" account, has drawn the most rebuttal:

- Axios's Barak Ravid publicly questioned it.
- A Jerusalem Post opinion column argued it is incompatible with the bank's published rules.
- The Jerusalem Post pulled its own news write-up of the excerpt.
- Former US ambassador David Friedman denies the role the book gives him.

The Gabbard whistleblower complaint is real and well documented, but it is a **separate** story. According to the complaint as the Wall Street Journal reported it in Feb 2026, it concerns an intercept held by the NSA of two foreign nationals talking about Kushner. Its contents are classified, and some sources characterize it as "gossip." No outlet ties it to Cole's accounts. Kushner's legal threats against Condé Nast and Simon & Schuster are confirmed. No lawsuit had been reported by mid-Nov 2026.

## Scope and method

This builds on C37, which covers the phone-hack chain (GRU, then the MIVD, then screenshots), the basic Kushner, Trump and Raskin responses, and the 2024 Salt Typhoon targeting. That material is not repeated here. This file goes deeper on the money allegations, the rebuttals, the Gabbard complaint, the legal threats and the official and congressional responses.

**Method note:** the original pages at cbsnews.com, jpost.com, ynetnews.com, commondreams.org and democracynow.org were not read directly. Everything below comes from search-result text across many outlets, including:

## Sub-claims

1. **Cole's book and the Vanity Fair excerpt make these allegations. Confirmed, as a fact of publication.**
   - Vanity Fair ran the excerpt on 22 Oct 2026.
   - The book's full title, as listed by the publisher: *Princes and Thieves: Jared Kushner and Mohammed bin Zayed's Secret Plot to Remake the Middle East*. It is published by Atria/One Signal (Simon & Schuster).
   - Per Political Wire, citing Status, Simon & Schuster released the book early, on 26 Oct, after Kushner's legal threats (other listings give 25 or 27 Oct).
   - Cole told Democracy Now! (2 Nov) that the book "is a result of more than 50 sources" and was fact-checked and legally vetted. That quote comes via search summaries; the original was not read.

2. **"Alpha": a ~$750M First Abu Dhabi Bank account with Kushner as signatory. Unverified, single source.** As reported from the excerpt:
   - The account was opened in Dubai at First Abu Dhabi Bank (FAB) from 2017.
   - It was held by the Watani Al Emarat Foundation, a UAE government-funded charity. Kushner is described as a **signatory, not owner**.
   - It was funded with Saudi and Emirati money and run as a "foreign policy fund" partly managed by Kushner and Mohamed bin Zayed.
   - Some coverage says the book has Tahnoon bin Zayed, FAB's chairman, overseeing it.

   No bank statement, account number, wire record or transaction date has been published. Kushner's team stresses this point. No statement from FAB, the foundation or the UAE government turned up in search. Asia Times reports that four former senior Trump officials said they knew of no presidential authorization that would have allowed Kushner access to a foreign government's account. That comes from a search summary and was not read directly.

3. **The money was routed via Azerbaijan. Unverified, single source.** The book has Saudi and UAE money moving through an Azerbaijani bank, and MBZ's roughly $1B reaching Israel "through Azerbaijan" by spring 2018. No outlet has identified the bank, the dates or the amounts. JAMnews (Azerbaijan/Caucasus) recalled the earlier OCCRP "Azerbaijani Laundromat" (about $1.4B through Baktelekom / International Bank of Azerbaijan) but said **no confirmed link** exists between that scheme and the alleged Kushner accounts. No Azerbaijani government response was found.

4. **"Beta": a ~$1B transfer to an Israeli government (Bank of Israel) account. Unverified, and actively disputed.** As reported by Ynet, i24 and Common Dreams from the book:
   - The account was set up "for Kushner's personal benefit," according to one source Cole cites.
   - Netanyahu encouraged placing the money at the Bank of Israel so it would look like a government account.
   - Netanyahu wanted to be told before transfers, with messages relayed through then-Ambassador David Friedman.

   The rebuttals:
   - **Barak Ravid (Axios), on X:** "There are many things in this book excerpt ... that don't add up, but two are extremely questionable." He doubts such an account "is even technically possible" at the Bank of Israel and doubts that Netanyahu could be a signatory.
   - **Jerusalem Post opinion column:** the Bank of Israel holds accounts only for the government, banks, certain financial entities and foreign central banks. A private account controlled by Kushner would be "incompatible with the bank's published rules." The author allows that money could in theory move through an institutional account.
   - **Jerusalem Post news report:** the paper pulled its write-up of the excerpt, citing "significant factual questions" (reported by Mediaite). Chad Mizelle praised the removal.
   - **David Friedman:** denied any knowledge of or involvement in UAE money for Kushner, and denied passing messages for him (via search summary of Ynet/i24 coverage).
   - No response from the Israeli Prime Minister's Office or the Bank of Israel was found.

5. **A ~$2B headline total. Unverified, and inconsistent across outlets.** The book's phrase is "at least $2 billion into at least two bank accounts" from MBZ and MBS. Some outlets use $1.75B or "more than $700 million." C37 also mentions a third, defence-related account. I found no fresh reporting describing it, so it stays unverified.

6. **A whistleblower complaint involving DNI Gabbard concerns an NSA intercept of foreigners discussing Kushner. Confirmed as reported (WSJ, then NYT); its substance is unverified.**
   - The complaint was filed with the Intelligence Community Inspector General on 21 May 2025 by a whistleblower represented by Andrew Bakaj.
   - It alleges that Gabbard took an intercept of two foreign nationals to White House chief of staff Susie Wiles and then limited its distribution. It also alleges that the NSA general counsel did not refer a possible crime to the Justice Department.
   - Per the WSJ, a foreign service collected the intercept and passed it to the NSA, and the conversation was partly about Iran.
   - The WSJ could not determine the speakers' nationalities or what they said about Kushner. Ynet's "Iran-linked" framing is its own characterization.
   - Reports describe the content as uncorroborated "gossip"; some analysts could not tell whether it was gossip or disinformation.
   - Inspector General findings: acting IG Tamara Johnson said in June 2025 that she "could not determine if the allegations appear credible." Per AP, IG Christopher Fox found the political-purposes allegation "did not appear credible" and was "unable to assess" the crime-referral allegation.
   - The complaint reached Congress, heavily redacted, about eight to nine months after filing (Feb 2026).
   - Gabbard called the claim that she hid it "a blatant lie." Her office says she acted "fully within her legal and statutory authority."
   - Gabbard announced her resignation on 22 May 2026, effective 30 Jun, citing her husband's health.
   - **No outlet connects this intercept to Cole's accounts or to the phone hack.** The only link is Kushner as subject.

7. **Kushner's lawyer threatened Condé Nast and Simon & Schuster. Confirmed.**
   - Before publication, counsel sent Cole and Simon & Schuster letters dated 30 Jul and 10 Aug, described as a 13-page denial.
   - On 23 Oct Chad Mizelle wrote to Condé Nast's legal department demanding a takedown and record preservation, and warning of "serious legal risk."
   - Mizelle said: "We intend to pursue legal action against those responsible."
   - As of mid-Nov 2026 no lawsuit was reported, no report of Vanity Fair removing the excerpt was found, and Vanity Fair, Condé Nast and Simon & Schuster had not commented publicly (per TheWrap and Mediaite).

8. **Official responses. Confirmed.**
   - **Kushner:** "completely insane," "categorically false," "never happened."
   - **Mizelle:** "There are no accounts and never were."
   - **White House:** spokeswoman Anna Kelly called the reporting "baseless smears."
   - **Trump:** "a totally false story" (see C37).
   - **Friedman:** denies, as above.
   - **No response found from:** FAB, the Watani Al Emarat Foundation, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Azerbaijan, the Israeli Prime Minister's Office or the Bank of Israel.

9. **Congressional follow-up. Partly confirmed: thin, and on party lines.**
   - **Accounts:** the only documented reaction is Rep. Jamie Raskin's recusal demand (see C37). He calls Kushner "currently unfit to serve in any capacity" and asks for a "comprehensive list of bank accounts or other financial records." House Judiciary Democrats are in the minority and cannot subpoena. No Senate letter, hearing or Republican-led inquiry on the accounts was found, and no Wyden, Garcia or Warner statement on the Cole allegations surfaced.
   - **Gabbard complaint:** Sen. Mark Warner and Rep. Jim Himes wrote to Gabbard on 24 Feb 2026 about the more-than-nine-month delay. Warner called it "an effort to try to bury this whistleblower complaint." Republican intelligence chairs Tom Cotton and Rick Crawford rejected the complaint; Crawford called it an attempt to smear Gabbard. Reports say the White House invoked executive privilege to withhold an unredacted version (Daily Beast; not independently checked).

## Corrections and caveats

- The claim says "First Abu Dhabi Bank account with Kushner as signatory." In the book's account, the account holder is the Watani Al Emarat Foundation, and Kushner is a signatory, not the owner.
- The "Beta" account is described as at the Bank of Israel and "for Kushner's personal benefit." That is a single anonymous source quoted by Cole, and it is the most heavily disputed element.
- The Jerusalem Post retraction was of a **news write-up** of the excerpt. A JPost **opinion** column critiquing the excerpt remains published.
- The Gabbard complaint was filed in May 2025, before the book. Nothing in the public record links it to the alleged accounts, and its substance is unknown.

## Denials and other side

- Kushner, Mizelle, the White House and Trump deny that any accounts existed. Friedman denies his alleged role.
- Cole stands by the book, citing more than 50 sources, fact-checking and legal vetting.
- Skeptics who are not part of Kushner's camp (Ravid, the JPost column, the JPost newsroom) question specific mechanics.
- No independent outlet has reported seeing documents.
