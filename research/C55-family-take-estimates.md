# C55: How much has the Trump family made? Competing totals

**Verdict: mostly-accurate (confidence: medium)**

Every estimate named in the brief is real and traceable. They differ widely because each one measures something different. Some count cash received, some count paper gains in net worth, and some count gross revenue reported on a disclosure. They also cover different people (Trump alone or the whole family), different time windows, and different dates. Once you sort them on those three axes, they are broadly compatible rather than contradictory.

The brief has two weak points:
- The Fight Fight Fight "$200M-$1B TRUMP treasury" was only a proposal, reported by Bloomberg in October 2025. I found no report that it ever closed.
- "Where the gains went" (stocks and bonds) is documented only as correlation. The disclosure's value ranges do not trace any crypto dollars into specific securities.

Parents: C29 (Forbes figures and the 2025 OGE disclosure), C01 (TRUMP coin royalties), C31 (Gulf licensing / CREW). This file builds on those and does not repeat them.

*Method note: direct fetches of newyorker.com, citizensforethics.org, npr.org, mediaite.com and democracynow.org were blocked in this environment. The figures below come from search-result text across several outlets that quote these sources, and single-source items are attributed.*

## Sub-claims

1. **Kirkpatrick: ~$3.4B in cash. Confirmed, with caveats.** The New Yorker piece "The Number" appeared in August 2025. Kirkpatrick told MSNBC, CNN and NPR that the figure counts cash actually paid to the family, not paper wealth, and that it includes some pre-inauguration (2024 run-up) money he judged presidency-related. Secondary coverage gives conflicting breakdowns:
   - Crypto at about $2.37B, partly via the Trump Media bitcoin-treasury raise. On CNN he said "most of the crypto profit ran through Trump Media."
   - About $385M from TRUMP/MELANIA coin profit, although another summary says he excluded the meme coins.

   The total covers both terms in some descriptions and "his time in the White House" in others. He published an update to roughly $4B around January 2026. So the brief's "$3.4B cash, second term" is an outdated figure, and it is not strictly second-term only.
2. **Forbes: confirmed, but it measures a different thing.** Forbes measures net worth, not cash taken. Trump was at $7.3B in September 2025 (up $3B in a year) and $6.5B in March 2026 (up $1.4B year on year, after the crypto drawdown). The family was at about $10B in September 2025. The family-member figures are covered in C29.
3. **Bloomberg: confirmed.** Its tallies move with token prices, and it excludes locked tokens. The trajectory: ~$620M from crypto by mid-2025; $7.7B family worth in September 2025; ~$6.7B after the crash; a May 2026 revision adding $660M from previously undisclosed WLFI token sales.
4. **Reuters/NYT disclosure tallies of $1.4B crypto and $2.2B income: confirmed (see C29).** This is gross income Trump reported himself for calendar 2025, and much of it is reported in value brackets. It is not profit, and it covers Trump personally, not his sons' ventures such as American Bitcoin. The separate Reuters investigation (2026-06-09) is a different number: at least $2.3B to the family from four crypto ventures.
5. **CREW: foreign revenue and 24 foreign projects. Confirmed.** CREW counts more than $117M in foreign revenue in 2025 and 24 foreign Trump-branded projects in development, 13 of them announced since he became a 2022 candidate. That is revenue to Trump entities, not net profit. The secondhand "$59.5M licensing" figure matches CREW's ~$59M.
6. **Fight Fight Fight's $200M-$1B TRUMP treasury: partly accurate.** Bloomberg reported on 2025-10-08 that Bill Zanker's Fight Fight Fight LLC was seeking at least $200M, with ambitions up to $1B, for a digital-asset treasury company that would buy and hold TRUMP. Later coverage calls it conceptual and in early stages. I found no report of a closing or launch through November 2026. It is a proposed vehicle for supporting the token, not money the family made.
7. **Where the gains went (stocks and bonds): mostly accurate.** In July 2026, Reuters' review of the filings found Trump's stock and bond portfolios grew at least fourfold, to an estimated $703M-$2.6B at end-2025 from $225M-$608M a year earlier. Value ranges make it impossible to trace crypto dollars into specific securities. The White House says the assets sit in fully discretionary accounts run by independent third-party institutions.
8. **Reconciliation: the totals are compatible once you separate cash from paper.** Disclosure income for Trump in 2025 (≥$2.2B gross) plus the sons' and family's separate takes, and minus pre-2025 money, lands in the same range as Kirkpatrick's ~$4B family cash count. Net-worth trackers (Forbes, Bloomberg) show smaller net gains, because they mark the crypto holdings down after the 2025-26 crash and net out losses elsewhere. CREW's $117M is a subset (foreign revenue only).

## Corrections
- The "$3.4B" figure dates from August 2025. Kirkpatrick's own update is about $4B (around January 2026).
- Kirkpatrick's total is not strictly "second-term". He says it includes some money from the 2024 run-up.
- The $1.4B crypto / $2.2B income figures are Trump's personal gross income for calendar 2025, not a family total and not profit.
- The Fight Fight Fight treasury was a proposal. No raise has been confirmed.

## Denials and other side
- Press secretary Karoline Leavitt called the New Yorker figure "absolutely absurd" and said the president "has lost hundreds of millions of dollars" by serving. She has made the "lost money" claim repeatedly. No records support it.
- The White House says the assets are in a trust managed by his children and in discretionary accounts run by independent institutions. The Trump Organization says the disclosure shows "a strong financial position."
- Eric Trump disputed Forbes' valuation of him without giving an alternative (C29).
- In context: CREW, CAP and Kirkpatrick are critics of the administration, although Kirkpatrick says he uses conservative assumptions. Forbes and Bloomberg are neutral wealth trackers. Disclosure-based figures are revenue, not profit.
