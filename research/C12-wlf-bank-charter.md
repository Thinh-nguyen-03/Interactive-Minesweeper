# C12 — WLF bank charter from the OCC

**Verdict: mostly-accurate (confidence: high)**

## The claim
World Liberty "is applying for bank charters, getting provisionally approved"; the president "helps appoint the regulators, specifically the OCC"; World Liberty Financial "denies special treatment from the OCC" and says "the approval was conducted by career staffers."

## What the evidence shows

1. **WLF applied for a national trust bank charter — confirmed.** WLTC Holdings LLC, a World Liberty subsidiary, filed with the OCC to organize *World Liberty Trust Company, National Association* (application dated 5 Jan 2026, announced 7 Jan 2026). The proposed bank would issue, redeem, convert and custody the USD1 stablecoin. It would have no FDIC deposit insurance, and it would not take deposits or make loans. It was **one** application, not several "bank charters." [1][2][3]
2. **Provisionally/conditionally approved — confirmed.** The OCC granted *preliminary conditional approval* on 14 Aug 2026 (Corporate Decision #1385), about 221 days after filing. The approval is not final. The bank must meet the preopening requirements, including at least $20M of tier 1 capital and liquid assets equal to the greater of $10M or 50% of that capital. The capital must be raised within 12 months and the bank must open within 18 months, or the approval lapses. The OCC can modify, suspend or rescind it. [4][5][6][7]
3. **The president appoints the OCC head — confirmed.** The Comptroller of the Currency is nominated by the president and confirmed by the Senate for a five-year term. Trump nominated Jonathan Gould in February 2025, and the Senate confirmed him 50-45 on 10 July 2025 on a party-line vote. Gould had earlier been the OCC's chief counsel during Trump's first term. [8][9]
4. **The president's family benefits — confirmed (context).** The Trump family holds a large stake in World Liberty. One outlet reported that the family owns 38.25% of the bank; that exact figure was not checked against a primary filing. The OCC decision lists passivity commitments from affiliated investors, including DT Marks SC LLC (a Trump-family vehicle name) and StringZ Holding RSC (DE) LLC. In a February 2026 letter, Sen. Warren asked whether a UAE company was a principal shareholder; Gould declined to discuss the application's details. **Audit update:** on 2026-08-27 the WSJ reported, citing people familiar with the matter, that StringZ is backed by a group led by Abu Dhabi's Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed and holds **49% of WLTC Holdings**, the bank's parent. The same report says a Trump-family-linked entity holds about 38%. This rests on one outlet with anonymous sources (relayed by The Block and Cointelegraph). The OCC decision confirms StringZ as a passive investor but does not name its backer or give stake sizes. [4][10][11][12][17][18]
5. **"WLF denies special treatment; says approval was by career staffers" — partly accurate in attribution.** The "career staff" language comes mainly from the **OCC's own decision letter**: "Career OCC staff reviewed the application for consistency with the statutory, regulatory, and policy requirements". The OCC also said nonpolitical examiners would supervise the bank, and it rejected the conflict-of-interest objections from four commenters. WLF CEO Zach Witkoff publicly denied favoritism and said the company "welcome[s] continuous scrutiny from federal regulators." I found no primary quote in which WLF itself credits the approval to career staff, so the substance is right but the source is mostly the OCC. [5][6][13]

## Other side / context
- Gould refused Sen. Warren's 13 Jan 2026 request to pause the review until Trump divests. He wrote that the OCC would act "consistent with this duty rather than your demand" in "an apolitical and nonpartisan process." [10][14]
- Critics include Warren, NCRC, Americans for Financial Reform and House Democrats. They argue the OCC cannot act impartially toward a firm tied to the president who appoints its head. On 15 Aug 2026 Warren and colleagues introduced the *Ending Presidential Corruption in Banking Act* (S. 5389), which would bar such approvals and end this charter. [12][15][16]
- Context that cuts in WLF's favor: in December 2025 the OCC conditionally approved similar crypto trust charters for Circle, Ripple, BitGo, Fidelity Digital Assets and Paxos. So the approval fits a wider policy under Gould, though critics note that Gould pushed through charters that career staff once viewed skeptically. [2][6]
- The characterisation "I don't think we've ever seen anything like this" is opinion. Warren made a similar point, calling Trump the first president "to approve, operate, and supervise his own bank."

## Corrections
- Claimed: "bank charters" (plural). The record shows: one OCC national trust bank charter application (WLTC Holdings / World Liberty Trust Co., N.A.).
- Claimed: WLF said the approval was conducted by "career staffers". The record shows: the phrase is in the OCC's own approval letter (CD #1385), and WLF's public response was a general denial of favoritism.
- The charter is a limited-purpose trust charter (no deposits or loans, no FDIC insurance), not a full-service bank.
