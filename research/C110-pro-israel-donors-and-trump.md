# C110: Pro-Israel donors and Trump's campaign

**Verdict:** mostly-accurate (confidence: medium)

**Question:** What did the largest American donors identified with pro-Israel causes give to Donald Trump's 2024 campaign, allied committees and inauguration, what has Trump said on the record about them, what did AIPAC's affiliated committees spend in 2024 and 2026 and on whom, and what is documented about requests made to him?

Labelling note: every donor and organisation below is an American citizen or US organisation giving to US committees under US law. None of this is Israeli government money, and no Israeli-government act is covered by this check.

## Summary

- **Miriam Adelson** (American physician, Israeli-born, majority owner of the Dallas Mavericks) gave **$106M** to the pro-Trump super PAC Preserve America in six gifts in 2024 (FEC, queried directly). Counting all her gifts of $1M or more to Republican committees in 2024 the total is **$142M**. In the 2025-26 cycle the FEC shows $25M to MAGA Inc. (5 May 2026), $30M to the Senate Leadership Fund and $10M to the Congressional Leadership Fund (both 11 Mar 2026), about $66.8M in all.
- The **Adelson Family Foundation** is reported (Washington Post donor records, via the Boston Globe, Oct 2026) to have committed **$25M** to the White House ballroom, the top tier. This is the foundation, not Adelson personally.
- **Trump on the record:** at the Knesset on 13 Oct 2025 he credited the Adelsons with shaping his Jerusalem, embassy and Golan decisions and said "She's got $60 billion in the bank"; on 16 Dec 2025 he said she gave his campaign "indirectly and directly $250 million". His $250M figure is his own and does not match one filing total.
- **AIPAC's super PAC, United Democracy Project (UDP):** 2024 cycle raised $87.1M, spent about $61.4M, including $34.8M on independent expenditures in Democratic House races. 2026 cycle through 14 Oct: receipts about $108.6M, independent expenditures $53.6M. It is bipartisan, mostly congressional and mostly primaries. Its largest Republican target in 2026 was Rep. Thomas Massie, whom Trump also opposed.
- **Not documented:** any agreement, condition or payment tying donations to policy. A 2024 report of an annexation condition was denied by Adelson's spokesman, and Trump said in Oct 2025 that annexation "won't happen".

## What Trump has said

1. **Knesset, Jerusalem, 13 Oct 2025** (Reuters; other outlets vary in wording). "She loves Israel." He credited the couple with the 2017 Jerusalem recognition, the 2018 embassy move and the 2019 Golan recognition, said "Stand up, Miriam", recalled that the Adelsons would regularly call and visit the Oval Office, said "She's got $60 billion in the bank" and "I think she is saying, 'No, more.'" Other outlets quote him: "I actually asked her once, I said, 'So Miriam, I know you love Israel. What do you love more, the United States or Israel?' She refused to answer. That might mean Israel."
2. **White House Hanukkah reception, 16 Dec 2025** (Daily Beast, 17 Dec). Trump praised her for giving his campaign "indirectly and directly $250 million". Adelson said: "I met Alan Dershowitz and he said the legal thing about four more years, and I said, 'Alan, I agree with you. We can do it!' Think about it." Reports quote Trump relaying an offer of "another $250 million". The 22nd Amendment limits a president to two elected terms, and Trump has previously said he does not plan a third. This is an appeal about his candidacy, not about Israel.
3. **Massie.** 5 Jan 2026 post endorsing Ed Gallrein: Massie is "a true hater of Israel" and a "totally ineffective loser" (Algemeiner); 18 May 2026: "The worst Congressman in the long and storied history of the Republican Party is Thomas Massie" (Al Jazeera). His posts also cite Massie's other votes.
4. **On the lobby.** Daily Caller interview (late Aug 2025): Israel had the strongest lobby in Congress of any he had seen, "Today, it doesn't have that strong a lobby" and he was "a little surprised". Repeated in Dec 2025 and Aug 2026 (secondary reports).

These remarks acknowledge access and influence. None describes an agreement.

## AIPAC and UDP spending

AIPAC is a US lobbying group. AIPAC PAC gives candidates up to $5,000 per election; UDP, its super PAC (created Jan 2022), makes independent expenditures.

- **2024:** UDP raised $87.1M and spent about $61.4M, including $34.8M on House Democratic candidates (FactCheck, read in full). Biggest races: Bowman (NY-16), more than $14.5M including support for Latimer (Sludge excerpt); Bush (MO-1), about $5.2M against plus $3.3M for Bell. Intercept: UDP $41.4M direct in 2024 races.
- **2026 (FEC to 14 Oct):** receipts $108.6M, disbursements $85.1M, independent expenditures $53.6M, plus $13.1M to other federal committees. Intercept: AIPAC $53.3M and UDP $66.7M direct, spending in 333 of 473 seats; over $30M in the Michigan Democratic Senate primary (against El-Sayed or for Stevens; El-Sayed won). Others: about $5M for Conyears-Ervin (IL-7, lost), over $2M against Malinowski (NJ-11 special primary, lost), over $1M for Boafo (MD-5, won), over $3M against Massie plus about $0.5M for Gallrein. Politico reported over 40% of UDP's primary money went through pop-up and pass-through PACs.
- **On whom:** House and Senate candidates, mostly in primaries, both parties, and mostly Democratic races. No UDP spending for or against Trump personally was found (presidential filings not searched).
- **Massie race:** pro-Israel-linked groups spent more than $15.5M (Al Jazeera, FEC): UDP over $4.1M by 18 May, RJC Victory Fund about $3.9M, MAGA KY $7.5M. MAGA KY received $1M from Singer and $750,000 from Preserve America PAC on 27 Jun 2025 (FEC) and $250,000 from John Paulson (C86). Massie lost 54% to 45% on 19 May 2026. Both Trump and UDP opposed him; no source shows coordination or that anyone asked Trump to endorse.

Responses: UDP's spokesman Patrick Dorton called Massie "the most anti-Israel Republican in the House". AIPAC called him "one of the most consistently hostile voices in Congress toward the U.S.-Israel relationship". Massie said Trump and AIPAC were "panicked".

## Documented requests

- Public: Adelson's "Think about it" third-term appeal (above).
- Reuters, 13 Oct 2025: an unnamed senior US official said Adelson had a behind-the-scenes role in urging Trump to keep pressing for the hostages' return (single-source).
- Annexation: in 2024 Haaretz, drawing on a New York magazine profile, reported she wanted West Bank annexation and sovereignty recognition; other outlets said her support was reportedly tied to it. Her spokesman Andy Abboud denied she was urging Trump to publicly support annexation in exchange for her backing (reposted NYT text). Trump told Time (interview 15 Oct, published 23 Oct 2025) that annexation "won't happen" because "I gave my word to the Arab countries" and Israel would lose all US support if it did it. That counts against a delivered condition but does not prove the 2024 report false. Verdict: unverified.
- No FEC, FARA, court or official record documents a specific policy request from Singer, Lauder, Perlmutter, AIPAC or UDP to Trump or his team.

## What is not documented

- Any agreement, condition or payment-for-policy.
- That any donor acted for the Israeli government. FARA records were not searched.
- That UDP and Trump coordinated on Massie.
- The ballroom amounts beyond press reports of internal records, and the 2025 inaugural FEC lines.
- Adelson's, Singer's and Lauder's responses on the 2026 giving and ballroom, and any White House comment on the donations.
