# C104: Howard Lutnick's holdings, divestiture and recusals

**Verdict:** mostly-accurate (confidence: medium)

**Question:** What did Howard Lutnick divest, to whom, and which of his department's decisions touch his former businesses?

## Summary

Filings read in full document the divestiture step by step.

**What he agreed to (21 Jan 2025).**
- Resign from Cantor Fitzgerald, BGC and Newmark on confirmation.
- Sign agreements within 90 days of confirmation to sell all his and his trusts' interests in them "for a sum certain", and complete the sales after regulatory approvals.
- Recuse from matters affecting the three firms until he had divested.
- He was confirmed on 18 Feb 2025, so the 90-day signing deadline was 19 May 2025.

**What he did.**
- **16 May 2025:** he signed the sale agreements, within the window, and gave up all economic benefit from that date.
- **BGC and Newmark** bought back his shares for $151.5M and about $127M. His 278-T lists both sales on 16 May.
- **6 Oct 2025:** the Cantor sale closed. His second 278-T lists Cantor, its managing partner CF Group Management, BGC Class B shares and smaller interests as sold that day, "following regulatory approvals".
- **Buyers:** the White House waiver names 26North, Oak Hill Advisors and Cantor Fitzgerald, L.P. Cantor says his stake went to trusts for his adult children, with Brandon Lutnick as controlling trustee.
- **Price:** not public.

**Waivers and tax.**
- From 8 Jul to 6 Oct 2025, a limited White House waiver under 18 U.S.C. 208(b)(1) let him work on broad policy affecting the three firms. It did not cover specific deals or meetings with the firms.
- OGE issued six certificates of divestiture, which defer capital-gains tax if the proceeds are reinvested in Treasuries or diversified funds.

**Cantor's business before Commerce.** Cantor's tariff-refund pitch, its Tether ties, its Satellogic, data-centre and critical-minerals work all fall in or near Commerce's remit. No decision by Lutnick favouring Cantor or a Cantor client is documented.

**Investigations.** More than eight requests for investigation by Democratic members of Congress have produced no finding.

**What is not on the public record:**
- the Cantor sale price;
- whether Tether's loan to "Dynasty Trust A" funded the purchase;
- his 2025 annual report (described in the press but not posted by OGE);
- an OGE-listed "Conflict of Interest Waiver" that has not been uploaded.

**Evidence rule applied:** holding an asset, or a decision that benefits a former firm, is not a violation by itself. Each point below states the rule (18 U.S.C. 208, the ethics agreement, 5 C.F.R. 2635.502), whether a waiver or recusal exists, and whether any authority has made a finding.

This builds on C35 (Cantor and Tether), C50 (chips, the Tether loan probe, tariff refunds), C65 (officials and former firms) and C77/C60 (critical minerals).

## Sub-claims

### 1. Nominee disclosure and ethics agreement: confirmed
- **Nominee 278e** (e-signed 30 Dec 2024, read in full). It lists more than 800 positions and holdings. Income from his firms:

  | Firm | Income on the 278e |
  |---|---|
  | Cantor Fitzgerald, L.P. | $198,984,693 of partnership distributions |
  | Cantor Fitzgerald Securities | $16M salary/bonus |
  | Newmark | $30M salary/bonus and a $5M equity bonus |
  | BGC | $8M salary/bonus, an $11M equity bonus and a $61.3M "2023 BGC unit" item |

  No personal Tether holding is listed. The Tether convertible bond is Cantor's.
- **Ethics agreement** (signed 21 Jan 2025, read in full). Sections 2 to 4 set the sale terms for Cantor, BGC and Newmark:
  - sign within 90 days;
  - the buyer takes all economic interest from signing;
  - close after regulatory approvals;
  - request a 208(b)(1) waiver for the ownership still held while the sale is pending;
  - recuse from matters with a direct and predictable effect on the firms until divested;
  - stay out of specific-party matters involving the firms for one year after resignation or as long as he holds an interest, whichever is later.

  Section 8 requires him to sell GE Healthcare, GE Vernova, GE Aerospace, Nasdaq, Disney, Roivant, Kimberly Akimbo LLC and Kiajia LLC within 90 days. He keeps family trusteeships and about 30 property and vehicle LLCs. He also keeps promissory notes owed to him by the Lutnick 2020 and 1999 Descendants' Trusts, with a recusal on matters affecting their ability to repay. Appendix D shows that he left the Satellogic board in Nov 2024 and Cantor Equity Partners and Cantor Equity Partners I in Dec 2024.

### 2. BGC and Newmark buybacks, May 2025: confirmed
- **278-T** (signed 17 Jun 2025, certified by OGE 14 Jul 2025, read in full). Sales dated 16 May 2025:
  - Newmark: over $50M
  - BGC: over $50M
  - Nasdaq: $1M-$5M
  - Disney: $1K-$15K
  - Kimberly Akimbo LLC and Kiajia LLC: $50K-$100K each
  - Structured Services Investor LLC: $100K-$250K
- **Company announcements** (19 May 2025, via Dow Jones and InvestmentNews):
  - BGC repurchases 16,452,850 Class A shares for $151,501,133 (about $9.21 a share).
  - Newmark repurchases about 11 million shares for about $127M (about $11.58 a share).
  - Together that is about $278.5M. The FT's $361M total appears to include the BGC Class B shares sold to Cantor; we did not reconstruct it.
- The BGC 8-K could not be read directly because sec.gov refuses automated access.

### 3. Cantor sold to his children's trusts and outside investors, closed 6 Oct 2025: confirmed
- **278-T** (signed 5 Nov 2025, certified by OGE 8 Dec 2025, read in full). Sales dated 6 Oct 2025:
  - Cantor Fitzgerald, L.P.: over $50M
  - CF Group Management, Inc.: over $50M
  - BGC Class B shares: over $50M
  - 1% of Tangible Benefits LLC
  - 10% of KBCR Management Partners LLC: $5M-$25M
  - LFA LLC
  - BGC and Newmark shares in retirement accounts

  Each endnote reads: "entered into May 16, 2025 ... and consummated on October 6, 2025 following regulatory approvals."
- **The White House waiver** (8 Jul 2025, read in full) names the buyers as "26 North, Oak Hill Advisors and Cantor Fitzgerald, L.P." and says the sale was "at a fixed price".
- **Cantor's announcement** says the stake went to trusts for his adult children. Brandon Lutnick (chairman and CEO) is controlling trustee, and Kyle Lutnick is executive vice chair. 26North (Josh Harris) and Glenn August (founder of Oak Hill Advisors) are minority owners.
- **Price:** not disclosed. Cantor says the purchase was funded "through multiple sources ... at market rates and market prices".
- **Tether loan:** Bloomberg reported that Tether lent to "Dynasty Trust A" under a credit filing dated 7 Oct 2025, the day after closing (C35). Whether the loan funded the purchase is **unverified**.

### 4. "He missed the 90-day deadline": misleading
- **The terms.** The agreement required **signing** within 90 days (by 19 May 2025) and **closing** after regulatory approvals, with no outer date. He signed on 16 May and closed on 6 Oct 2025, which is within those terms.
- **His certifications** (both read in full):
  - On 24 May 2025 he certified that he had completed the divestitures within the time specified, with the 278-T "Forthcoming".
  - An updated certification dated 19 June 2025 explains that the agreements were signed and that closing awaited regulatory approvals. It gives 19 May as the signing date; the 278-Ts and the waiver say 16 May. Either date is inside the window.
  - Both certifications say no section 208 waiver had been received at those dates.
- **The criticism.** The 17 Dec 2025 letter to the Commerce inspector general asks why the divestiture took until 6 Oct. It reads the agreement more strictly than its text. It is accurate that he kept legal ownership for almost eight months as Secretary.
- **Official statements.** We found no OGE notice of non-compliance and no OGE or Senate committee statement either way. OGE certified both 278-Ts.
- **A side point.** Three GE sales dated 7 Mar 2025 (under $80K in total) first appear in the 17 June report, more than 100 days later and outside the 45-day limit printed on the form. The form gives no explanation, and we found no reporting on it.

### 5. Certificates of divestiture and tax deferral: confirmed
- **OGE's index** (read in full) lists certificates OGE-2025-070 to 075. They are available only on request through OGE Form 201; we did not read them.
- **Bloomberg** (via Accounting Today, read in full) reports:
  - they were issued on 25 Apr 2025;
  - they cover BGC, Newmark, his Cantor stake, Nasdaq, Disney and Kimberly Akimbo;
  - the certificates for the Cantor holding carry no values.
- **The rule.** Under 26 U.S.C. 1043, tax is deferred if the proceeds are reinvested in Treasuries or diversified funds within 60 days.
- **The rollover.** Reporting on his 2025 annual report says he bought at least $166M of index and money-market funds. That is consistent with the rollover, but we did not verify it on the form.

### 6. White House waiver, 8 Jul to 6 Oct 2025: confirmed
- **What it is.** A limited interim 208(b)(1) waiver signed by White House Counsel David Warrington (read in full).
- **What it allows.** He may take part in particular matters of general applicability affecting Cantor, BGC and Newmark until the sales close.
- **What it does not allow:**
  - specific-party matters in which the firms are or represent a party, which a "gatekeeping agreement" with the Commerce ethics office screens out;
  - meetings with representatives of the three firms;
  - anything after the closing.
- **The rationale.** He had forgone all gain or loss from 16 May. OGE was consulted.
- **A second entry.** OGE's index also lists a "Conflict of Interest Waiver" marked as not uploaded. Whether that entry is this memo or a separate document is unknown.

### 7. 2025 income of more than $250M including a $192M distribution: mostly-accurate
- **Source.** WSJ (4 Sep 2026) and CNBC (30 Sep 2026) obtained his 2025 annual 278e. It is not posted on OGE's site, where the index marks it "need to upload". We did not read the form.
- **Reported figures:**
  - at least $250M of income and proceeds;
  - a $192M Cantor distribution (WSJ: at least $190M), which the filing calls a tax distribution under the ethics agreement;
  - at least $259M of sales between May and October 2025;
  - at least $166M of fund purchases;
  - $4.2M of Newmark salary and bonus, plus $19.6M tied to exchanging Newmark partnership units;
  - assets of at least $237M at the end of 2025, against at least $805M on the nominee report.
- Sen. Blumenthal's 8 Oct 2026 letter cites the same figures.
- **Open question.** How the $192M distribution fits with forgoing all economic benefit from 16 May depends on Cantor's tax-distribution terms, which are not public.

### 8. Tariff-refund trading: partly-accurate (pitch documented; no trade or profit documented)
- **The pitch.** Wired (Jul 2025) reported a Cantor letter offering importers 20-30% of their duties for their refund rights. The letter claimed a ~$10M trade had already been done and capacity for "several hundred million".
- **The letters:**
  - Wyden and Warren wrote to Brandon Lutnick on 14 Aug 2025.
  - After the Supreme Court ruling, Raskin wrote to Howard and Brandon Lutnick on 27 Feb 2026 (read in full), seeking documents and Commerce communications by 9 Mar 2026.
- **Cantor's position.** It says it "has never executed any transactions or taken risk on the legality of tariffs". It says a salesman "erroneously" believed the trade would be approved. Semafor reported that Cantor did not profit from the ruling.
- **What is missing.** No document shows any communication between Lutnick and Cantor about tariffs. No reply to the letters and no finding was found.
- **Commerce's response.** Lutnick "has no insight or strategic control over Cantor Fitzgerald".

### 9. Tether and stablecoins: partly-accurate
- **His own account.** In written answers to the Senate Commerce Committee (Jan 2025, read in full), he said that:
  - Cantor bought a convertible debt instrument in Tether's holding company in April 2024;
  - he "participated in the negotiations";
  - the instrument is not equity;
  - neither he nor his family holds any other Tether investment.

  Asked about recusal on Satellogic, Pebble Mine and similar matters, he said each time that he would follow guidance from Commerce's ethics office.
- **The letters:**
  - Warren and Wyden (29 Apr 2026, read in full) asked whether the Tether loan financed the divestiture and what role he played in the GENIUS Act.
  - Blumenthal (8 Oct 2026, read in full, addressed to Brandon Lutnick) asked for the terms of his exit and of any Tether loan, due 23 Oct 2026.
  - No replies were found.
- **What is not documented.** Commerce has no stablecoin regulatory role, and no Commerce decision favouring Tether is documented.
- **The rule.** After 6 Oct 2025, his adult children's interests are not imputed to him under section 208.

### 10. Satellites, data centres, critical minerals, chips: partly-accurate (overlaps documented; no favouring decision documented)
- **Satellites.**
  - Cantor took Satellogic public through a SPAC and is a principal investor. Lutnick sat on its board until Nov 2024.
  - Tether agreed to buy $30M of Satellogic notes (per a Senate question for the record).
  - NOAA, part of Commerce, licenses Satellogic's remote sensing. Lutnick did not commit to recusing.
  - No Commerce licensing decision on Satellogic during his tenure was found.
- **Data centres.**
  - The NYT (Nov 2025) reported that Cantor and Newmark earned about $6M advising Fermi America. Kyle Lutnick sits on Newmark's board.
  - 25 Democrats asked the Commerce IG to review (17 Dec 2025). Commerce says he had no direct role.
- **Critical minerals.** Cantor led USA Rare Earth's $1.5B PIPE alongside Commerce's $1.6B letter of intent, and banks NOAA seabed-mining applicants (C77, C60). USAR says Cantor had no role in the government deal.
- **Chips.** Cantor's Nvidia exposure and the Megaspeed reporting are covered in C35 and C50. No source ties Cantor to a licence decision.
- **Spectrum.** No Cantor-linked spectrum matter was found.
- **The rules that apply:**
  - The agreement's one-year bar on specific-party matters involving Cantor, BGC and Newmark ran to about Feb 2026.
  - After that, 5 C.F.R. 2635.502 asks an official to weigh impartiality where a close relative is or represents a party. Whether Commerce applied this to matters his sons handle is not public.

## What goes beyond the record
- That the Cantor sale missed its deadline: the agreement's own terms allowed the October closing.
- That Tether financed the purchase: the loan's timing is documented, but its purpose is not.
- That Lutnick steered tariff, chip, satellite, data-centre or minerals decisions to Cantor or its clients: no decision or communication is documented.
- That Cantor traded or profited from tariff refunds: Cantor denies it; only the pitch is documented.
- That the waiver was breached: its enforcement has not been examined publicly.

## Corrections to earlier checks
- **C35.** The buyers named in the waiver are 26North, Oak Hill Advisors and Cantor Fitzgerald, L.P. (not "26North and Glenn August"). The closing date is exactly 6 Oct 2025 (his 278-T). The assets sold also include CF Group Management and his BGC Class B shares. All sale agreements were signed on 16 May 2025; the 6 Oct items are Cantor, CF Group Management, BGC Class B and retirement-account shares.
- **C35/C50.** Commerce's "fully complied with recusal requirements" should be read alongside the primary record: a limited 208(b)(1) waiver ran from 8 Jul to 6 Oct 2025.
- **C50.** Raskin's ~$10M trade claim comes from the Cantor letter Wired reviewed. Cantor's later explanation is a salesman's error. The $250M and $192M figures in Blumenthal's letter come from his 2025 annual 278e as reported by WSJ and CNBC, which describe the $192M as a tax distribution.
- **C77.** The "106 entities" recusal figure attributed to the NYT could not be matched to the ethics agreement, so treat it as unverified. The one-year specific-party bar on Cantor ended around Feb 2026.

## Responses
- **Commerce:** Lutnick "has fully complied with the terms of his ethics agreement, including all divestiture and recusal requirements", and "has no insight or strategic control over Cantor Fitzgerald".
- **Cantor:** the trusts' purchase was funded "through multiple sources ... at market rates and market prices". It declined to say whether a Tether loan was part of it. It says it never executed a tariff-refund trade, and its executives say they run the firm independently.
- **Lutnick (Senate questions, Jan 2025):** the Tether position is debt, not equity, and "I have never suggested to anyone that I would do anything improper with respect to Tether".
