# C108: Pam Bondi and Todd Blanche: prior clients, holdings and recusals

**Verdict:** mostly-accurate (confidence: medium)

**Question:** Which Justice Department decisions touched the former clients or holdings of Pam Bondi and Todd Blanche, and were they recused?

## Summary

Both officials' own OGE filings document the core of this lead. All of the filings listed below were read in full from extapps2.oge.gov:

- Bondi: nominee report, ethics agreement, compliance certification, 278-T and termination report.
- Blanche: two nominee reports, two ethics agreements, 278-T, 2025 compliance certification and 2026 annual report.
- Blanche's 2026 Attorney General compliance certification was read only in part.

**Bondi**
- Her nominee report lists $1,067,000 from Ballard Partners and $2,969,563 of consulting pay from Renatus LLC (Trump Media's predecessor), paid in DWAC shares.
- She held Trump Media shares and warrants worth $1M-$5M each on that report.
- Her ethics agreement required her to divest Trump Media within 90 days of her 4 February 2025 confirmation. It barred her for one year from matters in which Ballard, or any client she had served in the past year, was a party.
- She sold the shares ($1M-$5M) and warrants ($250K-$500K) on 2 April 2025, the day of the tariff announcement, and reported the sale on 5 May 2025.
- Her certification reports no waiver or authorization.

**Blanche**
- Blanche, Trump's former defence lawyer, held $158K-$470K in ten cryptocurrencies, plus Coinbase shares, when he signed the 7 April 2025 memo "Ending Regulation By Prosecution". The memo disbanded the National Cryptocurrency Enforcement Team (NCET).
- His 278-T shows the crypto and Coinbase shares disposed of between 29 May and 2 June 2025, within his deadline. A note says bitcoin, solana, cardano and ether were "gifted in their entirety" to his grandchild and adult children.
- His June 2025 certification answers "No" to receiving a section 208 waiver.
- In July 2026 he told the Senate he had received a written 208(b)(1) determination before the memo, but would not disclose it.
- An OGE note dated 2 September 2026 on his annual report reads: "DOJ IG declined further action" on seven of the crypto lines.

**What remains allegation or inference:** that the FARA and FCPA enforcement changes, the Qatar jet memo, the Warner Bros. review or the Mangione case were shaped by the officials' former clients. The same applies to the CLS Global prosecution, a former Blanche Law client sentenced while he was Deputy AG; that pairing is our own cross-referencing. No inspector-general, OGE or court finding that either official breached 18 U.S.C. 208 or an ethics agreement was found as of 17 November 2026.

**Current posts:**
- Bondi was AG from 4 February 2025. Her OGE termination report gives 1 April 2026 as her termination date; the removal was announced on 2 April. She was named to PCAST in late May 2026.
- Blanche was confirmed as Deputy AG on 5 March 2025 and sworn in on 6 March 2025. He was acting AG from April 2026. The Senate confirmed him as AG 50-49 on 8 August 2026, a date his own OGE certification confirms, and he was sworn in on 10 August 2026.

**Evidence rule applied:** holding an asset, or a decision that benefits a former employer or client, is not by itself a violation. Each point states the rule (18 U.S.C. 208, the ethics agreement, the 5 C.F.R. 2635.502 recusal period), whether a waiver or recusal exists, and whether any authority has made a finding.

**Related checks:** C64, C65 and C73 (Qatar ties, the jet memo, Brad Bondi's clients), C36 (NCET), C100 (the IRS settlement), C102 (Bondi's sale) and C103 (oversight).

## Sub-claims

### 1. Bondi's disclosure: Ballard clients, Trump Media and other income. Confirmed
- **Source:** OGE 278e nominee report, signed 3 January 2025 and certified 14 January 2025 [2].
- **Income:**
  - Ballard Partners $1,067,000
  - America First Policy Institute $520,000
  - Panza, Maurer & Maynard $203,738
  - Newsmax $27,600 and a bitnile.com honorarium of $20,000, both through PB Advisory LLC
  - Renatus LLC $2,969,563, "compensated with shares of Digital World Acquisition Corp"
- **Assets:** Trump Media shares $1M-$5M; vested Trump Media warrants $1M-$5M.
- **Clients she served (Part 4):** six Ballard clients (Aiden Torch Financial, Florida Sheriffs Association, Florida Sheriffs Risk Management Fund, Safety Net DC, Major County Sheriffs of America, iGas USA), plus Pfizer through the law firm.
- **Qatar is not listed.** The form covers 2023-24, and Ballard's FARA work for Qatar's embassy, with Bondi listed, was in 2019-20 (C64, C65, C73).
- **Other clients:** Senate Judiciary Democrats (15 January 2025, read in full [30]) say she lobbied for more than 30 Ballard clients, including Amazon, Uber, the GEO Group and Qatar. Their line that she was "earning $115,000 per month" from Qatar overstates the record. The fee went to Ballard, and her own share is not on record.

### 2. Bondi's ethics agreement and recusals. Confirmed
- **Ethics agreement** (14 January 2025 [1]):
  - Resign from Ballard, the law firm and AFPI on confirmation.
  - For one year after resigning, under 5 C.F.R. 2635.502, stay out of particular matters involving specific parties in which Ballard, the law firm, AFPI or SOMA Global is a party or represents a party.
  - Stay out of such matters involving any former client for one year after last serving that client.
  - Newsmax: recusal for one year unless she receives written authorization.
  - Under section 208, stay out of matters affecting Trump Media until divested, and matters affecting CGI's and the law firm's ability to pay her.
  - Divest Trump Media "not later than 90 days after my confirmation".
- **Compliance certification** (signed 5 May 2025, due 12 May [3]):
  - She was confirmed on 4 February 2025.
  - Divestitures completed and reported on a 278-T on 5 May 2025.
  - Interim recusals complied with.
  - "No" to a section 208 waiver, a 2635.502(d) authorization and a 2635.503(c) waiver.
- **Effect:**
  - The Ballard recusal ran until about February 2026.
  - The former-client recusal did not reach Qatar, which she last served in 2020-21.
  - No public recusal list naming specific matters was found.

### 3. Trump Media sale and the inspector-general request. Confirmed (sale); misuse unverified
- **The 278-T** (read in full [4]): signed 5 May 2025 and OGE-certified 12 May 2025. It lists two sales on 2 April 2025: shares at $1,000,001-$5,000,000 and warrants at $250,001-$500,000, neither marked as reported late.
- **Warrant values:** $1M-$5M on her January report, $250K-$500K at sale. The forms give ranges at different dates and no prices.
- **Deadline:** the sale fell inside the 90-day window (deadline about 5 May 2025), so the sale itself was required; its date is what drew attention.
- **Inspector-general request:** Rep. Raskin asked Inspector General Horowitz to investigate on 20 May 2025 [32].
- **Her response:** at a June 2025 House hearing she said the sale complied with her ethics agreement (secondary summary [33]).
- **Termination report:** signed 16 June 2026 and OGE-certified 18 September 2026 [5]. It has no reviewer note about an inspector-general matter.
- **Outcome:** no inspector-general response or finding was found.
- **Evidence rule:** a dated sale is not evidence that non-public information was used (see C102).

### 4. The Qatar jet memo. Mostly-accurate
- **Already established** (C64, C65, C73): the prior FARA registration and Bondi's signature on the May 2025 memo.
- **Recusal rule:** on its terms, her agreement's one-year former-client rule did not cover Qatar. A DOJ official told CNN that career ethics officials found no conflict requiring recusal [34]. Critics, including CREW and Sen. Durbin, say she should have recused under the general impartiality provision.
- **The memo itself is still unreleased:**
  - FOIA request filed 15 May 2025; DOJ estimated 620 days to produce it.
  - Freedom of the Press Foundation, represented by American Oversight, sued on 28 July 2025 (D.D.C. 25-2432) [35].
  - DOJ moved to strike background allegations from the complaint [36].
  - The case is listed as open [37]; no production was found.
- **What the record shows:** a former paid relationship and a later favourable decision. It does not show that the one influenced the other.

### 5. FARA and FCPA enforcement changes. Partly-accurate
- **Bondi's 5 February 2025 charging memo** (relevant section read [16]):
  - Disbanded the Foreign Influence Task Force.
  - Limited criminal FARA and section 951 charges to "conduct similar to more traditional espionage by foreign government actors".
  - Told the FARA Unit to focus on civil enforcement and guidance.
- **FCPA:** Executive Order 14209 paused FCPA enforcement on 10 February 2025. Blanche's 9 June 2025 guidelines [17] focus prosecutors on individuals and cartel-linked bribery and require senior sign-off for new cases.
- **Critics** say the FARA change lets foreign influence go undisclosed [44]. A September 2025 presidential memorandum reportedly pointed task forces back at FARA [45].
- **Not shown:** these are department-wide policies. We found no reporting that any matter involving Ballard, Qatar or a Blanche Law client was dropped because of them.

### 6. Other matters touching former Ballard clients. Unverified (as to improper participation)
- **Warner Bros. Discovery review:**
  - On 17 December 2025, Sens. Warren and Blumenthal asked Bondi to recuse from DOJ's antitrust review of bids for WBD. They noted that Ballard took on Netflix and Paramount as clients after her nomination [38][39].
  - DOJ: "All officials at the Department of Justice follow the guidance of career ethics officials." Ballard called the letter "blindly partisan".
  - The request fell inside her one-year Ballard period. That rule applies when Ballard is a party or represents a party before DOJ, which is not shown.
- **Mangione case:**
  - Defence lawyers argued in December 2025 that her April 2025 death-penalty decision was tainted because Ballard lobbies for UnitedHealth Group [40].
  - Prosecutors defended her impartiality (excerpts [41]).
  - Judge Garnett dismissed the death-eligible count on 30 January 2026 on statutory grounds [42]. No ruling on the conflict claim was found.
  - Her Ballard 401(k) holds only a money-market fund, according to her nominee and termination reports.
- **No finding** of a breach was found in either matter.

- **Clients:**

  - One confidential client under a non-public grand-jury investigation
- **Income:** Blanche Law partnership draw of $2,160,000 and salary of $96,972 (2025 report); final draw of $490,000 (2026 reports).
- **Crypto (Coinbase account):**
  - Bitcoin $100,001-$250,000
  - Ether $50,001-$100,000
  - Eight smaller coins at $1,001-$15,000 each
  - Coinbase shares $1,001-$15,000
- **Totals:** $158,010-$470,000 for the coins, or $159,011-$485,000 with the shares. This is why ProPublica and the senators give different ranges.
- **2026 report:** no crypto remaining.

### 8. The crypto memo and the dispositions. Confirmed
- **Ethics agreement** (10 February 2025 [6]): divest the coins and Coinbase shares within 90 days of confirmation. Until then, no participation in matters with a direct and predictable effect on his crypto, without a written 208(b)(1) waiver.
- **Timing:** confirmed 5 March 2025 and sworn in 6 March, so the deadline was about 3 June.
- **The memo (7 April 2025, read in full [15]):**
  - Stop targeting exchanges, mixers and wallets "for the acts of their end users".
  - Do not charge certain registration violations without proof of wilfulness.
  - Close inconsistent investigations.
  - The fraud unit ceases crypto enforcement.
  - NCET is "disbanded effective immediately".
- **The 278-T** (signed 3 June 2025, OGE-certified 10 July 2025 [8]) lists these dispositions:

  | Holding | Date | Value |
  |---|---|---|
  | Bitcoin | 2 June | $100,001-$250,000 |
  | Ether | 2 June | $15,001-$50,000 |
  | Solana | 30 May | $1,001-$15,000 |
  | Polygon | 30 May | $1,001-$15,000 |
  | Polkadot | 31 May | $1,001-$15,000 |
  | Quant | 31 May | $1,001-$15,000 |
  | Ethereum classic | 2 June | $1,001-$15,000 |
  | Coinbase shares | 29 May | $1,001-$15,000 |

- **The gifts:** the reviewer note says bitcoin, solana, cardano and ether were "gifted in their entirety to my grandchild and adult children". Three of these also appear as "Sale" lines, so the form is ambiguous. ProPublica reads it as $116K-$315K gifted and $5K-$75K sold [22].
- **Gap:** Basic Attention Token and "Decentralized" appear in no later filing we read. CLC flagged the same gap.
- **Rules:** the deadline was met. Gifts to adult children and grandchildren end the imputed interest under section 208; only a spouse's and minor children's interests are imputed.

### 9. Waiver or clearance for the memo. Unverified (the record conflicts)
- **2025 certification** (signed 5 June 2025 [9]):
  - "No" to a section 208 waiver and to a 2635.502(d) authorization.
  - "Yes" to complying with interim recusals.
  - Comment: "some assets were gifted to family members, whose financial interests are not imputed to me".
- **DOJ** (23 January 2026): "appropriately flagged, addressed, and cleared in advance" [23].
- **Blanche's written answers** (July 2026 [27]):
  - The memo "set general enforcement priorities and did not constitute a particular matter".
  - Asked whether he received a written 208(b)(1) determination before 7 April 2025: "Yes, but such determinations are not subject to disclosure."
- **The conflict:** a 208(b)(1) determination sits uneasily with both the 2025 "No" and his own position that there was no particular matter.
- **Disclosure rule:** section 208(d)(1) provides for public release of such determinations on request, subject to FOIA exemptions.
- **Open legal question:** CLC argues the memo is a particular matter of general applicability under OGE advisory LA-23-12 [18]. Blanche disagrees. No authority has ruled.

## Corrections
- **C65, Blanche crypto ranges:** C65 has the two ranges reversed. ProPublica (22 Dec 2025) gives $159K-$485K; the senators' 28 Jan 2026 letter gives $158K-$470K. The difference is whether his Coinbase shares are included.
- **C65, divestment dates:** C65 says "May 31-June 3, 2025". The 278-T shows crypto disposed of 30 May-2 June and Coinbase shares on 29 May; 3 June is when he signed the form.
- **C65, what was gifted:** C65 says the holdings were "gifted in their entirety". The note names only bitcoin, solana, cardano and ether. Other coins and the Coinbase shares were sold. BAT and "Decentralized" are unaccounted for in the filings read.
- **C65, waiver and findings:** "No waiver published, no finding" still holds. Three things should be added:
  - His 2025 certification says "No" to a section 208 waiver.
  - In July 2026 he said he had received an undisclosed 208(b)(1) determination.
  - OGE's 2 September 2026 note reads "DOJ IG declined further action".
- **C65 and C102, Bondi's 278-T:** the form was signed on 5 May 2025 and OGE-certified on 12 May 2025; it has now been read in full. Her confirmation date was 4 February 2025.
- **C102, Bondi's deadline:** her agreement says "90 days after my confirmation", not after taking office.
- **C65, C73, Bondi's removal date:** her OGE termination report gives 1 April 2026; the removal was announced on 2 April 2026.
- **Senate Judiciary Democrats (Jan 2025):** the release says Bondi was "earning $115,000 per month" from Qatar. That fee was paid to Ballard.
- **CLC complaint:** the first version gave Blanche's holdings as $274,013-$785,000. The 23 January correction gives $159,011-$485,000, but its conclusion still says "up to $785,000".

## Denials and context
- **DOJ and Blanche on the crypto memo:**
  - DOJ said the memo was "cleared in advance" and called the complaint "baseless character assassination".
  - Blanche says the memo was not a particular matter, and that divesting "required time to accomplish in an orderly manner".
  - In December 2025 a Blanche staffer said he and DOJ would not comment.
- **Blanche on Trump matters:** he is recused from the matters in which he represented Trump, and DOJ says he complies with his ethics obligations.
- **DOJ on Bondi:**
  - Jet memo: career ethics officials found no conflict requiring recusal.
  - WBD review: officials follow career ethics guidance.
  - Ballard called the WBD letter partisan.
- **Bondi on her sale:** it complied with her ethics agreement.
- **Deadlines:** both officials met their 90-day divestiture deadlines.
- **Policy scope:** the FARA, FCPA and crypto policies apply department-wide.
- **No findings:** an inspector-general declination and the absence of an inspector-general result are not findings. No authority has found a violation by either official.
