# C113: US decisions on Israel since January 2025

**Verdict:** mostly-accurate (confidence: medium)

**Question:** What has the administration decided on Israel since January 2025, with dates and amounts?

## Summary

The record since 20 Jan 2025 documents many US decisions that Israel's government sought: release of held bomb shipments (Jan-Feb 2025); arms-sale notifications to Congress whose texts were read in the Federal Register (about $6.75B and $660M on 7 Feb 2025, about $2.04B, $675.7M and $295M on 28 Feb 2025, $180M on 14 Apr 2025, $510M on 30 Jun 2025, about $6.67B in four packages on 30 Jan 2026, $151.8M on 6 Mar 2026 and $992.4M on 1 May 2026); Secretary of State emergency determinations waiving congressional review (the 28 Feb 2025 notices, announced 1 Mar; March 2026 and May 2026, the last two reported by outlets quoting the State Department); the ICC sanctions order (EO 14203, 6 Feb 2025) with designations through the court itself on 9 Oct 2026; lifting of settler sanctions (20 Jan 2025); two US vetoes of Gaza ceasefire drafts (4 Jun and 18 Sep 2025, each 14-1); joint strikes on Iran; and Trump's repeated public calls for a Netanyahu pardon (Knesset 13 Oct 2025, letter 12 Nov 2025). It also records decisions that cut against Israel's government: the Qatar security guarantee and Netanyahu's apology (29 Sep 2025), annexation warnings, a 15% tariff rate from 7 Aug 2025 (17% had been announced in April; the IEEPA tariffs were struck down on 20 Feb 2026) and exclusion from US-Iran talks. No pardon has been reported granted. dsca.mil refused plain curl; values come from the notice texts published in the Federal Register (read in full via govinfo.gov). Nothing here shows that any donation or investment caused any decision.

Every row is an act of the US government unless stated; none is an Israeli company or an American donor. Today is 17 Nov 2026.

### The administration used emergency determinations under the Arms Export Control Act to skip congressional review of sales to Israel.

**Verdict:** confirmed (corrected)

Act of the US government (Secretary of State Rubio) at Israel's request. Grade: (a) documented in the notice texts; (b) and (c) documented by two or more outlets quoting State Department statements, but the Federal Register text of those two notices carries no determination wording.
(a) 28 Feb 2025 (announced by Rubio 1 Mar): the Federal Register texts of Transmittal 25-34 ($2.04B) and 25-26 ($675.7M) state that 'the Secretary of State has determined and provided detailed justification that an emergency exists that requires the immediate sale to the Government of Israel ... thereby waiving the Congressional review requirements under Section 36(b)'. Correction: the D9 bulldozer notice (24-38, $295M) has no such wording in its text, although several outlets (Media Line, Times of Israel, per search) say the waiver covered '$2.04 billion for munitions and $295 million for Caterpillar D9 bulldozers' and about $4B in all. The audit found 25-26 ($675.7M) was also under the determination, which those reports omit. Our arithmetic only: $2.04B + $675.7M + $295M + the $893M JDAM case increase (25-0C) is about $3.9B, close to the '$4 billion' in the announcement; no source states that breakdown. Arms Control Association: Rubio's announcement did not cite a specific emergency.
(b) 6 Mar 2026, a week after the war with Iran began: 12,000 BLU-110A/B bomb bodies, $151.8M, principal contractor Repkon USA. Reuters (via Al-Monitor, read in full): 'Rubio determined that an emergency existed that required the immediate sale'; The New Arab agrees. Rep. Gregory Meeks called it 'an emergency of the Trump administration's own creation' (a criticism). The Federal Register text of 26-32 does not contain the determination. One NYT-based report put two packages at nearly $660M; the notice is $151.8M.
(c) 1 May 2026: 10,000 APKWS-II rounds, $992.4M. JNS quotes the State Department: 'The secretary of state has determined and provided detailed justification that an emergency exists that requires the immediate sale to Israel'; Ynet reports the same approach in a package of more than $8.6B to Middle Eastern allies, including a same-size APKWS sale to Qatar. The Federal Register text of 26-60 does not contain the wording. The earlier 'search summary only' grade is replaced: the notice is verified, the emergency basis rests on two outlets.
Context: the Biden administration also used emergency waivers for Israel (December 2023). Democratic members and arms-control groups objected; Republicans and pro-Israel groups welcomed the sales.

### Tariffs were applied to Israel and a trade arrangement followed.

**Verdict:** partly-accurate

Act of the US government; Israel's government sought lower rates. Grade: documented in trade and Israeli press and law-firm notes; the Federal Register order text (EO 14326, 31 Jul 2025, 'Further Modifying the Reciprocal Tariff Rates', published 6 Aug 2025) was located but its country annex was not read, so Israel's line rests on press. A US-Israel agricultural arrangement removes Israeli duties on many US fruit and vegetable products from 1 Jan 2026 (a Federal Register notice implementing it appeared on 8 Jan 2026), and a proclamation of 29 Dec 2025 extended tariff-rate-quota access for specified Israeli farm products through 31 Dec 2026. No signed comprehensive agreement was confirmed. A tariff-tracker claim of a 12.5% Section 301 duty from 24 Jul 2026 could not be verified and is not included. The 15% rate was a general rate set for many partners, not an Israel-specific act.

### Trump has publicly called for a pardon for Netanyahu, including by letter to President Herzog.

**Verdict:** confirmed

Public statements by Trump; Israeli President Isaac Herzog holds the pardon power and Netanyahu is on trial in Israel on corruption charges which he denies. Grade: documented by multiple outlets; the letter text is reproduced in part by outlets. Late Jun 2025: Truth Social posts calling the trial a 'political witch hunt'. 13 Oct 2025, Knesset address: 'Mr. President, why don't you give him a pardon?' ## Corrections

- Some summaries describe the March 2025 emergency as a single $4B package: the notifications behind it were about $2.04B (munitions) and $295M (bulldozers), with the roughly $4B figure referring to the wider fast-tracked aid as described by one outlet.
- The 2026 emergency sale is $151.8M for the notice in question; a figure of 'nearly $660 million' from one NYT-based report refers to two packages and does not match it.
- The West Bank settler order is often dated 24 Jan 2025 (OFAC action); the revocation of EO 14115 is in the 20 Jan 2025 rescissions order, with OFAC's follow-up on 24 Jan.
- Audit corrections (17 Nov 2026): the 28 Feb 2025 emergency determination appears in the notice texts for $2.04B and $675.7M, not for the $295M bulldozer notice; the March 2026 and May 2026 determinations are reported by outlets quoting State, not in the Federal Register text; the May 2026 APKWS notice ($992.4M, 1 May) is verified; the JLTV ($1.98B) and AW119Kx ($150M) values are filled; the ICC designation dates are set (13 Feb, 5 Jun, 20 Aug, 18 Dec 2025; 18 Aug and 9 Oct 2026); the 14-1 count for 18 Sep 2025 is sourced; the tariff entry now records the 20 Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling.

## Denials and context

- The administration describes sales as in the US national security interest and says the sales do not change the regional military balance. The notices list Foreign Military Financing (US assistance) as the funding source for every sale, with Israel's national funds added for some.
- Democratic members (for example Rep. Meeks) and arms-control groups objected to the use of emergency waivers; Republicans and pro-Israel groups welcomed them. Biden-era emergency waivers for Israel and the earlier hold on bombs show the instruments are not new.
- Not every decision favoured Israel's government: the Qatar security guarantee, Netanyahu's apology to Qatar, the annexation warnings, the 15% tariff (above the 10% Israel sought) and Israel's exclusion from the Apr 2026 US-Iran talks.
- Herzog's office disputes Trump's account that a pardon was promised. Netanyahu denies the charges and has called the trial a political persecution; critics in Israel and the US say Trump's pressure is interference in Israeli justice.
- This is a dated record only. Nothing here asserts that donations, investments or business ties of any person caused any decision; related money flows are in C110 and sibling checks.
