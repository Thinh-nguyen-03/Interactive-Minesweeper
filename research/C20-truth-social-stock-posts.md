# C20: Trump's stock purchases and Truth Social posts

**Verdict: mostly accurate** (confidence: medium)

## The claim

- TMTG is "largely owned" by the Donald J. Trump Revocable Trust, and it owns Truth Social.
- On Truth Social, Trump "pumps up" stocks and has named companies by their ticker while in office.
- "Hundreds of pages of financial disclosures" show the president bought equities before posting ("truthing") about them.
- His office says Trump did not buy them himself. Someone else did it on his behalf, so he could not have known about the trades in advance.
- He posted a meme of himself trading Intel stock in the Oval Office, and the price went up after he posted it.

## Evidence by sub-claim

### 1. The Revocable Trust is TMTG's largest owner, and TMTG owns Truth Social: confirmed
In December 2024, Trump moved his whole TMTG (DJT) stake, 114,750,000 shares or about 52% of the 220.7M shares then outstanding, into the Donald J. Trump Revocable Trust without payment. Trump is the trust's sole beneficiary and Donald Trump Jr. is its trustee [1][2]. Truth Social is TMTG's flagship platform. The share count has not changed, but the percentage has. TMTG sold 55,857,181 new shares in a private placement on 29 May 2025, so the same holding was 41.5% of 276,497,911 shares on 15 Dec 2025 (Schedule 13D/A) and about 41% in November 2026 (TAE merger prospectus). "Majority-owned" was true only until the end of May 2025. "Largely owned" by the largest shareholder remains a fair description.

### 2. Trump names companies and tickers in posts while in office: confirmed
On April 10, 2026, Trump posted praise of Palantir's "war fighting capabilities" and included the ticker "(PLTR)". Outlets described this as the first time a sitting president had endorsed a stock by its ticker [3][4]. Palantir had been down as much as 6% intraday. It briefly rose about 3% after the post and then closed lower [4]. Calling this "pumping" is a characterization.

### 3. Disclosures show purchases made before posts about those companies: confirmed, with nuance
Trump's Q1 2026 OGE Form 278-T runs 113 pages and lists about 3,642-3,711 transactions, with a total value between $220M and $750M. The annual disclosure for 2025, released on 30 June 2026, lists about 21,000 transactions in 2025 [5][6][7][8]. Among them are 327 purchases ($3.6M to $12.8M) in one investment account on 8 April 2025, the day before the tariff pause, and 273 purchases in the same account on 10 April. A reviewer's note on that filing says late fees were paid for transactions not previously reported. The White House and the Trump Organization say the accounts are fully discretionary and managed by independent third parties; the filing does not show who placed or directed any trade. See C102. Taken together, the filings plausibly add up to "hundreds of pages." The filing shows Palantir purchases of $247k-$630k in Q1 2026, including at least seven buys in March, a few weeks before the April 10 ticker post [3][9]. CBS News reported that some trades came before administration actions or before the president's own public statements about the companies [5].

There is also context that cuts the other way:
- The disclosures give only value ranges.
- The same filing shows a sale of up to $5M of Palantir on Feb. 10 [9].
- A CNN review found that most of the thousands of trades were never followed by a related post.
- Fortune's analysis says the pattern looks like large-scale direct indexing and tax-loss harvesting, not stock-picking [7].

No public evidence shows that Trump directed specific trades.

### 4. His side says others trade on his behalf: confirmed in substance
The Trump Organization says independent third-party investment managers run the portfolio and make all trading decisions without input from the family [5][6]. White House spokesman David Ingle said the assets sit in a trust managed by Trump's children and that "there are no conflicts of interest" [9]. Critics point out that this is not a blind trust. Trump certifies that he knows what the trust holds, and Rep. Don Beyer called Eric Trump's "blind trust" claim false [6][10]. NOTUS also reported that Trump missed STOCK Act 45-day reporting deadlines and paid a late fee [6].

### 5. Intel "trading in the Oval Office" meme, then the price rose: partly accurate
Trump posted the meme twice:
- **September 2025:** An AI image with no caption, posted around midnight, showed Trump at the Resolute Desk with trading screens reading "Bought Intel at $20" and "Intel now at $30." It referred to the government's 9.9% stake in Intel, bought at $20.47 a share under an August 22, 2025 agreement. It followed Nvidia's $5B investment in Intel [11][12]. Price reaction was mixed. Intel slipped slightly after hours and was down more than 2% the next premarket. The big jump that week (about 23%) came from the Nvidia deal, before the post [12][13].
- **September 2026 (posted 2026-09-06):** A similar image showed Intel going from $20 to $95, and Trump claimed he had made "hundreds of billions" for the US. Intel then rose more than 4% overnight, extending a three-session rally after a year-to-date gain of about 160% [14][15].

If the claim refers to the 2026 post, "the price went up" afterward is literally true, but nothing shows the post caused the rise. If it means the 2025 post, the claim is weak. In both posts the "trade" is the US government's stake, not Trump's personal holding. CNN separately reported that Trump's managed accounts bought Intel shortly before the government stake was announced, and the White House says Trump does not direct those trades [12]. The sarcastic remark "who knew the president would have such powers" implies the post caused the move, and that is not established.

## Corrections
- The Intel "Oval Office" meme is a meme about the US government's Intel stake, which Trump presents as a gain for the country. It does not show Trump trading personally.
- The documented ticker example is Palantir (PLTR) on April 10, 2026. Palantir closed lower that day, after a brief roughly 3% bounce.
- The "they trade on his behalf" defense comes mainly from the Trump Organization (independent managers). The White House version is that Trump's children manage the trust.

- Claimed: the trust holds about 52-53% of TMTG. The record shows: about 52% from December 2024 to the end of May 2025, and about 41% since (correction of 16 Nov 2026).

## Denials and context
- Trump Organization: independent third-party managers make all trades with no family input.
- White House (David Ingle): the assets are in a trust managed by his children, and "there are no conflicts of interest."
- Fortune and CNN: the trading pattern fits automated or indexed strategies, and most trades were never followed by a related post.
- CNBC (relayed by Mediaite) reported that several of the Q1 2026 Palantir buys were marked "unsolicited", meaning not made on a broker's or adviser's recommendation. The filing does not say who placed them, so this neither proves nor rules out Trump's involvement, but it sits awkwardly with the "independently managed" defense [16].
- Critics (CREW, Rep. Beyer, NOTUS): it is not a blind trust, filings were late, and Palantir has sponsored administration events and donated to the ballroom project.
