# C82: Abu Dhabi layers: unannounced Trump licensing entities, StringZ and the Delaware referral

**Verdict: mostly-accurate (confidence: medium)**

This is a second-layer connection hunt that builds on C63, C57 and C70 (and on C06 and C12) without repeating them. The entity names, formation dates, fee payments, the Khanna referral and the Warren/Kim CFIUS request are all documented. The weakest part of the brief is the hoped-for registry overlap between StringZ Holding RSC and Aryam Investment 1. I found no ADGM register entry and no shared officer, agent or address. That overlap is **unverified**, not disproven. No answer to the Khanna referral and no CFIUS outcome have been reported as of Nov 2026.

Method note: citizensforethics.org, forbes.com, thenationalnews.com and gondola.cc could not be fetched from here. CREW, Forbes and The National findings therefore come from search snippets and syndicated copies (Forbes regional editions, Times Brasil for CNBC, Forklog for the WSJ). The Delaware and ADGM registers could not be queried.

## Connections (sub-claims)

1. **DT Marks Abu Dhabi entities formed 30 Jan 2025.** *Documented.* Forbes found two Delaware companies, formed ten days after the inauguration and following the "DT Marks <place>" licensing template. The same filings pointed to unannounced deals in Abu Dhabi, Serbia and Hungary. The formation came 14 days after the Aryam signing. That proximity is **our observation (inferred)**: the entity was later paid by Damac, not by a Tahnoon vehicle.
2. **DT Marks Al Raha Beach LLC formed on the day of the data-centre permitting EO.** *Single-source (CREW).* This extends C70. Sajwani had pledged $20B or more for US data centres on 7 Jan 2025. CNBC found no evidence that Damac's payments influenced the order, and the order is general policy.
3. **Damac's two $5M fees for unannounced Abu Dhabi projects.** *Documented.* The payers were two Damac subsidiaries. CNBC notes the Trump Organization has no active Abu Dhabi developments. The National ties the fees to "an estate project at Abu Dhabi's Al Raha Beach". No project had been publicly launched by Nov 2026. Like Reliance's $10M Mumbai fee (C70), money was paid for a licence with no visible project.
4. **The Abu Dhabi partner switched.** *Documented for each element; the discrepancy is our observation.* In Dec 2024, Eric Trump and Dar Global's CEO presented Abu Dhabi as a Dar Global tower-and-golf project. The 2025 Abu Dhabi fees came from Damac instead. The same Dec 2024 Abu Dhabi trip preceded the Aryam signing by about five weeks. No source ties Eric's meetings there to Tahnoon's vehicles.
5. **StringZ registration and overlap with Aryam.** The April 2025 Abu Dhabi and May 2025 Delaware registrations are *single-source* (WSJ via Forklog). The ADGM register entry and any shared officers or addresses with Aryam are *unverified*. "RSC" is an ADGM restricted-scope company category. Aryam's own Delaware formation is unconfirmed (C63 flagged a non-news dossier claim). The two vehicles are linked only by their reported Tahnoon backing and the same 49% stake size.
6. **Al Raha Beach and the Tahnoon group.** The chain is *documented*: Aldar is a master developer of Al Raha, Alpha Dhabi holds about 30% of Aldar and is its parent, and IHC owns about 45% of Alpha Dhabi, inside Tahnoon's Royal Group. The link to the Trump project is *inferred and weak*. Al Raha Beach has several developers, and no source puts the Damac/Trump site on Aldar land.
7. **Khanna referral to the Delaware US Attorney.** *Documented.* The 4 Feb 2026 letter to Benjamin Wallace says at least one entity in Aryam's purchase is Delaware-registered and raises the foreign emoluments clause. The parallel request to Zach Witkoff had a 1 Mar deadline. As ranking member, Khanna has no subpoena power. No reply, opened matter or WLF production was found. The letter does not name which Delaware entity it means.
8. **Warren/Kim CFIUS request.** The request is *documented*; the outcome is *unverified*. No Treasury reply or CFIUS review was found through Nov 2026. No CFIUS request was found for StringZ's later stake in the bank parent, which the OCC handled through passivity commitments instead.
9. **Denials.** *Documented.* WLF (David Wachsman) says neither Trump nor Steve Witkoff had any involvement in the Aryam deal. Trump said "I know nothing about it". Damac and Dar Al Arkan did not respond to CNBC.

## Corrections

- The brief treats "DT Marks Abu Dhabi" and "Al Raha Beach" as one formation. They were formed about six months apart: 30 Jan 2025, and July 2025 per CREW.
- The Abu Dhabi fees came from Damac, not from Dar Global (the partner named in Dec 2024) and not from any Tahnoon vehicle.

## Not connected in the coverage we found

These links come from our own cross-referencing. No outlet asserts them.

- DT Marks Abu Dhabi was formed 14 days after the Aryam signing. *Inferred, timing only.*
- StringZ's Abu Dhabi registration (Apr 2025) sits beside MGX's USD1 deal (1 May 2025), and its Delaware registration (May 2025) came about eight months before the WLTC application. *Inferred, timing only.*
- Al Raha Beach's master developer, Aldar, sits under the Tahnoon-chaired IHC group. *Inferred; the corporate chain is documented, but its tie to the Trump project is not.*
- StringZ's "(DE)" entity as the "Emirati company domiciled in Delaware" in Khanna's letter. *Inferred.* The letter concerns the Aryam/WLF deal and names no entity.
- The Dar Global to Damac switch as the Abu Dhabi partner. *Inferred from documented statements and payments.*
- A shared officer, agent or address between StringZ and Aryam. *Not found.*

## What this does and does not show

It shows that the Trump Organization opened Abu Dhabi licensing vehicles in the same months that Abu Dhabi vehicles bought 49% stakes in WLF and its bank parent. It also shows that Damac paid $10M for Abu Dhabi licences with no public project. It does not show that Tahnoon's vehicles had any role in the licensing deals, that any US decision was traded for them, or that the StringZ and Aryam vehicles share management.
