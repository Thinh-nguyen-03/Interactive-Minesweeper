# C23: The SEC drops crypto-exchange cases, and the donors

**Verdict: mostly accurate (confidence: high on the facts, low on any causal link)**

## What the video says (transcript lines 139 and 651-655)
Molly White, in an interview segment, says that "basically every major cryptocurrency exchange," including Coinbase, Kraken, Binance and Gemini, faced SEC enforcement. She says "over a dozen lawsuits" against exchanges "were one by one dropped very shortly after Trump took office." She also says several of these exchanges gave "a million dollars or more" to Trump's campaign or inauguration fund, sponsored the military parade, and sponsored the White House ballroom. She frames this as something that "looks an awful lot like" a quid pro quo. Earlier, the narrator says SEC cases were "getting dropped left and right."

## Evidence by sub-claim

1. **Coinbase, Kraken, Binance and Gemini faced SEC enforcement. Confirmed.**
   - Coinbase: sued June 2023.
   - Kraken (Payward): sued November 2023.
   - Binance and Changpeng Zhao: sued June 2023.
   - Gemini: sued January 2023 over the Gemini Earn lending program, alongside Genesis. A separate SEC investigation of Gemini included a Wells notice in 2024.
2. **The cases were dropped one by one after Trump took office. Confirmed, with nuance on timing.**
   - Coinbase: the SEC dismissed the case with prejudice by joint stipulation on Feb 27, 2025 (SEC press release 2025-47).
   - Gemini: Cameron Winklevoss said the SEC closed its separate investigation of Gemini with no action on Feb 26, 2025.
   - Kraken: agreement in principle on Mar 3, 2025. The case was formally dismissed with prejudice on Mar 27, 2025, together with the Consensys and Cumberland DRW cases.
   - Binance: the case was stayed in February 2025 and dismissed with prejudice on May 29, 2025 (SEC litigation release LR-26316).
   - Ripple: the case was not dismissed. Both sides dropped their cross-appeals on Aug 7, 2025, and the $125M penalty and the injunction stand.
   - Gemini Earn: the case was stayed in April 2025. A resolution in principle came in September 2025, and a joint stipulation to dismiss with prejudice followed on Jan 23, 2026. The SEC cited the full return of investors' assets through the Genesis bankruptcy.
   - So the first wave came within about five to ten weeks of the inauguration, but some cases took up to a year. "Very shortly" fits Coinbase, Kraken and Gemini's investigation better than Binance, Ripple or Gemini Earn.
3. **"Over a dozen" cases. Mostly accurate, depending on what counts.**
   - The SEC's own FY2025 accounting lists 7 crypto enforcement actions formally dismissed between Feb 27 and May 29, 2025: Coinbase, Binance, Cumberland, Consensys, Kraken, Dragonchain and Balina. The Gemini Earn dismissal (January 2026) came later.
   - Decrypt counted at least 17 firms whose actions or investigations were ended, including closed probes: Robinhood, Uniswap, OpenSea, Crypto.com, Yuga Labs and Gemini.
   - A New York Times analysis, reported in secondary coverage, found that the SEC paused, dropped or dismissed about 60% of the crypto cases it inherited. I could not open the Times article.
   - "Over a dozen *lawsuits against exchanges*" overstates it: fewer than a dozen were lawsuits against exchanges specifically. "Over a dozen cases and probes against crypto companies" holds up.
4. **Several of these firms gave $1M or more to the campaign or inauguration. Confirmed for Coinbase, Kraken and Crypto.com, and for Gemini's founders. Not found for Binance.**
   - Coinbase: $1M to the inaugural committee.
   - Kraken: $1M to the inaugural committee, confirmed by a company representative.
   - Crypto.com: $1M to the inaugural committee (Bloomberg).
   - Ripple: about $4.9M to $5M in XRP to the inaugural committee.
   - Gemini: the company itself was not found among inaugural donors. Its founders, Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss, each gave about $1M in bitcoin to the Trump campaign in June 2024. The amount over the $844,600 limit (about $155,400 each) was refunded.
   - Binance: no direct donation to the campaign or inauguration found. Its Trump ties run through the MGX $2B investment settled in USD1 and the pardon of Changpeng Zhao (see C07 and C09).
5. **They sponsored the military parade. Confirmed for Coinbase only.**
   - Coinbase was a named sponsor of the June 14, 2025 Army 250th anniversary parade and festival, through a donation to America250, which is nonpartisan.
   - No other exchange was found among the parade sponsors.
   - Total sponsorship was reported at about $885K.
6. **They sponsored the ballroom. Confirmed.**
   - The White House's October 2025 list of 37 ballroom donors includes Coinbase, Ripple, Tether America, and Cameron and Tyler Winklevoss.
   - Kraken and Binance were not found on the list.
7. **The insinuation of a quid pro quo. Unproven.**
   - The timing and the donations are documented.
   - No evidence was found that any dismissal was a reward for a donation.

## Corrections
- Not all of these cases were "very shortly after" the inauguration. The Binance dismissal came in May 2025, the end of the Ripple case in August 2025, and the Gemini Earn dismissal in January 2026.
- Ripple's case was not dropped outright. The $125M judgment stands.
- Gemini's $1M gifts were personal campaign contributions from the Winklevoss twins in 2024, before the election, and part of them was refunded.
- No Binance donation to the campaign or inauguration was found.
- Of the exchanges, only Coinbase is documented as a sponsor of the parade.
- Formal SEC dismissals of crypto lawsuits number about 7 to 8. "Over a dozen" is only true if closed investigations are included.

## Denials and context
- The SEC said that political favoritism played no role and that its decisions rested on legal and policy considerations (as reported in coverage of the NYT analysis). Its dismissals said they were "in the exercise of its discretion and as a policy matter."
- The SEC later said its earlier crypto enforcement had "flaws."
- The industry argues that the Gensler-era cases were "regulation by enforcement," and that several courts had already split on the legal theories. In Ripple, the Torres ruling found that programmatic sales were not securities transactions.
- Coinbase says it supported the transition.
- Coinbase's parade sponsorship went through America250, which is nonpartisan.
- The ballroom donors include many non-crypto companies, such as Apple, Amazon and Google.
- Other donors on the same list did not have cases before the SEC. Some firms, such as Uniswap and OpenSea, had their probes dropped without any known Trump donation. This weakens a simple pay-to-play reading.
- On the other side, Public Citizen and Democrats argue that the industry's political spending "paid off."

## Audit (second-opinion pass, 2026-11-14)
**Result: upheld.**
- **Checks:** fresh searches re-checked the Gemini Earn dismissal with prejudice (Jan 23, 2026; Cointelegraph, Baker McKenzie) and the Apr 20, 2025 inaugural FEC filing. That filing shows Coinbase, Kraken/Payward and Crypto.com/Foris DAX at $1M each and Ripple at $4.8-5M (Benzinga, Cointelegraph).
- **Ballroom list:** the AP's ballroom donor list includes Coinbase, Ripple, Tether America and the Winklevoss twins. The AP calls the list incomplete, so Kraken's and Binance's absence means "not found", not "did not give".
- **Binance:** no Binance donation was found.
- **Changes:** none to the findings.
