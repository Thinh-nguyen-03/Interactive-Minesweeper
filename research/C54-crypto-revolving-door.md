# C54: The crypto revolving door

**Verdict: mostly-accurate** (confidence: medium)

 It is a follow-up lead to C35 (Lutnick/Tether), C25 (prediction markets/CFTC), C12 (OCC/WLF charter) and C34 (David Sacks). The claim: administration crypto officials have moved into industry, or come from it, and Trump family business partners hold government posts. The job is to verify each move with dates, ethics and cooling-off facts, and responses.

## Summary

Most of the named moves are on the record:

- **Bo Hines.** He left the White House crypto council on 9 Aug 2025 and joined Tether as an adviser on 19 Aug. On 12 Sep 2025 he became CEO of Tether's US arm (USAT).
  - The WSJ reported on 14 Oct 2026 that Tether had agreed in May 2025, while Hines was in office, to pay his co-owned firm Nxum $415K a month. About $6.3M was reportedly paid.
  - Sen. Blumenthal opened an inquiry on 22 Oct 2026.
- **CFTC leaders who left for industry.** Caroline Pham went to MoonPay and Summer Mersinger to the Blockchain Association.
- **Brian Quintenz.** His path ran from a16z and Kalshi, to a CFTC nomination that was withdrawn, to a seat on SUI Group's board.
- **Officials who came from industry.** Mike Selig (Willkie Farr crypto clients), Paul Atkins (Patomak, whose clients included FTX), Jonathan Gould (Bitfury) and Taylor Lindman (Chainlink) all brought industry ties into office. Sacks is covered in C34.
- **Family business partner in government.** Steve Witkoff co-founded WLF, became special envoy, and still held WLF assets at his Aug 2025 disclosure.

What is missing:

- No ethics waiver or post-employment record for Hines or Gould.
- No named SEC staffer who moved to Coinbase or a16z in 2025-26.
- No finding by any authority that any of these moves broke an ethics rule. The concerns rest on timing, payments and congressional letters.

## Sub-claims

### 1. Hines: White House crypto council to Tether/USAT CEO (confirmed)

- **In government.** Hines was executive director of the President's Council of Advisers on Digital Assets from January 2025. He announced on 9 Aug 2025 that he was leaving for the private sector.
- **Tether, 19 Aug 2025.** Tether named him strategic adviser for digital assets and US strategy, 10 days after he left. Some outlets called him "CEO of Tether USA" at this point; Bloomberg and others said adviser.
- **USAT, 12 Sep 2025.** Tether unveiled USAT and named him CEO of the US business, based in Charlotte. Anchorage Digital is the issuer, and Cantor Fitzgerald is reserve custodian and primary dealer (see C35).
- **Launch.** USAT launched on 27 Jan 2026.

### 2. Hines's ethics and cooling-off issues (partly-accurate)

The payments, per the WSJ of 14 Oct 2026 as summarised by IBTimes and AMBCrypto:

- Tether agreed in May 2025 to pay Nxum $415,000 a month for lobbying, PR and advisory work.
- Hines co-founded Nxum and kept a one-third stake while in government.
- Total payments were about $6.3M. Early payments went through a marketing firm, Notorious. Later ones, including two of $2.49M in December 2025 and April 2026, went directly to Nxum after Hines left.

The policy link: the WSJ says Hines pushed for the GENIUS Act's three-year transition period, which favoured Tether. Coverage stresses that the reporting does not show the money changed any provision.

The Senate inquiry:

- On 22 Oct 2026 Sen. Blumenthal, ranking member of the Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, wrote to Paolo Ardoino at Tether and to Todd Hines, Bo's father and Nxum's CEO.
- He asked for contracts, payment records and communications by 3 Nov 2026. One outlet gives 6 Nov.
- He says the arrangement implicates ethics, anticorruption and lobbying laws, and that the lobbying was never registered.

The other side:

- Nxum says Hines did not know about the deal and got no Tether money while in the White House.
- Tether says an external-counsel review found no wrongdoing.

No published waiver or 18 U.S.C. 207 determination for Hines was found.

### 3. Quintenz: Kalshi board and withdrawn CFTC nomination (confirmed)

- **Background.** He was a CFTC commissioner in Trump's first term, then a16z crypto's global head of policy. Reports put him on Kalshi's board since about 2021.
- **Nomination.** Trump nominated him to chair the CFTC in early 2025. He pledged to leave the Kalshi board if confirmed, and his Kalshi ties drew gaming-industry opposition.
- **Withdrawal.** The nomination stalled through a feud with the Winklevoss twins, who lobbied Trump against him; Quintenz published their texts. The White House withdrew it on 30 Sep 2025, reported 1 Oct. An official told NOTUS he "remains a trusted ally." Selig was nominated instead.
- **Since then.** He became an independent director and audit-committee member of SUI Group (Nasdaq: SUIG) effective 5 Jan 2026. In July 2026 the Coalition for Prediction Markets (members include Kalshi, Crypto.com, Coinbase, Robinhood and Underdog) named him a senior adviser (audit addition).
- **Caveat.** He never held office in this administration. This is an industry-to-nominee-to-industry path, not an official leaving for industry.

### 4. Selig's prior crypto clients (confirmed; client list rests on one outlet)

- **Clients.** As a Willkie Farr partner he represented eToro and Paradigm. The Intercept (26 Nov 2025), citing his ethics records, adds venture firms, a Cayman Islands blockchain company, Architect Financial Technologies and the Blockchain Association.
- **Path into office.** He was chief counsel of the SEC Crypto Task Force in 2025, was confirmed CFTC chair on 18 Dec 2025 (53-43) and was sworn in on 22 Dec 2025.
- **Ethics terms.** Per The Intercept, his ethics agreement requires only the standard one-year step-back from former clients.

### 5. Atkins's prior crypto clients and holdings (confirmed)

- **Patomak's clients.** They reportedly included banks, crypto exchanges and DeFi platforms. FTX signed on in early 2022.
- **Holdings.** His OGE disclosure, public on 25 Mar 2025, showed:
  - up to about $327M in family assets
  - up to about $6M in crypto-related assets: Anchorage Digital equity (about $1M), Securitize call options ($2.5-5M) and a small Pontoro stake
  - no direct crypto
- **Ethics pledge.** He committed to divest Patomak within 90 days of confirmation and to stay out of matters involving Patomak or its clients for one year.
- **The sale.** Warren asked on 6 Aug 2025 who bought his stake and for how much. No disclosure of either was found. The SEC says he complies with his ethics agreement.
- **Trump Media subpoena.** Barron's reported in August 2025 that defendants in a Trump Media lawsuit sought Patomak records on any TMTG engagement. TMTG called this "meritless."

### 6. Gould: OCC Comptroller, formerly at Bitfury (mostly-accurate)

- **Career.** OCC chief counsel 2018-21, then Bitfury chief legal officer from 2022, after an earlier stint as a Jones Day partner.
- **Confirmation.** Confirmed Comptroller 50-45 on 10 Jul 2025.
- **WLF charter.** His OCC gave World Liberty's trust-bank charter preliminary conditional approval on 14 Aug 2026 (C12).
- **Gap.** No public recusal or waiver record was found.

### 7. Sacks (confirmed; see C34)

He served as a special government employee under ethics waivers dated 5 Mar 2025 (crypto) and mid-2025 (AI) while keeping Craft Ventures stakes. He stepped down as czar around 26-27 Mar 2026.

### 8. SEC/CFTC departures to crypto firms (partly-accurate)

Departures to industry:

- **Caroline Pham.** MoonPay announced on 17 Dec 2025 that its new chief legal and administrative officer would be the acting CFTC chair, starting when her CFTC service ended. She will lead MoonPay's Washington policy strategy.
- **Summer Mersinger.** Her move to Blockchain Association CEO was announced on 14 May 2025, and she started on 2 Jun 2025. Her CFTC term ran to April 2028. Cointelegraph reported in September 2026 that she is leaving the association at year-end.
- **Brett Redfearn.** He was SEC Trading & Markets director in Trump's first term, then worked at Coinbase. He became Securitize's president in April 2026; Securitize is a former Atkins holding.

Moves into government from industry:

- **Taylor Lindman.** Chainlink's deputy general counsel became SEC Crypto Task Force chief counsel on 23 Feb 2026, succeeding Selig.

Not confirmed: any named 2025-26 SEC staff move to Coinbase or a16z. The often-cited count of 200+ officials (28 from the SEC, 15 from the CFTC) comes from a Tech Transparency Project study used in October 2022 congressional letters, so it predates this administration.

### 9. Hines's successor Patrick Witt (unverified onward move)

He was deputy, then executive director from August 2025, and had been a Pentagon technology and Office of Strategic Capital official. He announced his exit on 14 Oct 2026 as the CLARITY Act stalled, heading to the private sector. No employer was named.

### 10. Trump family business partners in government roles (mostly-accurate)

- **Steve Witkoff's role.** He co-founded WLF with the Trump sons, and his son Zach is WLF's CEO. He is now Special Envoy.
- **His holdings.** WLF said in May 2025 that he was divesting, but his 13 Aug 2025 disclosure still listed WLF crypto and WC Digital Fi LLC.
- **Congressional scrutiny.** Schiff and other senators queried OGE in October 2025, and Warren and Murphy opened an inquiry on 18 Nov 2025.
- **His response.** His spokesperson says he does not take part in matters affecting his finances.
- **Lutnick.** He is a second example (C35).
- **Gap.** No systematic list of family business partners in government posts was found.

## Corrections

- Quintenz never held office in this administration. His Kalshi seat predates his nomination, which was withdrawn on 30 Sep 2025.
- Hines was first a Tether adviser (19 Aug 2025) and became CEO of the US arm on 12 Sep 2025.
- The "200+ officials" count is a 2022 figure.
- The best-documented 2025-26 departures are from the CFTC (Pham, Mersinger), not SEC staff to Coinbase or a16z.

## Denials and context

- Nxum says Hines did not know about the deal and got no money while in office. Tether says a counsel review found no wrongdoing.
- The SEC says Atkins complies with his ethics agreement. TMTG called the Patomak subpoena inquiry meritless.
- Witkoff's office says he does not take part in matters affecting his interests. WLF says Trump and Witkoff had no role in the MGX deal.
- The one-year recusals for Selig and Atkins are the standard federal pledge. Critics argue one year is too short for officials writing rules for a whole industry.
- Hiring from industry is defended as bringing expertise. Crypto's revolving door predates 2025, as the 2022 Tech Transparency Project study shows.
- No authority has found that any of these moves violated an ethics rule.

- The undisclosed buyer of Atkins's Patomak stake.
