# C38: Polymarket probes dropped before 1789 Capital invested; federal push for prediction markets

**Verdict: mostly-accurate** (confidence: medium)

The dated facts in the claim hold up:
- The DOJ and the CFTC closed their Polymarket probes by 2025-07-15, with no charges.
- That was about six weeks before 1789 Capital invested on 2025-08-26 and Don Jr. joined Polymarket's advisory board.
- Selig's CFTC sued AZ, CT, IL, NY and WI in April 2026, and later MN and UT.
- Sen. Curtis asked the Senate Judiciary Committee to investigate on 2026-09-21.

Three things are not established:
- Who decided to close the probes.
- Any link between the closure and the Trump family.
- White House "lobbying" beyond one reported briefing of North Carolina lawmakers.

## Origin
This check builds on C25 (prediction markets, Don Jr.) and C26 (1789 Capital). C25 already covered these points, and they are not repeated here:
- Don Jr.'s Kalshi and Polymarket roles.
- The McGovern–Selig exchange.
- Trump's post of 2026-05-26.
- Don Jr.'s March 2026 pitch to GOP AGs.

## Sub-claims

### 1. The DOJ and CFTC closed their Polymarket probes in July 2025: confirmed
- **The report:** Bloomberg reported on 2025-07-15 that Polymarket had received formal declination notices from both agencies [1][2].
- **Who ran the probe:** According to CNBC, the criminal inquiry was run by the Manhattan U.S. Attorney's Office (SDNY). It declined to comment [3].
- **Polymarket's account:** CEO Shayne Coplan said the company was "cleared of any wrongdoing" [4].
- **No official confirmation:** Neither agency has confirmed the closure publicly.
- **Background:**
  - The probes began after the 2024 election, under Biden, and looked at US users who traded despite the CFTC's 2022 settlement (a $1.4M penalty).
  - The FBI searched Coplan's home in November 2024.
  - No one was charged.

### 2. The closure came about a month before 1789 invested and Don Jr. joined the board: confirmed (about six weeks)
- **Dates:** The probes closed on 2025-07-15. The investment and board seat were announced on 2025-08-26 [5][6].
- **Amount:** Not disclosed. Reuters reported "double-digit millions" and Axios "eight figures" [5][7].
- **How the deal came about:** Axios reported that founder Omeed Malik had been in talks with Coplan for about 18 months, and that 1789 waited for a clear legal path to US operations [5]. That sequence suggests the investment followed the relief. No reporting shows the investment or Don Jr. causing the relief.
- **What followed:**
  - Polymarket bought QCEX for $112M (July 2025).
  - A CFTC staff no-action letter (about 2025-09-03) cleared its US return [8][9].
  - In August 2026, 1789 confirmed a planned ~$300M more in a $1B round at a $21B valuation, on top of ~$200M already invested [10].

### 3. Who decided to close the probes: unverified
- **No named decision-maker:** No document or official names one, and the declination letters are not public.
- **Who was in charge:**
  - The DOJ was under the Trump administration at the time.
  - The CFTC was led by Acting Chair Caroline Pham. Selig was confirmed only on 2025-12-18.
- **McGovern's question:** Rep. McGovern asked Selig on 2026-04-16 about White House pressure. Selig called the suggestion "insulting" [11].
- **Evidence of intervention:** None found for the July 2025 closures.

### 4. The White House, not only Don Jr., lobbied state officials: partly-accurate
- **What the NYT reported (2026-08-27):** The White House Office of Intergovernmental Affairs gave North Carolina lawmakers information on federal policy toward state regulation [12][13][14].
- **Who said so:** A spokesman for NC House Speaker Destin Hall, not the White House.
- **Industry role:** The same story says Kalshi lobbyist Jim Harrell, a former legislator, helped shape the NC provision.
- **How far it goes:** One briefing of one state's lawmakers is documented. "Lobbied" goes further than the record. The administration's public push is clear (Trump's post and the CFTC/DOJ suits).
- **White House response:** No response specific to the story was found. Separately, White House spokeswoman Anna Kelly said "There are no conflicts of interest" [15].

### 5. Selig's CFTC sued AZ, CT, IL, NY and WI: confirmed (the list is incomplete)

| State | Filed | Details |
|---|---|---|
| Arizona, Connecticut, Illinois | 2026-04-02 | [16][17] |
| New York | 2026-04-24 | SDNY. Defendants: Gov. Hochul, AG Letitia James and the Gaming Commission [18] |
| Wisconsin | 2026-04-28 | E.D. Wis. Defendants: Gov. Evers, AG Kaul and gaming administrator Dillett [19] |
| Minnesota | 2026-05-19 | Over the first outright state ban [20] |

- **Total count:** A tracker cited by The New Republic counts nine states [22], and one report calls Utah the 10th.
- **The courts are split:**
  - The 3rd Circuit sided with Kalshi (April 2026).
  - The 9th Circuit (2026-08-28, Nevada) and the 6th Circuit (2026-09-25, Ohio and Tennessee) sided with the states [23][24].
- **Also in August:** On 2026-08-11 the CFTC used emergency authority to keep Kalshi operating after New York sued it [25].

### 6. Sen. Curtis sought a Senate Judiciary probe: confirmed
- **The letter:** On 2026-09-21, Curtis (R-Utah) wrote to Chair Grassley and Ranking Member Durbin. He asked for an investigation into whether presidential family members profited from access [26][27][28].
- **Subpoenas:** He urged subpoenas for **both Donald Trump Jr. and Hunter Biden**. The claim leaves this out.
- **What he cited:** Don Jr.'s Kalshi and Polymarket roles, 1789's stake, crypto, foreign real estate and defense deals.
- **Outcome:** No committee action was found.

## Corrections
- "About a month" was about six weeks (2025-07-15 to 2025-08-26).
- The CFTC sued more than five states: MN and UT also, and nine or ten in total by trackers' counts.
- The documented White House outreach is a briefing of NC lawmakers, as described by the NC Speaker's spokesman.
- Curtis's request also targeted Hunter Biden.

## Denials and the other side
- **Polymarket:** It was cleared of wrongdoing, and the Biden-era raid was politically motivated.
- **DOJ, CFTC and SDNY:** Declined to comment.
- **Don Jr.'s spokesperson:** He "does not interface with the federal government on behalf of any company he invests in or advises," and does not trade on prediction markets.
- **Selig:** The CFTC treats all market participants alike.
- **Kalshi:** The NYT story was one-sided, and Don Jr. advises on marketing, not regulation.
- **What remains insinuation:** The idea that the closure was a favor linked to the later investment. No reporting documents that.

## Audit (second-opinion pass, Nov 2026)

Result: **upheld**.

- **Probe closures.** Several outlets (CNBC via NBC, The Block, Decrypt, Bloomberg Law) agree that formal notices came in early July 2025 and were reported on 15 Jul 2025. Every account rests on Bloomberg and CNBC sources and on Polymarket's own statements. The DOJ and CFTC declined to comment, so the write-up's caution stands.
- **CFTC state suits.** A late-Oct 2026 report (DeFi Rate) counts Utah as the 10th state, D. Utah No. 2:26-cv-01055. It lists the others as Arizona, Connecticut, Illinois, Kentucky, Minnesota, New Mexico, New York, Rhode Island and Wisconsin, and says the CFTC won preliminary injunctions in Arizona, Minnesota and Illinois. This is a single report and was not independently confirmed.
- **Curtis letter.** Confirmed (SBC Americas, 23 Sep 2026). Besides the prediction-market ties, the letter cites Don Jr.'s acceptance of wedding gifts from Umar Kremlev and his promotion of the family's crypto ventures. It says it does not allege that he broke any law. No committee action was found.
