# C32: Don Jr.'s other government-contractor ties (Unusual Machines, 1789 portfolio)

**Verdict: partly accurate** (confidence: medium)

The best-documented part of this lead is Unusual Machines. Donald Trump Jr. joined its advisory board on November 27, 2024 and was issued 331,580 shares. About eleven months later, on October 15, 2025, the company announced its largest US government order: 3,500 drone motors for the Army's 101st Airborne Division. In May 2026 it was reported to be in talks for a Pentagon equity-stake deal under the $1.1B Drone Dominance program.

The 1789 Capital part holds up less well. The holdings themselves are real: SpaceX, xAI, Anduril and Cerebras are listed as portfolio companies, and 1789 led Firehawk Aerospace's $60M Series C. Firehawk has since won small Air Force and Army awards. The audit found that large awards also came after 1789's stakes were public: Anduril's Army enterprise agreement (ceiling up to $20B, March 2026) and the SpaceX and Anduril Golden Dome interceptor awards (April 2026). Those awards were competitive or multi-vendor, the companies had large government businesses before 1789 invested, and no reporting ties any award to 1789 or Don Jr.

The Executive Branch club is documented as a Don Jr. / 1789 venture. No record found connects it to a federal contract.

Every denial on record says Don Jr. did not intervene, and no reporting found shows that he did.

**Research note.** The original pages at citizensforethics.org and dronexl.co were not read directly. The research ended before every thread was checked, so I could not run searches on Cerebras/CFIUS, the Anduril/xAI/SpaceX contracts, or 1789's own statements. Findings below rest on search-result summaries from several outlets (NBC/CNBC, FT via TipRanks/The Fly, The Intercept, Quartz, Aviation Week, Fortune, Fox5, CREW) and on the parent report C26 (ProPublica). Anything I could not confirm is marked unverified.

## The claim
It suggests that Don Jr. holds advisory roles or stakes in other companies that won federal contracts or policy benefits after he joined. The examples it gives are Unusual Machines (an Army contract), the 1789 portfolio companies Anduril, SpaceX/xAI, Firehawk and Cerebras, and the Executive Branch club. The video itself covers only 1789/Vulcan (C26), the New America SPAC (C27) and Eric Trump's ties to Foundation (C28).

## Evidence by sub-claim

1. **Don Jr. joined Unusual Machines as an adviser and shareholder in November 2024: confirmed.**
   - The company announced him as an advisory-board member on November 27, 2024, and its stock closed about 85% higher that day.
   - The S-1 lists 331,580 shares: 131,580 from a private placement at $1.52 per unit, and 200,000 under an RSU/advisory agreement [1][2].
   - The terms of the advisory agreement were not disclosed; the CFO declined to comment on them [2].
   - The Intercept (July 2025) reported that only two executives held more shares than he did [3].
   - Some 2024 coverage of the share offering said he then held no shares, which conflicts with later reporting. The FT put his stake at about $4M in October 2025 [4].

2. **Unusual Machines won an Army contract after he joined: confirmed. The amount is unverified.**
   - On October 15, 2025, the company announced an order for 3,500 NDAA-compliant motors and components for the 101st Airborne's Attritable Battlefield Enabler (A.B.E.) V1.01 drones.
   - CEO Allan Evans called it the company's largest US government order but would not disclose its value.
   - The Army said it planned to order another roughly 20,000 parts in 2026 [4][5][6].
   - The order came about 11 months after Don Jr. joined.
   - A $12.8M figure appears in one aggregator. It is unconfirmed and conflicts with the CEO's refusal to give a value.
   - The Army did not comment.

3. **Don Jr. helped secure the Unusual Machines contract: unverified (no evidence).**
   - A Trump Jr. spokesperson told the FT that he had never communicated with anyone in the administration on behalf of Unusual Machines or about the contract. The company also denied that he was involved [4][7]. Search summaries attribute this wording to the FT, but I could not read the original article to confirm it.
   - CREW found no evidence of a clear quid pro quo. It argues that the appearance of preferential treatment is itself the problem [8].
   - Context: the 2025 DJI restrictions and the Pentagon's drone push give Unusual Machines a policy tailwind. Don Jr. has publicly championed both, but that is policy, not a documented intervention [3].

4. **Unusual Machines is in line for further federal benefits (equity stake, loan): mostly accurate, but nothing is finalized.**
   - In May 2026, Quartz and others reported that the Pentagon's National Economic Resilience Office was negotiating equity-stake funding with Performance Drone Works, Unusual Machines and Neros under the $1.1B Drone Dominance program [9][10].
   - ProPublica (cited in C26) reported that Unusual Machines was under review for its own Pentagon loan [11].
   - I found no report that either deal had closed. A July 2026 prediction-market write-up still treated "no stake this year" as the likeliest outcome.

5. **Don Jr.'s Unusual Machines advisory role is ongoing: unverified.** CREW reports that his advisory agreement appears to have expired automatically after one year, by which point the company had become an Army supplier [8]. I could not confirm whether he still holds the shares or the role. *Audit:* 2026 coverage, including the WSJ via Quartz in May 2026 [9] and IBTimes, still calls him a shareholder and advisory-board member. That conflicts with CREW, and no filing settling the question was found.

6. **1789 Capital's portfolio includes Anduril, SpaceX, xAI, Cerebras and Firehawk: mostly accurate.**
   - Firehawk is firmly documented. 1789 led its $60M Series C, reported at about a $290M valuation [12][13]. Outlets disagree on whether the round was in January or September 2025.
   - SpaceX, xAI, Anduril and Cerebras are listed as 1789 holdings by investor databases and trade press [14][15]. Several of those sources are aggregators.
   - Don Jr. became a partner in November 2024 (see C26). Axios reports that he is not on the growth fund's investment committee.

7. **Firehawk won federal awards after 1789's investment: partly accurate.**
   - Firehawk received a $4M Air Force TACFI contract [16].
   - In August 2026 it was named one of four companies to receive Army prototype agreements for the Hydra-70 rocket (AGR-21C) [17].
   - It also holds an Army Applications Lab SBIR Phase III contract and earlier AFRL and AFWERX awards of about $4.9M and $1.2M. Some of these predate 1789's round.
   - Hanwha Defense USA made a strategic investment later.
   - The amounts are small, and nothing ties them to Don Jr.

8. **Anduril, SpaceX/xAI and Cerebras won contracts or policy benefits because of, or timed to, Don Jr.'s involvement: partly accurate (changed from unverified at audit). Timing is documented; causation is not.**
   - *Audit additions (Nov 2026):*
     - **Anduril.** On March 13, 2026 the Army signed a 10-year enterprise agreement with Anduril with a ceiling of up to $20B. It consolidates about 120 existing contracts, the ceiling is not an obligated amount, and the first task order was about $87M [A2][A3]. 1789's Anduril stake was public by mid-May 2025 [A5].
     - **SpaceX and Anduril.** Both were among the 12 companies in the Space Force's Golden Dome space-based-interceptor awards in April 2026, worth up to $3.2B in total [A4]. Accountable.US had flagged in May 2025 that 1789-linked firms were positioned for Golden Dome [A5].
     - **xAI.** In July 2025 the CDAO gave xAI a prototype deal with a $200M ceiling. It was one of four identical awards, alongside OpenAI, Google and Anthropic [A6]. When 1789 invested in xAI is not documented.
     - **Cerebras.** 1789 joined Cerebras's $1.1B Series G on September 30, 2025 [A1]. The CFIUS clearance for G42's stake was announced in March 2025, before 1789 invested. Cerebras listed on Nasdaq in May 2026, and I found no federal contract tied to the investment.
     - **What this shows.** These awards were competitive or went to several vendors, and no source ties any of them to 1789 or Don Jr.
   - Anduril is building the YFQ-44 autonomous fighter for the Air Force, and all of these companies hold major federal business or face federal regulators [14].
   - I found no reporting that dates any specific award or regulatory decision relative to 1789's investment, and none linking one to Don Jr.
   - These are late-stage companies in which 1789 holds minority stakes, and their government relationships predate the fund.
   - This part of the claim is an inference, not a documented claim.

9. **The Executive Branch club is a Don Jr. venture tied to government benefits: partly accurate.**
   - The club itself is documented. It is an invite-only club in Georgetown, Washington DC, co-founded by Don Jr. with 1789's Omeed Malik and Chris Buskirk and with Alex and Zach Witkoff. Membership is reported at about $500,000, and members are vetted by the founders [18][19][20].
   - Its April 2025 launch reportedly drew at least six members of the administration along with CEOs and tech founders [19][21].
   - CREW notes that a 1789-co-hosted summit ended with a dinner at the club [8].
   - I found no documented contract, grant or policy benefit flowing to the club or arising from it.
   - Its founders say it "isn't Donald Trump's club" and that most members are private citizens.

## Corrections
- The claim lists the Executive Branch club alongside government contractors. It is a private members' club, and no federal contract or benefit to it was found.
- The value of the Unusual Machines Army order was never disclosed. Treat any dollar figure, including $12.8M, as unconfirmed.
- *(Corrected at audit.)* An earlier version said only Firehawk had federal awards after 1789's investment. That was wrong. Anduril's Army enterprise agreement (ceiling up to $20B, March 2026) and the SpaceX and Anduril Golden Dome interceptor awards (April 2026) also came after 1789's stakes were public. Those awards were competitive or multi-vendor, the companies had large government businesses before 1789, and nothing ties the awards to 1789 or Don Jr.

## Denials and context
- **Trump Jr. spokesperson:** Don Jr. "has never communicated with anyone in the administration on behalf of Unusual Machines or about the contract in question." This is the wording reported in summaries of the FT story [4][7].
- **Unusual Machines:** the company denies that he played any role in the Army order.
- **1789 Capital:** the firm describes itself as a passive minority growth-equity investor that does not lobby the government (see C26). A Trump Jr. spokesperson says he does not discuss portfolio companies with federal officials.
- **Ethics experts and CREW:** there is no legal bar on the president's son holding these roles. The concern is the appearance of an edge with the administration, and CREW found no clear quid pro quo [8][22].
- **Context in their favor:** drone motors free of Chinese parts and solid-rocket supply are genuine Pentagon shortfalls, and the Army orders went to small, NDAA-compliant suppliers.
