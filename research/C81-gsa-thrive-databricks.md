# C81: A federal procurement chief, Thrive Capital and sole-source awards

**Verdict: mostly-accurate (confidence: medium).** The procurement side is documented in contract records, congressional letters and several outlets. The personal-stake side rests on one WSJ report (late May 2026) that we saw only through aggregators. Some details in the brief are wrong or unsupported: the "$18M" figure, a 2026 "Connolly probe" and a Thrive stake in Anthropic.

**Disclosure.** Anthropic makes the model that wrote this report, and it is one of the companies with a GSA OneGov deal. It is treated here exactly like every other vendor. We found no primary source for a Thrive stake in Anthropic, and the report says so.

**Method note.** This report builds on C66 (sub-claim 11, the Thrive/GSA link) and C68 (the OpenAI and Musk/xAI nexus). Fetching Washington Technology, SamSearch, distillintelligence and valorep.com failed here because the hosts were not reachable. The WSJ original could not be reached either. Findings therefore rely on search-result summaries from several outlets, and each one is graded below.

## Connections found

### 1. Gruenbaum, Ramp and SmartPay (documented)
- ProPublica (2025-04-17) reported that senior GSA officials met Ramp executives at least four times before a public SmartPay opportunity opened, and that FAS commissioner Josh Gruenbaum organized some of those meetings.
  - The RFI was dated 2025-03-20.
  - The pilot was worth up to $25M, inside a program the coverage calls roughly $700B.
- Ramp's backers:
  - Founders Fund (Peter Thiel) is Ramp's first and largest investor.
  - Thrive took part in Ramp's June 2025 Series E ($16B valuation) and later rounds.
  - Bloomberg sources say Thrive and Dragoneer co-led a round of about $1.85B at about $60B in Oct 2026. Ramp has not confirmed it.
- GSA rejected any suggestion of preferential contracting.
- Gruenbaum's background: almost five years at KKR (director, credit team) and earlier at Moelis.
- We found no award to Ramp.

### 2. Connolly's probe (documented, with a correction)
- On 2025-05-09 Rep. Gerald Connolly wrote to Acting GSA Administrator Stephen Ehikian. He asked for:
  - Ramp's bids
  - the RFI timeline
  - communications with officials tied to the President and DOGE
- Connolly died on 2025-05-21. We found no public GSA reply.
- Some 2026 aggregators describe Connolly as "probing" the Thrive investment. That is a mistake: his letter came a year before the WSJ disclosure and does not mention Thrive.

### 3. Gruenbaum's Thrive investment (single-source)
- **What the WSJ reported** (as relayed by Crypto Briefing 2026-05-31, Edgen, EconoTimes and Wikipedia):
  - In March 2025 Gruenbaum put $100,001-$250,000 through a private wealth manager into a special-purpose vehicle tied to Databricks.
  - The vehicle is described as a $194M Thrive vehicle.
  - Some senior GSA officials did not know about the investment.
- **What Gruenbaum says:** he disclosed it, got ethics approval and recused himself. He gave the WSJ documentation.
- **What we could not check:** his OGE 278 form.
- Nobody disputes that the investment exists.

### 4. Databricks sole-source order (award documented; the link to his stake is single-source)
- **The order:** GSA's Technology Transformation Service, which sits inside FAS, posted a sole-source justification on 2026-02-10.
  - Order 47QPCA26F0011, "Databricks Engineering Services," under MAS contract 47QTCA25D00D3
  - Legal basis cited: FAR 8.401(b) and GSAR 538.7104-3(b)(3)
  - Fixed price, five months, ceiling **$1,824,000** (HigherGov)
- **Thrive's stake:** Thrive led Databricks's $10B Series J in Dec 2024 at a $62B valuation.
- **What this does not show:**
  - No source says Gruenbaum signed or steered the order.
  - The amount is immaterial to Databricks.
  - The issue is the appearance of a conflict and whether the recusal worked.

### 5. Gruenbaum's exit (departure documented; the reason is single-source)
- **Documented:** Federal News Network, Nextgov and MeriTalk reported in April 2026 that Gruenbaum was leaving FAS. Administrator Ed Forst named Laura Stanton acting commissioner.
- **Single-source** (WSJ via Edgen, citing people familiar): Forst blocked a ~$25M Ramp award in April and asked Gruenbaum to go. Gruenbaum says the exit was planned and voluntary.
- **Unresolved timing:** a SAM.gov mirror shows GSA cancelled the commercial-payments CSO (47QRAB25R0002) on 2026-02-11 and shifted to next-generation SmartPay. That makes the "April block" hard to place.
- GSA named a permanent FAS chief in Nov 2026.

### 6. OneGov and Thrive holdings (partly documented)
- Gruenbaum's FAS ran OneGov. The deals:

| Company | Deal | Date |
|---|---|---|
| OpenAI | ChatGPT Enterprise, $1 per agency | 2025-08-06 |
| Anthropic | Claude, $1 | 2025-08-12 |
| Google | Gemini for Government | 2025-08 |
| Meta | Llama | 2025-09 |
| xAI | (deal confirmed; no details found) | — |
| Perplexity | $0.25 per agency, the first direct-to-government deal | 2025-11-19 |
| OpenAI | Follow-on, consumption-based deal, after he left | 2026-09-10 |

- In the OpenAI release, Gruenbaum urged other AI firms to "follow OpenAI's lead."
- **Thrive's stake in each:**
  - OpenAI: documented (C66). OpenAI also took a stake in Thrive Holdings on 2025-12-01.
  - Perplexity: appears in aggregator portfolio tables only.
  - Anthropic: unverified. Anthropic's Series E and F announcements name ICONIQ, Lightspeed, Fidelity and others. Only one aggregator, F4 Fund, lists Thrive.
- **Limits:**
  - No source alleges any OneGov impropriety.
  - Vendors with no Thrive ties got similar deals.
  - Deals priced at $1 or $0.25 bring little direct revenue.

### 7. Family layer beyond Josh Kushner (roles documented; the overlap is inferred)
- While running FAS, Gruenbaum helped Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff negotiate the Gaza ceasefire (Jewish Insider; Politico via Raw Story).
- In Jan 2026 the White House named him a senior adviser to the Trump-chaired Board of Peace. Kushner sits on its executive board.
- He was later dropped from the Ukraine and Iran files. Sources say Kushner and Witkoff are still his allies.
- In June 2026 he left government but stayed on as an unpaid Board of Peace adviser.
- **Inferred, not sourced:** the official holding a stake in Josh Kushner's fund was at the same time working under Josh's brother Jared. Jared divested his Thrive interests in 2017 (C66). No source gives Jared any role in the investment or in the contracts.

### 8. Revolving door to Valor (single-source)
- Valor Equity Partners' own team page (seen only as a search snippet) says Gruenbaum joined Valor in 2026 as a Managing Director. Jewish Insider said in June that he was set to join an unnamed investment firm.
- Valor's founder, Antonio Gracias, a Musk ally, ran DOGE work at the Social Security Administration until 2025-07-01.
- Gruenbaum came into GSA with the Musk-linked DOGE contingent.

## Corrections
- The Databricks award ceiling is $1.824M, not $18M.
- Connolly's only action was the 2025-05-09 letter about Ramp. He died on 2025-05-21.
- The Databricks order was issued by TTS, within FAS. No source says Gruenbaum signed it.
- A Thrive stake in Anthropic is not supported by primary sources.
- Founders Fund, not Thrive, is Ramp's lead investor.

## Denials and context
- **Gruenbaum:** disclosed the investment, got ethics approval and recused himself. He says his departure was voluntary.
- **Thrive:** neither Thrive nor Josh Kushner discussed government contracts with him.
- **Ramp:** says it was unaware of the investment.
- **GSA:** denied preferential treatment.
- **GSA OIG:** its Oct 2025-Mar 2026 semiannual report has no item on the matter.
- A documented relationship is not proof of wrongdoing.

## Not connected in the coverage we found
- **Gruenbaum's Thrive stake and his work for Jared Kushner on Gaza.** *Inferred.* Both are documented separately; no outlet links them.
- **The Databricks order and the CSO cancellation one day apart (2026-02-10 and 2026-02-11).** *Inferred.* This is a timing coincidence only.
- **OpenAI's $1 OneGov deal (Aug 2025) and OpenAI's stake in Thrive Holdings (Dec 2025).** *Inferred.* No source ties them.
- **Gruenbaum's move to Valor and his DOGE origins with Gracias.** *Single-source* for the hire. The DOGE link is *inferred* as a pathway.
- **Thrive and Anthropic.** *Not found* in primary sources. We discard it.

## Audit (second review, Nov 2026)

Result: **upheld**. Re-checked: the Databricks order ceiling; the WSJ report of Gruenbaum's Thrive stake; his recusal.

- **Databricks order.** Order 47QPCA26F0011 (Databricks Federal LLC, TTS, posted 10 Feb 2026, five months, one bidder) has a ceiling of $1,824,000 in HigherGov, govcontractfinder and samsearch. The "$18M" figure is still wrong.
- **Thrive stake.** The WSJ's own Threads post says disclosure documents show Gruenbaum invested in "a Thrive Capital fund" while its portfolio companies were up for contracts. An Investing.com relay ("DOGE official discussed contracts tied to firm he invested in, WSJ reports") confirms that the WSJ published the story. It is still one outlet's reporting, so the rating stays unverified. Note that the WSJ post says "fund", while relays describe a Databricks-linked SPV.
- **Recusal and Ramp.** A GSA official told the WSJ that Gruenbaum recused himself and did not advocate for Ramp specifically; this is added to the denials. A contracting-news summary (single-source) reports an internal GSA review of his Ramp communications in Feb 2026.
